Introducing Loci

You now know how to bisect a line and an angle exactly. A locus is a wider idea: the set of every point that satisfies a rule - and today's rules give shapes you may not expect: circles, parallel lines, and racetracks.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A fixed distance d from a point gives a circle of radius d.
  • From a straight line, a pair of parallel lines, one either side, each d away.
  • From a line segment, a racetrack: two parallel segments joined by a semicircle of radius d at each end.
  • From a polygon, its outline with corners rounded into arcs of radius d.
By the end you can
G4Construct the locus of points at a fixed distance from a point, from a straight line, and around a shape.
G5Construct a line parallel to a given line at a fixed distance using the equidistant principle.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Locus of a fixed distance from a point

The locus of points d from a point A is a circle of radius d.

The locus of points a fixed distance d from a point A is a circle of radius d, drawn with compasses set to d.

Locus of a fixed distance from a straight line

The locus of points d from an infinite line is two parallel lines. There is one on each side, each d away.

The locus of points a fixed distance d from a straight (infinite) line is a pair of parallel lines, one on each side, each a distance d from the original. Constructing one: mark two points on the line, draw a perpendicular at each a distance d along it, then join.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Point B is fixed. Construct the locus of points 3 cm from B.
Compassesfixed at 3 cm, point on B
Locusthe whole circle of radius 3 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A straight line is drawn. Construct the locus of points 2 cm from the line, on one side.
Two pointsmark any two points on the line
Perpendiculars2 cm long at each of them, on the chosen side
Locusjoin the two ends, giving a line parallel to the original
YOUR TURN · a
Point C is fixed. Construct the locus of points 5 cm from C. (Hint: this needs just one setting of the compasses.)
a circle of radius 5 cm centred on C - every point on it is exactly 5 cm from C.
YOUR TURN · b
A straight line is drawn. Construct the locus of points 4 cm from the line, on both sides.
two straight lines parallel to the original, one 4 cm above it and one 4 cm below.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Locus around a line segment (racetrack)

A line segment has two ends. Its locus is two parallel sides, joined by a semicircle at each end.

Unlike an infinite line, a line segment has two ends. The locus of points a fixed distance d from it is a 'racetrack' shape: two straight parallel segments of length equal to the original, joined at each end by a semicircle of radius d, centred on the segment's endpoints.

Locus around a polygon

Around a polygon, the locus runs parallel to each side. At each corner it rounds into an arc of radius d.

The locus of points a fixed distance d from a polygon follows the same idea at every edge and corner: parallel straight sections along each side, with the corners rounded into arcs of radius d, centred on each vertex.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A line segment DE is 6 cm long. Construct the locus of points 2 cm from it.
Sidestwo segments 6 cm long, 2 cm either side of DE
Endsa semicircle of radius 2 cm centred on D, and one centred on E
Locusthe racetrack those four pieces make
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Construct the locus of points 1 cm outside a square of side 2 cm.
Sidesa straight section 1 cm out from each side of the square
Cornersa quarter circle of radius 1 cm centred on each vertex
Locusthe rounded outline those eight pieces make
YOUR TURN · c
A line segment FG is 5 cm long. Construct the locus of points 1.5 cm from it. (Hint: the two straight parts of your racetrack are the same length as FG.)
a racetrack: two straight lines 1.5 cm from FG, one each side and each 5 cm long, joined by a semicircle of radius 1.5 cm around F and another around G.
YOUR TURN · d
A rectangle 6 cm by 3 cm is drawn. Construct the locus of points 1 cm from it, outside the rectangle.
a rounded rectangle: a line 1 cm outside each of the four sides (6 cm, 3 cm, 6 cm, 3 cm long) joined by a quarter circle of radius 1 cm at each corner.

Exit ticket

On paper, on your own, then check
1
What is the locus of points 4 cm from a single fixed point?
A circle of radius 4 cm, centred on the point.
2
What shape is the locus of points at a fixed distance from a line segment (not an infinite line)?
A 'stadium' (racetrack) shape - two parallel straight segments joined by a semicircle at each end.
3
At each corner of a polygon, why is the fixed-distance locus rounded rather than sharp?
Because the locus stays a fixed distance from whichever point of the polygon is nearest, and at a corner that nearest point sweeps through an arc rather than jumping straight across.
