Measuring and Reading Scales

Rulers and scaled diagrams are only as accurate as the person reading them. Today you learn to read any scale correctly, whether it is a ruler, a dial, or a scaled drawing.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A length is measured from the zero mark, not from the end of the ruler.
  • A scale's smallest division must be worked out before a value is read, by counting the gaps between labelled marks.
  • 1 cm is 10 mm, so each small division on a ruler is 1 mm.
  • A map or drawing scale such as 1 cm : 2 m states what one unit on the page represents in real life.
By the end you can
G2Measure lengths accurately with a ruler in mm and cm.
G3Read values from a scale whose divisions are not single units, and use a simple scale (e.g. 1 cm : 2 m) to find real distances.

Reading from the zero mark

Measure from the ruler's zero mark, never from its end. The end may be worn or have a border before zero.

A length is always measured from the ruler's zero mark, never from its physical end - the end may be worn, chipped, or printed with a border before the zero. Line up the zero mark with the start of the object every time.

Finding what a division is worth

Count the gaps, not the marks, between two labelled values. Divide the difference by the number of gaps.

A scale's smallest division is worked out by counting the gaps, not the marks, between two labelled values, then dividing the distance between them by that number of gaps.

What a map scale states

A scale like 1 cm : 2 m says what one page unit represents. Measure on the page, then apply the scale.

A map or drawing scale such as 1 cm : 2 m states what one unit on the page represents in real life. Measuring a length on the page and applying the scale finds the real distance.

Multiplying up from the page

Measure the page length in the scale's own unit. Multiply by what one unit represents.

To find a real distance, measure the length on the page in the scale's own unit, then multiply by what one unit represents; a longer page length always gives a longer real distance in the same proportion.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A scale drawing uses 1 cm : 2 m. A wall measures 6 cm on the drawing. Find its real length.
Scaleeach 1 cm on the drawing is 2 m in real life
Multiply by 26×2=12 m
