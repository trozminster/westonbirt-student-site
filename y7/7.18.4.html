Rotating About a Centre

A rotation turns a shape about a fixed point, like a clock hand or a wheel. Today you rotate shapes exactly, from a centre, an angle and a direction.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A rotation is fully defined by three things: a centre, an angle and a direction.
  • Every point stays the same distance from the centre as it turns.
  • Clockwise and anticlockwise turns of the same angle give different images, except for 180°.
  • The centre may lie on the shape, inside it, or outside it.
By the end you can
G4Rotate a shape about a given centre through 90, 180 or 270°, clockwise or anticlockwise.
G4Rotate a shape about a centre lying on or outside the shape, using tracing paper to check.

Three things define it

A rotation needs a centre, an angle and a direction. Every point stays the same distance from the centre as it turns.

Direction matters

Clockwise and anticlockwise quarter turns give different images. A half turn is the same either way.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Centre on a corner

When the centre is a corner of the shape, that corner stays where it is. The rest of the shape turns around it.

Centre outside the shape

With the centre outside, the whole shape swings round it. Tracing paper pinned at the centre shows where it lands.

When the centre is outside the shape, the whole shape swings round it at the same distance. Tracing paper pinned at the centre shows where it lands.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Rotate the triangle 90° anticlockwise about (1,1).
Centre(1,1) is a corner, so it stays
Other corners(4,1) is 3 right of the centre, so it turns to 3 above, (1,4), and (4,3) turns to (−1,4)
Checktrace it and turn the paper about (1,1)
