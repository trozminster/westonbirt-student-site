Topic 7.1 · Properties of Numbers · Lesson 1 of 5

Factors, Multiples and Primes

Every whole number is built from smaller numbers multiplying together - and knowing exactly which ones is the key to simplifying fractions, sharing things equally, and even keeping computer passwords secure. Today you'll learn to spot the building blocks of any number: its factors, its multiples, and the special "unbreakable" numbers called primes that everything else is built from.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A factor divides into a number exactly; factors come in pairs that multiply to the number.
  • A multiple is a number multiplied by a positive integer.
  • Even numbers are multiples of 2; odd numbers are not.
  • A prime has exactly two factors, 1 and itself, so 1 is not prime and 2 is the only even prime.
  • In index notation 34 means 3×3×3×3: the base is multiplied by itself, the index says how often.
By the end you can

Factors

A factor of a number divides into it exactly, with no remainder. Factors come in pairs that multiply to give the original number.

Multiples

A multiple is a number times a whole number. Multiples are the reverse of factors. Every number is a multiple of each of its factors.

A multiple of a number is what you get by multiplying it by a whole number - the reverse relationship to a factor. Every number is a multiple of each of its factors.

Primes

A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. 1 has only one factor: it is neither prime nor composite. 2 is the only even prime.

A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. Numbers with more than two factors are called composite. 1 is neither prime nor composite, since it only has one factor. 2 is the only even prime - every other even number is also divisible by 2 as well as itself, so it has more than two factors.

Index notation

A power writes repeated multiplication compactly: 2×2×2=23
The raised number counts the 2s multiplied together.

Repeated multiplication of the same number can be written compactly using a power (index): 2×2×2=2 cubed (23), read as "2 cubed" or "2 to the power 3." The small raised number shows how many times the base multiplies itself.
