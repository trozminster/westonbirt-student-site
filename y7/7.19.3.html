Topic 7.19 · 3D Shapes and Volume · Lesson 3 of 4

Volume Problems and Units

Builders order concrete by the cubic metre and nurses measure medicine in millilitres. Today you convert between units of volume and capacity, and find volumes of solids built from cuboids.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Converting a length unit changes a volume unit by the cube of that factor: 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³ and 1 m³ = 1000000 cm³.
  • This is because all three dimensions are converted, not just one.
  • 1 cm³ holds 1 ml, so 1000 cm³ is 1 litre.
  • A compound solid is split into cuboids whose volumes are added.

Cubing the factor

A 1 cm cube is 10 mm along every edge. So its volume is 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 mm³. All three dimensions convert, not just one.

A 1 cm cube is 10 mm along every edge, so its volume is 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 mm³. All three dimensions convert, not just one.

Volume and capacity

Capacity is the volume a container holds, measured in millilitres and litres. 1 cm³ holds 1 ml. 1000 cm³ is 1 litre.

Capacity is the volume a container holds, measured in millilitres and litres. A 1 cm³ space holds 1 ml, so 1000 cm³ is 1 litre.

Split into cuboids

Split a compound solid into cuboids, then add their volumes. Each part uses its own length, width and height.

A compound solid can be split into cuboids whose volumes are added. Each part uses its own length, width and height.

Read the context

A worded problem often asks how many fit or how long filling takes. Find the volumes in one unit first, then divide.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the volume of this solid.
Splita low cuboid 5 by 3 by 1 and a tall cuboid 2 by 3 by 4
Low cuboid5 × 3 × 1 = 15 cm³
Tall cuboid2 × 3 × 4 = 24 cm³
Volume15 + 24 = 39 cm³
WORKED EXAMPLE D
How many 5 cm cubes fit inside a box 30 cm by 20 cm by 10 cm?
Along the length30 ÷ 5 = 6
Along the width20 ÷ 5 = 4
Along the height10 ÷ 5 = 2
Number of cubes6 × 4 × 2 = 48
