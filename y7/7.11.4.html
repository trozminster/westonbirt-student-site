Topic 7.11 · Solving Equations · Lesson 4 of 6

Equations with Brackets

A bracket packages several terms together. Today you unwrap it, two ways, before solving the equation inside.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation containing brackets can be solved by expanding first, or by dividing both sides by the number outside.
  • Dividing first is quicker only when every term on the other side divides exactly.
  • Like terms on the same side are collected before the equation is unpicked.
  • An unknown appearing more than once on one side is still a single unknown, so its terms combine.

Two routes into a bracket

Solve a bracket equation by expanding, or by dividing both sides first. Dividing first suits only a right side that divides exactly.

An equation with a bracket can be solved by expanding it first, or by dividing both sides by the number outside. Dividing first is quicker only when every term on the other side divides exactly by that number.

Two routes, same answer

Both routes reach a correct next line.

Both routes reach a correct next line; which is quicker depends on whether the outside number divides every term exactly.

Divide by 3n = 5

Divide both sides by 4n - 1 = 5

Combine before you unpick

Collect like terms on one side before unpicking. An unknown appearing more than once is still one unknown.

Like terms on the same side are collected before the equation is unpicked. An unknown appearing more than once on one side is still a single unknown, so its terms combine into one.

Collecting then unpicking

Then solve it like any two-step equation.

Once the like terms are combined into a single term, the equation is solved exactly as any other two-step equation.
