HCF and LCM by Listing

Bus routes that both stop at the same place again, or sharing sweets into equal groups with nothing left over - both problems come down to the same two ideas: the largest number that divides into both (HCF) and the smallest number both divide into (LCM). Today you'll learn to find both by listing, and just as importantly, to tell which one a problem is actually asking for.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Common factors are the factors two numbers share; the HCF is the largest.
  • Common multiples are the multiples they share; the LCM is the smallest.
  • The HCF is at least 1; the LCM is at most the two numbers multiplied.
  • Splitting or grouping into equal parts needs the HCF; two repeating events coinciding needs the LCM.

HCF and LCM

The HCF is the largest number that is a factor of both. The LCM is the smallest number that is a multiple of both.

The Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two numbers is the largest number that is a factor of both. The Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that is a multiple of both.

Listing method

List the factors for the HCF, or the multiples for the LCM. Take the largest or smallest value in both lists.

List the factors (for HCF) or multiples (for LCM) of each number, then find the largestsmallest value that appears in both lists.

Which one does the question need?

Sharing evenly with nothing left over needs the HCF. Events repeating until they next coincide need the LCM.

Some worded problems ask you to split or group things evenly with nothing left over - these need the HCF, the largest number that divides both amounts exactly. Other problems ask when two repeating events next happen together - these need the LCM, the smallest number both amounts divide into.

Reading the question for the signal words

"Share equally" and "nothing left over" point to the HCF. "Next together" and "repeats" point to the LCM.

"biggest equal group", "share equally", "cut into equal pieces with nothing left over" point to HCF - the answer must divide both numbers. "next happen together", "repeats", "coincide", "back at the start together" point to LCM - the answer must be a multiple of both numbers. If unsure, check which direction the answer has to go: dividing IN to the two numbers (HCF) or building UP to a value both reach (LCM).
