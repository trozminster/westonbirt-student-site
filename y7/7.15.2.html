Double Inequalities

Many real limits come in pairs: a ride might be for people at least 120 cm tall but under 200 cm. Today you write, draw and read inequalities with two boundaries.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A double inequality such as −2<x≤5 states two conditions at once.
  • It is read from the unknown outwards: x is greater than −2 and less than or equal to 5
  • On a number line it is a bounded region, with the circle at each end set by its own symbol.
  • The integers it contains lie between the bounds, and a bound itself counts only when its symbol allows it.
By the end you can

Two conditions at once

A double inequality like −2<x≤5 states two conditions at once. Read it from x outwards.

A double inequality such as −2<x≤5 states two conditions at once. It is read from x outwards: x is greater than −2 and less than or equal to 5

Each end has its own circle

Each end's circle comes from the symbol beside it. Use an open circle for < and a filled circle for ≤. The range between has no arrows.

Each end is set by the symbol beside it: < gives an open circle and ≤ a filled one. The range between the circles is a closed-off piece of the line, with no arrows.

Listing between the bounds

The integers in a double inequality lie between its two bounds. A bound itself is listed only when its own symbol includes it.

Negative bounds

With negative bounds, count up from the lower bound. Listing the integers is safer than subtracting the bounds.

With negative bounds, count upwards from the lower bound, remembering that −5 is less than −3
Listing the integers is safer than subtracting the bounds.
