Topic 7.6 · Introducing Algebra · Lesson 1 of 5

Expressions, Equations and Formulae

Every branch of maths from here on uses these three words precisely - and mixing them up is the single most common vocabulary slip in KS3 algebra. Today you learn to tell an expression, an equation and a formula apart on sight, and to build a formula from a description in words.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A term is a number, a letter, or numbers and letters multiplied together; terms are separated by + and - signs.
  • An expression is one or more terms with no equals sign: 3x+5
  • An equation says two expressions are equal and is true only for particular values: 3x+5=11
  • A formula is a rule linking two or more variables, with one of them as the subject: A=lw.

Terms and expressions

A term is a number, a letter, or numbers and letters multiplied together. An expression is one or more terms with no equals sign.

A term is a number, a letter, or numbers and letters multiplied together; terms are separated by + and - signs. An expression is one or more terms with no equals sign: 3x+5

Equations and formulae

An equation is only true for particular values of the letter. A formula links variables and is true for every value: A=lw.

An equation says two expressions are equal and is only true for particular values of the letter: 3x+5=11 is only true when x=2
A formula is a rule linking two or more variables, with one of them as the subject: A=lw is true for every rectangle, not just one value.

Reading the operations in order

Translate words in the order the calculation happens, not the order they appear. "5 less than double a number" is 2n−5, not 5−2n.

A worded description must be translated in the order the calculation actually happens, not the order the words appear. "5 less than double a number" means: double the number first (2n), then subtract 5 from it - giving 2n−5, not 5−2n.

Brackets group a later operation

Brackets group the operation that happens first. Add 5, then multiply by 3: 3(n+5), not 3n+5

"Add 5 to a number, then multiply by 3" needs the addition done first, so it must be bracketed: 3(n+5), not 3n+5
Whenever "then" or a comma separates two operations, the first operation is grouped in brackets before the second is applied.
