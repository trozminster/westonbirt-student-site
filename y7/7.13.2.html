Converting Units

Measurements are only useful if everyone reads them in the same units. Today you learn to convert confidently between metric units, and between metric and imperial where a conversion fact is given.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Metric units convert by powers of ten: 10 mm =1 cm, 100 cm =1 m, 1000m=1 km, 1000 ml =1 l, 1000g=1 kg, 1000 kg =1 t.
  • Converting to a smaller unit multiplies; converting to a larger unit divides.
  • Common imperial equivalents: 1 inch is about 2.5 cm, 1 kg is about 2.2 lb, 1 litre is about 1.75 pints.
  • An imperial conversion is only approximate, so answers are rounded sensibly.

Converting between metric units

Metric units convert by powers of ten, like 1000g=1 kg. Converting to a smaller unit multiplies. Converting to a larger unit divides.

Metric units convert by powers of ten - 10 mm =1 cm, 100 cm =1 m, 1000m=1 km, 1000 ml =1 l, 1000g=1 kg, 1000 kg =1 t. Converting to a smaller unit multiplies; converting to a larger unit divides.

Converting between metric and imperial

An imperial conversion like 1 inch ≈ 2.5 cm is only approximate. Round the answer sensibly.

An imperial conversion, such as 1 inch is about 2.5 cm, is only approximate, so an answer using it should be rounded sensibly rather than stated to unjustified precision.

Compound and mixed units

In 3m45 cm, each part has a different unit. Convert every part to one unit before adding or comparing.

A compound measurement like 3m45 cm has every part in a different unit, and every part must be converted to the same unit before it can be added or compared.

Using a given imperial fact sensibly

Multiply or divide by the given fact, as the direction needs. Then round sensibly: the fact is only approximate.

A given conversion fact, such as 1 kg is about 2.2 lb, is applied by multiplying or dividing as the direction requires, then the answer is rounded to a sensible degree of accuracy for an approximate fact.
