Topic 7.17 · Probability and Expected Outcomes · Lesson 1 of 4

The Probability Scale

Weather forecasts, games and medicine all measure how likely something is. Today you place events on a scale from 0 to 1 and calculate simple probabilities.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Probability measures how likely an outcome is, on a scale from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).
  • An even chance is 0.5, and a probability can be written as a fraction, decimal or percentage.
  • Outcomes are equally likely when there is no reason to expect one rather than another.
  • For equally likely outcomes, the probability of an event is the number of favourable outcomes over the total number of outcomes.

From impossible to certain

Probability runs from 0 for impossible to 1 for certain. An even chance is 12, or 0.5, or 50%.

Probability measures how likely an outcome is, on a scale from 0 for impossible to 1 for certain. An even chance is 12, which is also 0.5 or 50%.

Words on the scale

Unlikely events lie between 0 and 12, and likely events between 12 and 1. Only an impossible event sits at 0, and only a certain one at 1.

Counting equally likely outcomes

For equally likely outcomes, P = successful outcomes ÷ total outcomes. On a fair dice, P(5) = 16.

When the outcomes are equally likely, the probability of an event is the number of successful outcomes over the total number of outcomes. On a fair dice, P(5) = 16.

Three ways to write it

A probability can be written as a fraction, a decimal or a percentage. A probability of 34 is also 0.75 and 75%.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
