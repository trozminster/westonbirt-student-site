Topic 7.14 · Area of Triangles and Compound Shapes · Lesson 1 of 3

Area of a Triangle

A triangle is exactly half of the rectangle drawn around it. Today you find its area from a base and the height that meets it at a right angle, even when that height falls outside the triangle.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The area of a triangle is half the base times the perpendicular height.
  • The height must be perpendicular to the chosen base, so a slant side is not the height.
  • Any side may be taken as the base, provided the matching perpendicular height is used.
  • The perpendicular height can fall outside the triangle, and the formula still holds.
By the end you can
G3Calculate the area of a triangle, identifying the correct perpendicular height.
G4Calculate the area of a triangle where the perpendicular height falls outside the triangle, or where units must be converted first.

Half of a rectangle

A triangle fills half the rectangle on the same base and height. So its area is 12 × base × perpendicular height.

A triangle fills exactly half of the rectangle drawn around it on the same base and height. So the area of a triangle is 12 × base × perpendicular height.

The perpendicular height

The height meets the base at a right angle. A slant side is not the height. Any side can be the base.

The height must meet the base at a right angle, so a slant side is not the height. Any side can be the base, as long as the height used is perpendicular to it.

A height outside the triangle

In an obtuse triangle, the height can fall outside. The formula is unchanged: 12 × base × perpendicular height.

In an obtuse triangle the perpendicular height can fall outside, meeting the base line extended. The formula does not change: 12 × base × perpendicular height, with the base still the triangle's own side.

Matching the units

The base and the height must be in the same unit before they are multiplied. The area is then in the matching square unit: cm gives cm²
