Multiplying and Dividing Decimals

Recipes, exchange rates and unit prices all multiply and divide decimals. Today you use whole-number calculations and place value to do it without a calculator.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A decimal multiplication has as many decimal places in the answer as the two numbers have between them.
  • So the calculation can be done with whole numbers first, and the point placed at the end.
  • Dividing by a decimal is made easier by scaling both numbers by the same power of ten until the divisor is whole.
  • Scaling both numbers equally leaves the answer unchanged, because the ratio between them is unchanged.

Whole numbers first

Multiply as whole numbers first, then place the point. The answer has as many decimal places as the two numbers together.

A decimal multiplication can be worked with whole numbers first, and the point placed at the end. The answer has as many decimal places as the two numbers have between them.

Smaller answers can be right

Multiplying by a number less than 1 gives a smaller answer. So 0.3×0.2=0.06, not 0.6

Multiplying by a number less than 1 gives an answer smaller than the number you started with. So 0.3×0.2=0.06, not 0.6

Dividing by a whole number

Divide a decimal by a whole number with short division. Keep the answer's point above the number's point. So 7.2÷3=2.4

A decimal is divided by a whole number with short division, the point in the answer directly above the point in the number. So 7.2÷3=2.4

Dividing by a decimal

To divide by a decimal, scale both numbers until the divisor is whole. Scaling both equally leaves the answer unchanged.

To divide by a decimal, both numbers are multiplied by the same power of ten until the divisor is whole. The answer does not change, because both numbers are scaled equally.
