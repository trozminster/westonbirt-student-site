Topic 7.11 · Solving Equations · Lesson 3 of 6

Two-Step Equations

A two-step equation hides the unknown under two layers. Today you peel them off in reverse order, the same way you'd take off a coat before your jumper.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A two-step equation applies two operations to the unknown, so solving undoes them in the reverse order.
  • The operation applied last is undone first, working outwards from the unknown.
  • A fractional coefficient is undone by multiplying, since x4 means x divided by 4.
  • A solution can be negative, and a negative solution is checked the same way as a positive one.
By the end you can
G4Solve two-step equations, including those with negative solutions.

Undo in reverse

A two-step equation applies two operations to the unknown. Solve backwards, undoing the last operation first.

A two-step equation applies two operations to the unknown, so solving works backwards, undoing the last operation first.

Fractions and negatives

A term like n4 is undone by multiplying. Check a negative solution, like n = -2, by substituting back.

A fractional coefficient such as n4 is undone by multiplying, since it means the unknown has been divided. A negative solution is checked the same way as a positive one: substitute it back in.

A fraction is a division

The term n5 means the unknown divided by 5. So the inverse is multiplying by 5.

The term n4 means the unknown divided by 4, so the inverse is multiplying by 4. Multiplying first can clear the fraction before the equation is unpicked further.

Undoing a fractional term

The equation is undone in reverse just as before, with the multiplication saved for last.
