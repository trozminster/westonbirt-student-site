Topic 7.5 · Operations and Negative Numbers · Lesson 1 of 6

Adding, Subtracting and Multiplying

You've added and subtracted numbers before using a columnar method. Today's the same method applied to larger numbers, and the same discipline applied to multiplying - and you need to be completely fluent in both, without a calculator, before negative numbers are introduced next week.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A digit's value depends on its place: in 3407 the 4 is 4 hundreds.
  • Only digits of the same place value combine directly, which is why columns line up by place.
  • Ten of any place is worth one of the place to its left, so carrying trades ten ones for one ten.
  • Exchange in subtraction is that trade in reverse.
  • Addition works in any order; subtraction does not, since 7−3 and 3−7 differ.
  • Multiplication is repeated addition and works in any order.
  • Grid and column methods split a number by place value, multiply each part, then add the parts.

Place value and carrying

Line up the columns: only digits with the same place value combine. In 3407+968, ten ones carry as 1 into the tens column.

A digit's value depends on its place - in 3407 the 4 is 4 hundreds. Only digits of the same place value combine directly, which is why columns are lined up by place before adding. Ten of any place is worth one of the place to its left, so carrying trades ten ones for one ten (or ten tens for one hundred, and so on).

Exchange (the reverse of carrying)

When a top digit is too small, exchange one from the column to its left. That one is worth ten in the column it moves into.

Exchange in subtraction is carrying in reverse - if a column's top digit is smaller than the digit being subtracted, one unit is borrowed from the place to its left, trading one of that place for ten of the current place.

Order matters

Subtraction does not work in any order: 7−3 and 3−7 differ. Check a subtraction by adding the answer back on.

Unlike addition, subtraction does NOT work in any order: 7−3 and 3−7 give different answers. This means subtraction can't be checked by flipping the columns the way addition can - instead, check by adding the answer back to the number subtracted, which should return the original.

Multiplication is repeated addition, in any order

6×4 means 6 lots of 4, or 4 lots of 6
For 34×6, the grid splits 34 into 30 and 4

6×4 means 6 lots of 4 (or 4 lots of 6 - multiplication works in any order). The grid method splits a number by place value, multiplies each part separately, then adds the parts back together.
