Topic 7.15 · Coordinates, Graphs and Inequalities · Lesson 5 of 7

Coordinate Problem Solving

Real graphs rarely go up in ones: maps, charts and plans use scales. Today you read coordinates on any scale and use them to find midpoints and missing corners.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Axes may be scaled in steps other than one, so each square's value is read from the scale before plotting.
  • The two axes can carry different scales from each other.
  • The midpoint of a line segment has coordinates halfway between the endpoints in each direction.
  • A shape is completed from given vertices by using its properties, such as equal or parallel sides.
By the end you can

The midpoint

The midpoint of a line segment is halfway between its endpoints in each direction. Each of its coordinates is the mean of the two endpoints' coordinates.

Completing a shape

Find a missing vertex from the shape's properties. A rectangle's missing corner lines up with two of the others.

A missing vertex is found from the shape's properties, such as equal sides and parallel sides. A rectangle's missing corner lines up with one vertex across and another up or down.
