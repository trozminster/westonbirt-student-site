Adding and Subtracting Decimals

Receipts, race splits and measurements all add and subtract decimals. Today you line up the decimal points and calculate without a calculator.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Only digits of the same place value can be added or subtracted, which is why the decimal points are lined up.
  • Empty places can be filled with zeros without changing the value.
  • The decimal point in the answer sits directly below the points above it.
  • Money is written to two decimal places, and measures to the precision the question states.
By the end you can
G2Add and subtract decimals without a calculator, including numbers with different numbers of decimal places.
G3Solve worded problems involving addition and subtraction of decimals, including money and measures.

Line up the points

Line up the decimal points: only same-place digits can be added. The answer's point sits directly below.

Only digits of the same place value can be added, so the decimal points are lined up. The point in the answer sits directly below them.

Filling empty places

Fill an empty place with a zero: 7 becomes 7.00
Every digit in a subtraction then has a partner.

An empty place can be filled with a zero without changing the value, so 7 becomes 7.00
This matters most in subtraction, where every digit needs a partner above it.

Fill 8 with zeros so both numbers have two decimal places, then exchange: 7 ones, 9 tenths and 10 hundredths

Money and measures

Money is written to two decimal places: £4.50, never £4.5
Convert measures to one unit before adding or subtracting.

Money is written to two decimal places, so four pounds fifty is £4.50, never £4.5
Measures in different units are converted to one unit before they are added or subtracted.

Reading for the operation

Read a worded problem for its operation first. Change is the amount paid minus the cost.

A worded problem is read for its operation first: "total" and "altogether" add, "change" and "difference" subtract. Change is the amount paid minus the cost.
