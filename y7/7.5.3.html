Adding and Subtracting Negatives

You've been fluent adding and subtracting positive numbers. Today the number line extends below zero - and adding or subtracting a negative number does something that might surprise you.

By the end you’ll know…
  • On a number line, adding moves right and subtracting moves left, whatever the signs.
  • Adding a negative subtracts: 5+(−3) =5−3 =2
  • Subtracting a negative adds: 5−(−3) =5+3 =8
  • Two adjacent signs combine: '+ -' and '- +' give minus, '- -' gives plus.
  • A difference is the distance between two values, so it is never negative: from −4 to 3 is 7
By the end you can

Moving on a number line

On a number line, adding moves right and subtracting moves left. Adding a negative subtracts: 5+(−3) =5−3 =2

On a number line, adding moves right and subtracting moves left, whatever the signs involved. Adding a negative subtracts: 5+(−3) =5−3 =2, since moving 3 places left is the same whether you call it '+(−3)' or '-3'.

Subtracting a negative adds: 5−(−3) =5+3 =8, since 'taking away a debt' leaves you better off. Two adjacent signs combine: '+ -' and '- +' both give minus overall, '- -' gives plus. A difference is the distance between two values, so it's never negative: from −4 to 3 is 7
