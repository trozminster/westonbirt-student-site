Topic 7.18 · Symmetry and Transformations · Lesson 3 of 6

Symmetry and Reflections

Symmetry is everywhere, from faces to flags. Today you find every line of symmetry of a shape, then reflect shapes on a grid, including in the diagonal line y=x.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A line of symmetry divides a shape into two halves that are mirror images of each other.
  • In a reflection every point moves to the same perpendicular distance on the other side of the mirror line.
  • A point lying on the mirror line does not move.
  • The mirror line may be given as an equation: x=a, y=b, or y=x.

Mirror halves

A line of symmetry splits a shape into two mirror-image halves. Folding along it lays one half on the other.

A line of symmetry divides a shape into two halves that are mirror images of each other. Folding along it lays one half exactly on the other.

Diagonals can count

A square has four lines of symmetry, including both diagonals. A rectangle's diagonals are not lines of symmetry.

A square has four lines of symmetry, including both of its diagonals. A rectangle's diagonals are not lines of symmetry, so it has only two.

Across at right angles

In a reflection, each point moves at right angles to the mirror. It lands the same distance beyond the mirror. A point on the mirror line does not move.

In a reflection each point moves at right angles to the mirror line, to the same distance on the other side. A point on the mirror line does not move.

The line y=x

The line y=x joins (1,1), (2,2), (3,3) and so on. Reflecting in y=x swaps the coordinates: (4,1) goes to (1,4).

The line y=x joins (1,1), (2,2), (3,3) and so on. Reflecting in it swaps the coordinates, so (4,1) goes to (1,4).
