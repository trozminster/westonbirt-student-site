Equivalent Fractions

A pizza cut into 8 slices with 6 eaten and one cut into 4 with 3 eaten have lost the same amount. Today is about the many names one fraction can have, and choosing the name that makes two fractions easy to compare.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The denominator names how many equal parts the whole is split into; the numerator counts how many are taken.
  • Multiplying or dividing both by the same number gives an equivalent fraction, because the value is unchanged.
  • A fraction is in its lowest terms when the only common factor of numerator and denominator is 1
  • Dividing both by their HCF simplifies a fraction in one step.
  • Fractions can only be compared directly when they share a denominator.

One amount, many names

Equivalent fractions are the same amount cut into different-sized parts. Multiplying or dividing the numerator and the denominator by the same number keeps the value.

Lowest terms

Comparing needs equal parts

Two fractions can be compared directly only when their parts are the same size. Rewritten over a common denominator, the larger numerator is the larger fraction.

The lowest common denominator

Any common multiple of the denominators works as a common denominator. The LCM keeps the numbers smallest.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
