Forming Equations

An equation is a promise that two amounts balance. Today you learn to write that promise in algebra, starting from a sentence or a shape.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation states that two expressions are equal.
  • Forming an equation means naming the unknown with a letter and writing the described relationship in algebra.
  • A word equation states that relationship in words before letters are used.
  • The equals sign means 'has the same value as', not 'here comes the answer'.

Two sides, one balance

An equation says two expressions have the same value. A word equation writes that balance as a sentence first.

An equation states that two expressions have the same value, joined by an equals sign that means 'has the same value as'. A word equation writes that balance in a sentence before any letters appear.

Naming the unknown

Name the unknown with a letter first. Then write what the words describe, using that letter.

Forming an equation starts with naming the unknown quantity with a letter, then writing what the words describe using that letter.

From context straight to algebra

A worded or geometric context can be written straight as an equation. Name the unknown with a letter first.

A worded or geometric context can be written directly as an algebraic equation, naming the unknown with a letter first. Perimeter and 'more than'/'less than' phrasing translate the same way as a word equation, just without the sentence in between.

Reading a shape

A perimeter is the sum of every side. In a rectangle, each pair of equal sides counts twice.

A perimeter is the sum of every side; opposite sides of a rectangle are equal, so each pair is counted twice.
