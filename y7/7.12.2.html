Topic 7.12 · Representing Data · Lesson 2 of 4

Bar Charts and Pictograms

Bar charts and pictograms are the two most common ways to show discrete category data at a glance. Today you learn to draw and read both accurately.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A bar chart shows frequency by bar height, with equal bar widths and equal gaps for discrete data.
  • The axes must be labelled and the frequency scale must go up in equal steps.
  • A pictogram uses a repeated symbol with a key stating what one symbol represents.
  • A part-symbol represents that fraction of the key value.
By the end you can
G2Draw bar charts and pictograms accurately, with labelled axes, scale and key.
G3Interpret bar charts and pictograms, including part-symbols and comparisons between categories.

What a bar chart shows

Bar height shows frequency. Bars have equal widths and equal gaps, on a scale from 0
Label both axes and mark the frequency scale.

A bar chart shows frequency by bar height, with every bar the same width and an equal gap between bars, plotted against a frequency scale that goes up in equal steps from 0
Both axes need a label, and the frequency axis needs its scale marked.

What a pictogram shows

A pictogram's key says what one whole symbol represents. A part-symbol shows that fraction of the key's value.

A pictogram uses a repeated symbol to show frequency, with a key stating exactly what one whole symbol represents. A part-symbol - half a symbol, a quarter of a symbol - represents that same fraction of the key's value.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
A pictogram's key states one symbol =6 books. A row shows 3 full symbols and a half symbol. How many books does the row represent?
Write the total3×6+6÷2
Work out each part=18+3
Number of books=21

Learn

Reading a bar chart backwards

Read each bar's height against the frequency scale. Totals and comparisons follow from those readings.

Given a drawn bar chart, the height of each bar is read against the frequency scale to find how many items fall in that category, and totals or comparisons follow from those readings. A bar chart can also answer "how many more" or "how many altogether" once each height is read.

Comparing categories

Compare the frequency values, not the gap between bar tops.

Comparing two categories on a bar chart means comparing the frequency values the bars represent, not the visual gap between the bar tops - especially where the scale does not go up in steps of 1
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A bar chart shows Monday 15, Tuesday 9
How many more visitors were there on Monday than Tuesday?
Read Monday15
Read Tuesday9
How many more15−9=6
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the total frequency shown by this bar chart.
Read each bar6, 11, 4, 9
Add the frequencies6+11+4+9
Total frequency=30
