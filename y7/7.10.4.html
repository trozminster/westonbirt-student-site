Multiplying Fractions

Half of half a pizza is a quarter of a pizza - smaller, not bigger. Today multiplication meets fractions, and the word 'of' turns out to mean multiply.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Multiplying fractions multiplies the numerators and the denominators separately.
  • 'of' means multiply, so 34 of 20 is 34×20
  • A whole number is written over 1 before multiplying.
  • Cancelling a common factor from a numerator and a denominator before multiplying gives the same answer with smaller numbers.
  • Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes a quantity smaller.

Multiply across

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. No common denominator is needed: no parts are being combined.

Multiplying fractions multiplies the numerators together and the denominators together. No common denominator is needed, because no parts are being combined.

Cancelling first

Cancel a factor shared by any numerator and any denominator first. The answer is the same, with smaller numbers.

A factor shared by any numerator and any denominator can be cancelled before multiplying. The answer is the same, and the numbers stay small.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

'Of' means multiply

A fraction of an amount means multiply the amount by the fraction. The denominator makes the equal parts. The numerator counts how many are taken.

Finding a fraction of an amount multiplies the amount by the fraction. The denominator splits the amount into equal parts, and the numerator counts how many are taken.

Smaller, not bigger

Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 always gives less. Three quarters of 20 is 15

Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 always gives less than the starting amount. Three quarters of 20 is 15, which is less than 20
