Checkpoint · 7.3.1
(a)
Construct the perpendicular bisector of EF, 8 cm long.
Equal arcs from E and F, wider than 4 cm, cross above and below EF. Join the two crossings.
(b)
State the compass width you chose. Why does it work?
For example 5 cm: any width more than half of EF (4 cm) works, so the arcs meet.
Exit ticket · 7.3.1
What is the minimum compass width needed to construct the perpendicular bisector of a 10 cm line segment?
More than 5 cm.
Exit ticket · 7.3.1
Why must constructions use compasses and a straight edge only, and not a ruler for measuring or a protractor?
Because a construction must be provably exact using only fixed-radius arcs; a ruler or protractor only gives an approximate reading, which isn't an accepted construction method.
Exit ticket · 7.3.1
Arcs of radius 6 cm are drawn from both ends of a line segment GH that is 6 cm long, crossing above the line. What type of triangle is formed by G, H and the crossing point above the line?
Equilateral - GH, and both arcs to the crossing point, are all 6 cm.

Checkpoint · 7.3.2
(a)
Point V is above a line. Construct the perpendicular from V.
An arc from V cuts the line twice. Equal arcs from those two points cross below the line. Join V to that crossing.
(b)
Why is it exactly 90°, not just close?
V and the crossing are each the same distance from the two points on the line, so the line through them is the perpendicular bisector of those points.
Check · 7.3.2

Not quite. A line by eye won't split the angle exactly. Use arcs from D.
Yes. Centre on D and cross both arms: you need a point on each arm.

Exit ticket · 7.3.2

Exit ticket · 7.3.2
An angle of 50° is bisected. What size is each half?
25°.
Exit ticket · 7.3.2
What single construction (equal-radius arcs, then a second pair of equal-radius arcs, forming a rhombus) is shared by the perpendicular bisector, the perpendicular through/from a point, and the angle bisector?
Two equal-radius arcs from two points, joined at their crossing points - the 'rhombus' construction, used by all three.

Not quite. Points on the other side count too: two lines, one each side.

Checkpoint · 7.3.3
(a)
Construct the locus of points 3.5 cm from a line, on both sides.
Two lines parallel to it, one 3.5 cm each side: two perpendiculars on each side, 3.5 cm marked on each, joined.
(b)
How do you know your lines are truly parallel?
Each is 3.5 cm from the line at two points, along perpendiculars, so the gap is the same everywhere.

Not quite. A segment has ends, so the locus curves round them.
Not quite. Points near the ends count too, not just the middle: a racetrack.

Checkpoint · 7.3.3

A right-angled triangle has sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm.

(a)
Describe the locus of points 1 cm outside it.
Three lines parallel to the sides, 1 cm out, joined at each corner by an arc of radius 1 cm: a triangle with rounded corners.
Exit ticket · 7.3.3
What is the locus of points 4 cm from a single fixed point?
A circle of radius 4 cm, centred on the point.
Exit ticket · 7.3.3
What shape is the locus of points at a fixed distance from a line segment (not an infinite line)?
A 'stadium' (racetrack) shape - two parallel straight segments joined by a semicircle at each end.
Exit ticket · 7.3.3
At each corner of a polygon, why is the fixed-distance locus rounded rather than sharp?
Because the locus stays a fixed distance from whichever point of the polygon is nearest, and at a corner that nearest point sweeps through an arc rather than jumping straight across.

Checkpoint · 7.3.4

W and X are 6 cm apart.

(a)
Construct the region closer to W than to X.
The perpendicular bisector of WX: the side containing W.
(b)
Construct the region within 4 cm of W.
The circle of radius 4 cm, centre W, and its inside.
(c)
Shade the overlap of the two regions.
The part of the circle on W's side of the bisector.

Checkpoint · 7.3.4

Paths P and Q cross near lamppost L. A bench must be within 5 m of L, and closer to P than to Q.

(a)
Construct and shade the region for the bench.
The circle of radius 5 m round L, and both bisectors of the angles where the paths cross. Shade inside the circle, in the two wedges either side of P.
(b)
Describe the region in words.
Inside the circle of radius 5 m centred on L, and between the angle bisectors on P's side.
Exit ticket · 7.3.4
What does the boundary of 'closer to A than B' look like, and what construction produces it?
A straight line - the perpendicular bisector of AB.
Exit ticket · 7.3.4
Two conditions are shaded on the same diagram. Which region satisfies both?
The overlap (intersection) of the two shaded regions.
Exit ticket · 7.3.4
Describe in words the region that is more than 3 cm from a point M, but within 3 cm of a point N that is 4 cm from M.
The part of the 3 cm circle around N that lies outside the 3 cm circle around M.
