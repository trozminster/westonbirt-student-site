Volume of Cuboids

How much a box, a fish tank or a skip holds is its volume. Today you calculate the volume of a cuboid, and work backwards to a missing length.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Volume is the space a solid occupies, measured in cubic units such as cm³
  • The volume of a cuboid is length × width × height.
  • All three dimensions must be in the same unit before multiplying.
  • A missing dimension is found by dividing the volume by the product of the other two.

Counting cubes

Volume is the space a solid occupies, in cubic units such as cm³
A 3 by 2 by 2 cuboid is 2 layers of 6 cubes: 12 cm³

Volume is the space a solid occupies, measured in cubic units such as cm³
A cuboid 3 cm by 2 cm by 2 cm is 2 layers of 3×2=6 cubes, so 12 cm³

Length × width × height

The volume of a cuboid is length × width × height. All three lengths must be in the same unit before multiplying.

Working backwards

A missing length = volume ÷ the product of the other two. So 60 cm³ on a 5 by 4 base has height 3 cm.

A missing length is the volume divided by the product of the other two lengths. A cuboid of 60 cm³ with a base 5 cm by 4 cm has height 60÷20=3 cm.

Convert first

Convert lengths to one unit before multiplying or dividing. So 2 m becomes 200 cm: 200×30×10=60000 cm³

When the lengths are in different units, convert them to one unit before multiplying or dividing. 2 m is 200 cm, so a 2 m by 30 cm by 10 cm cuboid is 60000 cm³
