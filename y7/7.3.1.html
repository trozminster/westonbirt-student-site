Compasses and Perpendicular Bisectors

You've drawn shapes using a ruler and a protractor before. Today you meet a new tool - compasses - and a new kind of drawing where you're never allowed to measure: only draw arcs and let them cross exactly where they must.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A locus is the set of all points satisfying a given rule.
  • Equidistant means the same distance from.
  • A circle is the locus of points a fixed distance (the radius) from a point, which is what compasses draw.
  • Constructions use compasses and a straight edge only, never measurement.
  • Equal arcs from each end of a line segment form a rhombus, whose diagonals cross at right angles and bisect each other.
By the end you can
G3Use compasses accurately to construct arcs from two given points, drawing arcs rather than full circles for efficiency.
G5Construct the perpendicular bisector of a line segment, identifying the rhombus that the construction creates.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Loci and equidistant

A locus is every point that fits a rule. Equidistant means the same distance from. Compasses draw a circle, the locus of a fixed distance.

A locus is the set of all points that satisfy a rule - for example, 'exactly 3 cm from point A.' Equidistant means 'the same distance from.' Compasses draw a circle: the locus of every point a fixed distance (the radius) from the centre.

Constructing (not measuring)

A construction uses only compasses and a straight edge. Keeping one compass width for every arc keeps it accurate.

A construction uses only compasses and a straight edge - no ruler for measuring length, no protractor for measuring angle. Setting the compasses to a length once, then using that same width for every arc, is what keeps the construction accurate.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Set your compasses to 4 cm. Draw an arc above and below point A. What does the full locus of these points look like?
Compassesfixed at 4 cm, point on A
Arcsone above A and one below
Locusthe whole circle of radius 4 cm, and the arcs are pieces of it
WORKED EXAMPLE B
With compasses set to over half of AB, draw arcs above and below the line from A, then B.
Widthmore than half of AB, and unchanged throughout
From Aan arc above the line and one below
From Bthe same two arcs at the same width
Crossingsthe arcs meet at two points, one above AB and one below
YOUR TURN · a
Draw a line segment CD6 cm long. With compasses set to 5 cm, draw arcs from C and from D, above and below the line.
two pairs of arcs, one pair from C and one from D, crossing above the line and below it - the compass stays at 5 cm for all four.
YOUR TURN · b
Join the two crossing points with a straight line. What do you notice about where it crosses CD?
the new line cuts CD exactly in half, 3 cm from each end, and at right angles - it is the perpendicular bisector of CD.

To construct arcs from both ends of a line segment that will cross above and below the line, what must be true about the compass width?
Yes. More than half, and the same width from both ends, so the arcs cross above and below.
Not quite. Exactly half makes the arcs just touch on the line. Open them wider than half.
Not quite. Keep the same width from both ends, so the crossing points are equally far from A and B.
Not quite. Wider than half is enough. More than the whole length works but isn't needed.
Arcs of equal radius are drawn from A and from B, crossing above and below line AB. The four points A, B, and the two crossing points are joined. What shape is formed?
Yes. All four sides are the same arc width, so it's a rhombus.
Not quite. All four sides are equal (the same arc width), so it's a rhombus.
Not quite. Equal sides are certain, but right angles aren't: a rhombus.
Not quite. Both ends used the same width, so all four sides match: a rhombus.
The line joining the two crossing points of the arcs is drawn. What is true about where it crosses AB?
Yes. The rhombus's diagonals cut each other in half at right angles.
Not quite. A rhombus's diagonals always meet at right angles too.
Not quite. A rhombus's diagonals also cut each other in half: the midpoint.
Not quite. Any width over half of AB gives the same line.

Exit ticket

On paper, on your own, then check
1
What is the minimum compass width needed to construct the perpendicular bisector of a 10 cm line segment?
More than 5 cm.
2
Why must constructions use compasses and a straight edge only, and not a ruler for measuring or a protractor?
Because a construction must be provably exact using only fixed-radius arcs; a ruler or protractor only gives an approximate reading, which isn't an accepted construction method.
3
Arcs of radius 6 cm are drawn from both ends of a line segment GH that is 6 cm long, crossing above the line. What type of triangle is formed by G, H and the crossing point above the line?
Equilateral - GH, and both arcs to the crossing point, are all 6 cm.
