Bisecting Lines and Angles

Yesterday's construction bisected a line segment. Today you bisect two more things exactly, using compasses again: a line at right angles through a chosen point, and an angle.

By the end you’ll know…
  • To bisect is to cut exactly in half.
  • The perpendicular bisector of AB is the locus of points equidistant from A and B.
  • An angle bisector is the locus of points equidistant from the two lines forming the angle.
  • Both constructions build a rhombus: its diagonals bisect each other at right angles, and each bisects the angles it passes through.
By the end you can

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Perpendicular through a point ON a line

For a perpendicular at P, draw equal arcs from P on the line. Then draw wider arcs from those two points. Join their crossing to P.

A perpendicular through point P on a line comes from two arcs of equal radius drawn from P, which mark a point on the line each side of it. Then, using a wider radius, draw arcs from those two points above (or below) P - where they cross is joined to P.

Perpendicular from a point OFF a line

For a perpendicular from Q, draw one arc from Q cutting the line twice. Then bisect those two crossings.

To construct a perpendicular from a point Q not on a line, draw one arc from Q crossing the line twice. Then treat those two crossing points like the ends of a line segment and construct their perpendicular bisector - it will pass through Q.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Angle bisector

Draw one arc from the vertex W, crossing both arms. From those crossings, draw equal arcs that meet. The line from W through that point bisects the angle.

Bisecting an angle at vertex W starts from a single arc centred on W, crossing both arms of the angle. Then, from those two crossing points, draw two more equal-radius arcs that cross each other. The line from W through that crossing point bisects the angle exactly in half.

Why it works

Both bisectors build a rhombus from equal arcs. Its diagonal cuts the segment, or the angle, exactly in half.

Both constructions (perpendicular bisector and angle bisector) build the same rhombus shape from equal-radius arcs. In the perpendicular bisector, the rhombus's diagonal bisects the line segment at right angles; in the angle bisector, the rhombus's diagonal bisects the angle itself, since the rhombus is symmetrical about it.
