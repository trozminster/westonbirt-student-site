Topic 7.5 · Operations and Negative Numbers · Lesson 2 of 6

Dividing, Powers and Roots

You're already fluent adding, subtracting and multiplying large numbers without a calculator. Today's the same discipline applied to dividing - and a first look at squares, cubes, and their opposites: roots.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Division is the inverse of multiplication and does not work in any order, since 12 ÷ 3 and 3 ÷ 12 differ.
  • Short division works through a number one place at a time from the left, carrying any remainder into the next place.
  • n-squared is n × n; n-cubed is n × n × n.
  • A root is the inverse of a power: square-root-25 = 5 because 5² = 25, and cube-root-8 = 2 because 2³ = 8.
  • Squares to know: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144.
  • Cubes: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 1000.

Division is the inverse, and does not work in any order

Division undoes multiplication. Order matters: 12÷3 and 3÷12 are not the same. Short division carries each remainder into the next digit, as in 936 ÷ 4.

Division undoes multiplication, but 12÷3 and 3÷12 are not the same. Short division splits the number being divided by place value, carrying any remainder into the next digit.

Squares and cubes

Squaring n gives n × n. Cubing n gives n × n × n. Know the square numbers up to 144 by heart.

Squaring n gives n × n; cubing n gives n × n × n. The squares to know by heart are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144; the cubes to know are 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 1000.

Roots undo powers

A root undoes a power. 5² = 25, and the square root of 25 is 5. Only square numbers have whole-number square roots.

A root is the inverse of a power. The square root of 25 is 5, because 5²=25; the cube root of 8 is 2, because 2³=8. Every square number has a whole-number square root; most other numbers don't.
