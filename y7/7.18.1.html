Topic 7.18 · Symmetry and Transformations · Lesson 1 of 6

Rotational Symmetry

Logos, wheel trims and snowflakes look exactly the same after part of a turn. Today you measure that with the order of rotational symmetry, and use it to complete patterns.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The order of rotational symmetry is how many times a shape looks identical during one full turn about its centre.
  • Every shape has order at least 1, since a full turn always returns it to its starting position.
  • The centre of rotation is the point the shape turns about, usually its centre.
  • Order of rotational symmetry and number of lines of symmetry are separate properties and need not match.

Counting the fits

The order of rotational symmetry counts how often a shape fits in one full turn. An equilateral triangle fits three times.

The order of rotational symmetry is how many times a shape looks identical during one full turn about its centre. An equilateral triangle fits three times: after a third of a turn, two thirds, and the full turn.

A separate property

Every shape has order at least 1
Order and lines of symmetry are separate properties. A parallelogram has order 2 but no lines of symmetry.

Every shape has order at least 1, since a full turn always brings it back. Order and lines of symmetry are separate: a parallelogram has order 2 but no lines of symmetry.

Building from one part

An order 4 pattern is one part and three copies. Each copy is turned a further quarter turn about the centre. Turn the part, never reflect it.

A pattern with order 4 is one part and three copies, each turned a further quarter turn about the centre. So a pattern is completed by turning the part, not by reflecting it.

A half turn for order 2

For order 2, copy the part once by a half turn. Each copied point sits opposite its original, just as far from the centre.

For order 2 the part is copied once, by a half turn about the centre. Each point of the copy is directly opposite its original, the same distance from the centre.
