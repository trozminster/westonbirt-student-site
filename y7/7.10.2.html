Mixed Numbers and Reciprocals

A recipe asks for 234 cups of flour, but the only scoop holds a quarter of a cup. Today you switch between the two ways of writing the same amount, and meet the fraction that undoes another.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A mixed number is a whole number and a proper fraction together.
  • An improper (top-heavy) fraction has a numerator at least as large as its denominator.
  • A mixed number converts to an improper fraction because each whole is one denominator's worth of parts.
  • The reciprocal of a fraction is that fraction inverted, and a number multiplied by its reciprocal gives 1
  • A whole number n is n1, so its reciprocal is 1n.
By the end you can
G3Convert between mixed numbers and improper fractions in both directions.
G4State the reciprocal of a fraction, whole number or mixed number.

Wholes are made of parts

A mixed number is a whole number and a proper fraction together. Each whole is one denominator's worth of parts.

A mixed number is a whole number and a proper fraction together. Each whole is one denominator's worth of parts, so a mixed number can always be written as an improper fraction.

Back again

To convert back, divide the numerator by the denominator. The quotient gives the wholes. The remainder gives the parts left over.

An improper fraction converts back by dividing its numerator by its denominator. The quotient is the whole number, and the remainder counts the parts left over.

The fraction that undoes

The reciprocal of a fraction is that fraction turned upside down. A number multiplied by its reciprocal always gives 1

Whole and mixed numbers first

A whole number n is n1, with reciprocal 1n. Make a mixed number improper before turning it over.

A whole number n is n1, so its reciprocal is 1n. A mixed number is made improper before it is turned over.
