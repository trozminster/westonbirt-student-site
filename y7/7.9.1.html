Measuring and Naming Angles

You've compared and estimated angles before - today you measure them precisely with a protractor, and learn the exact language mathematicians use to name and classify them, which every angle-fact lesson from here on depends on.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An angle measures turn, in degrees, and a full turn is 360°.
  • Acute is under 90°, right is exactly 90, obtuse is between 90 and 180, and reflex is over 180
  • Angle ABC means the angle at vertex B, turning from A to C.
  • A protractor carries two scales, and the one to read is the one starting at zero on a line of the angle.
By the end you can
G2Measure and draw angles to the nearest degree using a protractor, estimating first.
G3Name angles using three-letter notation and classify them by type.

What an angle measures

An angle measures turn, in degrees. A full turn is 360°. Estimate before measuring, to check against a protractor misread.

An angle measures turn, in degrees, and a full turn is 360°. Estimating first, before measuring, builds a check against a protractor misread.

Reading a protractor

A protractor has two scales, running opposite ways. Read the scale that starts at zero on an arm of the angle.

A protractor carries two scales, one running each way round - the one to read is whichever one starts at zero on a line (arm) of the angle being measured.

Naming with three letters

Angle ABC is the angle at vertex B. The middle letter always names the vertex.

Angle ABC means the angle AT vertex B, turning from A to C - the middle letter always names the vertex where the angle actually is.

Classifying by type

Classify an angle by its measured size, not by how it looks.

Acute is under 90°, right is exactly 90, obtuse is between 90 and 180, and reflex is over 180 - classification is decided by the actual measured size, not by how the angle looks on the page.
