Factorising with a Common Factor

Expanding multiplies a bracket out; factorising runs that backwards. Today you take the highest common factor out of an expression and check your answer by expanding.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Factorising is the inverse of expanding: the expression is rewritten as a product with a bracket.
  • The factor taken outside is the highest common factor of all the terms.
  • The bracket holds what remains when each term is divided by that factor.
  • Fully factorised means the terms left inside the bracket share no common factor.
  • The HCF of algebraic terms includes numbers and letters, so the HCF of 6x2 and 9x is 3x.

Expanding in reverse

Factorising is the inverse of expanding. The factor outside the bracket divides every term.

Factorising is the inverse of expanding: the expression is rewritten as a product with a bracket. The factor outside the bracket divides every term.

What goes in the bracket

The bracket holds what is left when each term is divided by the common factor. Expanding the answer must give back the original expression.

Fully factorised

Fully factorised means the bracket's terms share no common factor. 12x+18 =2(6x+9) is not fully factorised: the HCF is 6

An expression is fully factorised when the terms left in the bracket share no common factor. 12x+18 =2(6x+9) is factorised but not fully, because 6 is the highest common factor.

Numbers and letters together

The HCF of algebraic terms includes numbers and letters. The HCF of 6x2 and 9x is 3x.

The highest common factor of algebraic terms includes numbers and letters. The HCF of 6x2 and 9x is 3x, so 6x2+9x =3x(2x+3).
