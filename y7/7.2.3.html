Topic 7.2 · Sets and Venn Diagrams · Lesson 3 of 6

Union and Intersection

Yesterday you compared one set to another (subset) and to everything outside it (complement). Today you combine two sets: put everything from both together (union), or find just what they share (intersection).

By the end you’ll know…
  • A union B is the elements in A, in B, or in both, each listed once.
  • A intersect B is the elements in both; it is empty when the sets share nothing.
  • n(A union B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A intersect B), since shared elements would otherwise count twice.

Union

A ∪ B is every element in A, in B, or in both. List each element only once.

A union B, written A ∪ B, is every element that is in A, in B, or in both - each element listed only once even if it's in both sets.

Counting a union

The union count is n(A) + n(B) - n(A ∩ B). Adding n(A) and n(B) counts the overlap twice.

n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A ∩ B). Adding n(A) and n(B) counts the shared elements twice, so the overlap is subtracted once to correct it.

Intersection

A ∩ B is every element in both A and B. Sets that share nothing are disjoint: A ∩ B = ∅.

A intersect B, written A ∩ B, is every element in BOTH A and B. If the sets share nothing, A ∩ B = ∅ (the empty set), and A and B are called disjoint.

Notation review

∈ and ∉ describe single elements. ∪ and ∩ combine two sets. ⊆ and ' compare sets.

∈/∉ describe single elements; ∪/∩ combine two sets; ⊆/' compare a set to another or to everything outside it. Fluent use of all six pieces of notation lets a whole relationship be written in one line.
