Topic 7.17 · Probability and Expected Outcomes · Lesson 4 of 4

Probability of Two Events

Games often use two dice or two spinners at once. Today you complete probability tables and find the probability of two events happening together.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A probability distribution lists every outcome with its probability, and these must total 1
  • A missing probability is found by subtracting the others from 1
  • A sample space diagram lists every possible outcome of two events, so probabilities can be counted from it.
  • Two events are independent when one does not affect the other.
  • For independent events, the probability of both is found by multiplying their probabilities.
By the end you can
G4Complete a probability distribution table and find a missing probability.
G6Find probabilities for two successive events, using sample space diagrams or multiplication.

Two dice

Two dice give 6×6=36 equally likely outcomes. The six that total 7 give a probability of 16

Rolling two dice gives 6×6=36 equally likely outcomes, shown in a sample space diagram. Six of them total 7, so the probability of a total of 7 is 636=16

Multiplying independent events

Independent events do not affect each other. P(both) is the product of their probabilities. P(head and a six) =12×16=112

Two events are independent when one does not affect the other. The probability that both happen is the product of their probabilities: P(head and a six) =12×16=112
