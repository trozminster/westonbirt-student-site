Topic 7.11 · Solving Equations · Lesson 5 of 6

Solving Equations in Context

An equation is only useful if its answer means something. Today you check what you find against the real situation it came from, and use perimeter and angle facts to build the equation in the first place.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Substituting a solution back into the original equation checks it, since both sides must then give the same value.
  • A contextual problem is solved by forming an equation, solving it, and answering in the context with units.
  • Perimeter and angle facts give ready-made equations: sides sum to the perimeter, angles on a line sum to 180 degrees.
  • A solution that makes no sense in context, such as a negative length, signals an error in the equation rather than the arithmetic.
By the end you can
G4Solve equations of mixed types fluently, checking solutions by substitution.
G5Form and solve equations to answer worded and geometric problems (perimeter, angle facts).

Checking by substitution

Substitute a solution back in: both sides must match. A negative length signals an error in the equation.

Substituting a solution back into the original equation checks it, since both sides must then give the same value. A solution that makes no sense in context, such as a negative length, signals an error in the equation rather than the arithmetic.

Forming, solving, answering

Form the equation, solve it, then answer in context with units.

A contextual problem is solved in three stages: form the equation, solve it, then answer the original question in context, with units.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Solve 3n - 4 = 11, then check it.
Write the equation3n - 4 = 11
Add 4 to both sides3n = 15
Divide by 3n = 5
Check3 × 5 - 4 = 15 - 4
= 11
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A rectangle's width is w, and 2w + 6 = 4. Solve for w and explain the result.
Write the equation2w + 6 = 4
Subtract 6 from both sides2w = -2
Divide by 2w = -1
A width cannot be negative
Explanationthe equation, not the arithmetic, must be wrong

Ready-made equations

Perimeter and angle facts give ready-made equations. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°.

Perimeter and angle facts give ready-made equations: the sides of a shape sum to its perimeter, and angles on a straight line sum to 180°.

Three facts, three equations

Each fact turns a picture or a sentence straight into an equation, ready to solve.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Two angles on a straight line are 3x and x + 40. Find x.
Write the equation3x + x + 40 = 180
Simplify the left side4x + 40 = 180
Subtract 40 from both sides4x = 140
Divide by 4x = 35
WORKED EXAMPLE D
An isosceles triangle has two sides of length 2n and a base of n + 3, and a perimeter of 27. Find n.
Write the equation2n + 2n + n + 3 = 27
Simplify the left side5n + 3 = 27
Subtract 3 from both sides5n = 24
Divide by 5n = 4.8
YOUR TURN · c
Three angles in a triangle are 2x, 3x and 40. Find x.
Equation: 2x + 3x + 40 = 180; solve: 5x = 140; x = 28
YOUR TURN · d
A rectangle has length n + 2 and width n, and perimeter 28. Find n.
Equation: 2(n + 2) + 2n = 28; solve: 4n + 4 = 28, 4n = 24; n = 6
