Topic 7.11 · Solving Equations · Lesson 2 of 6

One-Step Equations

A pair of scales stays level only if you do the same thing to both sides. Today's equations are solved by undoing, one step, the operation that was done to the unknown.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation is solved by finding the value of the unknown that makes both sides equal.
  • Whatever is done to one side must be done to the other, keeping the equation balanced.
  • Applying the inverse operation undoes what was done to the unknown: + and - are inverses, as are x and /.
  • Squares and square roots are inverses, as are cubes and cube roots.

Balance and inverse

Solving finds the value that makes both sides equal. Do the same to each side to keep the balance. The inverse operation undoes what was done to the unknown.

Solving an equation means finding the value that makes both sides equal, keeping the balance by doing the same thing to each side. Applying the inverse operation undoes what was done to the unknown.

Four inverse pairs

Addition and subtraction are inverses, and so are multiplication and division.

Squares, cubes and their roots

Squares and square roots are inverses, as are cubes and cube roots. A squared unknown usually has two solutions: positive and negative.

Squares and square roots are inverses, and so are cubes and cube roots. A squared unknown usually has two solutions, a positive and a negative, because both give the same square.

Matching each operation to its inverse

Squaring and cubing each have their own inverse. Match each one correctly when solving.

Squaring and cubing each have their own inverse, and matching them correctly is what makes root-solving reliable.
