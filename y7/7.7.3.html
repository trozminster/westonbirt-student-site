Topic 7.7 · Like Terms and Brackets · Lesson 3 of 3

Expanding and Forming Expressions

You can expand one bracket - today you expand several in the same expression and collect everything together, and you also flip the skill around: building an expression from a description in words or a shape, instead of simplifying one that's already given.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An expression with several brackets is expanded one bracket at a time, then like terms are collected.
  • Subtracting a whole bracket changes the sign of every term in it: 5−(x−2) =5−x+2
  • Forming an expression means naming the unknown with a letter and writing the relationship in algebra.
  • Perimeter adds all the side lengths, and '3 more than double n' is 2n+3
By the end you can
G4Expand two or more single brackets and simplify by collecting like terms.
G5Form expressions from worded and geometric contexts (e.g. perimeters, '3 more than double n').

Expanding several brackets

Expand one bracket at a time, then collect like terms. Subtracting a bracket changes every sign inside it. So 5−(x−2) =5−x+2

An expression with several brackets is expanded one bracket at a time, then like terms are collected. Subtracting a whole bracket changes the sign of every term in it: 5−(x−2) =5−x+2

Collecting after expanding

After expanding, collect like terms as before. Skipping this step leaves the answer unsimplified.

Once every bracket is expanded, the resulting terms are collected exactly as before - like terms combine, unlike terms don't. Forgetting this last step leaves an unsimplified answer, even if every bracket was expanded correctly.

Learn

Forming an expression from words

Name the unknown with a letter, then write the calculation in order. "3 more than double n" is 2n+3, not 3n+2

Forming an expression means naming the unknown with a letter and writing the relationship in algebra, in the order the calculation actually happens. "3 more than double n" means double n first, then add 3: 2n+3, not 3n+2

Forming a perimeter expression

Perimeter adds all four side lengths. For sides n and n+3, that is 2n+2(n+3).

Perimeter adds all the side lengths. A rectangle has two pairs of equal sides, so if one pair is n and the other is n+3, the perimeter is n+n+(n+3)+(n+3) =2n+2(n+3), not just one of each side added once.
