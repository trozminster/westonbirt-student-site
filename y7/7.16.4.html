Topic 7.16 · Decimals and Rounding · Lesson 4 of 6

Order of Operations with Decimals

A calculator follows the order of operations, but you still need to know what it should give. Today you apply the order of operations to decimals and check each answer with an estimate.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The order of operations applies to decimals exactly as it does to integers.
  • A decimal answer can be checked for plausibility by estimating with rounded values first.
  • Multiplying by a number less than 1 makes a quantity smaller, and dividing by one makes it larger.
  • In a worded problem the operations are identified before any calculation is started.
By the end you can
G4Evaluate multi-operation calculations involving decimals using the correct order of operations.

The same order

Decimals follow the same order of operations as integers. So 1.5+2×0.4=1.5+0.8=2.3

The order of operations applies to decimals exactly as it does to integers: brackets, indices, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. So 1.5+2×0.4=1.5+0.8=2.3

Operations before numbers

In a worded problem the operations are decided before any calculation is done. Writing the whole calculation first shows the order it needs.

Reading beyond one keyword

The whole problem decides the operation, not one keyword. "Shared equally" divides, but "each" may multiply or divide. Check the answer against an estimate.

Keywords help, but the whole problem decides the operation: "shared equally" divides, while "each" may multiply or divide. The answer is then checked against an estimate and against the context.
