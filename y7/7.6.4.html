Multiplying Algebraic Terms

Multiplying algebraic terms works differently from multiplying numbers: the letters combine as well as the numbers. Gathering them the right way today sets you up for collecting like terms next lesson, and for the indices work to come.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Multiplication works in any order, so numbers and letters in a product can be gathered: 2a×3b=2×3×a×b=6ab.
  • The coefficient is the number part of a term, and coefficients multiply together.
  • A letter multiplied by itself gives an index: a×a=a2, a×a×a=a3
  • By convention the number is written first and letters in alphabetical order: 6ab, not ba6

Gathering numbers and letters separately

Multiplication works in any order. Multiply the coefficients, never add them: 2a×3b=6ab.

Multiplication works in any order, so numbers and letters in a product can be gathered together: 2a×3b=2×3×a×b=6ab. The coefficient is the number part of a term, and coefficients multiply together just like ordinary numbers.

Writing convention

Write the number first and the letters in alphabetical order. So 6ab, not ba6 or 6ba.

By convention, the number is written first and letters in alphabetical order: 6ab, not ba6 or 6ba.

A letter times itself

A letter times itself gives an index: a×a=a2
Multiplying repeats a factor: a×a is a2, not 2a.

A letter multiplied by itself gives an index: a×a=a2, a×a×a=a3 - multiplying repeats a factor, it does not double it.

Combining an existing index with an extra factor

Multiplying by more of the same letter adds the indices. a2×a=a2+1=a3

When a term already has an index and is multiplied by more of the same letter, the indices add: a2×a=a2+1=a3, since the total count of a's being multiplied together is what the index counts.
