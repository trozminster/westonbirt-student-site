Decimal Place Value and Rounding

Race times, prices and measurements are all decimals, and a hundredth of a second can decide a medal. Today you compare decimals place by place and round them to a given number of places.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Each place to the right of the decimal point is worth a tenth of the one before: tenths, hundredths, thousandths.
  • Decimals are compared place by place from the left, so 0.45 is smaller than 0.5
  • Trailing zeros after the decimal point do not change a value, so 0.5=0.50
  • Rounding to a given number of decimal places is decided by the next digit, rounding up at 5 or above.

Places after the point

Each place after the point is worth a tenth of the one before. A digit's value is set by its place. In 3.407, the 4 is 4 tenths.

Each place to the right of the decimal point is worth a tenth of the place before: tenths, hundredths, thousandths. A digit's value is set by its place, not by how many digits follow it.

The deciding digit

Rounding to a number of decimal places keeps that many digits after the point. The next digit decides: 5 or more rounds up.

Rounding to a number of decimal places keeps that many digits after the point. The next digit decides: 5 or more rounds up, and 4 or less rounds down.

Round once, keep the places

Round once, from the original, never step by step. Keep every place, even a zero: 2.996 rounds to 3.00

A number is rounded once, from the original, never step by step. The answer keeps every place it was rounded to, even a zero: 2.996 to 2 decimal places is 3.00
