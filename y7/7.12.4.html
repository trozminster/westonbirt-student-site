Averages from Diagrams

A chart holds the same information as a list of numbers - you just have to know how to read it. Today you find averages and the range directly from bar charts and frequency tables.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A diagram holds the same information as a list, so averages and range can be read from it directly.
  • In a frequency table the mode is the value with the highest frequency, and the median is found by counting through the frequencies.
  • The mean of a frequency table is the total of value x frequency, divided by the total frequency.
  • Two data sets are compared using an average and the range, stated in context.

Reading averages from a diagram

A diagram holds the same information as a list. In a frequency table, the mode has the highest frequency.

A diagram holds the same information as a list, so the mode, median, mean and range can all be found directly from it without writing the list out first. In a frequency table, the mode is the value with the highest frequency - read straight off, no counting needed.

Finding the median from a frequency table

Find the median by counting through the frequencies to the middle position. With 12 values, the middle is position 6.5

The median of a frequency table is found by counting through the frequencies, in order, until the middle position is reached - not by counting how many different values there are.

The mean from a frequency table

Mean = total of (value × frequency) ÷ total frequency. Each value counts once for every time it occurred. Here, 14÷5=2.8

The mean of a frequency table is the total of (value × frequency) for every row, divided by the total frequency - not the values simply added and divided by how many rows there are. Each value counts once for every time it occurred.

Comparing two data sets

Compare two data sets with an average and the range. The range shows which is more consistent. State both in context.

Two data sets are compared using an average - usually the mean or median - together with the range, both stated back in the context of the question. An average alone says which is typically higher or lower; the range alongside it says which is more consistent.
