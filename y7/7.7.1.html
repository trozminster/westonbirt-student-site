Like Terms with Indices

Last lesson you multiplied powers of the same base. Today's trap is applying that same rule where it doesn't belong - adding like terms with indices needs a completely different rule, and telling the two apart under pressure is exactly the skill this lesson builds.

By the end you’ll know…
  • x and x2 are different powers of the same letter, so they are not like terms and cannot be combined.
  • Adding terms combines the coefficients and leaves the powers unchanged: x2+x2=2x2
  • Multiplying terms multiplies the coefficients and adds the powers: x2×x2=x4
  • These two rules are easily confused because the same terms appear in both.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Two rules, same-looking terms

Adding like terms keeps the powers: x2+x2=2x2
Multiplying adds the powers: x2×x2=x4

Adding like terms combines the coefficients and leaves the powers unchanged; multiplying terms multiplies the coefficients and adds the powers. These two rules are easily confused because the exact same terms - x2 and x2 - appear in both a sum and a product.

Checking the operation before applying a rule

Before simplifying, check the symbol between the terms. A mixed question needs a check for each part.

Before simplifying, check the symbol between the terms - a + or - means combine coefficients and keep the power; a × means multiply the coefficients and add the powers. A mixed question may use both operations in the same line, and each part needs its own check.
