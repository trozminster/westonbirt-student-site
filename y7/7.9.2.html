Topic 7.9 · Measuring and Calculating Angles · Lesson 2 of 3

Angle Facts: Points and Lines

You already know angles are measured in degrees - today you learn the fixed totals that govern angles meeting at a point or along a line, and use them to write equations from a geometric picture, deliberately linking straight back to the algebra you've just learned.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Angles around a point sum to 360 degrees.
  • Angles on a straight line sum to 180 degrees.
  • Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • Angles that sum to a known total can be written as an equation: 2x + (x + 30) = 180.
By the end you can
G3Calculate missing angles around a point and on a straight line.
G4Form equations from angle contexts (e.g. angles labelled 2x and x+30 on a line).

Learn

Angles around a point and on a line

Angles around a point sum to 360°. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°.

Angles around a point sum to 360°, since a full turn is 360. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°, since a straight line is half a turn.

Vertically opposite angles

Where two straight lines cross, vertically opposite angles are equal.

When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other (vertically opposite) are equal - not because they add to anything, but because each pair sits exactly opposite the crossing point.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Three angles around a point are 120, 150 and x. Find x.
Angles around a point add to 360°120 + 150 + x = 360
Add 120 and 150270 + x = 360
Subtract 270 from both sidesx = 90°
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Two angles on a straight line are 65 and y. Find y.
Angles on a straight line add to 180°65 + y = 180
Subtract 65 from both sidesy = 115°
YOUR TURN · a
Four angles around a point are 90, 80, 110 and z. Find z. (Hint: what do angles around a point always add up to?)
z = 360 - (90 + 80 + 110) = 360 - 280 = 80° - angles around a point add up to 360°.
YOUR TURN · b
Two angles on a straight line are 48 and w. Find w.
w = 180 - 48 = 132° - angles on a straight line add up to 180°.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Writing an equation from an angle fact

An angle fact can be written as an equation. Angles 2x and x + 30 on a straight line sum to 180.

Angles that sum to a known total can be written as an equation, using exactly the same angle facts as before. Two angles labelled 2x and x + 30 on a straight line give the equation 2x + (x + 30) = 180.

Substituting a given value

Substitute the given x into each angle's own expression. With x = 32, 4x = 128° and x + 20 = 52°.

Once the value of x is given, each angle is found by substituting it into that angle's own expression. With x = 32, angles of 4x and x + 20 on a straight line are 128° and 52°, which add to 180°.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Two angles on a straight line are x + 20 and 3x. Form an equation in x.
Angles on a straight line add to 180°3x + (x + 20) = 180
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Three angles around a point are 100, 2x and 3x. Form an equation, and use x = 52 to find the two unknown angles.
Angles around a point add to 360°2x + 3x + 100 = 360
Angle 2x2 × 52 = 104°
Angle 3x3 × 52 = 156°
YOUR TURN · c
Angles of 4x and 2x + 30 sit on a line. Form an equation in x. (Hint: what do the two angles have to add up to?)
4x + (2x + 30) = 180 - angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
YOUR TURN · d
Angles x, 2x and 150 meet at a point. Form an equation, then find both unknown angles when x = 70.
x + 2x + 150 = 360 - angles around a point add up to 360°. When x = 70 the angles are 70° and 140° (70 + 140 + 150 = 360).
