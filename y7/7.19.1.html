Topic 7.19 · 3D Shapes and Volume · Lesson 1 of 4

Naming 3D Shapes

Boxes, tins and tents are all solids, and each name tells you how the shape is built. Today you name solids and count their faces, edges and vertices.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A face is a flat surface, an edge is where two faces meet, and a vertex is where edges meet.
  • A prism has the same cross-section along its whole length, and is named after that cross-section.
  • A pyramid narrows from a base to a single vertex.
  • A cylinder, cone and sphere have curved surfaces, so they are neither prisms nor pyramids.
By the end you can
G2Name common 3D shapes (cube, cuboid, prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone, sphere).
G3Count and state the number of faces, edges and vertices of a given 3D shape.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Counting a prism

Count the two end faces, then the faces round the side. A triangular prism has 2+3=5 faces, 9 edges and 6 vertices.

Hidden edges count

Dashed edges are hidden at the back, and they still count. A square-based pyramid has 5 faces, 8 edges and 5 vertices.
