Topic 7.3 · Constructions and Loci · Lesson 4 of 5

Combining Loci

So far each locus has been drawn on its own. Real problems usually give more than one condition at once - today you combine loci, and learn that words like 'closer to' or 'within' describe not just a line, but a whole region.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An exact distance ('exactly 3 cm from A') defines a locus: a line or curve.
  • An inequality ('at least', 'no more than', 'within', 'closer to') defines a region bounded by that locus.
  • 'Closer to A than B' is the region on A's side of the perpendicular bisector of AB.
  • 'Closer to one line than another' is the region on that line's side of the angle bisector.
  • Several conditions at once give the region where they all overlap.
By the end you can

Exact distance vs region

An exact distance, like 'exactly 3 cm from A', gives a line or curve. 'Within 3 cm' gives a region bounded by that locus.

An exact distance ('exactly 3 cm from A') gives a locus - a line or curve. An inequality ('at least 3 cm', 'no more than 3 cm', 'within 3 cm') gives a region bounded by that locus - everywhere on one side of it (or between two loci).

'Closer to' regions

'Closer to A than B' is A's side of the perpendicular bisector of AB. 'Closer to one line' uses the angle bisector instead.

'Closer to A than B' is the region on A's side of the perpendicular bisector of AB - since every point ON the bisector is equally close to both, one side must be closer to A. 'Closer to one line than another' works the same way, using the angle bisector as the boundary instead.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Point M is fixed. Shade the region of points within 4 cm of M.
Boundarythe circle of radius 4 cm around M
Regionshade inside it, because within 4 cm means up to and including the circle
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Points N and P are marked. Shade the region closer to N than to P.
Boundarythe perpendicular bisector of NP, since every point on it is equally close to both
Regionshade the side that contains N
YOUR TURN · a
Point Q is fixed. Shade the region of points more than 3 cm from Q. (Hint: is the boundary circle itself included in this region, or excluded?)
draw the circle of radius 3 cm centred on Q and shade everything OUTSIDE it. The circle itself is not included - 'more than 3 cm' excludes the boundary, so it is drawn dashed.
YOUR TURN · b
Shade the region closer to S than to R.
construct the perpendicular bisector of RS and shade the side of it that contains S - every point on that side is nearer to S than to R.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Combining conditions

Construct each condition as its own locus or region. The answer is where all of them overlap.

When a problem gives several conditions at once, each is constructed as its own locus or region, and the final answer is wherever ALL the conditions overlap - not just one of them.

Reading a real-life problem

Real problems describe loci in words. A distance becomes a circle or a parallel line. 'Closer to' becomes a bisector.

Real problems describe loci in words, not symbols: 'a fire station within 5 km of both towns,' 'a path staying more than 2 m from the wall.' Each phrase is translated into a construction: a distance becomes a circle or parallel line, 'closer to' becomes a bisector, and the final shaded region answers the question.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A garden has two trees, Y and Z, 10 m apart. A path must be closer to Y than Z, AND within 6 m of Z. Construct and shade the required region.
Closer to Yshade Y's side of the bisector of YZ
Within 6 m of Zshade inside a 6 m circle
Regionwhere both shadings overlap
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A diagram shows a shaded region that is inside a 5 cm circle around point A but outside a 3 cm circle around point B. Describe in words what this region represents.
Inside the first circlewithin 5 cm of A
Outside the secondmore than 3 cm from B
In wordspoints within 5 cm of A but more than 3 cm from B
YOUR TURN · c
A warehouse must be within 8 km of town C and closer to C than to town D, which is 12 km from C. Construct and shade the region.
draw the circle of radius 8 km centred on C, then the perpendicular bisector of CD; shade only where the inside of the circle and the C side of the bisector overlap.
YOUR TURN · d
Roads E and F cross. Describe the region closer to E than to F and more than 2 cm from the crossing.
the region on the E side of the angle bisector of the two roads, more than 2 cm from the point where they cross - so it is bounded by the bisector on one side and by an arc of radius 2 cm around the crossing.
