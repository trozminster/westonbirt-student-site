Substitution with Powers

You've substituted into expressions with just letters and numbers - today powers join the mix, and where the brackets sit around an index changes the answer completely. Get the placement wrong and you'll be out by a factor of the base itself.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An index attaches only to the letter or bracket it follows, so 3x² means 3 × (x²).
  • (3x)² squares the whole term: 3x x 3x = 9x².
  • Squaring a negative gives a positive, cubing gives a negative: (-2)² = 4, (-2)³ = -8.
  • Indices are evaluated before multiplication, so -x² with x = 2 is -4, not 4.

The index attaches to what it directly follows

An index attaches only to the letter or bracket it follows. 3x² means 3 × (x²): the 3 is not squared. (3x)² squares the whole term: 9x².

An index attaches only to the letter or bracket it follows, so 3x² means 3 × (x²) - the coefficient 3 is NOT raised to the power. (3x)² is different: the bracket means the whole term 3x is squared: 3x × 3x = 9x².

Squaring and cubing negatives

Squaring a negative gives a positive: (-2)² = 4. Cubing a negative gives a negative: (-2)³ = -8.

Squaring a negative gives a positive, cubing gives a negative: (-2)² = (-2) × (-2) = 4, (-2)³ = (-2) × (-2) × (-2) = -8.

Squaring vs cubing a substituted negative

The sign rules still apply after substituting a negative. With a = -10, a² = 100 but a³ = -1000.

Squaring a negative gives a positive and cubing keeps it negative - this applies exactly the same way once a negative value is substituted in: with a = -10, a² = 100 but a³ = -1000.

A lone negative outside the index

-x² means -(x²): the minus is outside the power. With x = 2, -x² = -4, not 4. Only brackets put the minus inside: (-x)².

Indices are evaluated before multiplication, so -x² with x = 2 means -(x²) = -4, not (-x)² = 4 - the minus sign is not inside the power unless brackets say so.
