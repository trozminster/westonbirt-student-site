Substitution with Powers

You've substituted into expressions with just letters and numbers - today powers join the mix, and where the brackets sit around an index changes the answer completely. Get the placement wrong and you'll be out by a factor of the base itself.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An index attaches only to the letter or bracket it follows, so 3x2 means 3×(x2).
  • (3x)2 squares the whole term: 3x×3x=9x2
  • Squaring a negative gives a positive, cubing gives a negative: (−2)2=4, (−2)3=−8
  • Indices are evaluated before multiplication, so −x2 with x=2 is −4, not 4

The index attaches to what it directly follows

An index attaches only to the letter or bracket it follows. 3x2 means 3×(x2): the 3 is not squared. (3x)2 squares the whole term: 9x2

An index attaches only to the letter or bracket it follows, so 3x2 means 3×(x2) - the coefficient 3 is NOT raised to the power. (3x)2 is different: the bracket means the whole term 3x is squared: 3x×3x=9x2

Squaring and cubing negatives

Squaring a negative gives a positive: (−2)2=4
Cubing a negative gives a negative: (−2)3=−8

Squaring a negative gives a positive, cubing gives a negative: (−2)2 =(−2)×(−2) =4, (−2)3 =(−2)×(−2)×(−2) =−8

Squaring vs cubing a substituted negative

The sign rules still apply after substituting a negative. With a=−10, a2=100 but a3=−1000

Squaring a negative gives a positive and cubing keeps it negative - this applies exactly the same way once a negative value is substituted in: with a=−10, a2=100 but a3=−1000

A lone negative outside the index

−x2 means −(x2): the minus is outside the power. With x=2, −x2=−4, not 4
Only brackets put the minus inside: (−x)2

Indices are evaluated before multiplication, so −x2 with x=2 means −(x2)=−4, not (−x)2=4 - the minus sign is not inside the power unless brackets say so.
