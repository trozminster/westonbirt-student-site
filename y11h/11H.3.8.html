Fraction Equations Leading to Quadratics

A journey in two legs at different speeds, or two machines sharing a job, gives an equation with the unknown in two denominators, and clearing them lands on a quadratic. Today joins two things you can already do: clear the fractions, then solve the quadratic.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation with two algebraic denominators is cleared by multiplying every term by both denominators, whose product is the common denominator.
  • Clearing usually leaves a quadratic, which is rearranged to =0 and solved by factorising or by the quadratic formula.
  • A value that makes an original denominator zero is not a solution and is rejected.
  • Exam questions often ask to show that the equation simplifies to a given quadratic first; the working must reach that quadratic exactly.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Multiply by both denominators

Multiply every term by the product of the denominators. A term with no denominator picks up both.

Two different algebraic denominators share no factor, so their product is the common denominator, and every term on both sides is multiplied by it. Each fraction keeps the factor it was missing, and a term with no denominator picks up both.

Finish on the given quadratic

Collect every term on one side so it reads =0
A 'show that' ends on the exact quadratic given.

After clearing, every term is collected on one side so that the equation reads =0, in the order the question gives. A 'show that' is finished only when the working reaches that exact quadratic.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Solve, then check the denominators

Solve the cleared quadratic by factorising or by the formula. Reject a value that makes an original denominator zero.

Once cleared, the equation is an ordinary quadratic: factorise it if it factorises, otherwise use the quadratic formula. Each solution is checked against the original denominators, because a value that makes one zero is not allowed.

When it will not factorise

Use the formula when it will not factorise. Read a, b and c, signs included, from the =0 form.

A cleared quadratic that does not factorise is solved with the quadratic formula, reading a, b and c with their signs from the =0 form. Rounding happens only at the end, to the accuracy asked.
