Factorising to Cancel Fractions

Algebraic fractions appear in rearranged formulae and in equations at the top of the GCSE. Today we factorise first so that common factors, including brackets, can cancel.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A fraction whose numerator or denominator is a sum can only cancel once that sum has been factorised.
  • Factorising turns the sum into a product, and only then are the common factors visible.
  • A bracket appearing in both numerator and denominator cancels, leaving 1 in its place.
  • A quadratic factorises into two brackets, either of which may be the common factor.
  • A difference of two squares factorises as (x+a)(x−a), which is often what makes the cancelling possible.

Factorise, then cancel

Only a common factor cancels, never a single term of a sum. Factorising turns the sum into a product.

A cancelled bracket leaves 1

A bracket over itself is 1
The factor left behind is the answer.

=x+3

Non-monic quadratics factorise too

A quadratic with a coefficient on x2 still factorises into two brackets. Either bracket may be the common factor.

Reversed brackets give −1

A bracket and its reverse differ only in sign. Since 2−x =−(x−2), they cancel to −1
