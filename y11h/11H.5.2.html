Show That and Algebraic Identities

Exam questions often give the answer and ask you to show it: every mark is in the working. Today: how to write a chain of steps that a reader can check line by line, with nothing taken on trust.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A 'show that' question gives the result, so every mark is for the working: each step is written and none is skipped.
  • An identity is true for every value of its letters, while an equation is true only for some values; an identity is written with the symbol 'is identical to', three lines.
  • An identity is proved by starting from one side, usually the more complicated, and expanding and simplifying until it becomes the other side.
  • Checking with numbers can disprove an identity but can never prove it.
  • In a geometric 'show that', each length or area is written as an expression first, then a fact about the shape gives the equation, which is rearranged into exactly the given form.
By the end you can
G6Prove an algebraic identity by expanding and simplifying one side until it equals the other, such as (n + 3)² - (n - 3)² = 12n.
G6Show that a given equation follows from a geometric situation, such as an area, a perimeter or Pythagoras, by forming expressions and rearranging into the given form.

An identity holds for every value

An equation is true for only some values. An identity is true for every value.

An equation is true only for some values of its letter, while an identity is true for every value. So an identity cannot be solved: there is nothing to find.

Simplify one side into the other

A proof simplifies the harder side until it becomes the other. Numbers can check an identity but never prove it.

A proof starts from one side, usually the harder one, and simplifies it until it becomes the other side. Numbers can check an identity, but can never prove it.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Prove that (n + 5)² - (n - 5)² = 20n for every n.
Expand both bracketsn² + 10n + 25 - (n² - 10n + 25)
Remove the bracket= n² + 10n + 25 - n² + 10n - 25
Collect like terms= 20n
Conclusionthe left side is 20n, so proved
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Show that (x + 2)(x + 6) - 4(2x + 3) = x².
Expand the double bracketx² + 8x + 12 - 4(2x + 3)
Expand the single bracket= x² + 8x + 12 - 8x - 12
Collect like terms= x²
Conclusionthe left side is x², as required
YOUR TURN · a
Show that (x + 3)² - 6x = x² + 9.
x² + 6x + 9 - 6x; = x² + 9
YOUR TURN · b
Prove that (2n + 1)² - (2n - 1)² = 8n.
4n² + 4n + 1 - (4n² - 4n + 1); = 4n² + 4n + 1 - 4n² + 4n - 1; = 8n

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Every length becomes an expression

Each length or area is written as an expression first. A fact about the shape then gives the equation.

In a geometric 'show that', each length, area or angle is written as an expression first. A fact about the shape, such as its area or Pythagoras, then gives the equation.

End exactly on the given form

The working ends exactly on the given equation. Solving it is a different question.

The working expands, collects every term on one side, and ends exactly on the given equation. Solving it is a different question, and earns none of the marks.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A rectangle is (x + 5) cm by (x - 2) cm, with area 30 cm². Show that x² + 3x - 40 = 0.
Use the area(x + 5)(x - 2) = 30
Expand the bracketsx² + 3x - 10 = 30
Subtract 30 from both sidesx² + 3x - 40 = 0
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A right-angled triangle has shorter sides x cm and (x + 7) cm, and hypotenuse 13 cm. Show that x² + 7x - 60 = 0.
Use Pythagorasx² + (x + 7)² = 169
Expand the bracketx² + x² + 14x + 49 = 169
Collect on one side2x² + 14x - 120 = 0
Divide by 2x² + 7x - 60 = 0
YOUR TURN · c
A rectangle is x cm by (x + 4) cm, with area 45 cm². Show that x² + 4x - 45 = 0.
x(x + 4) = 45; x² + 4x = 45; x² + 4x - 45 = 0
YOUR TURN · d
A rectangle is (2x + 1) cm by (x - 3) cm, with perimeter 32 cm. Show that x = 6.
2(2x + 1 + x - 3) = 32; 2(3x - 2) = 32; 6x - 4 = 32; 6x = 36, so x = 6
