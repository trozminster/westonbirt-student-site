Topic 11H.8 · Vectors · Lesson 4 of 8

Vector Routes in Shapes

In geometry problems the vectors come as letters, a and b, not numbers. Today we reach any point in a shape by a route along known vectors.

By the end you’ll know…
  • In a diagram, the vector between two points is found by a route along known vectors, adding each step: vector AC = vector AB + vector BC.
  • A step taken against a vector's arrow takes a minus sign.
  • Any route between the same two points gives the same vector, so the simplest route is chosen.
  • With vector OA=a and vector OB=b, vector AB =−a+b =b−a.
  • Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal vectors, and a side parallel to a and twice as long is 2a.

A route of known steps

Any vector in a diagram is a route along known vectors. A step against an arrow takes a minus sign.

Opposite sides are equal vectors

In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal vectors. So a route can use whichever side is known.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
In triangle OAB, OA→ = a and OB→ = b. Write AB→ and BA→ in terms of a and b.
Go A to O to BAB→=−a+b
=b−a
Reverse the routeBA→=a−b
WORKED EXAMPLE B
OABC is a parallelogram with OA→ = a and OC→ = c. Write OB→ and AC→ in terms of a and c.
Go along a, then cOB→=a+c
Go back along a, then cAC→=c−a

A multiple for a longer side

A side parallel to a and twice as long is 2a. The diagram's ratio of lengths gives the multiplier.

A regular hexagon

Opposite sides of a regular hexagon are parallel and equal. So two letters describe all six sides.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
ABCD is a trapezium with AB→ = a, AD→ = d and DC→ = 2a. Find BC→.
Go B to A to D to CBC→=−a+d+2a
Collect termsBC→=a+d
WORKED EXAMPLE D
OABCDE is a regular hexagon with OA→ = a, AB→ = b and BC→ = b−a. Find DE→ and OC→.
Use the opposite sideDE→=−b
Go O to A to B to COC→=a+b+b−a
OC→=2b
YOUR TURN · c
PQRS is a trapezium with PQ→ = 3a, PS→ = b and SR→ = a. Find QR→.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Go B to A to D to C → Collect terms

Hint. Follow the worked example: Use the opposite side → Go O to A to B to C
