Topic 11H.7 · Differentiation · Lesson 2 of 6

Differentiating Powers of x

Drawing tangents by eye only ever gives an estimate. Differentiation gives the exact gradient of a curve at every point from its equation, with no drawing at all.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The gradient function dy/dx gives the gradient of a curve at every point.
  • For y=xn, dy/dx =nxn−1: the power multiplies, then drops by one; so y=axn gives dy/dx =anxn−1
  • A term in x differentiates to its coefficient, and a constant differentiates to 0, because its graph is flat.
  • A sum or difference is differentiated term by term.
  • The gradient at a point is found by substituting its x-value into dy/dx, not into y.
By the end you can
G8Differentiate expressions of the form a xn for positive integer n, term by term, including x terms and constants.
G8Find the gradient of a curve at a given point by differentiating and substituting the x-value.

The power comes down and drops by one

The gradient function dy/dx gives the gradient at every point. For y=xn, the power comes down and drops by one.

The gradient function dy/dx gives the gradient of the curve at every point. For y=xn, dy/dx =nxn−1: the power multiplies and then drops by one.

Substitute into the gradient function

The gradient at a point puts its x into dy/dx, not into y. On y=x2, the gradient at x=3 is 6

The gradient at one point comes from putting its x-value into dy/dx, not into y. On y=x2, dy/dx =2x, so the gradient at x=3 is 6

The sign shows the direction

A negative gradient means the curve falls there. Zero means it is momentarily flat.

A negative value of dy/dx means the curve is falling there, and zero means it is momentarily flat. On y=x2, the gradient is −4 at x=−2 and 0 at x=0
