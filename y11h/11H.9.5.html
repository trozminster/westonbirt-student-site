Composite Functions

A shop takes 20% off, then a voucher takes £5 off: two functions, one after the other, and the order changes the price. Today we combine functions and see why the order matters.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A composite function applies one function to the output of another.
  • fg(x) means f(g(x)): g acts first and f second, because the function written next to x acts first.
  • fg(x) and gf(x) are usually different, and ff(x) means f(f(x)), not f(x) squared.
  • fg(x) is found algebraically by putting the whole of g(x), in a bracket, in place of every x in f(x).

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The inside acts first

In fg(x), g acts first and f acts on its output. The function written next to x is used first.

Order matters

Swapping the order usually changes the result. So fg(3) and gf(3) are different.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
f(x) = 2x - 1 and g(x) = x². Find fg(3).
Work out g firstg(3) = 9
Then apply ff(9) = 17
WORKED EXAMPLE B
f(x) = 2x - 1. Find ff(-2).
Apply f oncef(-2) = -5
Apply f againf(-5) = -11

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The whole of g(x) goes in

To form fg(x), all of g(x) replaces x in f. Writing it in a bracket makes it reach every term.

Then an equation

An equation like fg(x) = k is in x. The composite is formed first, then the equation is solved.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
f(x) = x² + 1 and g(x) = 2x - 3. Find fg(x).
Replace x with g(x)fg(x) = (2x - 3)² + 1
Expand the bracket= 4x² - 12x + 9 + 1
= 4x² - 12x + 10
WORKED EXAMPLE D
f(x) = x² + 1 and g(x) = 2x - 3. Solve gf(x) = 15.
Form gf(x)gf(x) = 2(x² + 1) - 3
= 2x² - 1
Set it equal to 152x² - 1 = 15
x² = 8
x = 2√2
or x = -2√2
YOUR TURN · c
f(x) = 4x + 1 and g(x) = x - 2. Find fg(x) and gf(x).
fg(x) = 4(x - 2) + 1 = 4x - 7; gf(x) = 4x + 1 - 2 = 4x - 1
