Vector Notation and Magnitude

A pilot needs more than a speed: the plane must also head the right way. A vector carries both, a size and a direction, and today we write vectors down, measure them and find their bearings.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A vector has both a magnitude (its size) and a direction; a scalar has a size only.
  • A vector can be written as a column vector, as vector AB from the point A to the point B, or as a single bold letter such as a, underlined when handwritten.
  • In a column vector the top number is the move right (negative for left) and the bottom number is the move up (negative for down).
  • Equal vectors have the same magnitude and the same direction wherever they are drawn, and vector BA = -vector AB.
  • The magnitude of a column vector with components x and y is the square root of (x2+y2), by Pythagoras.

Size and direction together

A vector has a size and a direction. A column vector gives the move: across on top, up below.

The same vector anywhere

Equal vectors have the same size and direction, wherever they are drawn. Reversing the direction changes every sign: BA→ = -AB→.

A bearing for a vector

A bearing is measured clockwise from north. On a grid, north is straight up.

The triangle under the arrow

The components are the two short sides of a right-angled triangle. Inverse tan gives the angle from north.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
