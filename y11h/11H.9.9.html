Topic 11H.9 · Functions and Graph Transformations · Lesson 9 of 11

Vertical Graph Transformations

A graph can be moved, stretched or flipped without plotting it again, just by changing its equation. Today: changes outside the bracket, which act on the outputs and move a graph up and down.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A change outside the bracket acts on the outputs, so it moves the graph vertically and every x-coordinate stays the same.
  • y=f(x)+a translates the graph up by a, or down when a is negative, so the point (p, q) maps to (p, q+a).
  • y = af(x) stretches the graph vertically by scale factor a, so (p, q) maps to (p, aq) and points on the x-axis stay put.
  • y=−f(x) reflects the graph in the x-axis, so (p, q) maps to (p, −q).
By the end you can

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Outside acts on outputs

Adding a outside the bracket adds a to every output. So y=f(x) + a moves the graph up a. Every x stays put.

A minus outside flips

A minus outside the bracket changes the sign of every output. So y=−f(x) is a reflection in the x-axis.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Multiply the outputs

Multiplying outside the bracket multiplies every output. So y = af(x) is a vertical stretch, scale factor a.

The roots stay put

A point on the x-axis has output 0, and a×0=0
So a vertical stretch never moves a root.
