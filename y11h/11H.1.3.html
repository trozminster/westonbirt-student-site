Rationalising Simple Denominators

A fraction with a surd below, such as 63, is hard to estimate or to compare. Rationalising moves the surd to the top, where it is easy to work with.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Rationalising a denominator rewrites a fraction so that no surd is left in its denominator.
  • Multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the same number leaves the value of the fraction unchanged.
  • For a ÷ the square root of b, multiplying top and bottom by the square root of b gives (a x the square root of b)÷b, since the square root of b x the square root of b=b.
  • The answer is then simplified, cancelling any common factor of the whole numbers outside the roots.
By the end you can
G7Rationalise a denominator of the form the square root of b, such as 6 ÷ the square root of 3=2 x the square root of 3
G8Rationalise a denominator of the form c x the square root of b, or one that needs simplifying first, such as 10÷(3 x the square root of 5).

Same value, new form

Multiplying top and bottom by the same number keeps a fraction's value. Choosing √b clears the surd from the denominator.

Cancel outside the root only

After rationalising, whole numbers outside the roots may cancel. A number inside a root never cancels with one outside.
