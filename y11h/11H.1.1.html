Simplifying Surds

2 cannot be written exactly as a decimal, so an exact answer keeps it as a surd. Today we simplify surds, the first step in every exact calculation with roots.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A surd is a square root that cannot be written exactly as a whole number or a fraction, such as the square root of 2; the square root of 9=3 is not a surd.
  • The square root of (ab) = the square root of a x the square root of b, so the square root of 12 = the square root of 4 x the square root of 3=2 x the square root of 3
  • A surd is simplified by taking out the largest square factor of the number inside.
  • The square root of a x the square root of a=a, and the square root of a ÷ the square root of b = the square root of ab.
  • The square root of (a+b) is not the square root of a + the square root of b.

Multiply the insides

Roots multiply by multiplying the numbers inside. And √a × √a = a, a whole number.

Divide the insides

Roots divide by dividing the numbers inside. Numbers outside the roots divide separately.
