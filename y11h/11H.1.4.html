Topic 11H.1 · Surds · Lesson 4 of 4

Rationalising Harder Denominators

A denominator such as 3−5 is not cleared by multiplying by 5
Changing the sign between its terms makes the surds cancel, by the difference of two squares.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A denominator such as a + the square root of b is rationalised by multiplying top and bottom by a - the square root of b, which changes only the sign between the terms.
  • The product (a + the square root of b)(a - the square root of b)=a2−b is a whole number, because the surd terms cancel.
  • The numerator is expanded fully, every term times every term.
  • The result is simplified by cancelling a factor common to every term of the numerator and the denominator.
By the end you can
G8Rationalise a denominator of the form a + the square root of b or a - the square root of b, such as 4÷(3 - the square root of 5).
G8Rationalise and simplify a fraction where both the numerator and the denominator contain surds, such as (2 + the square root of 3)÷(2 - the square root of 3).

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Change the sign

Multiplying a+b by a−b clears the surd. The middle terms cancel, leaving a2−b.

Top and bottom together

The numerator is multiplied by the same bracket, to keep the value. Its expansion uses every term.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Expand the top fully

When the numerator also holds a surd, it expands like any double bracket. The denominator still becomes a whole number.

Cancel every term or none

A factor cancels only if it divides every term of the numerator. So (8+45)÷4 =2+5
