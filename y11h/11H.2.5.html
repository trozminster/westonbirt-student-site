Topic 11H.2 · Further Trigonometry · Lesson 5 of 6

Cosine Rule Angles and Area

A triangular plot of land is often measured by its three sides, or by two sides and an angle. Today we find an angle from three sides, and the area of any triangle without its height.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Rearranged, the cosine rule finds an angle from three sides: cos A=(b2+c2−a2)÷(2bc), where a is the side opposite A.
  • A negative value of cos A means that A is obtuse.
  • The area of any triangle is 12 ab sin C, where C is the angle between the sides a and b.
  • A given area can be used in the same formula to find a missing side or angle.
By the end you can
G7Use the cosine rule to find an angle, given all three sides.
G7Find the area of a triangle using 12 ab sin C, and find a missing side or angle from a given area.

Rearrange for the angle

The cosine rule rearranges to find an angle from three sides. The side opposite the angle is the one subtracted.

Negative means obtuse

A negative cosine is not a mistake. It means the angle is obtuse, and the inverse cosine gives it directly.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
In triangle ABC, a=7 cm, b=5 cm and c=8 cm. Find A.
Substitutecos A=25+64−492×5×8
Simplifycos A=40÷80=0.5
A=60°

Two sides and the angle between

The area of any triangle is half of ab sin C. The angle C must sit between sides a and b.

Work back from an area

Substitute the known area and solve for the missing value. An angle found this way could also be 180° minus it.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
In triangle ABC, a=8 cm, b=11 cm and C=40°. Find the area.
Two sides and the angle between12×8×11 × sin 40°
Evaluate44 sin 40°
area = 28.3 cm²
