Adding and Subtracting Vectors

A boat's own velocity and the current's velocity add up to where the boat really goes. Today we add and subtract vectors, by drawing and by column vectors.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Vectors add head to tail: vector AB + vector BC = vector AC, which is called the resultant.
  • Column vectors add and subtract component by component.
  • Subtracting a vector adds its negative: a−b=a+(−b), where −b has the same length as b and points the other way.
  • A route is reversed by changing every sign: vector CA = -vector AC.
  • The magnitude of a resultant is found from its components, not by adding magnitudes.
By the end you can
G6Add and subtract column vectors, including scalar multiples such as 2a−3b, and draw the resultant.
G7Find the magnitude of a resultant, and solve a vector equation such as 2a+kb=c for an unknown scalar.

Head to tail

Vectors add head to tail, and the sum is called the resultant. So AB→ + BC→ = AC→.

One component at a time

Column vectors add and subtract one component at a time. Subtracting b is the same as adding −b.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Magnitudes do not just add

A resultant's magnitude comes from its own components, by Pythagoras. So 3 across and 4 up make 5, not 7

A vector equation is two equations

A vector equation holds component by component. It gives one equation for the top and one for the bottom.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
a = (5−3) and b = (111). Find the magnitude of a+b.
Add the componentsa+b = (68)
Use Pythagoras36+64=10
