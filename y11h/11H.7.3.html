Differentiating after Rewriting

The rule only works on single powers of x, but real expressions arrive as brackets and fractions. Today: rewriting any expression into powers of x first, including negative powers, so the rule always applies.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The rule differentiates one power of x at a time, so an expression is rewritten as a sum of powers of x first.
  • Brackets are expanded before differentiating, never differentiated factor by factor.
  • A fraction with a single term underneath splits into separate terms: x3+2xx =x2+2
  • A power of x underneath becomes a negative power: 1x=x−1 and 4x2=4x−2
  • The same rule works for negative integer powers: the power multiplies and drops by one, so x−2 gives −2x−3

Learn

A power underneath is a negative power

A power underneath becomes a negative power. So 4x2 is rewritten as 4x−2 first.

A power of x in the denominator is written as a negative power before differentiating. So 4x2 becomes 4x−2, which the same rule then handles.

The power still drops by one

The power still comes down and drops by one. So x−2 gives −2x−3, which is −2x3

The power still multiplies and drops by one, so −2 becomes −3
So x−2 differentiates to −2x−3, which is −2x3
