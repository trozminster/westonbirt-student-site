Solving Equations with Fractions

Formulas in science and finance are full of fractions, and finding an unknown in one means clearing them first. One move, multiplying every term on both sides by a denominator, turns each of today's equations into one you can already solve.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation with fractions is cleared by multiplying every term on BOTH sides by a common denominator, usually the LCM of the denominators.
  • A numerator that is a sum is bracketed before it is multiplied, so the multiplication reaches every term of it.
  • A term that is not a fraction is multiplied too: it does not keep its old value.
  • An unknown in a denominator is cleared the same way, by multiplying both sides by that whole denominator.
  • The cleared equation is linear, is solved by the balance method, and the answer is checked in the original equation.

Multiply both whole sides

Clear a fraction by multiplying both whole sides by its denominator. A sum that is multiplied goes in a bracket first.

An equation stays balanced only when both whole sides are multiplied by the same number, so every term on both sides is multiplied by the denominator. A sum that is multiplied goes in a bracket first, so the multiplication reaches every term of it.

An unknown below the line

An unknown in a denominator is cleared the same way. The whole denominator becomes a bracket on the other side.

An unknown in a denominator is cleared the same way, by multiplying both sides by that whole denominator. What was below the line then appears as a bracket on the other side.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Clear every denominator at once

Multiply every term by the LCM of the denominators. Each fraction clears to a bracket; a whole term is multiplied too.

When an equation has two or more fractions, multiplying every term by the LCM of the denominators clears them all in one step. Each fraction becomes its numerator, in a bracket, times what is left of the LCM, and a whole-number term is multiplied too.

A subtracted fraction keeps its bracket

A subtracted fraction clears to minus a bracket. Every term inside changes sign.

A fraction that is subtracted clears to minus its whole numerator, so that numerator stays in a bracket until it is expanded. The minus then changes the sign of every term inside.
