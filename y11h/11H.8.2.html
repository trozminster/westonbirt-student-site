Scalar Multiples and Parallel Vectors

Doubling a velocity keeps the direction and doubles the speed. Today we multiply vectors by numbers, and use that to spot parallel vectors.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Multiplying a vector by a scalar k multiplies each of its components by k.
  • The vector ka is parallel to a and k times as long; when k is negative, ka points the opposite way to a.
  • Two vectors are parallel exactly when one is a scalar multiple of the other: the vector with components 2 and 3 is parallel to the one with 6 and 9, but not to the one with 4 and 5
  • The scalar gives the ratio of the lengths: if vector PQ=3 x vector AB, then PQ is three times as long as AB.
By the end you can

Scaling keeps the direction

Multiplying by a scalar multiplies every component. 3a points the same way as a and is three times as long.

Parallel means a multiple

Two vectors are parallel when one is a scalar multiple of the other. A negative multiple is parallel too, pointing the other way.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
a = (3−2). Find 4a and −2a.
Multiply each part by 44a = (12−8)
Multiply each part by −2−2a = (−64)

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One multiplier for both

Parallel vectors share one multiplier for both components. So a known pair gives the multiplier, and the multiplier finds the unknown.

The multiplier is the length ratio

The scalar multiple gives the ratio of the lengths. PQ→ = 3AB→ makes PQ three times as long as AB.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
(4k) is parallel to (10−15). Find k.
Find the multiplier10÷4=2.5
Apply it to k2.5k=−15
k=−6
WORKED EXAMPLE D
PQ→ = (6−9) and AB→ = (−46). Show they are parallel and give PQ : AB.
Write PQ→ as a multiplePQ→=−1.5 × AB→
Parallel, so the ratioPQ : AB=3 : 2
