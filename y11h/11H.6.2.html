Interior and Exterior Angles

Exterior angles are easy to share out; interior angles are what a builder or a designer actually measures. Today: getting from one to the other, and finding unknown angles in any polygon at all.

By the end you’ll know…
  • In a regular polygon all interior angles are equal, so each one is (n−2)×180÷n.
  • At every vertex the interior and exterior angles lie on a straight line, so they sum to 180°.
  • Each interior angle can therefore be found more quickly as 180−(360÷n).
  • The number of sides follows from a given interior angle by finding the exterior angle first.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Interior and exterior make a straight line

Interior and exterior angles sit on a straight line, summing to 180°. So each interior angle is 180°−360°÷n.

At every vertex the interior and exterior angles lie on a straight line, so they sum to 180°. So a regular polygon's interior angle is 180° minus 360°÷n.

The angle sum splits into triangles

Diagonals from one vertex make n−2 triangles. So the interior angles sum to (n−2)×180°.

Diagonals from one vertex split any polygon into n−2 triangles. So its interior angles sum to (n−2)×180°.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the interior angle of a regular 12-sided polygon.
Find the exterior angle360÷12=30°
Interior angle180−30=150°
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A regular polygon has interior angles of 165°. How many sides does it have?
Find the exterior angle180−165=15°
Number of sides360÷15=24The exterior angle comes first: 360÷165 would be meaningless.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The angle sum gives the equation

Any polygon's interior angles sum to (n−2)×180°. Expressions are added and set equal to that sum.

An irregular polygon's interior angles still sum to (n−2)×180°. When the angles are expressions, their total is set equal to that sum.

Solve, then substitute back

Solving gives x, usually a step and not the answer. Each angle comes from substituting x back.

Solving the equation gives x, which is usually a step and not the answer. Each angle comes from substituting x back into its expression.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A quadrilateral has interior angles x, 2x, x+30 and 2x+30
Find the largest angle.
Use the angle sumx+2x+x+30+2x+30 =360
Collect like terms6x+60=360
Subtract 60 from both sides6x=300
Divide by 6x=50
Largest angle2x+30=130°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A hexagon has interior angles 4x, 4x, 5x, 5x, 6x and 6x. Find the smallest angle.
Use the angle sum30x=720
Divide by 30x=24
Smallest angle4x=96°
