The Sine Rule for Lengths

Most triangles in surveying and navigation have no right angle, so SOH CAH TOA cannot be used. Today we use the sine rule to find a missing side in any triangle.

By the end you’ll know…
  • In any triangle, each side divided by the sine of its opposite angle gives the same value: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C.
  • Side a is opposite angle A, so a side and the angle opposite it form a pair.
  • The sine rule needs one complete pair plus one more side or angle.
  • To find a length, the rule is written with the sides on top and rearranged: a=b x sin A ÷ sinB.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Each side faces an angle

Side a lies opposite angle A. Each side over the sine of its opposite angle gives one value.

Sides on top to find a side

To find a side, write the rule with sides on top. Then multiply by the sine under the unknown.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
In triangle ABC, A=40°, B=65° and a=8 cm. Find b.
Write the ruleasinA=bsinB
Substitute8sin40°=bsin65°
Rearrangeb=8sin65°÷sin40°
b=11.3 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE B
In triangle PQR, P=72°, Q=48° and q=15 cm. Find p.
Write the rulepsinP=qsinQ
Substitutepsin72°=15sin48°
Rearrangep=15sin72°÷sin48°
p=19.2 cm

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Angle sum first

The sine rule needs a complete pair. When the given side's angle is missing, find it from the angle sum.

Degrees, not radians

A calculator in radians gives wrong sines. Check for D on the screen before you start.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
In triangle ABC, A=35°, B=80° and c=12 cm. Find a.
Find angle C180°−35°−80°=65°
Use the pairasin35°=12sin65°
Rearrangea=12sin35°÷sin65°
a=7.59 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Coastguards A and B are 5 km apart. A ship S has angle SAB=52° and angle SBA=71°. Find AS.
