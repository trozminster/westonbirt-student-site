Expressions with Surds

Exact answers often combine numbers and surds, such as 3+25
Today we add, subtract and expand surds exactly as we would in algebra.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Like surds collect like terms: 3 x the square root of 2+5 x the square root of 2=8 x the square root of 2, but the square root of 2 + the square root of 3 cannot be simplified.
  • Surds with different numbers inside may become like surds once simplified: the square root of 8 + the square root of 18=2 x the square root of 2+3 x the square root of 2=5 x the square root of 2
  • Brackets containing surds expand as in algebra, every term times every term, using the square root of a x the square root of a=a.
  • (a + the square root of b)(a - the square root of b)=a2−b, a whole number, because the surd terms cancel.

Like surds collect

Surds with the same number inside are like terms. So 32+52=82, but 2+3 stays as it is.

Simplify, then collect

Every term times every term

Brackets with surds expand like algebra. Then a×a= a turns a surd into a whole number.

The surds can cancel

In (a+b)(a−b) the surd terms cancel. The result, a2−b, is a whole number.
