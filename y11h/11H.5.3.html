Proving Factors and Multiples

Odd plus odd is always even, but no list of examples can show that it is always. Today: writing any integer as a letter, so that one line of algebra covers every case at once.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Any integer is n, where n is an integer, and consecutive integers are n, n+1, n+2
  • An even number is 2n and an odd number is 2n+1; consecutive even numbers are 2n and 2n+2, and consecutive odd numbers are 2n+1 and 2n+3
  • Two unrelated numbers need two letters, such as 2m+1 and 2n+1, because one letter would make them equal.
  • An expression is a multiple of k when it can be written as k × (an integer), so a proof factorises k out of every term and states that the bracket is an integer.
  • A proof ends with a sentence saying what has been shown.

Learn

A multiple of k is k times an integer

A multiple of k is k times an integer. The proof factorises k out and names the bracket an integer.

An expression is a multiple of k when it can be written as k times an integer. So the proof factorises k out and says that the bracket is an integer.

The factor must divide every term

Every term must share the factor taken out. So 6n+4 is a multiple of 2, not of 6

Every term must share the factor that is taken out. So 6n+4 is a multiple of 2, as 2(3n+2), but not a multiple of 6

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Integers written with letters

Consecutive integers are n, n+1 and n+2
Even numbers are 2n and odd numbers are 2n+1

Consecutive integers are n, n+1 and n+2, each one more than the last. An even number is 2n and an odd number is 2n+1

Two unrelated numbers need two letters

Consecutive odd numbers are 2n+1 and 2n+3
Two unrelated odd numbers need two letters.

Consecutive odd numbers are 2n+1 and 2n+3, two apart. Two unrelated odd numbers need two letters, 2m+1 and 2n+1
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Prove that the sum of three consecutive integers is a multiple of 3
Write the integersn, n+1, n+2
Add themn +(n+1) +(n+2)=3n+3
Take out 3=3(n+1)
Conclusionn+1 is an integer, so a multiple of 3
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Prove that the product of any two odd numbers is odd.
Write two odds2m+1 and 2n+1
Multiply them(2m+1)(2n+1)=4mn +2m +2n +1
Take out 2=2(2mn+m+n) +1
Conclusion2 × an integer + 1, so oddTwo letters, because the two odd numbers need not be related.
