Parallel Lines and Bearings

Pilots, sailors and surveyors navigate with bearings, and every bearings diagram is a parallel-lines diagram in disguise. Today: angle chains with parallel lines, and the North lines that make bearings work.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A transversal is a line crossing two or more other lines.
  • Where a transversal crosses parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal (the F shape).
  • Alternate angles are equal (the Z shape).
  • Co-interior angles sum to 180° (the C or U shape).
  • These relationships hold only when the lines are genuinely parallel, which is why diagrams mark them with arrows.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A back bearing is a half turn

A back bearing differs by 180°. Add 180 below 180°, and subtract 180 above it.

The bearing back from B to A differs from the bearing from A to B by 180°, a half turn. Add 180 to a bearing under 180°, and subtract 180 from one over.

North lines are parallel

North lines at different points are parallel. So parallel-line facts find bearings without measuring.

The North lines at any two points are parallel. So alternate and co-interior angle facts find bearings without a protractor.
