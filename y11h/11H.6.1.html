Polygons and Exterior Angles

Tiles, honeycombs and road signs are all polygons, and their angles decide whether they fit together. Today: the one fact that is true of every polygon, however irregular, and what it tells us about regular ones.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A polygon is a closed shape with straight sides, named by how many it has: pentagon 5, hexagon 6, heptagon 7, octagon 8, nonagon 9, decagon 10
  • A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal; an irregular one does not.
  • The exterior angles of any polygon sum to 360°, because walking once round the shape is one full turn.
  • In a regular polygon every exterior angle is the same, so each one is 360÷n.
  • The number of sides follows from a given exterior angle, as n=360 ÷ exterior angle.

Exterior angles make one full turn

The exterior angles of any polygon sum to 360°. Walking round the shape is one full turn.

Walking once round any polygon turns through one full turn, so its exterior angles always sum to 360°. Each exterior angle sits between one side and the extension of the side before it.

Regular means equal shares

A regular polygon's exterior angles are equal, each 360°÷n. So the sides number 360 ÷ the exterior angle.

In a regular polygon the n exterior angles are equal, so each one is 360°÷n. Turned round, the number of sides is 360 ÷ the exterior angle.

A whole number of sides

The number of sides must be a whole number. An exterior angle of 25° gives 14.4, so no polygon exists.

The number of sides must be a whole number, so 360 ÷ the exterior angle must divide exactly. An exterior angle of 25° gives 14.4 sides, so no such regular polygon exists.

Expressions still total 360°

Exterior angles written with x still sum to 360°. Their total set equal to 360 gives an equation.

Exterior angles written as expressions still sum to 360°. Setting their total equal to 360 gives an equation for x.
