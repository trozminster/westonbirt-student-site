Topic 11H.6 · Angles, Polygons and Parallel Lines · Lesson 3 of 4

Angle Facts and Angle Proofs

Every angle fact turns into an equation the moment an angle is written with a letter. Today: solving for those letters, and then proving an angle fact for every triangle at once.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Angles around a point sum to 360°.
  • Angles on a straight line sum to 180°.
  • Angles in a triangle sum to 180°.
  • Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • The base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
  • Every step in an angle calculation is justified by naming the fact used, not by the answer alone.
By the end you can
G4Form equations from angle contexts (e.g. angles labelled 2x and x+30 on a line).
G6Prove a geometric result using angle facts, giving a reason for every step, such as the exterior angle of a triangle equalling the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

Angle facts become equations

An angle fact gives an equation. So 2x and x+30 on a line give 3x+30=180

An angle fact is an equation waiting to be written. Angles labelled 2x and x+30 on a straight line give 2x+x+30=180

Opposite angles are equal

Opposite angles where two lines cross are equal. So 3x−20=2x+15 when they are opposite.

Where two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other are equal. So angles of 3x−20 and 2x+15 opposite each other give 3x−20=2x+15
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Angles of 3x+10 and 2x−5 lie together on a straight line. Find x.
Use the straight line3x+10+2x−5=180
Collect like terms5x+5=180
Subtract 5 from both sides5x=175
Divide by 5x=35
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Two straight lines cross. Vertically opposite angles are 4x−30 and 2x+50
Find the size of each angle.
Use vertically opposite4x−30=2x+50
Subtract 2x from both sides2x−30=50
Add 30 to both sides2x=80
Divide by 2x=40
Each angle2×40+50=130°

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A reason for every step

A geometric proof uses letters, so it covers every case. Each line states a fact and its reason.

A geometric proof uses letters for the angles, so it holds for every triangle at once. Each line states a fact and names the reason for it.

The exterior angle of a triangle

A triangle's exterior angle equals the two interior opposite angles added. It follows from two facts.

An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles. This follows from the triangle sum and the straight line together.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Triangle ABC has angles a, b and c. Side AC is extended to make exterior angle d next to c. Prove that d=a+b.
State the triangle suma+b+c=180°
State the straight linec+d=180°
Compare the twoa+b+c=c+d
Conclusion, as requiredd=a+b
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Two straight lines cross, making angles a, b and c in turn. Prove that a=c.
Use the first linea+b=180°
Use the second lineb+c=180°
Compare the twoa+b=b+c
Conclusion, as requireda=cThe proof works for any crossing, because no angle has a value.
YOUR TURN · c
Prove that the angles of a quadrilateral sum to 360°.
A diagonal splits it into two triangles; each triangle's angles sum to 180°; 2×180°=360°
YOUR TURN · d
Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB=AC and angle A=x. Prove that angle B=90°−x2
Angles B and C are equal, as base angles; x+2B=180°; B=180°−x2=90°−x2
