Adding Fractions with Number Denominators

Adding algebraic fractions is the step before solving equations with fractions in them. Today we give the fractions a common denominator and combine them into one.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Fractions can only be added or subtracted once they share a denominator, since only equal-sized parts combine.
  • A common denominator is a common multiple of the denominators, and the LCM gives the smallest.
  • Each numerator is multiplied by whatever its own denominator was multiplied by.
  • Only the numerators are then added; the denominator names the part size and stays as it is.
  • With an algebraic numerator the whole numerator is bracketed before subtracting, so that every term changes sign.
By the end you can
G5Add and subtract algebraic fractions with numerical denominators.
G6Write a sum or difference of three algebraic fractions, or of a fraction and a whole term, as a single fraction in its simplest form.

Same denominator, then add

Fractions add only once they share a denominator. Then only the numerators add.

Scale every numerator

Each numerator is multiplied by whatever its own denominator was. The whole numerator is scaled, so it goes in a bracket.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Write x + 23 + x - 14 as a single fraction.
Common denominator12
Scale each numerator4(x + 2) + 3(x - 1)12
Expand4x + 8 + 3x - 312
= 7x + 512

A whole term over 1

A whole term is a fraction over 1. Write it over the common denominator before adding.

Three fractions, one denominator

The LCM of all three denominators is the common denominator. Each numerator scales by its own factor.
