Topic 11H.4 · Conditional and Algebraic Probability · Lesson 6 of 6

Solving Algebraic Probability Problems

Sometimes the unknown is a probability itself, or counters are added to a bag before a draw. Two facts, that probabilities sum to 1 and that 'and' multiplies, turn every one of these into an equation.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The probabilities of all the outcomes of one event, or of one set of branches, sum to 1; when they are written with x, that sum is an equation.
  • Adding or removing items changes the total as well as the count of one colour, so a new probability is written over the new total.
  • Two items taken from two different bags are independent, so their probabilities multiply.
  • The value of x is usually a step, not the answer: it is substituted back into the expression the question asks about, and a value that makes a probability negative or greater than 1 is rejected.
By the end you can
G7Use the fact that probabilities sum to 1 to find an unknown probability written with x.
G8Solve multi-step algebraic probability problems, such as items added to a bag or a choice from two bags.
