Solving Non-Linear Simultaneous Equations

A straight line can cross a curve twice, touch it once, or miss it. Today we solve a linear and a quadratic equation together, and find where a line meets a circle.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When one equation is linear and the other is quadratic, the linear one is rearranged to make x or y the subject and that expression is substituted, in a bracket, into the quadratic one.
  • The result is a quadratic equation in one unknown: it is rearranged to = 0 and solved by factorising or by the formula.
  • It can have two, one or no real solutions, matching a line that crosses a curve twice, touches it once, or misses it.
  • Each value is substituted into the LINEAR equation to find its partner, and the solutions are given as pairs.
  • x² + y² = r² is a circle centred on the origin with radius r, so a line and this circle are solved the same way.
By the end you can
G9Solve a linear and a quadratic equation simultaneously by substitution, giving the solutions as pairs.
G9Find the points where a straight line meets the circle x² + y² = r².

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A line meets a curve

Substitute the linear expression into the quadratic. Each crossing of the two graphs is one solution pair.

Bracket, then square all of it

The substituted expression goes in a bracket. Squaring the bracket gives a middle term.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Solve y = x² - 3 and y = 2x.
Equatex² - 3 = 2x
Rearrange to zerox² - 2x - 3 = 0
Factorise(x - 3)(x + 1) = 0
Partners from y = 2x: x = 3, y = 6 or x = -1, y = -2
(3, 6) and (-1, -2)
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Solve y = x² + x - 4 and y = 2x + 2.
Equatex² + x - 4 = 2x + 2
Rearrange to zerox² - x - 6 = 0
Factorise(x - 3)(x + 2) = 0
(3, 8) and (-2, -2)
YOUR TURN · a
Solve y = x² + 1 and y = x + 3.
x² - x - 2 = 0, so (2, 5) and (-1, 2)
YOUR TURN · b
Solve y = 2x² - 3x and y = x + 6.
2x² - 4x - 6 = 0, so (3, 9) and (-1, 5)

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A line and a circle

A circle is solved with a line in the same way. Substitute the line, then solve the quadratic.

Divide through first

A common factor in the quadratic can be divided out. The smaller numbers are quicker to factorise.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find where y = x + 1 meets x² + y² = 25.
Substitutex² + (x + 1)² = 25
Expand2x² + 2x - 24 = 0
Divide by 2x² + x - 12 = 0
Solvex = 3
or x = -4
(3, 4) and (-4, -3)
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find where x + y = 5 meets x² + y² = 13.
Rearrangey = 5 - x
Substitutex² + (5 - x)² = 13
Simplifyx² - 5x + 6 = 0
(2, 3) and (3, 2)
YOUR TURN · c
Find where y = x - 1 meets x² + y² = 13.
x² - x - 6 = 0, so (3, 2) and (-2, -3)
YOUR TURN · d
Find where y = 2x meets x² + y² = 20.
5x² = 20, so (2, 4) and (-2, -4)
