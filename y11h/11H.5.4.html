Proofs with Squares of Integers

The hardest proofs on the paper involve squares, and they are worth the most marks. Today: expanding squares of integers in full, and choosing the form that shows a result is odd, even or a multiple.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Squaring an algebraic integer means expanding the whole bracket: (2n+1)2=4n2+4n+1, keeping the middle term.
  • To show a result is even, write it as 2 × (an integer); to show it is odd, write it as 2 × (an integer) + 1; to show it is a multiple of k, factorise k out of every term.
  • A difference of two squared brackets is subtracted inside a bracket, so every sign of the second square changes.
  • A proof must hold for every integer, so it uses letters throughout and ends with a sentence stating what has been proved.
By the end you can
G8Prove a statement involving the squares of consecutive integers or of odd and even numbers, by expanding and factorising.
G9Construct a complete algebraic proof from a worded statement, choosing the expressions to use and concluding in words.

Squaring keeps the middle term

Squaring an expression expands the whole bracket. An odd number squared is 4n2+4n+1

Squaring an algebraic integer means expanding the whole bracket. The square of an odd number is (2n+1)2=4n2+4n+1, middle term and all.

The form shows the property

Odd means 2 × an integer + 1
So 4n2+4n+1 is written as 2(2n2+2n)+1

An expression is odd only when it is written as 2 × (an integer) + 1
So 4n2+4n+1 becomes 2(2n2+2n)+1, which shows it.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Prove that the difference between the squares of two consecutive odd numbers is a multiple of 8
Write the squares(2n+3)2−(2n+1)2
Expand both brackets=4n2+12n+9−(4n2+4n+1)
Collect like terms=8n+8
Take out 8=8(n+1)
Conclusionn+1 is an integer, so a multiple of 8
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Prove that the sum of the squares of two consecutive integers is always odd.
Write the squaresn2+(n+1)2
Expand the bracket=n2+n2+2n+1
Collect like terms=2n2+2n+1
Take out 2=2(n2+n)+1
Conclusion2 × an integer + 1, so odd

The words choose the algebra

A worded statement becomes algebra before anything is proved. Each phrase, like 'consecutive' or 'odd', picks the expression.

A worded statement becomes algebra before anything is proved. Each phrase, like 'consecutive', 'odd' or 'square', picks the expression to write.

The last line says what is proved

A proof ends with a sentence linking the form to the statement. Without it, the argument is unfinished.

A proof ends with a sentence that links the final form to the statement. Without it, the algebra has not yet said anything.
