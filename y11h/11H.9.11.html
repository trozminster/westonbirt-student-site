Topic 11H.9 · Functions and Graph Transformations · Lesson 11 of 11

Combining Graph Transformations

Most exam graphs are a basic graph moved more than once, such as y =(x−2)2+3
Today we follow a point through two transformations, and read the transformations straight from an equation.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When two transformations act in turn, a point is followed through each one in order.
  • y =f(x+a)+b combines a horizontal and a vertical translation, so (p,q) maps to (p−a,q+b), whichever is done first.
  • y =(x−p)2+q is y=x2 translated right p and up q, so its turning point is (p,q).
  • When a reflection or a stretch is combined with a translation, order matters: in y=−f(x)+c the reflection is done first, then the translation.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The turning point is in the equation

In y =(x−p)2+q, y=x2 moves right p, up q. So the turning point is (p,q).

Each move has its own place

A sideways move changes the inside of the bracket. A vertical move changes the outside.
