Topic 11H.9 · Functions and Graph Transformations · Lesson 11 of 11

Combining Graph Transformations

Most exam graphs are a basic graph moved more than once, such as y = (x - 2)² + 3. Today we follow a point through two transformations, and read the transformations straight from an equation.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When two transformations act in turn, a point is followed through each one in order.
  • y = f(x + a) + b combines a horizontal and a vertical translation, so (p, q) maps to (p - a, q + b), whichever is done first.
  • y = (x - p)² + q is y = x² translated right p and up q, so its turning point is (p, q).
  • When a reflection or a stretch is combined with a translation, order matters: in y = -f(x) + c the reflection is done first, then the translation.
By the end you can
G9Find the image of a point, such as a turning point, under a combined transformation such as y = f(x - 2) + 3 or y = -f(x) + 1.

One move, then the next

Two transformations act on every point in turn. A key point, such as a turning point, is followed through each.

Reflect first, then move

In y = -f(x) + 1, the sign change comes first. Then every point moves up 1.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The turning point is in the equation

In y = (x - p)² + q, y = x² moves right p, up q. So the turning point is (p, q).

Each move has its own place

A sideways move changes the inside of the bracket. A vertical move changes the outside.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
