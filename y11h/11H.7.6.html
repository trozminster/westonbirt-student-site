Topic 11H.7 · Differentiation · Lesson 6 of 6

Displacement, Velocity and Acceleration

Engineers and physicists describe motion with a formula for position over time. Differentiating it once gives the velocity and twice gives the acceleration, at any instant they choose.

By the end you’ll know…
  • For displacement s as a function of time t, velocity is the rate of change of displacement, v=dsdt, and acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, a=dvdt.
  • The velocity at a time is found by substituting t into v, not into s.
  • A particle is momentarily at rest when v=0, so the times are found by solving v=0, rejecting any negative time.
  • The units follow the chain: s in m, v in m/s, a in m/s²

Each rate is a derivative

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement: v=dsdt. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity: a=dvdt.

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement, so v=dsdt. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, so a=dvdt.

Substitute after differentiating

Velocity at a time puts t into v, not s. The units go m, then m/s, then m/s²

The velocity at a time comes from substituting t into v, not into s. The units follow the chain: m, then m/s, then m/s²

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

At rest means zero velocity

At rest means the velocity is zero. So v=0 is solved for t, keeping sensible times.

A particle is momentarily at rest when its velocity is zero. So v=0 is solved for t, keeping only times that make sense.

Then use that time

The time found goes into s for the position. Put into a, it gives the acceleration there.

The time found is substituted into s for the position. The same time put into a gives the acceleration there.
