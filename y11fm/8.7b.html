Curve Sketching and Optimisation

Stationary points and intercepts are enough to sketch a curve. The same calculus finds the largest area or volume a fixed amount of material can give, which is where the course has been heading.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A curve can be sketched from its stationary points, intercepts and end behaviour.
  • An optimisation problem is solved by expressing the quantity as a function of one variable and finding its stationary point.

What a sketch needs

A sketch needs the stationary points and the axis intercepts. The leading term fixes how each end goes.

A cubic's ends

A positive x³ term means the curve rises to the right. A negative one means it falls to the right.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Sketch y = x³ - 3x².
Collect every feature before drawing a single line.
Stationary points3x² - 6x = 0, so (0, 0) a maximum and (2, -4) a minimum
Interceptsx²(x - 3) = 0
x = 0
x = 3
Endspositive x³, so it rises to the right
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Sketch y = 12x - x³.
The negative x³ term flips the shape.
Stationary points12 - 3x² = 0, so (2, 16) a maximum and (-2, -16) a minimum
Interceptsx(12 - x²) = 0
x = 0
x = ±3.46
Endsnegative x³, so it falls to the right

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One variable

The quantity is written in terms of one variable first. A constraint, such as a fixed total, removes the other.

Differentiate, then check

Setting dAdx = 0 gives the best x. The second derivative confirms it is a maximum.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A farmer has 40 m of fencing for three sides of a rectangle against a wall. Find the largest area.
Let x be the two sides at right angles to the wall and y the side parallel to it.
Constraint2x + y = 40
A = x(40 - 2x)
= 40x - 2x²
StationarydAdx = 40 - 4x
= 0
x = 10
Checkd²Adx² = -4 < 0, a maximum
Largest area400 - 200 = 200 m²
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Squares of side x cm are cut from the corners of a 12 cm by 12 cm sheet, and the sides are folded up into an open box. Find the greatest volume.
x = 6 gives a box with no base, so reject it.
VolumeV = x(12 - 2x)²
= 4x³ - 48x² + 144x
Stationary12x² - 96x + 144 = 0
x = 2
or x = 6
Checkd²Vdx² = 24x - 96 = -48 < 0 at x = 2
Greatest volume2 × 8 × 8 = 128 cm³
YOUR TURN · c
Two positive numbers add to 20. Find their largest possible product.
P = x(20 - x) = 20x - x². dPdx = 20 - 2x = 0, so x = 10, and d²Pdx² = -2 < 0. The largest product is 100.
YOUR TURN · d
Two numbers add to 12. Find the smallest possible value of the sum of their squares.
S = x² + (12 - x)², so dSdx = 4x - 24 = 0 and x = 6. d²Sdx² = 4 > 0, a minimum. The smallest value is 36 + 36 = 72.

In an optimisation problem, what must be done before differentiating?
Yes. One variable first, so that there is one derivative to set to zero.
Not quite. Use the constraint to remove one variable first.

Not quite. 9 cm is the side. The area is 9 × 9 = 81 cm².
