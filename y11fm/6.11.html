Trigonometric Identities

Two identities let any mix of sin, cos and tan be rewritten in a single function. That turns hard-looking trig equations into quadratics you already know how to solve.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Applying Pythagoras to the unit circle definition gives sin2x+cos2x =1, and the same definitions give tanx=sinx÷cosx.
  • Both hold for any angle, not only acute ones.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Pythagoras on the unit circle

The point (cosθ,sinθ) lies on a circle of radius 1
Pythagoras then gives sin2θ+cos2θ=1

Tan from sin and cos

On the unit circle, tanθ is y÷x. So tanθ=sinθcosθ, for any angle.

One function only

Each identity swaps one function for another. So cos²x becomes 1 − sin²x, and sinxcosx becomes tanx.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Show that cos²x − sin²x = 2cos²x - 1
Work on the left-hand side only, and keep the bracket when sin²x is replaced.
Use sin²x =1 − cos²x
cos²x - (1 − cos²x)
Remove the bracketcos²x - 1 + cos²x
= 2cos²x - 1

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Into one function

A mixed equation is turned into one function with an identity. It is then often a quadratic.

Keep every root

A root outside −1 to 1 gives no angle. Factorising keeps the roots that dividing by cosx would lose.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Solve 2cos²x − sinx−1=0 for 0°≤x≤360°.
Swap cos²x for 1 − sin²x, then it is a quadratic in sinx.
Use cos²x =1 − sin²x
2sin²x + sinx−1=0
Factorise(2sinx−1)(sinx+1) =0
sinx=0.5 gives 30° and 150°
sinx=−1 gives 270°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Solve 3sinx=4cosx for 0°≤x≤360°.
Dividing by cosx is safe hereif cosx=0
then sinx=0 too, which is impossible.
Divide by 3cosxtanx=43
Principal valuex=tan−1(43) ≈53.1°
Add 180°x=233.1°
YOUR TURN · c
Solve 2sin²x =1+cosx for 0°≤x≤360°.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Factorise
2(1 − cos²x) =1+cosx, so 2cos²x + cosx−1=0 and (2cosx−1)(cosx+1) =0
So x=60°, 180° or 300°.
YOUR TURN · d
Solve sin×cosx=0.5cosx for 0°≤x≤360°.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Dividing by cosx is safe here → Principal value → Add 180°
cosx(sinx−0.5) =0
cosx=0 gives 90° and 270°
sinx=0.5 gives 30° and 150°.
