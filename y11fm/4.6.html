Negative and Fractional Indices

Indices let you write roots, reciprocals and repeated multiplication in one notation, and the same laws then solve equations where the unknown is the power itself, which is where exponential growth in the sciences comes from.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The three index laws extend to zero, negative and fractional indices: a0=1, a−m is the reciprocal of am, and a1/n is the nth root of a.

Negative and fractional powers

A negative power means a reciprocal. A power of one half means a square root. Neither makes the number negative or halved.

A negative power means a reciprocal, and a power of one half means a square root. Neither makes the number itself negative or halved.

Root first

In 82/3, the denominator is the root and the numerator is the power. Taking the root first keeps the numbers small.

In a power such as 82/3 the denominator is the root and the numerator is the power. Taking the root first keeps the numbers small.

Fractions with negative powers

A negative power on a fraction flips the fraction. The positive power then applies to the flipped fraction.

A negative power on a fraction flips the fraction, and the positive power is then applied to the flipped fraction.

Same base

With the unknown in the power, both sides are written with the same base. Then the indices are compared.

To solve an equation with the unknown in the power, write both sides with the same base and then compare the indices. For 4ˣ=32 both sides become powers of 2

Unknown base

An unknown base is freed by raising both sides to the reciprocal power. For x3/2=8, that power is 23

To solve x3/2=8, undo the power by raising both sides to its reciprocal, which is 23
