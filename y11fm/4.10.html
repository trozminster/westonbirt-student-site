Simultaneous Equations in Three Unknowns

Problems with three unknowns, from mixing three ingredients to finding the constants of a quadratic, are solved by one idea used twice: eliminate the same unknown from two different pairs to get back to a pair you can already solve.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Three unknowns require three independent equations.
  • Eliminating one variable from two different pairs reduces the problem to a pair of simultaneous equations in two unknowns.

The plan

One variable is eliminated twice, from two different pairs. That leaves two equations in the other two unknowns. Solving those, then substituting back, finds the third.

Pick one variable and eliminate it twice, from two different pairs of equations, to get two equations in the other two unknowns. Solve that pair, and then substitute back to find the third.

An example

Eliminating z twice gives a pair in x and y. That pair gives y=2 and x=1, then z=3

Eliminating z from equations 1 and 3 and from equations 1 and 2 gives a pair in x and y. That pair gives y=2 and x=1, and then z=3

Align first

Every equation is aligned first: the same variable order, the number on the right. Terms in different orders lead to slips.

Before eliminating, rearrange every equation so the variables are in the same order on the left and the number is on the right. Terms in different orders lead to slips.

Finding a quadratic nth term
