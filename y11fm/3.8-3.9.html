Inverse and Exponential Functions

An inverse function runs a calculation backwards, and the exponential graph is the first curve that is not a polynomial, so this lesson gives you both a way to undo a function and a model for growth and decay.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An inverse function undoes the original, so it exists only where the function is one-to-one over its domain.
  • An exponential graph y = aˣ passes through (0,1), and replacing x by -x reflects it in the y-axis.
By the end you can
G7Find an inverse function by reversing the operations, or algebraically by interchanging x and y and rearranging.
G8Recognise and sketch exponential graphs, and find the constants a and b in y = ab⁻ˣ from two given points.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Undoing a function

An inverse function undoes the original. It applies the opposite operations in reverse order.

An inverse function undoes the original, so it applies the opposite operations in the reverse order. The function f(x) = 2x + 3 multiplies by 2 and then adds 3, so its inverse subtracts 3 and then divides by 2.

Swap and rearrange

The algebraic method swaps x and y in y = f(x). Then y is made the subject again.

The algebraic way to find an inverse is to write y = f(x), swap x and y, and then make y the subject again. This works for functions that are hard to undo in your head.

One-to-one

Only a one-to-one function has an inverse. On all numbers, x² fails: 2 and -2 both give 4. Restricting it to x ≥ 0 repairs it.

A function has an inverse only if each output comes from just one input, which is what one-to-one means. The function x² over all numbers fails because 2 and -2 both give 4, but restricting it to x at least 0 repairs it.

f multiplies by 3, then subtracts 5

The inverse is f⁻¹(x) = x + 53

Find the inverse of f(x) = x + 24 by swapping x and y.Writing y for f(x) and swapping gives an equation to rearrange for y.

Outputs of aˣ

Every graph y = aˣ passes through (0, 1). Its outputs are always positive. The table shows y = 2ˣ.

Every exponential graph y = aˣ passes through (0, 1), because any number to the power 0 is 1. The outputs are always positive and grow quickly to the right of zero.

Reflecting in the y-axis

Replacing x by -x reflects the graph in the y-axis. So y = 2⁻ˣ falls where y = 2ˣ rises.

Replacing x by -x reflects the graph in the y-axis, so y = 2⁻ˣ falls where y = 2ˣ rises. Both pass through (0, 1).
WORKED EXAMPLE C
The graph of y = a × b⁻ˣ passes through (0, 8) and (3, 1). Find a and b.The point with x = 0 gives a, and the second point then gives b.
Use (0, 8)a × b⁰ = 8
a = 8
Use (3, 1)8 × b⁻³ = 1
b⁻³ = 18
Then b³ = 8, so b = 2 and y = 8 × 2⁻ˣ
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Compare the graphs of y = 2ˣ and y = 2⁻ˣ.Both are exponentials, and replacing x by -x is a reflection.
Both pass through (0, 1), since 2⁰ = 1
y = 2ˣ rises to the right and stays above the x-axis
y = 2⁻ˣ is the reflection in the y-axis, so it falls to the right
YOUR TURN · c
The graph of y = a × 2⁻ˣ passes through (0, 6). Find a, and find y when x = 1.
a = 6 because 2⁰ = 1. At x = 1, y = 6 × 2⁻¹ = 6 × 12 = 3.
YOUR TURN · d
The graph of y = a × b⁻ˣ passes through (0, 5) and (1, 10). Find a and b.
a = 5 from x = 0. Then 5 × b⁻¹ = 10, so b⁻¹ = 2 and b = 12.
