Tangents to a Curve

A tangent touches a curve and runs in its direction at one point. Differentiation gives its gradient, and the straight-line work from Chapter 5 gives its equation.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The derivative evaluated at a point gives the gradient of the tangent there, so the tangent's equation follows from y−y1=m(x−x1).

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The gradient at a point

The derivative is a formula for the gradient at any x. Substituting one x-value gives the gradient there.

A given gradient

To find where the gradient is k, dy/dx is set equal to k. The curve's equation then gives each y-coordinate.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the gradient of y=x3−4x+1 at the point (2,1).
Differentiate first, then substitute the x-value into dy/dx, not into y.
Differentiatedy/dx =3x2−4
Substitute x=2: dy/dx =12−4
Gradient8
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Find the point on y=x2−5x where the gradient is 3
Set dy/dx equal to 3, not y.
Differentiatedydx=2x−5
Solve 2x−5=3: x=4
Substitute into yy=16−20
=−4, the point (4,−4)

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The tangent's gradient

The tangent at a point has the curve's gradient there. So m comes from dy/dx at that x.

Three steps

The point gives x1 and y1
The derivative at x1 gives m. Then y−y1=m(x−x1) gives the tangent.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the equation of the tangent to y=x2−3x+4 at the point (2,2).
The point gives x1 and y1
The derivative at x=2 gives m.
Gradientdydx=2x−3=1 at x=2
Liney−2=1(x−2)
Tangenty=x
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the equation of the tangent to y=2x3−5 at x=−1
Only x is given, so find y1 from the curve first.
Pointy=−2−5
=−7, so (−1,−7)
Gradientdydx=6x2=6 at x=−1
Liney+7=6(x+1)
y=6x−1
YOUR TURN · c

dy/dx =3x2−2=10 at x=2
y−4=10(x−2), so y=10x−16

y−2=−1(x−2), so y=−x+4
