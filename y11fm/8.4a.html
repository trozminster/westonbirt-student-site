Tangents to a Curve

A tangent touches a curve and runs in its direction at one point. Differentiation gives its gradient, and the straight-line work from Chapter 5 gives its equation.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The derivative evaluated at a point gives the gradient of the tangent there, so the tangent's equation follows from y - y1 = m(x - x1).

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The gradient at a point

The derivative is a formula for the gradient at any x. Substituting one x-value gives the gradient there.

A given gradient

To find where the gradient is k, dydx is set equal to k. The curve's equation then gives each y-coordinate.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the gradient of y = x³ - 4x + 1 at the point (2, 1).Differentiate first, then substitute the x-value into dydx, not into y.
Differentiatedydx = 3x² - 4
Substitute x = 2: dydx = 12 - 4

Solve 2x - 5 = 3: x = 4

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The tangent's gradient

The tangent at a point has the curve's gradient there. So m comes from dydx at that x.

Three steps

The point gives x1 and y1. The derivative at x1 gives m. Then y - y1 = m(x - x1) gives the tangent.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the equation of the tangent to y = x² - 3x + 4 at the point (2, 2).The point gives x1 and y1. The derivative at x = 2 gives m.
Gradientdydx = 2x - 3 = 1 at x = 2
Liney - 2 = 1(x - 2)
Tangenty = x
WORKED EXAMPLE D

YOUR TURN · c
Find the equation of the tangent to y = x³ - 2x at the point (2, 4).
dydx = 3x² - 2 = 10 at x = 2. y - 4 = 10(x - 2), so y = 10x - 16.
YOUR TURN · d
Find the equation of the tangent to y = 4x at x = 2.
The point is (2, 2). dydx = -4x⁻² = -1 at x = 2. y - 2 = -1(x - 2), so y = -x + 4.
