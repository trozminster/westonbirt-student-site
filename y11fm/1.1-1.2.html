Number and Algebra Review

Nothing in this lesson is new, which is exactly why it is dangerous: multipliers, ratio and algebraic fractions are assumed without comment from chapter 2 onwards, so a gap you leave open today is one you meet again in the middle of something harder.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A percentage change applies as a decimal multiplier: increasing by r% multiplies by (1+r100) and decreasing by r% multiplies by (1−r100).
  • Simplifying a ratio across different units requires converting to a common unit first.
  • Expanding a bracket with a negative sign outside it means every term inside the bracket changes sign.
  • An algebraic fraction simplifies by cancelling common factors of the whole numerator and denominator, never a term within a sum.
By the end you can
G5Simplify ratios across different units, find percentages using multipliers, and carry out mixed fraction arithmetic.
G7Collect like terms, expand and simplify brackets, factorise by common factor, and simplify, multiply and divide algebraic fractions.

Multipliers

A percentage change is one multiplier. An increase of 8% multiplies by 1.08, and a decrease of 8% by 0.92

Ratios across units

A ratio compares like with like. So both parts are put in the same unit before simplifying.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
A price of £240 rises by 15%, then falls by 15%. Find the final price.
The second change acts on the new price, so multiply twice.
Multipliers1.15, then 0.85
240×1.15×0.85=234.6
Final price£234.60
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Work out 213÷134−12 without a calculator.

Subtract86−36=56

A negative outside

A negative outside a bracket multiplies every term inside. So −2(x−3) gives −2x+6

Cancel factors, not terms

An algebraic fraction cancels only factors of the whole top and bottom. So the top is factorised first.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Expand and simplify 4(2x−3)−3(x−5).
The −3 multiplies both terms in its bracket.
Expand8x−12−3x+15
Collect5x+3
WORKED EXAMPLE D
