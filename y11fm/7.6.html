Lines and Planes in Three Dimensions

In three dimensions two lines can miss each other without being parallel, and the angle a line makes with a plane has to be built before it can be measured. Both ideas turn a solid back into triangles.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two lines in three dimensions meet, are parallel, or are skew.
  • The angle between a line and a plane is measured to the line's projection onto that plane.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Three ways

Two lines in 3D meet, are parallel, or are skew. Skew lines never meet and are not parallel.

A line and a plane

A line lies in a plane, meets it once, or is parallel to it. Extending a line shows whether it meets the plane.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Cuboid ABCDEFGH has base ABCD and E above A. Are AB and CG parallel, meeting or skew?Ask the two questions in order: do they meet, and are they parallel?
Do they meetno, AB is in the base and CG rises from C, off the line AB
Are they parallelno, AB is horizontal and CG vertical
So AB and CG are skew
WORKED EXAMPLE B
In the same cuboid, how does the line EG relate to the plane ABCD?The top face and the base never meet.
Where EG liesin the top face EFGH
The top face is parallel to the base
So EG is parallel to the plane ABCD
YOUR TURN · a

Parallel: both are vertical edges.
YOUR TURN · b
In the same cuboid, how does the line AG relate to the plane ABCD?
It meets the plane at the point A only.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The projection

The angle between a line and a plane is measured to its projection. The projection is the line's shadow, straight down onto the plane.

The right-angled triangle

The line, its projection and the perpendicular make a right-angled triangle. The angle then comes from trigonometry.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Cuboid ABCDEFGH has base ABCD and E above A. AB = 8 cm, BC = 6 cm and AE = 4 cm. Find the angle between EC and the plane ABCD.E is above A, so the shadow of EC on the base is AC.
Projection of EC onto ABCDAC
AC = √(8² + 6²) = 10 cm
tan ECA = 410, so angle ECA = 21.8°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A square-based pyramid VABCD has base side 6 cm and VA = 9 cm, with V above the centre M. Find the angle between VA and the base.V is above M, so the shadow of VA is AM. Here the line itself is known, so use cos.
Projection of VA onto the baseAM
AM = half of AC = 3√2 = 4.24 cm
cos VAM = 4.249, so angle VAM = 61.9°
YOUR TURN · c
Cuboid ABCDEFGH has base ABCD and E above A. AB = 5 cm, BC = 12 cm and AE = 8 cm. Find the angle between CE and the base ABCD.
The projection is AC = 13 cm. tan ECA = 813, so the angle is 31.6°.
YOUR TURN · d
A rod 13 cm long rests with one end on a table and the other end 5 cm above it. Find the angle between the rod and the table.
The rod is the hypotenuse and the height is opposite. sin θ = 513, so θ = 22.6°.
