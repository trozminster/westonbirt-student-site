Quadratic Equations

Most equations in the rest of this course turn out to be quadratics in disguise, so being able to solve one three different ways, and to choose the quickest, is the skill that Chapter 4 and everything after it relies on.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quadratic has two roots, which may be equal.
  • It can be solved by factorising, by completing the square, or using the quadratic formula x=(−b +/- b2−4ac)÷(2a), which is itself derived by completing the square on the general form ax2+bx+c =0
By the end you can
G8Solve a quadratic by rearranging to zero and factorising, and by completing the square including with a non-unit leading coefficient.
G8Solve a quadratic using the formula, giving answers in exact surd form or rounded, and form and solve quadratics from context.

Make one side zero

A product is zero only when one factor is zero. So one side must be zero before factorising.

A product is zero only when one factor is zero, so the equation must be rearranged to have zero on one side before it is factorised. An equation such as x(x+3)=10 tells you nothing about x until 10 has been moved across.

Completing the square with a coefficient

A coefficient on x2 is taken out of the x terms first. The number subtracted is then multiplied by that factor too.

When the x2 term has a coefficient other than 1, take that factor out of the x terms first, and then complete the square inside the bracket. The number you subtract is multiplied by the factor as well.

The quadratic formula

The quadratic formula is x =−b±b2−4ac2a. It solves ax2+bx+c =0
Write down a, b and c with their signs first.

The formula x =−b±b2−4ac2a solves any quadratic ax2+bx+c =0, and it comes from completing the square on the general form. Write down a, b and c with their signs before substituting.

From context

Each root of a context quadratic is checked against the situation. A negative length is rejected.

A quadratic from a word problem is set up, rearranged to zero and solved, and then each root is checked against the situation. A length cannot be negative, so that root is rejected.
