Circles, Lines and Tangents

A line meets a circle in two points, one point or none, and substitution plus the discriminant decides which; a tangent is the special case that touches once, at right angles to the radius.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A tangent meets the radius at the point of contact at a right angle, so the tangent's gradient is the negative reciprocal of the radius gradient at that point.
  • Substituting a line's equation into a circle's gives a quadratic whose number of real roots tells you the number of intersection points: two roots means the line is a secant, one repeated root means it is a tangent, and no real roots means the line misses the circle.

Counting intersections

Substitute the line into the circle to get a quadratic in x. Its discriminant tells you how many points there are: two, one (a tangent) or none.

An example

Each root for x gives its own point. Here x=−4 and x=3 give (−4,−3) and (3,4).

For x2+y2=25 and y=x+1, substitute to get x2+x−12=0
The roots are x=−4 and x=3, and the matching y values give the points (−4,−3) and (3,4).
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find where y=x+2 meets x2+y2=10
Substitute y, expand carefully and solve.
x2+(x+2)2 =10 gives 2x2+4x−6 =0, so x2+2x−3=0
(x+3)(x−1) =0, so x=−3 or x=1
y=x+2 gives (−3,−1) and (1,3)
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Show that y=x−4 is a tangent to x2+y2=8, and find the point.
A repeated root means the line touches the circle once.
x2+(x−4)2 =8 gives 2x2−8x+8 =0, so x2−4x+4=0
(x−2)2=0 has one repeated root, so the line is a tangent
x=2 and y=−2, so the point of contact is (2,−2)
YOUR TURN · a
Find where y=x+1 meets x2+y2=13
x2+(x+1)2 =13 gives 2x2+2x−12 =0, so x2+x−6=0 and (x+3)(x−2) =0
x=−3 or 2, so the points are (−3,−2) and (2,3).
YOUR TURN · b
How many times does y=x+5 meet x2+y2=9?
x2+(x+5)2 =9 gives 2x2+10x+16 =0, so x2+5x+8=0
The discriminant is 25−32=−7, which is negative, so the line does not meet the circle.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The tangent and the radius

A tangent meets the radius at the point of contact at a right angle. So the tangent gradient is the negative reciprocal of the gradient of the radius.

Three steps

Find the gradient of the radius from the centre to the point. Flip it and change the sign, and then write the line through the point.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the tangent to x2+y2=25 at P(3,4).
The centre is the origin, so the radius goes from (0,0) to P.
Gradient of the radius =43, so the tangent gradient is −34
y−4 =−34(x−3), so 4y−16 =−3x+9
3x+4y=25
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the tangent to (x−2)2 +(y+1)2 =20 at P(4,3).
The centre is (2,−1), so the radius goes from there to P.
Gradient of the radius =3+14−2 =2, so the tangent gradient is −12
y−3 =−12(x−4), so 2y−6=−x+4
x+2y=10
YOUR TURN · c
Find the tangent to x2+y2=13 at (2,3).
The radius gradient is 32, so the tangent gradient is −23
y−3 =−23(x−2), so 3y−9 =−2x+4 and 2x+3y=13
YOUR TURN · d
Find the tangent to (x+1)2 +(y−2)2 =25 at (3,5).
The centre is (−1,2), so the radius gradient is 34 and the tangent gradient is −43
y−5 =−43(x−3), so 3y−15 =−4x+12 and 4x+3y=27
