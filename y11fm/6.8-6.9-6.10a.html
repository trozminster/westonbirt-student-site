Trigonometric Graphs and Equations

Every sine, cosine or tangent equation has infinitely many answers. The graphs show where they all are, and why a calculator only ever gives one.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Sine and cosine graphs have period 360°, amplitude 1 (oscillating between −1 and 1), with roots where the function is zero; the tangent graph has period 180° with vertical asymptotes wherever cos is zero.
  • A trigonometric equation therefore has infinitely many solutions, of which a calculator's inverse function gives only the principal value; the graph's own symmetry is what locates the rest.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Sine and cosine waves

Sine and cosine repeat every 360°, between −1 and 1
Cosine is the sine wave shifted 90° to the left.

The tangent graph

Tan repeats every 180°, not every 360°. It has a vertical asymptote wherever cos is zero.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Sketch y=sinx for 0°≤x≤360°.
Plot the five key points first, then join them in a smooth wave, never with straight lines.
Key points(0,0), (90,1), (180,0), (270,−1), (360,0)
Rootsx=0°, 180°, 360°
Maximum 1 at x=90°, minimum −1 at x=270°
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Sketch y=tanx for 0°≤x≤360°.
Draw the asymptotes as dashed lines first
each branch climbs towards the next one.
Asymptotes where cosx=0: x=90° and x=270°
Roots where sinx=0: x=0°, 180°, 360°
Each branch rises through its root towards the asymptote
YOUR TURN · a
Sketch y=cosx for 0°≤x≤360°. Mark where it crosses the axes.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Key points → Roots
It crosses the y-axis at (0,1). Its roots are x=90° and x=270°, and its minimum is −1 at x=180°.
YOUR TURN · b
State the period of y=tanx and the equations of its asymptotes for −180°≤x≤180°.
The period is 180°. The asymptotes are x=−90° and x=90°.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Many solutions

A repeating graph meets a horizontal line infinitely often. A calculator gives just one solution: the principal value.

Symmetry finds the rest

The graph's symmetry places the other solutions. Sine's peak at 90° mirrors 30° onto 150°.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Solve sinx=0.4 for 0°≤x≤360°.
Sketch the sine curve and the line y=0.4 first: it cuts the curve twice.
Principal valuex=sin−1(0.4)≈23.6°
Symmetry about 90°x=180−23.6
=156.4°
x=23.6° or x=156.4°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Solve cosx=0.3 for 0°≤x≤360°.
The cosine curve is symmetrical about 180°, not 90°.
Principal valuex=cos−1(0.3)≈72.5°
Symmetry about 180°x=360−72.5
=287.5°
x=72.5° or x=287.5°
YOUR TURN · c
Solve tanx=2 for 0°≤x≤360°.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Principal value → Symmetry about 90°
The principal value is tan−1(2) ≈63.4°. Tan repeats every 180°, so x=63.4° or x=243.4°.
YOUR TURN · d
Solve sinx=−0.5 for 0°≤x≤360°.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Principal value → Symmetry about 180°
The principal value is −30°, outside the range. The curve is below the axis from 180° to 360°, so x=180+30=210° or x=360−30=330°.
