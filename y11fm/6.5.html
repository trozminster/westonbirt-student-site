Geometric Proof

A proof is an argument that works for every case, so it uses letters instead of numbers and a stated reason for every line.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A geometric proof states a reason for every step in unambiguous language, and uses a letter for an unknown angle so the argument holds generally rather than for one case.

Learn

Proving the angle in a semicircle

Join the centre O to P. OA, OP and OB are all radii, so triangles OAP and OBP are isosceles. Use letters for the base angles.

Writing it down

Each line is a statement with a reason. The angle sum gives x+y+x+y=180
So angle APB=x+y=90

Each line is a statement with a reason beside it. The angle sum of triangle ABP gives x+y+(x+y) =180, so x+y=90 and angle APB=x+y=90
WORKED EXAMPLE A
OA=OP are radii and angle OAP=x. Write the next line of a proof with a reason.
The reason must name the fact, not just describe the picture.
OA=OP, since both are radii of the same circle
So triangle OAP is isosceles
Angle OPA=x (base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal)
WORKED EXAMPLE B
AB is a diameter and P is on the circle. Angle OAP=x, angle OBP=y. Show angle APB=x+y.
Use the radius OP to split the angle at P.
OP=OA=OB (radii), so OPA =x and OPB =y (base angles, isosceles)
Angle APB = angle OPA + angle OPB
Angle APB=x+y
YOUR TURN · a
OA=OB are radii and angle OAB = a. Write the next line with a reason.
OA=OB are radii, so triangle OAB is isosceles. Angle OBA = a, because the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
YOUR TURN · b
In triangle ABP, angle PAB=x, angle PBA=y and angle APB=x+y. Prove x+y=90
The angles in a triangle add to 180, so x+y+(x+y) =180
So 2(x+y)=180 and x+y=90

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Using a letter

The proof names the angle at A as x and at C as y. Their centre angles, 2x and reflex 2y, make a full turn.

In a proof, let one angle be x. The centre theorem then makes the angle at the centre 2x, and the other angle at the circumference y gives a reflex centre angle of 2y. The two centre angles make a full turn.

The proof

The centre theorem gives centre angles 2x and 2y. Angles at a point give 2x+2y=360
So x+y=180

The angle at the centre is 2x, and the reflex angle at the centre is 2y, because the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. Angles at a point add to 360, so 2x+2y=360, and x+y=180
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Prove that opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD add to 180 degrees.
Use letters, the centre theorem and angles at a point.
Let angle A=x. The angle at the centre on the same arc BD is 2x (centre theorem)
Let angle C=y. The reflex angle at the centre is 2y (centre theorem)
2x+2y=360 (angles at a point), so x+y=180
WORKED EXAMPLE D
TP is a tangent at P and angle OPQ=a. Prove that angle TPQ=90−a.
The tangent-radius fact is a one-line route.
Angle OPT=90 degrees (tangent meets the radius at the point of contact)
Angle TPQ = angle OPT - angle OPQ
Angle TPQ=90−a
YOUR TURN · c
Prove that the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment when the chord is a diameter.
The chord PQ is a diameter, so angle PRQ=90 degrees (angle in a semicircle). The tangent at P meets the diameter PQ at 90 degrees. So both angles are 90 degrees, and they are equal.
YOUR TURN · d
ABCD is cyclic with angle A=2x+10 and angle C=3x−5
Find x, giving a reason.
Opposite angles add to 180, so (2x+10) +(3x−5) =180, 5x+5=180 and x=35
