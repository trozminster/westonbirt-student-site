Dividing a Line in a Given Ratio

A point that divides a line in a ratio sits a fixed fraction of the way along it, and that fraction applies to the horizontal and vertical steps separately.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A point dividing AB in the ratio p:q sits pp+q of the way along, which similar triangles show applies to each coordinate separately.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The fraction along

A ratio p:q splits AB into p+q equal parts. So P is pp+q of the way from A to B.

If P divides AB in the ratio p:q then AB is split into p+q equal parts and AP is p of them. So P is pp+q of the way from A to B.

An example

The point P adds a fraction of each step to the coordinates of A. For 2:3 the fraction is 25

Find the steps from A to B, take the fraction of each step, and add to the coordinates of A. For A(1,2), B(11,7) and AP:PB = 2:3 the point is P(5,4).
WORKED EXAMPLE A
A(2,−1), B(12,4). Find P on AB with AP:PB = 3:2
Turn the ratio into a fraction, then use the steps from A.
3+2=5, so P is 35 of the way. The steps are 10 and 5
35 of 10 is 6 and 35 of 5 is 3
P =(2+6,−1+3) =(8,2)
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A(−3,5), B(9,−3). Find P on AB with AP:PB = 1:3
The y step is negative, so the fraction of it is negative too.
1+3=4, so P is 14 of the way. The steps are 12 and −8
14 of 12 is 3 and 14 of −8 is −2
P =(−3+3,5−2) =(0,3)
YOUR TURN · a
A(0,3), B(10,18). Find P on AB with AP:PB = 2:3
2+3=5, so P is 25 of the way. The steps are 10 and 15, and 25 of them are 4 and 6
P =(0+4,3+6) =(4,9).
YOUR TURN · b
A(−2,6), B(10,0). Find P on AB with AP:PB = 5:1
5+1=6, so P is 56 of the way. The steps are 12 and −6, and 56 of them are 10 and −5
P =(−2+10,6−5) =(8,1).

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Working backwards

The step from A to P is a fraction of the whole step. Dividing by that fraction gives the whole step from A to B.

When P and the ratio are known, the step from A to P is a fraction of the whole step from A to B. Divide by the fraction to get the whole step, and then add it to A.

Counting the parts

The fraction always uses the total number of parts, p+q. A ratio 2:3 has 5 parts in all and a ratio 3:1 has 4

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A(1,3), P(7,6), AP:PB = 3:1
Find B.
The step A to P is 34 of the step A to B.
Step A to P is (6,3), and this is 34 of AB
So AB is (6×43,3×43) =(8,4)
B =(1+8,3+4) =(9,7)
WORKED EXAMPLE D
B(14,1), P(8,4), AP:PB = 2:1
Find A.
The step P to B is 13 of the step A to B.
Step P to B is (6,−3), and this is 13 of AB
So AB is (18,−9)
A =B−(18,−9) =(−4,10)
YOUR TURN · c
A(2,5), P(6,9), AP:PB = 2:3
Find B.
Step A to P is (4,4), which is 25 of AB. So AB is (10,10), and B =(2+10,5+10) =(12,15).
YOUR TURN · d
B(10,−4), P(4,2), AP:PB = 1:2
Find A.
Step P to B is (6,−6), which is 23 of AB. So AB is (9,−9), and A =(10−9,−4+9) =(1,5).
