Introducing Differentiation

Differentiation finds the exact gradient of a curve at any point, without drawing a tangent. Every later idea in this chapter, from tangents to optimisation, is built on it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The gradient of a curve changes from point to point.
  • Taking a chord PQ and letting Q approach P gives the gradient at a point, and this limiting process yields the general rule that y = xⁿ differentiates to n xⁿ⁻¹.
By the end you can
GFMDifferentiate y = k xⁿ for positive, negative and zero integer powers, and differentiate sums and differences term by term.
GFMRewrite expressions with fractional or negative indices into differentiable form before differentiating.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A changing gradient

A curve's gradient changes from point to point. A chord PQ gives an average gradient between two points.

Q slides to P

As Q slides towards P, the chord's gradient settles. On y = x² at P(1, 1), it settles at 2.

The rule

Differentiating y = xⁿ gives dydx = nxⁿ⁻¹. The power comes down as a multiplier, then drops by 1.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the gradient of y = x² at the point (3, 9) using a chord.
Take Q at x = 3 + h, then let h shrink to 0.
Chord to (3 + h, (3 + h)^2)gradient = (3 + h)² - 9h
Expand= 6h + h²h
= 6 + h
As h → 0, the gradient → 6
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Differentiate y = 4x³ - 5x² + 2x - 7.
One term at a timemultiply by the power, then reduce it by 1.
4x³ gives 12x², and -5x² gives -10x
2x gives 2, and the constant -7 gives 0
dydx = 12x² - 10x + 2
YOUR TURN · a
Differentiate y = 6x⁴ + 3x - 9.
dydx = 24x³ + 3.
YOUR TURN · b
Find the gradient of y = x³ - 2x at x = 2.
dydx = 3x² - 2, so at x = 2 the gradient is 3 × 4 - 2 = 10.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Rewrite first

Every term must be kxⁿ before the rule applies. So 3x² becomes 3x⁻², and √x becomes x⁽¹²⁾.

Expand or split

Brackets are expanded first. A fraction over a single term is split into separate terms.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Differentiate y = 3x².
Write it as a power of x first. The power goes down from -2 to -3.
Rewritey = 3x⁻²
Multiply by -2 and reduce the powerdydx = -6x⁻³
dydx = -6x³
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Differentiate y = x³ + 2x.
Divide each term on top by x.
Splity = x² + 2x⁻¹
Differentiatedydx = 2x - 2x⁻²
dydx = 2x - 2x²
YOUR TURN · c
Differentiate y = (2x - 1)².
Expand: y = 4x² - 4x + 1, so dydx = 8x - 4.
YOUR TURN · d
Differentiate y = 4√x + 1x.
y = 4x⁽¹²⁾ + x⁻¹, so dydx = 2x⁽⁻¹²⁾ - x⁻² = 2√x - 1x².
