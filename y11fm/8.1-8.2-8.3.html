Introducing Differentiation

Differentiation finds the exact gradient of a curve at any point, without drawing a tangent. Every later idea in this chapter, from tangents to optimisation, is built on it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The gradient of a curve changes from point to point.
  • Taking a chord PQ and letting Q approach P gives the gradient at a point, and this limiting process yields the general rule that y=xn differentiates to n xn−1
By the end you can
GFMDifferentiate y=kxn for positive, negative and zero integer powers, and differentiate sums and differences term by term.
GFMRewrite expressions with fractional or negative indices into differentiable form before differentiating.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A changing gradient

A curve's gradient changes from point to point. A chord PQ gives an average gradient between two points.

Q slides to P

As Q slides towards P, the chord's gradient settles. On y=x2 at P(1,1), it settles at 2

The rule

Differentiating y=xn gives dydx =nxn−1
The power comes down as a multiplier, then drops by 1

WORKED EXAMPLE A

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Rewrite first

Every term must be kxn before the rule applies. So 3x2 becomes 3x−2, and x becomes x1/2.

Expand or split

Brackets are expanded first. A fraction over a single term is split into separate terms.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Differentiate y=3x2
Write it as a power of x first. The power goes down from −2 to −3
Rewritey=3x−2
Multiply by −2 and reduce the powerdydx=−6x−3
dydx=−6x3
WORKED EXAMPLE D
