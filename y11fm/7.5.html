Problems in Three Dimensions

A 3D problem is a 2D problem in disguise. Pick the right triangle out of the solid, draw it flat, and the trigonometry you already know does the rest.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A 3D problem is solved by extracting the true shape of a relevant 2D cross-section, following the drawing convention that verticals are vertical, east-west lines horizontal and hidden edges dotted.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Drawing a solid

Vertical edges are drawn vertical. Hidden edges are drawn dotted.

The true shape

A 3D drawing distorts angles. A separate sketch of one triangle shows its true shape.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Cuboid ABCDEFGH has base ABCD and E above A. AB=6 cm, BC=8 cm and CG=5 cm. Sketch the true shape of triangle ACG.
In the 3D drawing angle ACG looks obtuse. It is a right angle.
Where the sides lieAC is in the base and CG is vertical
Right angleangle ACG=90°
SketchAC across, CG straight up from C
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A square-based pyramid has base ABCD of side 10 cm and its apex V above the centre M, with VM=12 cm. Sketch the true shape of triangle VMA.
M is the centre of the base, so AM is half of a diagonal, not half of a side.
Where the sides lieVM is vertical and MA is in the base
Right angleangle VMA=90°
LengthAM = half of AC=52≈7.07 cm

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A diagonal first

A space diagonal needs a face diagonal first. Two uses of Pythagoras give d2 =a2+b2+c2

An angle in 3D

An angle in 3D is found inside one right-angled triangle. That triangle needs two known sides first.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Cuboid ABCDEFGH has AB=6 cm, BC=8 cm and CG=5 cm. Find AG.
Two trianglesABC in the base, then ACG standing up.
Base diagonalAC=62+82
=10 cm
Triangle ACG, right-angled at CAG²=102+52
=125
AG=125≈11.2 cm (3 s.f.)
WORKED EXAMPLE D
In the same cuboid, find the angle between AG and the base ABCD.
The angle sits at A, between AG and the base diagonal AC.
The angle is GAC, in triangle ACG
tan GAC =CG/AC=510
GAC =26.6°
YOUR TURN · c
A cube has edge 6 cm. Find the length of a space diagonal.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Two triangles → Base diagonal
The face diagonal is 62+62=72
Then d=72+36=108≈10.4 cm
YOUR TURN · d
A square-based pyramid has base side 10 cm and height 12 cm, with its apex V above the centre M. Find the angle between VA and the base.
AM=52≈7.07 cm. tan VAM =127.07, so the angle is 59.5°
