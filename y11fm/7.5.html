Problems in Three Dimensions

A 3D problem is a 2D problem in disguise. Pick the right triangle out of the solid, draw it flat, and the trigonometry you already know does the rest.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A 3D problem is solved by extracting the true shape of a relevant 2D cross-section, following the drawing convention that verticals are vertical, east-west lines horizontal and hidden edges dotted.
By the end you can
G7Draw a clear 2D representation of a 3D object and extract the true shape of a given cross-section.
G8Solve 3D problems using Pythagoras and trigonometry on the extracted triangles.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Drawing a solid

Vertical edges are drawn vertical. Hidden edges are drawn dotted.

The true shape

A 3D drawing distorts angles. A separate sketch of one triangle shows its true shape.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Cuboid ABCDEFGH has base ABCD and E above A. AB = 6 cm, BC = 8 cm and CG = 5 cm. Sketch the true shape of triangle ACG.In the 3D drawing angle ACG looks obtuse. It is a right angle.
Where the sides lieAC is in the base and CG is vertical
Right angleangle ACG = 90°
SketchAC across, CG straight up from C
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A square-based pyramid has base ABCD of side 10 cm and its apex V above the centre M, with VM = 12 cm. Sketch the true shape of triangle VMA.M is the centre of the base, so AM is half of a diagonal, not half of a side.
Where the sides lieVM is vertical and MA is in the base
Right angleangle VMA = 90°
LengthAM = half of AC = 5√2 = 7.07 cm
YOUR TURN · a
Cuboid ABCDEFGH has base ABCD and E above A. Which angle of triangle ABF is the right angle?
Angle ABF, at B: AB lies in the base and BF is vertical.
YOUR TURN · b
A cube has edge 4 cm. Sketch the true shape of the triangle made by a base diagonal, a vertical edge and a space diagonal. Label its sides.
A right angle between the base diagonal, 4√2 = 5.66 cm, and the vertical edge, 4 cm. The space diagonal is the hypotenuse.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A diagonal first

A space diagonal needs a face diagonal first. Two uses of Pythagoras give d² = a² + b² + c².

An angle in 3D

An angle in 3D is found inside one right-angled triangle. That triangle needs two known sides first.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Cuboid ABCDEFGH has AB = 6 cm, BC = 8 cm and CG = 5 cm. Find AG.Two triangles: ABC in the base, then ACG standing up.
Base diagonalAC = √(6² + 8²)
= 10 cm
Triangle ACG, right-angled at CAG² = 10² + 5²
= 125
AG = √125 = 11.2 cm (3 s.f.)
WORKED EXAMPLE D
In the same cuboid, find the angle between AG and the base ABCD.The angle sits at A, between AG and the base diagonal AC.
The angle is GAC, in triangle ACG
tan GAC = CGAC = 510
GAC = 26.6°
YOUR TURN · c
A cube has edge 6 cm. Find the length of a space diagonal.
The face diagonal is √(6² + 6²) = √72. Then d = √(72 + 36) = √108 = 10.4 cm.
YOUR TURN · d
A square-based pyramid has base side 10 cm and height 12 cm, with its apex V above the centre M. Find the angle between VA and the base.
AM = 5√2 = 7.07 cm. tan VAM = 127.07, so the angle is 59.5°.
