Manipulating Surds

A surd is only awkward while it sits in the denominator, and multiplying by the conjugate is what makes it vanish - that one trick is the whole reason rationalising works, and it is what lets you leave an exact answer rather than a rounded one.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A surd simplifies by extracting the largest square factor.
  • Multiplying a two-term surd expression by its conjugate eliminates the surd, which is what makes rationalising a two-term denominator possible.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The largest square factor

A surd simplifies by taking out its largest square factor. So √72 = √36 × √2 = 6√2.

Like surds

Only like surds can be added. So √8 + √2 becomes 2√2 + √2 = 3√2.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One-term denominator

Multiplying top and bottom by the surd clears it from the denominator. So 6√3 = 6√3 ÷ 3 = 2√3.

The conjugate

The conjugate of 3 + √2 is 3 - √2. Their product, 9 - 2 = 7, has no surd.
