The Binomial Expansion

Pascal's triangle turns expanding (a+b)n from twenty minutes of multiplying into copying down one row, and the real payoff is being able to pull out the single term you are asked for without expanding any of the others.

By the end you’ll know…
  • (a+b)n has n+1 terms of the form P aqbʳ where q+r=n.
  • The coefficients P are the entries of the nth row of Pascal's triangle, each built by summing the two entries above it.
By the end you can
GFMBuild Pascal's triangle and use a row of it to expand (a+b)n for a positive integer n.
GFMExpand where a and b are compound terms such as 2x or −w, and find a specified term or the first few terms without expanding fully.

Learn

Pascal's triangle

Each entry in Pascal's triangle is the sum of the two above it. Row n gives the coefficients of (a+b)n.

The powers

The expansion of (a+b)n has n+1 terms. In each term the powers of a and b add to n.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Expand (a+b)4
Coefficients from row 4
powers of a fall and powers of b rise.
Row 41, 4, 6, 4, 1
Powersa4, a3b, a2b2, ab3, b4
a4 +4a3b +6a2b2 +4ab3 +b4
WORKED EXAMPLE B

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Compound terms

A compound term is bracketed before it is raised to a power. So (2x)3 is 8x3, not 2x3

One term only

A single term needs only its own coefficient and powers. The powers must still add to n.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Expand (2x−3)3
Bracket both terms(2x) and (−3). The signs then take care of themselves.
Row 31, 3, 3, 1
Term 1(2x)3=8x3
Term 23(2x)2(−3)=−36x2
Term 33(2x)(−3)2=54x
Term 4(−3)3=−27
8x3 −36x2 +54x −27
