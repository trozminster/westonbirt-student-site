Circle Theorems

Circle theorems give you angle facts that straight-line and triangle facts cannot reach, and each one only counts as an answer when you name it as the reason.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The circle theorems are:
  • The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc.
  • The angle in a semicircle is 90 degrees.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal.
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180 degrees.
  • A tangent meets a radius at 90 degrees, and two tangents from a point are equal.
  • The alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
By the end you can
G7Apply the circle theorems to find missing angles, including reflex cases, stating a reason at each step.
G8Solve multi-step circle problems combining two or more theorems with other angle facts.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The angle at the centre

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. Both angles must stand on the same arc.

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference when both stand on the same arc. Name the arc first, then double or halve.

The angle in a semicircle

A diameter makes the angle at the centre 180 degrees. So the angle in a semicircle is 90 degrees.

When the chord is a diameter, the angle at the centre is a straight line, 180 degrees, so the angle at the circumference is 90 degrees.

Angles in the same segment

Angles on the same chord and the same side of it are equal.

Angles at the circumference standing on the same chord, and on the same side of it, are equal.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
A, B and P lie on a circle with centre O. Angle APB = 38 degrees. Find angle AOB.
Name the arc, then double the circumference angle.
The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc AB
Angle AOB = 2 × 38 = 76 degrees
Reason statedangle at the centre = 2 x angle at the circumference
WORKED EXAMPLE B
PQ is a diameter of a circle and R lies on the circle. Angle QPR = 34 degrees. Find angle PQR.
The angle in a semicircle is 90 degrees, then use the angles in a triangle.
Angle PRQ = 90 degrees, since PQ is a diameter (angle in a semicircle)
Angle PQR = 180 - 90 - 34
Angle PQR = 56 degrees (angles in a triangle add to 180)
YOUR TURN · a
A, B and C lie on a circle with centre O. Angle ACB = 47 degrees. Find angle AOB.
The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc AB. Angle AOB = 2 × 47 = 94 degrees.
YOUR TURN · b
PQ is a diameter and R lies on the circle. Angle PQR = 61 degrees. Find angle QPR.
Angle PRQ = 90 degrees (angle in a semicircle). Angle QPR = 180 - 90 - 61 = 29 degrees (angles in a triangle add to 180).

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Cyclic quadrilaterals

The four vertices lie on a circle. Opposite angles add up to 180 degrees.

Tangent and radius

A tangent meets the radius at the point of contact at 90 degrees. Two tangents from the same outside point are equal in length.

The alternate segment theorem

The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment. That segment is on the other side of the chord.

The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment, which is the segment on the other side of the chord.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
ABCD is cyclic with angle A = 3x + 10 and angle C = 5x - 30. Find x and angle A.
Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180.
A + C = 180 (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral)
(3x + 10) + (5x - 30) = 180, so 8x - 20 = 180 and x = 25
Angle A = 3(25) + 10 = 85 degrees
WORKED EXAMPLE D
TP is a tangent at P and O is the centre. Angle OPQ = 28 degrees. Find angle TPQ, then angle PRQ for R on the major arc.
Use the tangent-radius angle first, then the alternate segment theorem.
Angle OPT = 90 degrees (tangent meets the radius at 90), so TPQ = 90 - 28 = 62 degrees
Angle PRQ is in the alternate segment to the tangent-chord angle TPQ
Angle PRQ = 62 degrees (alternate segment theorem)
YOUR TURN · c
ABCD is cyclic with angle ABC = 4x + 5 and angle ADC = 2x + 25. Find x and angle ABC.
Opposite angles add to 180, so 6x + 30 = 180 and x = 25. Angle ABC = 4(25) + 5 = 105 degrees.
YOUR TURN · d
TP is a tangent at P and O is the centre. Angle TPQ = 70 degrees. Find angle POQ.
Angle OPQ = 90 - 70 = 20 degrees (tangent meets the radius at 90). Triangle OPQ is isosceles with OP = OQ, so angle OQP = 20 degrees. Angle POQ = 180 - 20 - 20 = 140 degrees.
