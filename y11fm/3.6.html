Finding the Equation of a Line

Once you can read a gradient and an intercept, you can run the process backwards and build the equation of a line from whatever information is given, which is the skill coordinate geometry in the next chapter relies on.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A line is determined either by its gradient and one point, giving y−y1 =m(x−x1), or by two points.
  • The most efficient route depends on which information is supplied.
By the end you can
G6Find the equation of a line from its gradient and intercept, or from its gradient and one point.
G7Find the equation of a line through two given points and rearrange it into a required form.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Gradient and intercept

A given gradient and y-intercept go straight into y=mx+c. Gradient 3 and intercept −2 give y=3x−2

When the gradient and the y-intercept are given, they go straight into y=mx+c. A line with gradient 3 that crosses the y-axis at −2 has the equation y=3x−2

Gradient and a point

A gradient and one point (x1,y1) give y−y1 =m(x−x1). The point keeps its own signs inside the brackets.

When the gradient and one point (x1,y1) are given, the equation is y−y1 =m(x−x1), and it can then be rearranged. The point keeps its own signs inside the brackets.

Learn

Through two points

Through two points, the gradient comes first. Either point then goes into y−y1 =m(x−x1).

To find a line through two points, work out the gradient first, then use one of the points in y−y1 =m(x−x1). Either point gives the same line.

Required form

Some questions want ax + by + c=0 with whole-number coefficients. Clearing the fractions comes before collecting terms.

Some questions ask for the answer as ax + by + c=0 with whole-number coefficients. Multiply to clear any fractions, then move every term to one side.
