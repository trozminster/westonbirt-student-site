Stationary Points

Where a curve turns, its gradient is zero for an instant. Finding and classifying those points is the key to sketching curves and to every optimisation problem.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A stationary point occurs where the derivative is zero.
  • It is a maximum where the second derivative is negative and a minimum where it is positive.

Flat for an instant

At a stationary point the gradient is zero. So stationary points come from solving dy/dx =0

Both coordinates

Solving dy/dx =0 gives only the x-coordinate. The y-coordinate comes from the curve's own equation.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the stationary point of y=x2−8x+5
One stationary pointthe vertex. Put x back into y, not into dy/dx.
Set dy/dx =0: 2x−8=0, so x=4
Substitute into yy=16−32+5
=−11
The stationary point is (4,−11)

A cubic's dy/dx is a quadratic, so expect two points.
Set dy/dx =0: 3x2−6x−9 =0, so x2−2x−3=0
Factorise(x−3)(x+1)=0
x=3

Points(3,−25) and (−1,7)

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The sign test

A maximum has a positive gradient before it and a negative one after. A minimum has the reverse.

The second derivative test

At a stationary point, a negative d2ydx2 means a maximum. A positive d2ydx2 means a minimum. Zero needs the sign test instead.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Classify the stationary points (3,−25) and (−1,7) of y =x3−3x2−9x+2
Differentiate dy/dx =3x2−6x−9 again, then substitute each x.
d2ydx2 =6x−6
At x=3: 12>0, so (3,−25) is a minimum
At x=−1: −12<0, so (−1,7) is a maximum
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Classify the stationary point (0,0) of y=x4
d2ydx2=0 says nothing. Fall back on the gradient either side.
d2ydx2 =12x2 =0 at x=0: no decision
Sign testdy/dx =4x3 is negative at x=−0.1
positive at x=0.1
Gradient - then +(0,0) is a minimum
