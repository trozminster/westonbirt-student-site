The Cosine Rule and Choosing a Method

Two sides and the angle between them, or three sides, leave the sine rule stuck. The cosine rule unlocks those triangles, and choosing between the two rules is the real exam skill.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The cosine rule, a2 =b2 +c2 −2bccosA (and its relabelled versions), applies when the sine rule cannot: given two sides and the included angle, or all three sides. Rearranged as cosA =(b2+c2−a2)÷(2bc), it finds an angle from three known sides.
  • A triangle has only three independent measurements, so knowing any three unlocks the rest.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The cosine rule

The cosine rule is a2 =b2 +c2 −2bccosA. It finds a side from two sides and the angle between them.

The order of working

The term 2bccosA is worked out first, then subtracted. The square root comes last.

Three sides

Rearranged, it gives cosA =b2+c2−a22bc. That finds any angle from three sides.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
In triangle ABC, b=8 cm, c=11 cm and angle A=52°. Find a.
A is between b and c, so the cosine rule. Work out 2bccosA before subtracting.
Substitutea2=82 +112 −2×8×11×cos52°
Evaluatea2=185−108.36
=76.64
a=76.64=8.75 cm (3 s.f.)
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A triangle has sides 7 cm, 9 cm and 12 cm. Find its largest angle.
The largest angle faces the longest side, so 12 plays the part of a.
Largest angle faces the 12 cm side
cosA =72+92−1222×7×9 ≈−0.111
A =cos−1(−0.111) ≈96.4°

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Which rule

A side with its opposite angle known means the sine rule. Otherwise the cosine rule starts the problem.

Three measurements

Three measurements fix a triangle, as long as one is a side. The other three then follow, one rule at a time.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A ship sails 12 km on a bearing of 060° from P to Q, then 8 km on a bearing of 170° to R. Find PR.
Draw north lines at P and Q. The bearing back from Q to P is 240°.
Angle at Q240°−170°=70°
Two sides and the angle betweenPR²=122 +82 −2×12×8×cos70° ≈142.3
PR=11.9 km (3 s.f.)
WORKED EXAMPLE D
In the same voyage, PQ=12 km, QR=8 km, angle PQR=70° and PR=11.9 km. Find the bearing of R from P.
Now a side faces a known angle, so the sine rule.
Sine rulesin QPR =8×sin70°÷11.93 ≈0.630
Angle QPR=39.1°
Bearing =060°+39.1°=099°
YOUR TURN · c
In triangle ABC, a=9 cm, b=7 cm and angle C=48°. Which rule finds c? Find c.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Angle at Q → Two sides and the angle between
The cosine rule, since C is between a and b.c2 =81 +49 −126×cos48° ≈45.7, so c=6.76 cm.
YOUR TURN · d
In triangle DEF, angle D=35°, angle E=80° and DE=10 cm. Find EF.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Sine rule
F =180−35−80 =65°, and DE faces F. EF/sin 35°=10sin65°, so EF=6.33 cm.
