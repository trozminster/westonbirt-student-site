Gradients and Distance

Coordinate geometry turns shapes into numbers: gradients tell you whether lines are parallel or perpendicular, and Pythagoras turns two points into a length.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Parallel lines have equal gradients, and perpendicular lines have gradients whose product is -1, so each is the negative reciprocal of the other.
  • The distance between two points (x1,y1) and (x2,y2) is sqrt((x2-x1)² + (y2-y1)²), from Pythagoras applied to the horizontal and vertical separation.

Parallel and perpendicular

Parallel lines have the same gradient. Perpendicular gradients multiply to give -1.

Parallel lines have the same gradient. Perpendicular lines have gradients that multiply to give -1, so each gradient is the negative reciprocal of the other.

Finding a perpendicular gradient

To find the perpendicular gradient, flip the fraction and change the sign. Rearrange to y = mx + c first if the equation is in another form.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Are y = 2x - 3 and 2y + x = 8 parallel, perpendicular or neither?Find both gradients, then test their product.
The first line has gradient 2
Rearranged, 2y = -x + 8 gives gradient -12
The product is 2 × (-12) = -1, so the lines are perpendicular
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Find the gradient of a line perpendicular to 3x + 4y = 12Rearrange to find the gradient, then flip and change the sign.
4y = -3x + 12, so the gradient is -34
Flip to 43 and change the sign to get 43
The perpendicular gradient is 43

YOUR TURN · b

The distance formula

The distance between two points is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle. So Pythagoras gives d = √((x2 - x1)² + (y2 - y1)²).

The distance between two points is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle, so Pythagoras gives d = √((x2 - x1)² + (y2 - y1)²).

Negative coordinates

Subtract in brackets so that the signs are handled correctly, and square before adding. A distance that is not a whole number is left as a surd.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the distance between A(1, 2) and B(7, 10).Find the horizontal and vertical steps, then use Pythagoras.
Across 7 - 1 = 6 and up 10 - 2 = 8
d² = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100
d = 10
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the distance between P(-3, 5) and Q(2, -1).Subtract in brackets, then square, add and take the root.
Across 2 - (-3) = 5 and up -1 - 5 = -6
d² = 25 + 36 = 61
d = √61
YOUR TURN · c
Find the distance between (-1, 2) and (4, 14).
Across 4 - (-1) = 5 and up 14 - 2 = 12. d² = 25 + 144 = 169, so d = 13.
YOUR TURN · d
Find the distance between (-4, -2) and (1, 3) as a surd.
Across 1 - (-4) = 5 and up 3 - (-2) = 5. d² = 25 + 25 = 50, so d = √50 = 5√2.
