Transformation Matrices

You already know how to reflect and rotate a shape by hand; today those same transformations get written down as four numbers, which is what lets you combine two of them into a single matrix in 9.5 instead of drawing every step.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The columns of a 2×2 transformation matrix are the images of (1,0) and (0,1).
  • The identity matrix leaves every point unchanged, so AI=IA = A.

What the columns record

The columns of a transformation matrix record the images of (1,0) and (0,1). Every other point follows from those two.

The columns of a transformation matrix record where the points (1,0) and (0,1) are sent, so the matrix carries the whole transformation. Every other point follows from those two.

The identity matrix

The identity matrix I sends every point to itself. Multiplying by I on either side changes nothing.

The identity matrix I sends every point to itself, so multiplying any matrix by I on either side leaves it unchanged. Its columns are the points (1,0) and (0,1) themselves.

Building a matrix

A transformation matrix is built from the images of (1,0) and (0,1). Those images become its columns.

A transformation matrix is built by finding where (1,0) and (0,1) are sent and writing those images as the columns. A half turn about the origin sends them to (−1,0) and (0,−1).

A product that equals I

A product equal to I has a known value in every entry. Each entry gives an equation for the unknown.

When a product must equal the identity matrix, every entry of the product has a known value. Each entry therefore gives an equation for the unknown.
