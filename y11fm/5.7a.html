The Equation of a Circle

A circle is every point at a fixed distance from its centre, so Pythagoras gives its equation, and completing the square turns an expanded equation back into centre and radius.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A circle is the locus of points at a fixed distance from its centre, so Pythagoras gives x² + y² = r² for a circle centred on the origin and (x-a)² + (y-b)² = r² for centre (a,b).

Learn

Pythagoras gives the equation

Every point on a circle is a distance r from its centre. For centre (a, b) this gives (x - a)² + (y - b)² = r².

Every point (x, y) on the circle is a distance r from the centre. For centre (a, b) this gives (x - a)² + (y - b)² = r². With the centre at the origin it is x² + y² = r².

Reading the centre and radius

The bracket signs are the opposite of the centre's. The right-hand side is the radius squared.

The signs in the brackets are the opposite of the centre, and the number on the right is the radius squared. So (x - 3)² + (y + 2)² = 49 has centre (3, -2) and radius 7.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Write the equation of the circle with centre (-2, 5) and radius 4.Opposite signs for the centre, and r squared on the right.
x - (-2) = x + 2 and y - 5
r² = 4² = 16
(x + 2)² + (y - 5)² = 16
WORKED EXAMPLE B
State the centre and radius of (x - 1)² + (y + 6)² = 20.The centre has the opposite signs, and the radius is the square root of 20.
x - 1 gives x = 1 and y + 6 gives y = -6, so the centre is (1, -6)
r² = 20
r = √20 = 2√5

x - 4 and y - (-1) = y + 1, and r² = 9. The equation is (x - 4)² + (y + 1)² = 9.

x + 3 gives x = -3 and y - 2 gives y = 2, so the centre is (-3, 2). r² = 36, so r = 6.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Recognising a circle

A circle's x² and y² have the same coefficient, with no xy term. Unequal coefficients mean it is not a circle.

In expanded form a circle has x² and y² with the same coefficient and no xy term. An equation with unequal coefficients is not a circle.

Completing the square twice

Completing the square in x and y separately reveals the centre. The right-hand side, r², must be positive.

Complete the square in x and in y separately, and move the numbers to the right-hand side. The right-hand side is r², and it must be positive.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the centre and radius of x² + y² - 8x + 2y - 8 = 0.Complete the square in x and in y, then move the constants across.
x² - 8x = (x - 4)² - 16 and y² + 2y = (y + 1)² - 1
(x - 4)² + (y + 1)² = 8 + 16 + 1 = 25
Centre (4, -1) and radius 5
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the centre and radius of x² + y² + 6x - 10y + 18 = 0.The constant 18 moves to the right as -18.
x² + 6x = (x + 3)² - 9 and y² - 10y = (y - 5)² - 25
(x + 3)² + (y - 5)² = -18 + 9 + 25 = 16
Centre (-3, 5) and radius 4
YOUR TURN · c
Find the centre and radius of x² + y² - 2x - 6y - 15 = 0.
x² - 2x = (x - 1)² - 1 and y² - 6y = (y - 3)² - 9. So (x - 1)² + (y - 3)² = 15 + 1 + 9 = 25. Centre (1, 3), radius 5.
YOUR TURN · d
Find the centre and radius of x² + y² + 4x + 8y + 4 = 0.
x² + 4x = (x + 2)² - 4 and y² + 8y = (y + 4)² - 16. So (x + 2)² + (y + 4)² = -4 + 4 + 16 = 16. Centre (-2, -4), radius 4.
