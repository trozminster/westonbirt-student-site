The Factor Theorem

Once an equation has a power of x above two, the old methods stop working, and the factor theorem is the tool that finds a first factor so that the quadratic methods can take over.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A polynomial is classified by its degree as cubic, quartic or quintic. The factor theorem states that if f(a)=0 then (x−a) is a factor of f(x), and conversely.
  • Candidate values of a are therefore found among the factors of the constant term.

Naming by degree

A polynomial is named by its highest power of x. The number of terms does not matter. A cubic has x3, a quartic x4 and a quintic x5

A polynomial is named by the highest power of x in it, so a cubic has x3, a quartic has x4 and a quintic has x5
The number of terms does not matter.

The theorem

If f(a)=0, then (x−a) is a factor of f(x). It works both ways. A non-zero value means the bracket is not a factor.

If f(a)=0 then (x−a) is a factor of f(x), and if (x−a) is a factor then f(a)=0
A value that does not give zero shows that the bracket is not a factor.

Which value to test

A bracket is tested at the x that makes it zero. So (x+3) is tested with f(−3).

To test a bracket, find the value of x that makes the bracket zero and evaluate f at that value. The sign flips: (x+3) is tested with f(−3).
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Show that (x−2) is a factor of f(x)=x3−3x2+x+2
The bracket is zero at x=2, so test f(2).
x−2=0 when x=2, so find f(2)
f(2)=8−12+2+2
f(2)=0, so (x−2) is a factor
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Is (x+1) a factor of f(x)=x3+2x2−3x+4?
The bracket is zero at x=−1, so test f(−1).
x+1=0 when x=−1, so find f(−1)
f(−1)=−1+2+3+4=8
f(−1) is not 0, so (x+1) is not a factor

Candidates

Any whole-number root divides the constant term. For a constant of 6, try ±1, ±2, ±3 and ±6

If (x - a) is a factor of a polynomial with whole-number coefficients, a must divide the constant term. For a constant of 6 the candidates are ±1, ±2, ±3 and ±6, including the negatives.

Test in turn

Candidates are tested one at a time, stopping at the first zero. Here f(x)=x3−2x2−9x+18 gives zero at 2

Test the candidates one at a time and stop as soon as one gives zero, because one linear factor is all that is needed to start. Here the candidates for a constant of 18 are tried in order.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find a linear factor of f(x)=x3−2x2−9x+18
Try the factors of 18 in turn, and stop when f(a)=0
Candidates are the factors of 18, positive and negative
f(1)=8 and f(−1)=24, so neither works
f(2)=8−8−18+18=0, so (x−2) is a factor
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find a linear factor of f(x)=x3+6x2+11x+6
Every term is positive for positive x, so a positive value cannot give zero.
Candidates are the factors of 6, but try the negatives first
f(−1)=−1+6−11+6=0
f(−1)=0, so (x+1) is a factor
YOUR TURN · c
Find a linear factor of f(x)=x3−4x2+x+6
f(1)=4, so no. f(−1)=−1−4−1+6=0, so (x+1) is a factor.
YOUR TURN · d
Find a linear factor of f(x)=x3−6x−9
f(1)=−14 and f(−1)=−4 fail. f(3)=27−18−9=0, so (x−3) is a factor.
