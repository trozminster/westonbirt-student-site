Midpoints and Coordinate Problems

The midpoint is the average of the endpoints, and together with gradients and the distance formula it lets you prove facts about shapes drawn on a grid.

  • A right angle in a coordinate problem is a statement about perpendicular gradients.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The midpoint is an average

The midpoint's coordinates are the averages of the endpoints' coordinates. The x and the y are averaged separately.

The midpoint of two points has coordinates that are the averages of the endpoint coordinates, found separately for x and for y.

Algebraic endpoints

With letters in the endpoints, like terms are added before halving. The midpoint is given in simplest form.

When the endpoints contain letters, add like terms before halving, and give the midpoint in simplest form.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the midpoint of A(-3, 4) and B(7, -10).
Average the x-coordinates and the y-coordinates separately.
x: half of (-3 + 7) = half of 4 = 2
y: half of (4 + (-10)) = half of -6 = -3
The midpoint is (2, -3)
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Find the midpoint of (2p, q + 3) and (4p, 3q - 1).
Add like terms, then halve each coordinate.
x: half of (2p + 4p) = half of 6p = 3p
y: half of (q + 3 + 3q - 1) = half of (4q + 2) = 2q + 1
The midpoint is (3p, 2q + 1)
YOUR TURN · a
Find the midpoint of (-5, 2) and (3, 8).
x: half of (-5 + 3) = -1. y: half of (2 + 8) = 5. The midpoint is (-1, 5).
YOUR TURN · b
Find the midpoint of (a + 2, 4b) and (3a, 2b - 6).
x: half of (4a + 2) = 2a + 1. y: half of (6b - 6) = 3b - 3. The midpoint is (2a + 1, 3b - 3).

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A right angle needs perpendicular gradients

A right angle needs the two lines' gradients to multiply to -1. The gradients come from the lines meeting at that vertex.

To show that an angle is a right angle, find the gradients of the two lines that meet at that point and show that their product is -1.

Choosing the right tool

Each tool answers one kind of question. Gradients answer angles and parallels, distances lengths, midpoints centres.

In a coordinate problem, decide what each question needs, and do one step at a time. Gradients answer questions about angles and parallel sides, distances answer questions about length, and midpoints answer questions about centres.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Show that the triangle A(1, 1), B(3, 5), C(11, 1) has a right angle at B.
Find the gradients of BA and BC and test the product.
Gradient BA = 1 - 51 - 3 = 2
Gradient BC = 1 - 511 - 3 = -12
The product is -1, so angle ABC is 90 degrees
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A(0, 1), B(4, 5), C(10, 1). Find the midpoint M of BC and the length AM.
Do the midpoint first, then the distance from A.
M: x = half of 14 = 7, y = half of 6 = 3, so M(7, 3)
AMacross 7 - 0 = 7
up 3 - 1 = 2
AM = √(49 + 4) = √53
YOUR TURN · c
Show that A(0, 2), B(4, 4) and C(3, 6) make a right angle at B.
Gradient BA = 2 - 40 - 4 = 12. Gradient BC = 6 - 43 - 4 = -2. The product is -1, so the angle at B is 90 degrees.
YOUR TURN · d
P(-2, 1) and Q(4, 9). Find the midpoint M of PQ and the length PM.
M: x = half of 2 = 1, y = half of 10 = 5, so M(1, 5). PM: across 1 - (-2) = 3 and up 5 - 1 = 4, so PM = √(9 + 16) = 5.
