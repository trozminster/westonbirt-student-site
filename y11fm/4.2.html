Simultaneous Equations

Two conditions that must both hold at once is how most real problems arrive, and solving them as a pair, including a line meeting a curve, is the tool that coordinate geometry in Chapter 5 uses constantly.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Simultaneous equations describe the intersection of two graphs.
  • A linear-quadratic pair generally gives two solution pairs, and x and y values must be correctly paired.

Elimination

Elimination adds or subtracts the equations so that one variable cancels. The value found is substituted back to find the other.

Elimination adds or subtracts the two equations so that one variable cancels, leaving a single equation in the other variable. Once that value is found, it is substituted back to find the second.

What the pair means

Each equation is a line. The solution is the point where the two lines meet.

Each equation is a line, and the solution is the single point where the two lines meet. That is why x and y have to be found as a pair.

A line and a curve

A line can cut a parabola at two points. Each x-value belongs with its own y-value.

A line can cut a parabola at two points, so a linear-quadratic pair usually has two solution pairs. Each x-value belongs with its own y-value.

Learn

Substitute and solve

In a linear-quadratic pair, y is replaced by the linear expression. Each x-value then pairs with its y from the linear equation.

To solve a linear-quadratic pair, replace y in the quadratic with the linear expression and solve the resulting quadratic in x. Each x-value is then paired with its y from the linear equation.

Brackets matter

A substituted expression for y goes in brackets before squaring. Expanding then needs the middle term.

When a linear expression is substituted for y in x2+y2, it goes in brackets so that the whole expression is squared. Expanding then needs the middle term.
