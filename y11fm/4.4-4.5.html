Linear and Quadratic Inequalities

An inequality describes a whole range of answers rather than one, which is how tolerances, limits and "at least" conditions are written, and the quadratic version shows up again when you study where a curve is above or below the axis.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An inequality is solved like an equation, except that multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the sign. A quadratic inequality is solved by finding the critical values and deciding, from the parabola's shape, whether the solution lies between them or outside them.

The sign reverses

Multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the inequality sign. Checking one number shows whether the answer is right.

Solving an inequality is like solving an equation, except that multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign. A check with one number shows whether the final answer is right.

Double inequalities

In a double inequality, each operation is done to all three parts. The middle stays between the outer two.

In a double inequality every operation is done to all three parts, so the middle stays between the outer two.

Combining ranges

Pairing the ends of two ranges gives the extremes of x+y. The smallest x−y uses the smallest x and the largest y.

When x and y each lie in a range, the extremes of x+y or x−y come from pairing the largest with the largest, or the smallest with the largest. For x−y the smallest result uses the smallest x and the largest y.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Sketch and read

A quadratic inequality is read from a sketch of its parabola. The roots are the critical values where the sign can change.

Solve the matching equation to find the critical values, sketch the parabola, and read off where it is below or above the axis. For x2−x−6 the curve is below the axis between its roots −2 and 3

Between or outside

For an upward curve, below the axis is between the roots. Above the axis is outside them, in two separate parts.

For a curve that opens upwards, the part below the axis is between the roots and the part above is outside them. Greater than zero gives two separate parts, and less than zero gives one.

A negative x2 term

With a negative x2 term, the curve opens downwards. Above the axis is then between the roots. Multiplying by −1 and reversing the sign agrees.

When the x2 term is negative, the curve opens downwards, so above the axis is between the roots. The same result comes from multiplying through by −1 and reversing the sign.
