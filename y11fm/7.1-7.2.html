Area of a Triangle and the Sine Rule

Right-angled trigonometry stops at the right angle. The area formula and the sine rule work in every triangle, from a plot of land to a ship's course.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The area of any triangle is half the product of two sides and the sine of the included angle - e.g. Area =12 ab sinC.
  • The sine rule, asinA =bsinB =csinC (or its reciprocal sinAa =sinBb =sinCc for finding an angle), follows from equating two area expressions for the same triangle; use the side-over-sine form to find a side, and the sine-over-side form to find an angle.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The included angle

Area =12 ab sinC uses two sides and the angle between them. The angle must be the one those two sides make.

From the height

The height of the triangle is bsinC. So the area is 12×a×bsinC.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
In triangle ABC, AB=8 cm, AC=5 cm and angle A=40°. Find the area.
Check that the angle sits between the two sides you use.
Included angleA is between AB and AC
Area =12×8×5×sin40°
Area =12.9 cm² (3 s.f.)
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Triangle PQR has area 30 cm², PQ=10 cm and angle P=50°. Find PR.
Substitute everything you know, then divide.
Substitute30=12×10×PR×sin50°
Simplify30=5×PR×sin50°
PR =30÷(5×sin50°) ≈7.83 cm

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Opposite pairs

The sine rule pairs each side with the angle opposite it. So asinA =bsinB =csinC.

Two answers

Two angles below 180° share each sine: B and 180° - B. The angle sum decides whether the second one fits.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
In triangle ABC, angle A=40°, angle B=75° and a=8 cm. Find b.
Side over sine to find a sideb goes with B and a goes with A.
Pair each side with its anglebsin75°=8sin40°
Rearrangeb=8×sin75°÷sin40°
b=12.0 cm (3 s.f.)
WORKED EXAMPLE D
In triangle ABC, angle A=35°, a=6 cm and b=9 cm. Find angle B.
Sine over side to find an angle. Then test the obtuse partner against the angle sum.
Sine over sidesinB9=sin35°6
sinB =9×sin35°÷6 ≈0.860
B=59.4° or 180−59.4=120.6°
Both fit, since 35+120.6<180
YOUR TURN · c
In triangle PQR, angle P=52°, angle Q=64° and q=15 cm. Find p.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Side over sine to find a side → Pair each side with its angle → Rearrange
psin52° =15sin64°, so p =15×sin52°÷sin64° ≈13.2 cm (3 s.f.).
YOUR TURN · d
In triangle ABC, angle A=70°, a=12 cm and b=9 cm. Find angle B. Is there a second triangle?
Hint. Follow the worked example: Sine over side
sinB =9×sin70°÷12 ≈0.705, so B=44.8°. The partner 135.2° does not fit, since 70+135.2>180
There is only one triangle.
