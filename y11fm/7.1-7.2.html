Area of a Triangle and the Sine Rule

Right-angled trigonometry stops at the right angle. The area formula and the sine rule work in every triangle, from a plot of land to a ship's course.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The area of any triangle is half the product of two sides and the sine of the included angle - e.g. Area = 12 ab sin C.
  • The sine rule, asin A = bsin B = csin C (or its reciprocal sin Aa = sin Bb = sin Cc for finding an angle), follows from equating two area expressions for the same triangle; use the side-over-sine form to find a side, and the sine-over-side form to find an angle.
By the end you can
G7Find the area of a triangle from two sides and the included angle, and use the formula in reverse.
G8Use the sine rule to find an unknown side or angle, recognising the ambiguous case.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The included angle

Area = 12 ab sin C uses two sides and the angle between them. The angle must be the one those two sides make.

From the height

The height of the triangle is b sin C. So the area is 12 × a × b sin C.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
In triangle ABC, AB = 8 cm, AC = 5 cm and angle A = 40°. Find the area.
Check that the angle sits between the two sides you use.
Included angleA is between AB and AC
Area = 12 × 8 × 5 × sin 40°
Area = 12.9 cm² (3 s.f.)
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Triangle PQR has area 30 cm², PQ = 10 cm and angle P = 50°. Find PR.
Substitute everything you know, then divide.
Substitute30 = 12 × 10 × PR × sin 50°
Simplify30 = 5 × PR × sin 50°
PR = 30 ÷ (5 × sin 50°) = 7.83 cm
YOUR TURN · a
Find the area of a triangle with sides 7 cm and 11 cm and an included angle of 125°.
Area = 12 × 7 × 11 × sin 125° = 31.5 cm² (3 s.f.).
YOUR TURN · b
An isosceles triangle has two equal sides of x cm with 40° between them. Its area is 50 cm². Find x.
50 = 12 × x² × sin 40°, so x² = 100 ÷ sin 40° = 155.6 and x = 12.5 cm (3 s.f.).

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Opposite pairs

The sine rule pairs each side with the angle opposite it. So asin A = bsin B = csin C.

Two answers

Two angles below 180° share each sine: B and 180° - B. The angle sum decides whether the second one fits.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
In triangle ABC, angle A = 40°, angle B = 75° and a = 8 cm. Find b.
Side over sine to find a sideb goes with B and a goes with A.
Pair each side with its anglebsin 75° = 8sin 40°
Rearrangeb = 8 × sin 75° ÷ sin 40°
b = 12.0 cm (3 s.f.)
WORKED EXAMPLE D
In triangle ABC, angle A = 35°, a = 6 cm and b = 9 cm. Find angle B.
Sine over side to find an angle. Then test the obtuse partner against the angle sum.
Sine over sidesin B9 = sin 35°/6
sin B = 9 × sin 35° ÷ 6 = 0.860
B = 59.4° or 180 - 59.4 = 120.6°
Both fit, since 35 + 120.6 < 180
YOUR TURN · c
In triangle PQR, angle P = 52°, angle Q = 64° and q = 15 cm. Find p.
psin 52° = 15sin 64°, so p = 15 × sin 52° ÷ sin 64° = 13.2 cm (3 s.f.).
YOUR TURN · d
In triangle ABC, angle A = 70°, a = 12 cm and b = 9 cm. Find angle B. Is there a second triangle?
sin B = 9 × sin 70° ÷ 12 = 0.705, so B = 44.8°. The partner 135.2° does not fit, since 70 + 135.2 > 180. There is only one triangle.
