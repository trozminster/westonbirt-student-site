Relative and Expected Frequency

When the outcomes are not equally likely, such as a drawing pin landing point up, an experiment estimates the probability. Today we estimate probabilities from data and predict how often events happen.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Relative frequency = (number of times the event happened) / (number of trials), and it estimates a probability from collected data.
  • The more trials there are, the closer the relative frequency is likely to be to the true probability, so a large experiment gives a better estimate than a small one.
  • Expected frequency = probability x number of trials, which is how many times the event should happen on average and not a guarantee.
  • For mutually exclusive events, which cannot happen at the same time, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B).

More trials, better estimates

Relative frequency is the number of times an event happens over the number of trials. It estimates the probability, and more trials usually give a better estimate.

What to expect

Expected frequency is the probability times the number of trials. It is how often the event should happen on average, not a guarantee.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
A drawing pin lands point up 36 times in 80 throws. Estimate P(point up).
P(point up)36 ÷ 80 = 0.45

Either one or the other

Mutually exclusive events cannot happen at the same time. For these, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). So P(red or blue) = 0.15 + 0.4 = 0.55.

Events that cannot happen at the same time are mutually exclusive. For these, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B), so P(red or blue) = 0.15 + 0.4 = 0.55.

The missing probability

The probabilities of all the outcomes add up to 1. A missing probability is 1 minus the sum of the others.

The probabilities of all the outcomes add up to 1, so a missing probability is 1 take away the sum of the others.
