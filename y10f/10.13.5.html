Percentage Problems in Context

A shop offers 38 off one item and 35% off another, and friends split the bill in a ratio. Real problems mix all the forms of a proportion at once.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A fraction, a decimal and a percentage are three ways of writing the same proportion, so a problem may be worked in whichever form is easiest and converted at the end.
  • A ratio a : b describes a whole of a+b parts, so the first share is a÷(a+b) of the whole.
  • Two proportions can only be compared in a common form: either over a common denominator, or both written as percentages.
By the end you can
G4Convert between fractions, decimals, percentages and ratios in order to compare two proportions.

One form to compare

A fraction, a decimal and a percentage are three ways of writing one proportion. Two proportions can be compared only once they are in the same form.

A ratio as a fraction

A ratio a : b splits a whole into a+b equal parts. So the first share is aa+b of the whole, not ab.

WORKED EXAMPLE A

One step at a time

A multi-step problem is worked in the order the story happens. Each step's answer is the next step's starting amount.

Two changes multiply

Two changes in a row multiply their multipliers. A 10% rise then a 10% fall is × 1.1×0.9=×0.99

Two percentage changes one after the other multiply their multipliers. A 10% rise then a 10% fall is × 1.1×0.9=×0.99, a 1% fall overall.
