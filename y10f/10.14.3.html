Rotating About a Point

A clock hand, a turning wheel and a spinning ride all rotate about one fixed point. Three facts pin down any rotation exactly: where, how far and which way.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A rotation is specified by a centre, an angle and a direction.
  • An anticlockwise rotation is a positive angle and a clockwise rotation is a negative angle.
  • Every point of the image is the same distance from the centre as its object point, and the centre itself does not move.
  • A rotation preserves length and angle, so the image is congruent to the object.

Centre, angle and direction

A rotation turns every point about a fixed centre. Every point turns through the same angle, the same way. Each point stays the same distance from the centre.

A rotation turns every point about a fixed centre, through the same angle and in the same direction. Each point stays the same distance from the centre, and the centre itself does not move.

A quarter turn on a grid

Turn the line from the centre to each point, keeping its length. Anticlockwise is positive and clockwise is negative. So −90° is a quarter turn clockwise.

Turn the line from the centre to each point through the angle, keeping its length. Anticlockwise is a positive angle and clockwise a negative one, so −90° is a quarter turn clockwise.

Finding the angle

Match a line on the object with its line on the image. The turn between them gives the angle and the direction.

Compare a line on the object with its matching line on the image. The turn from one to the other is the angle, and the way it turned is the direction.

Finding the centre

The centre is as far from each point as from its image. Test a point with tracing paper. If the tracing turns onto the image, it is the centre.

The centre is the same distance from every point as from its image. Try a point with tracing paper: if the traced shape turns exactly onto the image, that point is the centre.
