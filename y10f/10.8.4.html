Topic 10.8 · Forming and Solving Equations · Lesson 4 of 5

Forming Equations from Contexts

Nobody hands you an equation in real life: you are handed a situation. Turning the situation into an equation is the step that makes the algebra useful.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation is formed by naming the unknown with a letter, writing every other quantity in terms of that letter, and then using a fact that makes two expressions equal.
  • In a geometric diagram that fact is usually an angle sum - 180 degrees on a line or in a triangle, 360 degrees at a point or in a quadrilateral - or a perimeter given as a total, or two sides known to be equal.
  • What the letter stands for is written down before the equation is formed, including its units.
  • The value of the letter is not always the answer to the question, so the question is read again once the equation is solved.
By the end you can
G4Form and solve a linear equation from a worded context, stating what the letter stands for.
G5Form and solve a linear equation from a geometric diagram, such as a triangle whose angles are a, (a + 10) and (a + 20) degrees, or a rectangle whose perimeter is given as a total.

Name the unknown first

Name the unknown with a letter. Write every other quantity in terms of it. A fact that makes two things equal gives the equation.

An equation is formed by naming the unknown with a letter and writing every other quantity in terms of it. A fact that makes two things equal then gives the equation.

The letter is not always the answer

Solving gives the value of the letter. Reread the question: it may ask for something built from it.

Solving gives the value of the letter. Reading the question again shows whether that value, or something built from it, was asked for.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
How old is Ben, if he is 4 years older than Amy and their ages add to 30?
Let x be Amy's agex + (x + 4) = 30
Simplify2x + 4 = 30
Subtract 4 from both sides2x = 26
Divide by 2x = 13
Ben's age17
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A taxi charges £3 plus £2 per mile. How many miles is a £17 ride?
Let m be the miles3 + 2m = 17
Subtract 3 from both sides2m = 14
Divide by 2m = 7
Number of miles7

YOUR TURN · b

An angle sum makes the equation

In a diagram, the equal fact is often an angle sum or a perimeter. Add the expressions and set them equal to that total.

In a diagram the equal fact is usually an angle sum, a perimeter given as a total, or two sides that are equal. The expressions are added and set equal to that total.

Perimeter counts every side

A rectangle's perimeter is twice the length plus twice the width. It is never just length plus width.

A rectangle's perimeter is twice its length plus twice its width, never length plus width. So a rectangle x + 3 long and x wide has perimeter 2(x + 3) + 2x.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A triangle has angles x, 2x and 3x. Find the largest angle.
Add the anglesx + 2x + 3x = 180
Simplify6x = 180
Divide both sides by 6x = 30
Largest angle3x = 90°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A rectangle is x + 5 long and x wide. Its perimeter is 34 cm. Find its length.
Add the four sides2(x + 5) + 2x = 34
Expand the bracket2x + 10 + 2x = 34
Simplify4x + 10 = 34
Subtract 10 from both sides4x = 24
Divide by 4x = 6
Length11 cm
YOUR TURN · c
Angles on a straight line are 2a and a + 30. Find a, then both angles.
2a + a + 30 = 180, 3a = 150, a = 50, so the angles are 100° and 80°
YOUR TURN · d

2(2y) + 2(y + 4) = 44, 6y + 8 = 44, y = 6, so the width is 10 cm

mark as you go
Checkpoint. The angles of a quadrilateral are x, 2x, 3x and 90°. Form an equation and find the largest angle.Show your teacher before you go on
1
A triangle has angles a, (a + 15) and (a + 30) degrees. Find a and the largest angle.
a + (a + 15) + (a + 30) = 180
3a + 45 = 180, so 3a = 135
a = 45: largest angle 45 + 30 = 75°
2
Two angles on a straight line are 4x and (x + 25) degrees. Find both angles.
4x + (x + 25) = 180
5x + 25 = 180, so 5x = 155
x = 31: angles 124° and 56°
3
A rectangle is (2x + 1) cm long and (x - 2) cm wide. Its perimeter is 40 cm. Find its length.
2(2x + 1) + 2(x - 2) = 40
4x + 2 + 2x - 4 = 40, so 6x - 2 = 40
6x = 42, x = 7: length 2 × 7 + 1 = 15 cm
4
The two equal sides of an isosceles triangle are (3y - 4) cm and (y + 10) cm. Find y and the length of each equal side.
3y - 4 = y + 10
2y = 14, so y = 7
Each equal side: 7 + 10 = 17 cm
5
A quadrilateral has angles 2x, 3x, (x + 40) and 80 degrees. Find the largest angle.
2x + 3x + (x + 40) + 80 = 360
6x + 120 = 360, so 6x = 240
x = 40: largest angle 3 × 40 = 120°
EXT
Four angles around a point are 3a, (2a + 15), (a + 45) and 90 degrees. Find the largest angle.
3a + (2a + 15) + (a + 45) + 90 = 360
6a + 150 = 360, so 6a = 210 and a = 35
Largest: 3 × 35 = 105°
