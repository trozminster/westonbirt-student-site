Solving Quadratics by Factorising

A quadratic equation can have two answers, and factorising finds both at once. It is how a height, a length or a time is found from a quadratic.

By the end you’ll know…
  • If a product of two factors is zero then at least one factor is zero, so (x+p)(x+q)=0 gives x=−p or x=−q.
  • A quadratic equation must therefore be rearranged so that one side is zero before it is factorised.
  • A quadratic equation usually has two solutions, and both are stated.

A product of zero

If two numbers multiply to 0, one of them is 0
So if (x−2)(x+5)=0, one bracket is 0

If two numbers multiply to make 0, at least one of them is 0
So in (x−2)(x+5)=0, one of the two brackets must be 0

Two solutions, both stated

Each bracket gives its own solution. So x−2=0 gives x=2
And x+5=0 gives x=−5

Each bracket gives its own solution, so a quadratic equation usually has two. Adding 2 to both sides of x−2=0 gives x=2, and subtracting 5 from both sides of x+5=0 gives x=−5

One side must be zero

The rule works only when one side is 0

The rule works only when one side is 0, because only a zero product forces a factor to be 0
So x2+3x=10 is rearranged first, by subtracting 10 from both sides.
