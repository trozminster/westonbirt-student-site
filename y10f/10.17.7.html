Symmetry and the Turning Point

The turning point is the highest a ball goes or the lowest a cable sags. Today we find it, and use the curve's symmetry to find other points.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A parabola is symmetrical about a vertical line through its turning point, so points at equal horizontal distances either side have equal y values.
  • The turning point is the minimum of a valley-shaped curve or the maximum of a hill-shaped one.
  • When the curve crosses the x-axis at two roots, the line of symmetry lies exactly halfway between them.

The line of symmetry

A parabola is symmetrical about a vertical line through its turning point. For y =x2−4x+3, that line is x=2

A parabola is symmetrical about a vertical line through its turning point. For y =x2−4x+3, the line is x=2 and the turning point is (2,−1).

The turning point is the lowest point of a valley or the highest point of a hill. Its coordinates are read from the graph.

Halfway between the roots

The line of symmetry is halfway between the two roots. For roots −1 and 3, it is x=1

When a curve crosses the x-axis twice, its line of symmetry is exactly halfway between the two roots. For roots −1 and 3, it is x =(−1+3)÷2 =1

A second point from the first

Points the same distance either side of the line have the same y. With the line x=4, (2,5) matches (6,5).

Points the same distance either side of the line of symmetry have the same y value. With the line x=4, the point (2,5) is matched by (6,5).
