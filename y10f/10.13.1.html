Calculating with Decimals

A calculator does the arithmetic in the exam, but a wrong key press gives a wrong answer with confidence. Knowing where the point belongs is how you catch it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • To add or subtract decimals the digits are aligned by place value, so the decimal points line up.
  • To multiply decimals, multiply as if there were no points and then place the point so that the answer has as many decimal places as the two numbers together.
  • To divide by a decimal, multiply both numbers by the same power of 10 until the divisor is a whole number; the quotient is unchanged.
By the end you can
G3Add, subtract, multiply and divide decimals, checking each answer by estimating with values rounded to 1 significant figure.
G4Solve a multi-step money or measure problem involving decimals and give the answer to a sensible degree of accuracy.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Line up the points

Line up the decimal points to add or subtract. Each digit then sits in its own column. A zero in a gap keeps the columns straight.

Decimals are added and subtracted with their points lined up, so each digit sits in its own place value column. A zero written in a gap keeps the columns straight.

Count the decimal places

To multiply decimals, multiply them as whole numbers first. Then the answer has as many decimal places as the two numbers together.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Work out 12.56 + 3.4 - 0.75.
Line up the points12.56 + 3.40 = 15.96
Subtract 0.7515.96 - 0.75 = 15.21
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Work out 2.4 × 0.35, and check it with an estimate.
Multiply as whole numbers24 × 35 = 840
Count the decimal places1 + 2 = 3
Place the point0.840
= 0.84
Check2 × 0.4 = 0.8

Make the divisor whole

To divide by a decimal, make the divisor whole. Multiply both numbers by the same power of 10. The answer does not change.

To divide by a decimal, multiply both numbers by the same power of 10 until the divisor is whole. The answer does not change, because both numbers are scaled alike.

Round only at the end

In a money or measure problem, keep the full value until the last step. Then round to a sensible accuracy, such as the nearest penny.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
