Percentage Change and Reverse Percentages

Prices rise, sales cut them and wages go up by a percentage every year. One multiplier handles every one of these, forwards and backwards.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A percentage is a number of parts per 100, so 15% of an amount is (15÷100) x that amount.
  • An increase of p% multiplies by (100+p)÷100 and a decrease of p% multiplies by (100−p)÷100; that factor is the multiplier.
  • Percentage change = (change / original amount) × 100, and the original amount is always the denominator.
  • In a reverse percentage the amount given is the amount AFTER the change, so the original is found by dividing by the multiplier.

The amount given is after

In a reverse percentage the amount given is the amount after the change. After a 20% rise, £60 is 120% of the original, not 100% of it.

Undo the multiplier

The original × the multiplier = the new amount. So the original = the new amount ÷ the multiplier. Multiply back to check.
