Topic 10.11 · Volume and Surface Area · Lesson 3 of 3

Working Backwards from Volume

A fish tank must hold a set volume, and an engineer has to choose the height that gives it. Working backwards from a volume finds the missing length.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A compound solid is split into prisms and cylinders whose volumes are added, or treated as a larger solid with a piece removed.
  • When the volume is known and a length is not, the volume formula is treated as an equation and the unknown length is found by rearranging it.
  • Undoing a squared radius needs a square root, so a cylinder's radius is the square root of the whole of V divided by pi x h.
  • The answer is checked by substituting the length back into the volume formula.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Split into prisms and add

A compound solid is split into prisms whose volumes are added. Each part's own lengths are used, so no piece is counted twice.

Or subtract the piece

A solid with a piece removed is the whole solid minus the piece. Subtract the piece's volume, never its lengths.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the volume of this solid. Split it into two cuboids: the bottom one 12 × 3 × 6 = 216 and the top one 4 × 5 × 6 = 120.
Volume216 + 120 = 336 cm³
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A cuboid 10 cm by 8 cm by 5 cm has a 4 cm by 3 cm hole cut right through its 5 cm depth. Find the volume left.
Whole minus holeV = 10 × 8 × 5 - 4 × 3 × 5
= 400 - 60
Volume leftV = 340 cm³
YOUR TURN · a
A cuboid 8 cm by 5 cm by 2 cm has a cube of edge 2 cm on top. Find the volume of the solid.
80 + 8 = 88 cm³
YOUR TURN · b
A cylinder of radius 5 cm and height 10 cm has a cylinder of radius 2 cm drilled through it. Find the volume left, to 1 d.p.
π × 5² × 10 - π × 2² × 10 = 659.7 cm³

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The formula becomes an equation

If the volume is known, the volume formula becomes an equation. Solve it for the missing length. Check by putting the length back in.

When the volume is known and a length is not, the volume formula becomes an equation. Solve it for the length, then check by putting the length back in.

Divide first, square root last

For a cylinder, divide V by π × h to get r². Then square root the whole of V ÷ (π × h).

For a cylinder, divide V by π × h to get r², then take the square root. The root is taken of the whole of V ÷ (π × h).
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A cuboid has volume 360 cm³, length 8 cm and width 5 cm. Find its height.
Put in the values8 × 5 × h = 360
Simplify40h = 360
Divide both sides by 40h = 9
Height9 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A cylinder has volume 500 cm³ and height 10 cm. Find its radius to 1 d.p.
Put in the valuesπ × r² × 10 = 500
Divide both sides by 10πr² = 500 ÷ 10π
r² = 15.91...
Square rootr = 3.989...
Round to 1 d.p.r = 4.0 cm

YOUR TURN · d
