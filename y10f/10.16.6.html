Chords and the Centre

An archaeologist with a fragment of a round plate can find the size of the whole plate. Chords of a circle point the way back to its hidden centre.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The line from the centre of a circle that is perpendicular to a chord bisects that chord, and the converse also holds: the line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it.
  • The perpendicular bisector of any chord therefore passes through the centre.
  • A radius, half the chord and the perpendicular distance from the centre to the chord form a right-angled triangle.
By the end you can
G4Calculate the length of a chord, or the distance of a chord from the centre, using the right-angled triangle the perpendicular from the centre creates.
G5Locate the centre of a circle by constructing the perpendicular bisectors of two chords.

The perpendicular halves the chord

A perpendicular from the centre cuts a chord in half. A line from the centre to a chord's midpoint meets it at right angles.

A line from the centre at right angles to a chord cuts the chord in half. And the line from the centre to a chord's midpoint meets it at right angles.

A right-angled triangle inside

The radius OA, half the chord AM and the distance OM form a right-angled triangle. So OA2=AM2+OM2, by Pythagoras.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the length of the chord AB.
OA is a radius, 17 cm, and OM is 8 cm. AM is a shorter side of the right-angled triangle, and AB is twice AM.
AM=172−82=15
Length of AB: 2×172−82=30 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Find the distance OM from O to the chord.
AB=24 cm, so AM is half of it, 12 cm. OA is a radius, 13 cm.
Distance OM132−122=5 cm

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A chord's bisector meets O

The perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the centre of the circle. So the centre lies somewhere on it.

Two chords fix the centre

Draw two chords that are not parallel and construct the perpendicular bisector of each. The centre is where the two bisectors cross.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the centre of the circle.
Bisectconstruct the perpendicular bisector of PQ with equal arcs
Bisect againconstruct the perpendicular bisector of RS
Centrewhere the two bisectors cross
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the centre of the circle through A, B and C.
Bisectconstruct the perpendicular bisector of AB
Bisect againconstruct the perpendicular bisector of BC
Centrewhere they cross
set the compasses to reach A and draw the circle, which passes through B and C
YOUR TURN · c
The perpendicular bisectors of two chords of a circle cross at (3,4). What is that point?
The centre of the circle
YOUR TURN · d
Why must the two chords used to find the centre not be parallel?
Their bisectors would be parallel too, and would never cross
