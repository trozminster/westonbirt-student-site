Tangents and the Radius

A bicycle wheel meets the road at a single point, and the spoke to that point stands straight up from the road. That right angle between a tangent and a radius unlocks a whole family of problems.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of contact, so the angle between them is 90°.
  • The two tangents drawn to a circle from a point outside it are equal in length.
  • Those two tangents, the two radii to their points of contact and the line from the centre to the external point form a pair of congruent right-angled triangles, symmetric about that line.
By the end you can
G4Calculate a missing angle or length in a diagram containing a tangent and a radius.

A tangent meets the radius at 90°

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to its point of contact. So the angle between them is always 90°.

A right-angled triangle appears

Join the centre to a point on the tangent. The triangle has its right angle at the point of contact. Angle facts or Pythagoras then find what is missing.

Joining the centre to a point on the tangent makes a triangle with its right angle at the point of contact. Angle facts or Pythagoras then find what is missing.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Two tangents from one point

From a point outside a circle exactly two tangents can be drawn. They are equal in length.

Two mirror-image triangles

The radii and OP make two congruent right-angled triangles. They mirror each other in OP.

The radii to the two points of contact and the line OP make two congruent right-angled triangles. They mirror each other in OP, so an angle or a length on one side matches the other.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
