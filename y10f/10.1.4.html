HCF and LCM

HCF and LCM aren't just list-and-circle exercises - they answer real questions like 'what's the biggest equal group I can make' and 'when do two repeating events line up again'.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two numbers is the largest number that divides into both exactly; the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that both numbers divide into exactly.
  • Using prime factorisation, the HCF is the product of the primes common to both numbers, each raised to the lower of the two powers; the LCM is the product of every prime appearing in either number, each raised to the higher of the two powers.

The HCF is the largest number dividing into both exactly. The LCM is the smallest number both divide into exactly. List factors for the HCF, multiples for the LCM.

The Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two numbers is the largest number that divides into both exactly. The Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that both numbers divide into exactly. Both can be found by listing factors (for HCF) or multiples (for LCM) and picking out the ones the two numbers share.

Write both numbers as products of primes. The HCF multiplies the primes in the overlap. The LCM multiplies every prime in the diagram.

Prime factorisation gives a faster route to HCF and LCM for larger numbers. Write both numbers as products of primes. The HCF is the product of the primes common to both, each raised to the lower of the two powers. The LCM is the product of every prime that appears in either number, each raised to the higher of the two powers.
