Compound Interest and Depreciation

Savings grow, loans cost more the longer they run, and a new car loses value every year. Each of these is a percentage change repeated.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Compound interest is calculated on the amount at the start of each period, including interest already added, so the multiplier is applied once per period.
  • After n periods at r% per period, amount = starting amount x ((100 + r) ÷ 100)ⁿ.
  • Depreciation is the same calculation with a decrease, so the multiplier is (100 - r) ÷ 100.
  • Simple interest, by contrast, adds the same amount each period and is not compounded.
By the end you can
G4Calculate the value of an investment or of a depreciating asset after a given number of periods using a repeated multiplier.
G5Compare two savings or borrowing offers by calculating the total amount each produces over the same period.

Interest on the interest

Each year's interest is worked on the amount so far, interest included. So the multiplier is used once for every year. Here, £1000 at 5% is × 1.05 each year.

Compound interest is worked out on the amount at the start of each year, including interest already added. So the multiplier is used once for every year.

One power for n years

After n years at r%, multiply the start by ((100 + r) ÷ 100)ⁿ. Depreciation uses (100 − r) ÷ 100 instead.

After n years at r%, the amount is the start × ((100 + r) ÷ 100)ⁿ. Depreciation uses (100 − r) ÷ 100 instead.

Compare over the same time

Two offers are compared by the total each gives over the same period. On £1000, 5% simple can still lose to 4.5% compound over enough years.

The interest or the total

Read whether the question asks for the interest or the total amount. The interest is the total minus the amount first invested.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
