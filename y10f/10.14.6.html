Symmetry in Two Dimensions

Logos, snowflakes, flags and tiles are designed with symmetry because the eye reads it at once. Today we count both kinds of symmetry, and use them to finish a design.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A line of symmetry is a line about which a shape reflects onto itself.
  • The order of rotational symmetry is the number of positions, within one full turn, in which a shape looks the same; every shape has order at least 1
  • A parallelogram has no lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 2, so the two kinds of symmetry are independent of each other.

Learn

A line of symmetry

A line of symmetry is a mirror line that reflects a shape exactly onto itself. A rectangle has two, through the middles of its sides.

The order of rotational symmetry

The order is how many times a shape fits onto itself in one full turn. Every shape fits at the full turn, so the order is at least 1

The order is the number of positions in one full turn where the shape looks exactly the same. Every shape fits at the full turn, so every order is at least 1

Two separate kinds

A parallelogram has rotational symmetry of order 2
It has no line of symmetry. One kind does not bring the other.

A parallelogram looks the same after a half turn, so its order is 2, yet it has no line of symmetry. One kind of symmetry does not bring the other.
WORKED EXAMPLE A

Fold it in half down the middle: the halves match, so there is 1 line. Turn it: it only fits again at the full turn, so the order is 1

Reflect each corner

To complete a shape about a line of symmetry, reflect each corner in the line. Each new corner is the same distance from the line, on the other side.

Join the new corners

Join the reflected corners in the same order as the originals. Here, the finished shape is a kite.

Join the reflected corners in the same order as the originals. Here (1,4) reflects to (7,4), and the finished shape is a kite with one line of symmetry.

Complete for a rotational order

For order 2, turn the given part through a half turn about the centre. For order 4, turn it through each quarter turn.
