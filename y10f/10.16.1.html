Constructing Triangles Accurately

Architects, engineers and surveyors draw shapes that must be exact, with no guessing by eye. A ruler, a protractor and compasses are enough to draw a triangle exactly.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A triangle is determined by three sides (SSS), by two sides and the angle between them (SAS), or by two angles and a side (ASA).
  • A side is drawn with a ruler to the nearest millimetre, an angle is set with a protractor to the nearest degree, and a length from a point is marked with compasses as an arc.
  • Construction arcs are evidence of the method and are always left on the diagram.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One side, two arcs, then join

Start with the base, drawn with a ruler. Draw an arc from each end, set to the other two lengths. The arcs cross at the third corner.

An accurate triangle starts from one side, drawn with a ruler as the base. Arcs set to the other two lengths, one from each end, cross at the third corner. Measure the base from zero. Set the compasses to the second length and draw an arc from A; set them to the third length and draw an arc from B. Join the crossing point to A and to B to finish the triangle. The arcs stay on the drawing as evidence of the method.

Two sides and the angle between

Draw one side, then set the angle at one end. Measure the second side along that arm. The angle must lie between the two given sides.

Draw one side, set the angle at one end with a protractor, and measure the second side along that arm. The angle must be the one between the two given sides.

Two angles and a side

Then measure what was not given

Measure any unstated side or angle on the finished drawing. The answer is only as accurate as the drawing.

Once the triangle is drawn, measure an unstated side with a ruler, or an unstated angle with a protractor. The answer is only as accurate as the drawing.
