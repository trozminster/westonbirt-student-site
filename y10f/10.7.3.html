Angles in Triangles and Quadrilaterals

A roof truss, a kite and a road sign are built from triangles and quadrilaterals. Their angles always add to the same totals, so a missing angle can always be found.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The three angles of a triangle add to 180° and the four angles of a quadrilateral add to 360°.
  • An isosceles triangle has two equal sides, and the two angles opposite those sides are equal; an equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three angles of 60°; a right-angled triangle has one angle of 90°, so its other two add to 90°.
  • The exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior angles not next to it.

A triangle is half a turn

The three angles of a triangle add to 180°, the same as a straight line.

The three angles of a triangle add to 180°, the same as a straight line. Tear the three corners off a paper triangle and they fit together along a straight line.

A quadrilateral is two triangles

A diagonal splits any quadrilateral into two triangles. So its four angles add to 2×180°=360°.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Equal sides face equal angles

In an isosceles triangle the two angles opposite the equal sides are equal. Tick marks, not appearance, say which sides are equal.

The exterior angle

An exterior angle equals the two interior angles not next to it, added.

An exterior angle of a triangle equals the two interior angles not next to it, added together. The exterior angle and the third interior angle add to 180° on a straight line. The three interior angles also add to 180°, so the exterior angle must equal the other two added.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
An isosceles triangle has 40° between its equal sides. Find each base angle.
Angles in a triangle add up to 180°180−40=140°
Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equalb=140÷2
=70°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Interior angles of 55° and 72° are not next to exterior angle e. Find e.
Exterior angle is the sum of the opposite interior anglese=55+72
=127°
