Speed-Time Graphs

A speed-time graph looks like a distance-time graph, but it tells a different story: a flat line is a steady speed, not a stop. Its steepness is how fast the speed is changing - the acceleration.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A speed-time graph shows speed against time, not distance against time.
  • A horizontal line therefore means constant speed, not stationary.
  • A line sloping up means speeding up, and a line sloping down means slowing down.
  • The gradient is the acceleration, in speed units per unit of time; a negative gradient is a deceleration.

What the axes say

A speed-time graph plots speed against time. A horizontal section is a constant speed, not a stop.

Down means slowing, not going back

A line sloping down means slowing down. Reaching the time axis means the speed is zero: stopped.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The gradient is the acceleration

The gradient of a speed-time graph is the change in speed divided by the time taken: the acceleration. Its unit comes from the axes: m/s each second, written m/s²

Slowing down is a negative gradient

A line sloping down has a negative gradient: a deceleration. Steeper means the speed changes faster, not that the object is faster.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the acceleration in the first stage.
Read the stagefrom 0 to 24 m/s in 8 seconds
Acceleration24÷8=3 m/s²
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the deceleration in the second stage.
Read the stagefrom 20 m/s down to 4 m/s in 8 seconds
The change in speed is 20−4, not 20
Deceleration(20−4)÷8=2 m/s²

A stage rises from (0,0) to (5,15), with time in seconds and speed in m/s. Find the acceleration.
15÷5=3 m/s²
YOUR TURN · d
A later stage falls from (20,16) to (28,6). Find the deceleration.
(16−6)÷(28−20) =10÷8 =1.25 m/s²
