Topic 10.8 · Forming and Solving Equations · Lesson 2 of 5

Equations with Brackets and Fractions

Real problems rarely arrive as neat two-step equations. A bracket or a fraction is one more wrapper to take off before last lesson's steps.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A bracket in an equation is expanded first, because a(b+c) =ab+ac, and the sign in front of a term travels with it.
  • Like terms are collected on each side before the equation is undone.
  • An equation with the whole of one side written over a number is cleared by multiplying both sides by that number.
  • The number multiplying the letter may be a fraction, and the equation is undone in exactly the same way.
By the end you can

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Expand the bracket first

A number outside a bracket multiplies every term inside. The sign in front of a term travels with it. So 7(x+3) =7x+21

A number outside a bracket multiplies every term inside it, so a(b+c) =ab+ac. The sign in front of a term travels with it.

Then solve as before

Once the bracket has gone, the equation is an ordinary two-step equation. Like terms on each side are collected before anything is undone.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Clear the fraction first

When a whole side is over a number, multiply both sides by it. The fraction line disappears.

When a whole side is written over a number, both sides are multiplied by that number. The fraction line disappears and an ordinary equation is left.

A fraction in front of the letter

The number multiplying the letter can be a fraction, as in 3x4=9
It is undone the same way, by multiplying by 4 and then dividing by 3
