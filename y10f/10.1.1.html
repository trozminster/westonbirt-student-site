Topic 10.1 · Number Foundations · Lesson 1 of 6

Place Value and Powers of 10

Every price, measurement and exam mark you'll meet this year depends on reading place value correctly - get it wrong here and every calculation downstream inherits the error.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A digit's value comes from its place-value column, not just which digit it is.
  • To compare two numbers, line up place values and compare digit by digit from the most significant place - the first place that differs decides which number is larger; for negative numbers, the value further from zero is the smaller one.
  • Multiplying by 10 shifts every digit one place-value column to the left; dividing by 10 shifts every digit one column to the right - the decimal point doesn't move, the digits move around it.

Learn

Compare numbers place by place, from the largest place. The first place that differs decides which is larger. For negatives, the one further below zero is smaller.

A number's value is set by the position of each digit, not just which digits appear. To compare two numbers, line up the place values and compare digit by digit from the most significant place; the first place where they differ decides which number is larger. For negative numbers this flips: the value further from zero in the negative direction is the smaller number.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Multiplying by 10 moves every digit one place left. Dividing by 10 moves every digit one place right. The decimal point stays still.

Multiplying by 10 moves every digit one place value column to the left; dividing by 10 moves every digit one place value column to the right. The decimal point doesn't move - the digits move around it. Multiplying by 100 or 1000 repeats this shift two or three times; multiplying by 0.1 or 0.01 has the same effect as dividing by 10 or 100

3.6×100
Split the 100=3.6×10×10

Shift 1 place left=360
