Topic 10.10 · Standard Form · Lesson 3 of 3

Calculating in Standard Form

Scientists multiply, divide and add numbers such as the mass of the Earth every day. Working with a and the power separately keeps those calculations short.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Multiplying two numbers in standard form multiplies the values of a and adds the powers of 10; dividing divides the values of a and subtracts the powers.
  • The answer is rewritten afterwards if a is no longer between 1 and 10.
  • Adding or subtracting needs both numbers written with the SAME power of 10 first, or written out in full.
  • Both papers allow a calculator, which has a standard-form key, and its display may show an answer in standard form without writing the x 10 out.
By the end you can
G4Multiply and divide numbers written in standard form, giving the answer in standard form.
G5Add and subtract numbers in standard form, and solve a problem in context whose answer must be rewritten into standard form.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Multiply a, add the powers

Multiplying numbers in standard form multiplies the values of a and adds the powers. Dividing divides the values of a and subtracts the powers.

Rewrite the answer at the end

The answer is rewritten if a is no longer between 1 and 10. So 20 × 10⁵ becomes 2 × 10⁶.

The calculator's own key

The ×10ˣ key enters the power of 10. Key 3 × 10⁵ as 3, ×10ˣ, 5. A display of 6E9 means 6 × 10⁹.

The ×10ˣ key enters the power of 10, so 3 × 10⁵ is keyed as 3, ×10ˣ, 5. A display of 6E9 means 6 × 10⁹. Typing 3, multiply, 10, power, 5 can go wrong once the number sits inside a longer calculation, because you then have to put in the brackets yourself. The standard-form key keeps each number whole.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Match the powers first

Adding or subtracting needs both numbers written with the same power of 10 first. Then the values of a are added, and the power stays as it is.

Or write them out in full

Or write both numbers in full and add. Then write the total back in standard form.

Both numbers can also be written as ordinary numbers, added, and the answer turned back into standard form. So 30,000 + 5,000 = 35,000 = 3.5 × 10⁴.
