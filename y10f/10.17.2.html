Expanding Double Brackets

Two brackets multiplied together make a quadratic, the shape behind a thrown ball or a curved bridge. Today every term meets every term.

By the end you’ll know…
  • To expand (x + a)(x + b) every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second, giving four products.
  • Collecting the two x terms gives x² + (a + b)x + ab, so the x coefficient is the SUM of a and b and the constant is their PRODUCT.
  • (x + a)² means (x + a)(x + a) and expands to x² + 2ax + a²; it is not x² + a².

Four products from two brackets

Every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second. So (x + 3)(x + 5) has four products.

Every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second. So (x + 3)(x + 5) has four products, one in each cell of the grid.

Collect the two middle terms

Collect the two like x terms. (x + 3)(x + 5) = x² + 8x + 15. 8 is 3 + 5, and 15 is 3 × 5.

The two x terms are like terms, so they are collected. (x + 3)(x + 5) = x² + 8x + 15, where 8 is 3 + 5 and 15 is 3 × 5.

A negative in a bracket

The sign travels with its term into every product. So (x + 4)(x - 2) = x² + 2x - 8.

The sign travels with its term into every product. So (x + 4)(x - 2) = x² - 2x + 4x - 8 = x² + 2x - 8.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A squared bracket is two brackets

(x + 3)² means (x + 3)(x + 3): four products. So (x + 3)² = x² + 6x + 9, not x² + 9.

(x + a)² means (x + a)(x + a), so it has four products like any other pair. So (x + 3)² = x² + 6x + 9. x² + 9 is wrong: it misses both 3x terms.

The area of a square

A square of side x + 3 has area (x + 3)². It splits into x², two strips of 3x and 9.

A square of side x + 3 has area (x + 3)². Cut into four pieces, it is x², two strips of 3x and a corner of 9.
