Parts of a Circle

Wheels, clocks, pizzas and round windows are all circles, and each part of a circle has its own name. Naming the parts precisely is what lets us reason about them.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The centre is the point every point of the circle is equidistant from, and that distance is the radius; a diameter is a chord through the centre and is twice the radius.
  • A chord is a straight line joining two points on the circle, and it divides the circle into two segments.
  • A tangent is a straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point.
  • An arc is part of the circumference, and a sector is the region between an arc and two radii.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Centre, radius and diameter

Every point of a circle is the radius away from the centre. A diameter crosses through the centre. It is twice the radius.

Every point of a circle is the same distance from its centre, and that distance is the radius. A diameter is a line across the circle through the centre, twice the radius.

Chord and tangent

A chord joins two points on the circle; a diameter is the longest chord. A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point and does not cross into it.

Arc, sector and segment

An arc is part of the circumference. A sector lies between two radii and an arc. A segment lies between a chord and an arc.

An arc is part of the circumference. A sector is the region between two radii and an arc; a segment is the region between a chord and an arc.
