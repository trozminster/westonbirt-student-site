Regions from Inequalities

On a number line an inequality is a stretch of the line. On a coordinate grid it becomes a whole region, and two or three inequalities together pin down exactly the points that satisfy them all.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A boundary line such as x=2, y=3 or x+y=6 splits a coordinate grid into two regions.
  • An inequality such as x>2 is every point on one side of its boundary line, not just the points on the line.
  • Several inequalities together describe the region where all of them are true at once.
  • A point is in a region exactly when its coordinates make the inequality true, so substituting a point such as (0,0) shows which side is meant.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A line splits the grid in two

The line x=2 splits the grid in two. The region x>2 is every point to its right.

The line x=2 splits the grid into two regions. The inequality x>2 is every point to the right of it, not just the points on the line.

Test a point to find the side

A point is in a region exactly when its coordinates make the inequality true. Substituting (0,0) is the quickest test.

All of them at once

Two or three inequalities together describe the region where every one of them is true. That region is usually labelled R.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Name each boundary first

A region is described by naming each line round its edge. Each boundary line gives one inequality.

The side gives the sign

Above or right of a line is greater than; below or left is less than. For a sloping line, test a point inside R.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Write the two inequalities that describe R.
Boundariesx=2
y=1
R is left of x=2 and above y=1
R: x≤2 and y≥1
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Write the three inequalities that describe R.
Boundariesthe two axes, x=0
y=0
the line x+y=6
Test (1,1)1+1=2, which is less than 6
R: x≥0, y≥0 and x+y≤6
