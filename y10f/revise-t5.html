Exit ticket · 10.5.1
Explain why the height used in an area formula must be perpendicular to the base, and what goes wrong if a slant side is used instead.
The formula measures how far the shape reaches straight out from its base, which is the perpendicular distance; a slant side is longer than that distance, so using it gives an area larger than the shape actually covers.

Exit ticket · 10.5.2
Explain why a semicircle's area is half a circle's area but its perimeter is not half a circle's circumference.
Halving the shape halves the space it covers, so the area halves exactly; but cutting the circle in two creates a straight edge along the diameter that the circle never had, so the perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter.

Exit ticket · 10.5.4
Explain why a missing side is found from a perimeter by subtracting first, but from an area by dividing.
A perimeter is built by adding the sides, so it is unwound by subtracting the known ones; an area is built by multiplying two lengths, so it is unwound by dividing by the length that is known.
