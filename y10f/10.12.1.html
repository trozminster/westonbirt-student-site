Similar Shapes and Scale Factor

A photo printed large, a model car and the real one are the same shape at different sizes. Similarity says exactly what stays the same and what changes.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two shapes are similar when one is an enlargement of the other: corresponding angles are equal and corresponding lengths are all in the same ratio.
  • That ratio is the scale factor k, found by dividing a length on the image by the length it corresponds to on the object.
  • A length on the image =k x the matching length on the object, so k>1 enlarges and k<1 reduces.

One is an enlargement of the other

Two shapes are similar when one is an enlargement of the other. Their angles are equal, and every length is multiplied by the same scale factor.

The scale factor is a division

The scale factor k = image length ÷ matching object length. A k above 1 enlarges. A k below 1 reduces.

The scale factor k is a length on the image divided by the matching length on the object. A k above 1 enlarges and a k below 1 reduces.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Multiply by k, or divide back

An image length is k × the matching object length. To go back, divide by k.

Once k is known, a length on the image is k × the matching length on the object. Going back from the image to the object divides by k.

Two triangles sharing an angle

A line parallel to one side cuts off a similar triangle. The two triangles share an angle. Use the whole side of the large triangle, not just the extra piece.

When a line parallel to one side cuts across a triangle, the small triangle and the large one share an angle and are similar. Use the whole side of the large triangle, not only the extra piece.
