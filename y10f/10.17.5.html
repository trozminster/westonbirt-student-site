Topic 10.17 · Quadratics and Their Graphs · Lesson 5 of 8

Plotting a Quadratic Curve

The graph of a quadratic is a curve, the shape of a fountain's jet or a satellite dish. Today we plot one point by point and learn its shape.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quadratic function contains an x² term and its graph is a smooth curve called a parabola, never a set of straight segments.
  • A positive x² coefficient gives a curve shaped like a valley with a minimum; a negative one gives a hill with a maximum.
  • A table of values gives the y for each x, and squaring a negative x gives a positive result, which is what makes the curve symmetrical.
By the end you can
G4Complete a table of values for a quadratic function and plot its graph as a smooth curve.
G4Recognise from its equation whether a quadratic graph has a minimum or a maximum, and sketch its general shape.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A table of values first

Substitute each x to find y. A negative x squares to a positive. So x = -2 and x = 2 give the same y.

A quadratic such as y = x² - 2 gives one y for each x, found by substituting. Squaring a negative x gives a positive, so x = -2 and x = 2 give the same y.

One smooth curve

The points of a quadratic lie on a smooth curve called a parabola. They are joined with one curve, never with straight segments.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A valley or a hill

A positive x² term gives a valley with a minimum. A negative x² term gives a hill with a maximum.

When the x² term is positive, the curve is a valley with a lowest point, a minimum. When it is negative, the curve is a hill with a highest point, a maximum.

A sketch shows the shape

A sketch shows the shape and where the curve crosses the y-axis, not every point. The curve y = x² + 1 is a valley crossing the y-axis at 1.
