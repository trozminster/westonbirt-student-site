Constructing an Angle Bisector

A path that keeps exactly the same distance from two walls meeting at a corner runs straight down the middle of the angle. Compasses find that line exactly, with no protractor.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The bisector of an angle is the line that divides it into two equal angles.
  • Every point on it is the same distance from both arms of the angle, which is why it is drawn from an arc centred on the vertex and then two equal arcs from where that arc meets the arms.
  • The construction works for any angle and needs no protractor.

Arcs, then join to the vertex

Draw an arc from the vertex across both arms. Draw equal arcs from those two crossings to meet inside. Join the vertex to that meeting point.

An arc from the vertex crosses both arms, and equal arcs from those two crossings meet inside the angle. The line from the vertex through that meeting point is the angle bisector.The two crossings, P and Q, are the same distance from the vertex. Keep one width for the arcs from P and Q; they cross at R. The line OR splits the angle into two equal angles, and no protractor is needed.

Equidistant from two lines

Every point on the bisector of an angle is the same distance from both arms. The distance to a line is measured at right angles to it.

Nearer to one arm

Points on one side of the bisector are nearer to that arm. A region nearer to OA than to OB is shaded between OA and the bisector.
