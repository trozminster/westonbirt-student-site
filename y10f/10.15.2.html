Lengths of Line Segments

A drone flying straight between two points on a map travels further than its moves across and up added together. Pythagoras gives the true straight-line distance.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A line segment drawn on a coordinate grid is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle whose shorter sides are the horizontal and vertical differences between the end points.
  • Those differences are x2−x1 and y2−y1, and a negative difference is used as its positive size, because a length cannot be negative.
  • Pythagoras' theorem then gives length² =(x2−x1)2 +(y2−y1)2
By the end you can
G5Calculate the length of a line segment from the coordinates of its end points, giving the answer to a stated accuracy.
G5Decide whether two line segments on a grid are equal in length by calculating both.

Learn

The segment is a hypotenuse

A line segment on a grid is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle. Its shorter sides are the change across and the change up between its ends.

A length is never negative

Subtract the coordinates to find each change; a negative change squares to a positive number. Then square both changes, add, and take the square root.

Equal lengths need a calculation

Two segments can look different yet be the same length. Calculate both before deciding.

Compare the squared lengths

Compare the squared lengths before taking any root. Rounding first can make equal lengths look different, or different ones look equal.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
