Topic 10.22 · Direct and Inverse Proportion · Lesson 3 of 4

Graphs of Proportion

A graph shows at a glance which kind of proportion is at work, and a conversion graph works like a calculator. Today we read both.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Direct proportion graphs as a straight line through the origin; inverse proportion graphs as a curve that falls steeply and then flattens, approaching but never meeting either axis.
  • A conversion graph is a direct proportion graph used as a calculator: a value is read across to the line and down to the other axis.
  • A reading taken from a graph is an estimate limited by the scale.
By the end you can
G4Identify from its shape whether a graph shows direct proportion, inverse proportion or neither.
G5Read values from a conversion graph in both directions and use them to answer a question in context.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Direct is a straight line

A direct proportion graphs as a straight line through the origin. Its steepness is the multiplier.

Inverse is a falling curve

An inverse proportion graphs as a curve that falls steeply, then flattens. It gets closer and closer to each axis but never meets it.

Neither kind

A straight line that misses the origin shows neither kind of proportion. Nor does a curve that rises.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Read up, then across

A conversion graph is a direct proportion graph used as a calculator. A value is read up to the line and across to the other axis.

Both directions, as estimates

The graph converts both ways, starting from whichever axis holds the value. A reading is an estimate limited by the scale.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Convert £35 to euros.
Read up from £35 to the line, then across
In eurosabout €42
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Convert €54 to pounds.
Read across from €54 to the line, then down
In poundsabout £45
YOUR TURN · c
