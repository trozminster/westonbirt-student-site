Topic 10.4 · Probability, Sets and Two-Way Tables · Lesson 4 of 4

Choosing the Right Representation

Venn diagrams and two-way tables both organise a whole group so that every member is counted once, and they are not interchangeable: each fits a particular shape of problem. Reading the problem's structure before drawing anything is what makes the rest of the question short.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The structure of a problem decides which representation to use: a Venn diagram suits overlapping categories, a two-way table suits two characteristics at once, and a tree diagram suits a sequence of events. Whichever is used, every representation must account for the whole sample space, so its probabilities sum to 1

Let the structure choose

Overlapping categories with an overlap are a Venn diagram. Two characteristics at once are a two-way table. Events one after another are a tree diagram.

What decides the representation is how the information is organised, not which one was met most recently. Overlapping categories that a member can be in both of, neither of, or one only - a Venn diagram holds those, because the overlap is a place on the picture. Two separate characteristics, each of which every member has exactly one value of - year group and travel method, say - are a two-way table, because every member lands in one cell of a grid. A sequence of events happening one after another is a tree diagram. What all three share is that they account for the whole group, so their probabilities sum to 1

Keep to one representation

A circle total and a row total count different things. So a value read from one representation does not carry into the other.

The two representations count the same group in different arrangements, so a value read out of one does not carry into the other unchanged. A Venn diagram's circle total is the overlap plus one outer part; a two-way table's row total is two separate cells. Starting a table and finishing in Venn notation moves numbers between two different meanings of the same word, and the arithmetic gives no warning because every number involved is a genuine count of something.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Finished means every part answered

A question can carry a second demand that uses the first answer. Build the representation once and answer both from it.

A probability question can carry more than one demand, and the second usually depends on the first: find a missing value, then use it; or find one probability, then a related one. The representation is built once and answers both, which is why the count that closes the first part is worth writing on the diagram rather than only in the working. Stopping after the first answer leaves the question half done, and the arithmetic gives no warning because the first answer is correct.

Answer in the form asked for

A probability can be a fraction, a decimal or a percentage. The question decides which form, and the conversion comes last.

A probability is one quantity written three ways - a fraction, a decimal or a percentage - and converting between them changes nothing about how likely the event is. Which form is wanted is part of the question, not a matter of preference, so a fraction left where a percentage was asked for is an unfinished answer. The conversion is the last step and belongs after the probability is settled, not part-way through, where rounding a decimal early corrupts everything built on it.
SURVEY OF TWO APPS
120 people are surveyed about two apps. 54 use app A, 61 use app B, and 22 use both. Find the probability that a person chosen at random uses neither, as a percentage. Then find the probability that a person who uses app A also uses app B, as a fraction in its simplest form.
Step 1: The categories overlap with a stated overlap, so this is a Venn diagram. Step 2: Place the overlap, 22
App A only is 54−22=32, and all 54 app A users sit inside circle A. Find B only: 61−22=39
Step 3: Everyone outside both circles is what is left after circle A (54) and B only (39). Find neither: 120−54−39=27
Check22+32+39+27=120
Step 4: First demand
Put it over 120: P(neither) =27120
As a decimal that is 27÷120=0.225
Convert to %: =22.5%
Step 5: Second demand. The choice is restricted to app A users, so the denominator is n(A)=54, not 120
Step 6: The numerator is those who also use B, which is the overlap: 22
Put 22 over 54: P(uses B)=2254
Divide both parts by 2
Simplify=1127Both demands are now answered, and each is in the form asked for.
