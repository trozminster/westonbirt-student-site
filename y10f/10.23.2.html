Dual and Composite Bar Charts

A survey often splits every answer into two groups, such as boys and girls. Today we draw two kinds of bar chart that show both groups at once.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A dual bar chart places two bars side by side for each category to compare two sets of data, and it needs a key.
  • A composite bar chart stacks the two on top of each other, so the whole bar is the total and each part is a component.
  • A dual chart compares the parts directly; a composite chart compares the totals directly. The question decides which is right.
By the end you can
G3Draw a dual or composite bar chart from a two-way frequency table, with a key.
G4Compare two groups from a dual or composite bar chart and state a conclusion in the context of the data.

Learn

Side by side

A dual bar chart puts two bars side by side for each category. A key says which bar is which group. Then compare the groups category by category.

A dual bar chart puts two bars next to each other for each category, one for each group. A key says which bar is which, so the two groups can be compared category by category.

Stacked into one bar

A composite bar chart stacks one group on top of the other. The whole bar is the total for the category, and each part is one group.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The top part starts higher

The top part of a composite bar starts where the bottom part ends. Its frequency is where it ends take away where it starts.

Choose the chart for the question

A dual chart compares the groups directly, because their bars stand side by side. A composite chart compares the totals directly, because each whole bar is a total.
