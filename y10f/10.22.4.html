Travel Graphs

A distance-time graph tells the story of a journey: where someone went, when they stopped and how fast they moved. Each straight stage is a direct proportion, and its steepness is the speed.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A distance-time graph shows distance from a starting point against time.
  • Its gradient is the speed, in the units of the two axes.
  • A horizontal line means stationary, since time passes but distance does not change.
  • A steeper line means a faster speed, and a line returning to the axis means travelling back to the start.
  • Average speed over a whole journey is total distance divided by total time, including any time spent stopped.

What the axes say

A distance-time graph plots distance from the start against time. Each straight section is one stage of a journey at a steady speed.

Flat means stopped

A horizontal section means stationary: time passes, but distance does not change. A section back down to zero is a return to the start.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The gradient is the speed

The gradient of a distance-time graph is distance divided by time, which is the speed. Its unit comes from the axes: km and hours give km/h.

One speed for each straight stage

A straight stage keeps one speed, like a direct proportion's multiplier. Steeper means faster, and a flat stage means a speed of zero.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the speed for the first stage.
Read the stage30 km in 2 hours
Speed30÷2=15 km/h
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the speed of the return, in km/h.
Read the stage6 km back in 20 minutes
20 minutes is a third of an hour, so in 1 hour it would go 3 times as far
Speed6×3=18 km/h
YOUR TURN · c

45÷3=15 km/h

6 km in 15 minutes, a quarter of an hour, so 6×4=24 km/h
