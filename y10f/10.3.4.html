Harder Graphs: Negative and Fractional Gradients

A gradient is a comparison, and a comparison does not have to come out as a whole number or as a positive one. Lines that fall, and lines that climb slowly, are described by the same definition as every other line - what changes is only what the answer looks like when it arrives.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A gradient can be negative (the line falls left to right) or a fraction (the line rises or falls less steeply than a whole-number gradient) - the same vertical-change-over-horizontal-change definition applies in both cases.

Learn

A negative gradient is a direction

A falling line has a negative vertical change, so its gradient is negative. The size says how steep and the sign says which way.

The definition of gradient never required the answer to be positive. When a line falls from left to right, the vertical change between two points is negative while the horizontal change is positive, so their quotient is negative, and that sign is the part of the answer that records which way the line goes. A gradient of -4 is exactly as steep as a gradient of 4; the size says how steep and the sign says which way, and the two carry different information.

Where the sign is most easily lost

Once the points are off the line, only the subtraction records the direction. If y falls as x rises, the gradient must be negative.

The sign survives the picture more easily than it survives the arithmetic. The direction can be seen on the drawn line; once the two points have been taken off it, the only record of that direction is the subtraction that produced the vertical change. This is why the negative is dropped far more often once the reading has become a calculation, and why the answer is worth reading back against the line itself: if y falls as x rises, the gradient has to be negative.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A fractional gradient is shallow

A line rising less than one unit for each unit across has a fractional gradient. Rounding it to a whole number describes a steeper line.

When a line rises by less than one unit for each unit across, the quotient of the two changes is a fraction, and that fraction is the gradient exactly. Rounding it to the nearest whole number describes a different, steeper line. The fraction is also the clearest reading of the line's behaviour: a gradient of 13 says the line rises one unit for every three across, which is precisely what the picture shows. And a small positive fraction is not the same as a shallow negative gradient - both describe gently changing lines, but only one of them falls.

Simplify, and keep the sign

Simplify a gradient by dividing both parts by a common factor. The sign belongs to the whole fraction and stays.

A gradient read off a line arrives as one number over another, and it is simplified like any fraction - by dividing both parts by a common factor, never by subtracting from them. The sign belongs to the whole fraction and is carried through the simplification unchanged, so a gradient of -6 over 8 simplifies to -34 and stays negative. Written into an equation, the fraction is the coefficient of x like any other number, so it must appear there: leaving it out gives a line of gradient 1.
A FRACTIONAL GRADIENT
Find the gradient and the equation of the line through (2, 3) and (8, 5), whose y-intercept is 73.
Fix the order(2, 3) first, (8, 5) second.
Vertical change5 - 3 = 2. Horizontal change: 8 - 2 = 6.
Gradient26. This does not divide out to a whole number, and that is the answer, not a problem with it. Step 4: Simplify by dividing both parts by 2: 26 = 13. The line rises one unit for every three across.
Step 5: The intercept is 73, so the equation is y = (13)x + 73. The fraction is the coefficient of x and has to be written there.
FALLING AND FRACTIONAL
Now one that is fractional and negative. Find the gradient of the line drawn through (1, 9) and (9, 3). Step 1: Vertical change: 3 - 9 = -6. Horizontal change: 9 - 1 = 8.
Gradient-68. Step 3: Simplify by dividing both parts by 2: -68 = -34. The sign belongs to the whole fraction and is unchanged by simplifying.
Read it backthe line falls three units for every four across, so it is both shallow and falling - which a small negative fraction is exactly the right way to record.
