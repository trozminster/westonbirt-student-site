Topic 10.9 · Pythagoras' Theorem · Lesson 3 of 3

Pythagoras in Context

Sat-navs, ladders and TV screens all hide right-angled triangles. The skill is seeing the triangle when nobody has drawn it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A context question rarely draws the right-angled triangle, so it is sketched first, with the right angle marked and the three sides labelled.
  • A ladder against a wall, a diagonal across a rectangle, and the straight-line distance between two points on a grid are all right-angled triangles.
  • The answer is rounded only at the end, and its units are the units of the lengths given.
By the end you can
G5Solve a practical problem by identifying a right-angled triangle within it and applying Pythagoras' theorem.

Sketch the triangle first

A context question rarely draws the triangle. Sketch it with the right angle marked. Then place each length on its side.

A context question rarely draws the triangle, so it is sketched with the right angle marked. Then each length is placed on its side.

Which side is the hypotenuse?

The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse, whatever the story calls it. A leaning ladder, a diagonal and a straight-line path are all hypotenuses.

WORKED EXAMPLE A

Distanced=10 km

The diagonal of a rectangle

A diagonal splits a rectangle into two right-angled triangles. The length and width are the shorter sides, and the diagonal is the hypotenuse.

The distance between two points

The straight line between two points is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle. Its shorter sides are the differences across and up.
