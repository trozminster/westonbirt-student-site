Topic 10.10 · Standard Form · Lesson 2 of 3

Ordering and Rewriting Standard Form

A virus, a grain of sand and a planet can all be written in standard form. To say which is bigger, you need to read the power first.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two numbers in standard form are compared by their powers of 10 first, and only by the value of a when the powers are equal.
  • A number such as 34 × 10⁵ is not in standard form, because 34 is not less than 10; it is rewritten as 3.4 × 10⁶.
  • The power goes up by one for each place the decimal point moves left, and down by one for each place it moves right, so 0.62 × 10⁴ = 6.2 × 10³.
By the end you can
G4Rewrite a number given as a × 10ⁿ, where a is not between 1 and 10, into correct standard form.

When a is 10 or more

In 34 × 10⁵, a is too big. Writing 34 as 3.4 × 10 adds one to the power. So 34 × 10⁵ = 3.4 × 10⁶.

34 × 10⁵ is not in standard form, because 34 is not less than 10. Writing 34 as 3.4 × 10 adds one to the power, so 34 × 10⁵ = 3.4 × 10⁶.

When a is less than 1

When a is less than 1, multiply it by 10. Then take one off the power. So 0.62 × 10⁴ = 6.2 × 10³.

When a is less than 1, multiply it by 10 and take one off the power. The value never changes, so 0.62 × 10⁴ = 6.2 × 10³.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The power decides first

Numbers in standard form are compared by their powers of 10 first. The value of a decides only when the powers are equal.

Negative powers are small

A negative power makes a number between 0 and 1. Order the powers like directed numbers: 10⁻⁶ is smaller than 10⁻².

A negative power makes a number between 0 and 1, so it is below every positive power. The powers are ordered like any directed numbers, so 10⁻⁶ is smaller than 10⁻².
