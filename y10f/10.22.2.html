Topic 10.22 · Direct and Inverse Proportion · Lesson 2 of 3

Inverse Proportion in Context

More workers finish a job sooner, and a faster car arrives earlier. Today we meet inverse proportion, where one quantity rises as the other falls.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two quantities are in inverse proportion when doubling one halves the other, so their PRODUCT stays the same rather than their ratio.
  • Six workers taking 10 days and twelve workers taking 5 days is inverse proportion, because 6 × 10 = 12 × 5.
  • The constant product is found from one complete pair and then used to find any missing value.
By the end you can
G5Decide from a table or a context whether two quantities are in direct or in inverse proportion, giving a reason.
G5Find a missing value in an inverse proportion by using the constant product.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Double one, halve the other

In inverse proportion, doubling one quantity halves the other. So the product of each pair stays the same.

Direct or inverse

In direct proportion both quantities rise together and their ratio is fixed. In inverse proportion one falls as the other rises, and their product is fixed.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Is this direct or inverse proportion: 2 to 30, 3 to 20 and 5 to 12?
Multiply the first pair2 × 30 = 60
Multiply the second3 × 20 = 60
Multiply the third5 × 12 = 60
Direct or inverseinverse, as every product is 60
WORKED EXAMPLE B

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One pair gives the product

One complete pair gives the constant product. Every other pair must make the same product.

Divide for the missing value

The missing value is the constant product divided by the known value. More workers must mean fewer days, which checks the answer.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
5 painters take 12 days to paint a school. How long would 6 painters take?
Total work5 × 12 = 60 painter-days
Time for 6 painters60 ÷ 6 = 10 days
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A journey takes 3 hours at 40 mph. How long does it take at 60 mph?
Distance3 × 40 = 120 miles
Time at 60 mph120 ÷ 60 = 2 hours
