Volume of Prisms and Cylinders

A drinks company needs to know how much a can holds, and a builder how much concrete fills a trench. Both are volumes of prisms.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The volume of a prism is the area of its cross-section multiplied by its length.
  • A cuboid is a prism whose cross-section is a rectangle, so its volume is length x width x height.
  • A cylinder is a prism whose cross-section is a circle, so its volume is pi x r² × h.
  • Volume is measured in cubic units, and 1 litre = 1000 cm³.

Cross-section times length

A prism's volume is its cross-section area times its length. For a cuboid, that is length × width × height.

The volume of a prism is the area of its cross-section times its length. A cuboid is a prism with a rectangle as its cross-section, so its volume is length × width × height.

Volume counts cubes

Volume is measured in cubic units, such as cm³ and m³. A cube 10 cm on each side holds 1000 cm³, which is 1 litre.

WORKED EXAMPLE A

Volume160 cm³

A cylinder is a prism too

A cylinder is a prism with a circle as its cross-section. So V = π × r² × h.

A cylinder is a prism whose cross-section is a circle. So its volume is the circle's area times the height, V = π × r² × h.

Square the radius, not the diameter

Only the radius is squared, before multiplying by π. From a diameter of 10 cm, halve it first: π × 5² = 78.5 cm².

WORKED EXAMPLE C

Multiply by 950.26... × 9 = 452.38...
Round452.4 cm³
