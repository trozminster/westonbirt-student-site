Recurring and Terminating Decimals

A calculator shows 1÷3 as 0.3333333333 and has to stop somewhere. Knowing which fractions stop and which never do tells you when the display is exact.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A terminating decimal stops; a recurring decimal repeats the same block of digits for ever.
  • The recurring block is marked with a dot over its first digit and a dot over its last, so 0.4444... is 0.4 dotted and 0.323232... is 0.32 dotted at both ends.
  • A fraction in its simplest form terminates exactly when the only prime factors of its denominator are 2 and 5; otherwise it recurs.
  • Every terminating decimal is itself a fraction: 0.65=65100=1320
By the end you can
G3Convert a fraction to a decimal on a calculator and state whether it terminates or recurs, writing any recurring block in dot notation.
G4Predict from the prime factors of its denominator whether a fraction in its simplest form terminates or recurs, and check the prediction.

Only 2s and 5s stop

A fraction in simplest form terminates only if its denominator's primes are 2s and 5s. Any other prime factor, such as 3 or 7, makes it recur.

A fraction in its simplest form terminates exactly when its denominator has no prime factors but 2 and 5
