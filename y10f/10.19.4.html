Topic 10.19 · Trigonometry and Pythagoras · Lesson 4 of 4

Choosing Pythagoras or Trigonometry

Every right-angled triangle problem is either Pythagoras or trigonometry, and some need both. Today we learn to choose.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Pythagoras' theorem is used when three SIDES are involved and no angle; a trigonometric ratio is used when an angle is involved as well as sides.
  • The choice is made by listing what the diagram gives and what it asks for.
  • A problem may need both in turn: a side found by Pythagoras' theorem can then be used in a ratio, and an angle found from a ratio can then be used elsewhere.
  • Because both papers allow a calculator, answers are given to a stated accuracy rather than as exact values.
By the end you can
G5Decide, for a right-angled triangle problem, whether Pythagoras' theorem or a trigonometric ratio is needed, and say why.
G5Solve a two-dimensional problem, including one set with a bearing, that needs Pythagoras' theorem and a trigonometric ratio in turn.

Sides only, or an angle too

Pythagoras' theorem links three sides and no angle. A trigonometric ratio links an angle and two sides.

List what is given and wanted

The choice is made by listing what the diagram gives and what it asks for. If an angle is in the list, the method is trigonometry.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
The shorter sides of a right-angled triangle are 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
Two sides and no angle, so Pythagoras.
Square and addh² = 9² + 12²
Square eachh² = 81 + 144
h² = 225
Square rooth = 15 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE B
The hypotenuse is 15 cm and one angle is 28°. Find the side opposite the 28° angle.
An angle is given, so trigonometry, with sin.
Write the ratiosin 28° = x15
Multiply both sides by 15x = 15 × sin 28°
x = 7.042...
Round to 1 d.p.x = 7.0 cm
YOUR TURN · a
A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 10 cm and one side 7 cm. Which method finds the third side, and why?
Pythagoras: three sides, no angle; √(100 - 49) = 7.1 cm
YOUR TURN · b
In the same triangle, which method finds the angle opposite the 7 cm side?
Trigonometry: sin x = 7 ÷ 10, so x = 44.4°

One method, then the other

Some problems need both methods, one after the other. A side found by Pythagoras' theorem can then be used in a ratio.

A distance and a bearing

A journey east and then north makes a right-angled triangle against the north line. Pythagoras' theorem gives the distance, and tan gives the angle for the bearing.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A rectangle is 8 cm by 6 cm. Find d, then x.
Square and addd² = 8² + 6²
d² = 100
Square rootd = 10 cm
Write the ratiotan x = 68
Use inverse tanx = tan⁻¹ (68)
x = 36.869...°
Round to 1 d.p.x = 36.9°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A walker goes 5 km east, then 12 km north. Find d and x.
Square and addd² = 5² + 12²
d² = 169
Square rootd = 13 km
Write the ratiotan x = 512
Use inverse tanx = 22.619...°
Bearing023°
YOUR TURN · c
A rectangle is 12 cm long and 5 cm wide. Find its diagonal, then the angle the diagonal makes with the long side.
√169 = 13 cm; tan x = 5 ÷ 12, x = 22.6°
YOUR TURN · d

√100 = 10 km; tan x = 8 ÷ 6 from north, x = 53.1°: bearing 053°
