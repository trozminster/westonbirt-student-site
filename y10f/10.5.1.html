Topic 10.5 · Area and Perimeter · Lesson 1 of 4

Areas of Triangles and Quadrilaterals

Every straight-sided field, floor and sheet of material is measured by the same small set of rules, and each of those rules is a rectangle rearranged. Knowing which measurement the rule needs is what separates a right answer from a plausible one.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The area of a triangle is half the base times the perpendicular height; the area of a parallelogram is base times perpendicular height; the area of a trapezium is half the sum of the parallel sides times the height. In each case the height used must be perpendicular to the chosen base, not a slant side.

Three formulas, one idea

Each shape rearranges into a rectangle, which is where its formula comes from. A triangle is half a parallelogram, and a trapezium averages its two parallel sides.

Each of these shapes can be turned into a rectangle without changing how much space it covers, and that is where its formula comes from. A parallelogram becomes a rectangle when a triangle is cut from one end and moved to the other, so its area is base times height. A triangle is half a parallelogram cut along a diagonal, so its area is halved. A trapezium is what remains when the two parallel sides are averaged, so half their sum takes the place of a single base. The formulas are therefore not three separate facts to be remembered but one fact seen from three positions.

The height a picture can hide

The height is the perpendicular distance from the base, never the slant side. A slant side is longer, so it makes the area too large.

In every one of these formulas the height means the perpendicular distance between the base and the side opposite it, and never the slant side that happens to be drawn. A slant side is always longer than the perpendicular height, so using it makes the area too large. This matters most when a shape is drawn tilted, because the perpendicular height then runs through the inside of the figure rather than along an edge, and the only measurement on the drawing that is on an edge is the wrong one. The base and its own height belong together as a pair: change which side is taken as the base and the height changes with it.

Learn

Compound shapes borrow formulas

A compound shape is split into shapes that have formulas. Draw the splitting lines first, so no piece is counted twice or missed.

A compound shape has no area formula of its own, so it is treated as a collection of shapes that do. It can be split into pieces whose areas are added, or seen as a larger shape with a piece taken away, and both routes give the same answer because area is conserved however the figure is cut. The choice between them is a matter of which produces the fewer measurements to find. What must not happen is a piece being counted twice, or a gap between the pieces being left out, which is why the splitting lines are worth drawing on the figure before any arithmetic begins.

Perimeter counts only the outside

The perimeter is the distance around the outside only. A line drawn inside to split the shape is not part of it.

The perimeter of a compound shape is the distance around its outside edge, so a line drawn inside the figure to split it up is not part of it. That line is a working aid and disappears from the answer. Perimeter and area therefore behave differently under splitting: the areas of the parts add to the whole, but the perimeters of the parts do not, because each part has borrowed an edge that the whole figure does not have. A missing outside length is usually deduced rather than measured, since opposite sides of the surrounding rectangle must agree.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the area of the shape.
Split it at the join: the rectangle is 12×5=60 and the triangle is 12×4÷2=24
The join line is inside, so it is not measured.

1
Explain why the height used in an area formula must be perpendicular to the base, and what goes wrong if a slant side is used instead.
The formula measures how far the shape reaches straight out from its base, which is the perpendicular distance; a slant side is longer than that distance, so using it gives an area larger than the shape actually covers.
