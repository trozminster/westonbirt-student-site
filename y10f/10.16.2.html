Constructing a Perpendicular Bisector

A mobile mast placed fairly between two villages must be the same distance from each. Compasses and a straight edge find every such point exactly, with no measuring.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The perpendicular bisector of a line segment is the line that cuts it in half at right angles.
  • Every point on it is the same distance from both end points, which is why it is drawn with two arcs of equal radius centred on those end points.
  • The radius must be more than half the length of the segment or the arcs do not cross.

Learn: Equal arcs from both ends

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Equal arcs from both ends

Draw equal arcs from A and from B, wider than half of AB. Join the two crossings, P and Q. PQ cuts AB in half at right angles.

Arcs of one width, more than half of AB, are drawn from A and then from B, crossing at P and Q. The line through P and Q is the perpendicular bisector: it cuts AB in half at right angles. Less than half of AB, and the arcs from the two ends would never meet. Keep the same width for both ends. P and Q are each as far from A as from B.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Construct the perpendicular bisector of AB.
Widthset the compasses to more than 3.5 cm, half of AB
Arcsfrom A above and below, then from B at the same width
Joinrule the line through the two crossings, leaving the arcs
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Construct the perpendicular bisector of CD.
Widthmore than half of CD, and the same for both ends
Arcsfrom C on each side of CD, then from D
Jointhe line through the two crossings is at right angles to CD, though it is not upright

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Equidistant from two points

Every point on the perpendicular bisector is as far from A as from B. It is the locus of points equidistant from A and B.

Every point on the perpendicular bisector of AB is the same distance from A as from B. So the bisector is the locus of points equidistant from A and B.

Nearer to one point

Points on A's side of the bisector are nearer to A than to B. A region nearer to A is shaded on A's side of the line.
