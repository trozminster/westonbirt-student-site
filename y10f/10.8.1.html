Solving Simple Equations

An equation is a puzzle with one missing number. Every puzzle of this kind is solved the same way: keep both sides equal while the letter is unwrapped.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation says that two expressions are equal, and solving it means finding the value of the letter that makes that true.
  • Whatever is done to one side of an equation must be done to the other, so that the two sides stay equal.
  • The operations are undone in reverse order: what was done to the letter last is undone first.
  • A solution is checked by substituting it back into the original equation.

Both sides stay equal

An equation says two expressions are equal. Do the same thing to both sides to keep them equal.

An equation says that two expressions are equal. Whatever is done to one side must be done to the other, so they stay equal.

Put it back to check it

A solution is checked by substituting it into the original equation. If both sides give the same number, it is right.

Learn

Undo in reverse order

The operations done to the letter are undone in reverse order. What was done to it last is undone first.

A division is undone by multiplying

A division is undone by multiplying both sides. In x4−2=1, undo the - 2 first, then the ÷ 4

Dividing the letter is undone by multiplying both sides. In x4−2=1 the - 2 is undone first, then the ÷ 4
