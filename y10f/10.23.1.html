Bar Charts and Pictograms

Charts turn a table of numbers into a picture that can be read at a glance. Today we draw and read bar charts and pictograms.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A bar chart shows the frequency of each category as the HEIGHT of a bar, and the bars are equal in width and separated by gaps.
  • The vertical axis starts at zero and is labelled with what is being counted, or the chart misleads.
  • A pictogram shows frequency with repeated symbols, and the key states how many items one whole symbol stands for.
  • Part of a symbol stands for the matching fraction of that number, so half a symbol worth 10 is 5
By the end you can
G1Draw a bar chart from a frequency table with a sensible scale and labelled axes, and a pictogram using a stated key, including part symbols.
G2Read frequencies from a bar chart or a pictogram, including finding a total and the difference between two categories.

Height shows frequency

In a bar chart the height of each bar shows its frequency. The bars are equal in width with equal gaps, and the scale starts at zero.

A pictogram needs its key

A pictogram repeats a symbol to show each frequency. The key says what one whole symbol stands for. Part of a symbol stands for that fraction.

A pictogram shows each frequency with repeated symbols, and the key says what one whole symbol stands for. Part of a symbol stands for that fraction of it.

Learn

Read, then compare

Each frequency is read from the top of its bar across to the scale. A difference subtracts two frequencies, and a total adds them.

Between the gridlines

A bar can end between two gridlines. Judge where it sits on the scale. With steps of 2, halfway between 6 and 8 is 7

A bar can end between two gridlines, and is read by judging where it sits. With a scale in steps of 2, a bar halfway between 6 and 8 shows 7
