Angles on Lines and Points

A straight road, a crossroads and the hands of a clock all hide the same two totals. Every angle calculation this term starts from them.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An acute angle is less than 90 degrees, a right angle is exactly 90 degrees, an obtuse angle is between 90 and 180 degrees, and a reflex angle is more than 180 degrees.
  • Angles on a straight line add to 180 degrees and angles around a point add to 360 degrees.
  • When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other are equal; these are the vertically opposite angles.
By the end you can
G2Calculate a missing angle on a straight line or at a point, showing the subtraction that gives it.
G3Calculate a missing angle in a diagram where two straight lines cross, using vertically opposite angles together with angles on a line.

Angles measure turn

An angle measures turn, and a full turn is 360°. Its type depends only on its size.

An angle measures turn, and a full turn is 360°. Its type depends only on its size. A right angle is exactly 90°. A reflex angle is measured round the outside of the corner, so it is 360° minus the angle inside.

Half a turn and a whole turn

Angles on a straight line add to 180°, half a turn. Angles around a point add to 360°, a whole turn.

Angles on a straight line add to 180°, because a straight line is half a turn. Angles around a point add to 360°, a whole turn.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Two angles on a straight line are 128° and x. Find x.
Angles on a straight line add up to 180°x = 180 - 128
= 52°
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Three angles around a point are 90°, 145° and y. Find y.
Angles around a point add up to 360°y = 360 - 90 - 145
= 125°
YOUR TURN · a
Two angles on a straight line are 67° and x. Find x.
180 - 67 = 113°, so x = 113°
YOUR TURN · b
One angle at a point is 135°. Find the other angle there, and name its type.
360 - 135 = 225°, a reflex angle

Opposite across a crossing

When two straight lines cross, the angles directly opposite each other are equal. These are called vertically opposite angles.

Side by side on a line

Neighbouring angles at a crossing add to 180°. So one angle is enough to find all four.

Two angles next to each other at a crossing lie on a straight line, so they add to 180°. One angle is therefore enough to find all four.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
