Drawing and Reading Pie Charts

A pie chart shows how a whole is shared out, such as how a class travels to school. Today we draw pie charts and read frequencies from them.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A pie chart shows how a whole is shared out, so the whole circle is the total frequency and 360° represents that total.
  • The angle for a category is (frequency / total frequency) × 360°, and the angles must sum to 360°.
  • A pie chart shows proportions, not amounts, so two pie charts cannot be compared unless both totals are known.

The circle is the total

A pie chart shares a circle between the categories. The whole 360° stands for the total frequency. Half of the total gets half of the circle.

A pie chart shares a circle between the categories. The whole 360° stands for the total frequency, so a category with half of the total gets half of the circle.

Degrees for each item

The angle for one item is 360° ÷ total frequency. Each angle is the frequency times that, and the angles add up to 360°.

From an angle to a frequency

A sector's angle out of 360° is the fraction of the total it stands for. That fraction of the total frequency is the frequency.

Bigger is not always more

A pie chart shows proportions, not amounts. A larger sector may stand for fewer people than a smaller one in another pie.

A pie chart shows proportions, not amounts. A larger sector in one pie chart may stand for fewer people than a smaller sector in another.
