Expanding a Single Bracket

Brackets appear in almost every formula and equation. Today we open them up, the first step towards the quadratics of this topic.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Multiplying a bracket by a term outside it multiplies every term inside, because a(b+c) =ab+ac.
  • The sign in front of a term travels with it, so −3(x−4) =−3x+12
  • Index notation is used when a letter multiplies itself: x times x is written x2

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Every term inside is multiplied

A number outside a bracket multiplies every term inside it. So 4(x+3) =4x+12, and the area grid shows both parts.

The sign travels with its term

The sign in front of each term belongs to it. So −3(x−4) =−3x+12
Two negatives multiply to a positive.

The sign in front of each term belongs to it. So −3(x−4) =−3x+12, because two negatives multiply to give a positive.

A letter outside the bracket

When the letter is outside, a letter times itself is written with a power. So x(x+5) =x2+5x.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Two brackets, then collect

Each bracket is expanded on its own, and then like terms are collected. The x terms join the x terms, and the numbers join the numbers.

A bracket that is subtracted

A minus in front of a bracket multiplies every term inside. So −3(x−4) =−3x+12

A minus in front of a bracket multiplies every term inside it. So 5(x+2) −3(x−4) =5x +10 −3x +12 =2x+22
