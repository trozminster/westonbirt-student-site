Enlarging by a Positive Scale Factor

A photo blown up into a poster keeps its shape but changes its size. An enlargement says exactly how much bigger, and from where.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An enlargement is specified by a centre and a scale factor k.
  • Every length on the image is k times the matching length on the object, and the distance from the centre to an image point is k times the distance from the centre to its object point.
  • An enlargement preserves angles but not lengths, so the image is similar to the object and not congruent to it.
By the end you can

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Every length times k

An enlargement multiplies every length by the scale factor k. The angles stay the same, so the image is similar to the object, not congruent.

The centre fixes the place

Each image point is on the line from the centre through its object point. It is k times as far from the centre.

Each image point lies on the line from the centre through its object point, k times as far from the centre. So the centre decides where the image lands.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Scale factor from matching lengths

The scale factor is an image length divided by its matching object length. Divide image by object; never subtract.

Lines meet at the centre

A line through each point and its image passes through the centre. Two such lines cross at the centre, and a third checks it.
