Topic 10.17 · Quadratics and Their Graphs · Lesson 6 of 8

Roots from a Quadratic Graph

Some quadratics will not factorise, but every one can be drawn. Today the graph shows us the solutions, and how many there are.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The x-coordinates of the points where a curve crosses the x-axis are the solutions of y=0, and they are called the roots.
  • A quadratic graph may cross the x-axis twice, touch it once, or not meet it at all, which is why an equation can have two, one or no solutions.
  • A root read off a plotted graph is an estimate limited by the scale, while a root found by factorising is exact.

Roots are where y=0

The roots are where the curve crosses the x-axis. They solve x2−2x−3=0

The solutions of x2−2x−3=0 are where the curve y=x2−2x−3 crosses the x-axis. These x values are called the roots.

Two, one or no roots

A curve may cross the x-axis twice, touch it once, or miss it. So a quadratic has two, one or no solutions.

A curve may cross the x-axis twice, touch it once, or not meet it at all. So a quadratic equation can have two, one or no solutions.

A reading is an estimate

A root read from a graph is only as accurate as its scale. A crossing between two grid lines is judged, not measured exactly.
