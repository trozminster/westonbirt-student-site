Topic 10.17 · Quadratics and Their Graphs · Lesson 6 of 8

Roots from a Quadratic Graph

Some quadratics will not factorise, but every one can be drawn. Today the graph shows us the solutions, and how many there are.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The x-coordinates of the points where a curve crosses the x-axis are the solutions of y = 0, and they are called the roots.
  • A quadratic graph may cross the x-axis twice, touch it once, or not meet it at all, which is why an equation can have two, one or no solutions.
  • A root read off a plotted graph is an estimate limited by the scale, while a root found by factorising is exact.
By the end you can
G4Read the solutions of a quadratic equation from its plotted graph and state the accuracy the scale allows.
G5Compare the roots read from a graph with the solutions found by factorising the same equation, and account for any difference.

Roots are where y = 0

The roots are where the curve crosses the x-axis. They solve x² - 2x - 3 = 0.

The solutions of x² - 2x - 3 = 0 are where the curve y = x² - 2x - 3 crosses the x-axis. These x values are called the roots.

Two, one or no roots

A curve may cross the x-axis twice, touch it once, or miss it. So a quadratic has two, one or no solutions.

A curve may cross the x-axis twice, touch it once, or not meet it at all. So a quadratic equation can have two, one or no solutions.

A reading is an estimate

A root read from a graph is only as accurate as its scale. A crossing between two grid lines is judged, not measured exactly.

The graph and the algebra agree

Factorising and the graph give the same roots. The curve crosses the x-axis at x = -2 and x = 1.

x² + x - 2 = 0 factorises to (x + 2)(x - 1) = 0. So x = -2 or x = 1. The curve crosses the x-axis at exactly those two points.

Exact and estimated

For x² - x - 20 = 0, factorising gives the roots exactly. A graph reading is an estimate, limited by its scale.

Factorising gives the roots exactly, but a graph reading is an estimate limited by its scale. A small difference between them is the scale, not a mistake.
