Angles in Parallel Lines

Railway tracks, window frames and road markings are full of parallel lines. Where a line crosses them the angles repeat, so one angle unlocks the rest.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A transversal is a straight line that crosses a pair of parallel lines, and arrowheads on two lines are what say those lines are parallel.
  • Corresponding angles sit in matching positions at the two crossings and are equal.
  • Alternate angles sit on opposite sides of the transversal, between the parallel lines, and are equal.
  • Co-interior angles sit on the same side of the transversal, between the parallel lines, and add to 180°.

Parallel lines and a transversal

Matching arrowheads mean two lines are parallel. A transversal crosses them both. It makes the same angles at each crossing.

Matching arrowheads say that two lines are parallel, and a transversal is a straight line crossing them. It crosses both at the same slope, so it makes the same angles at each crossing.

Equal pairs and co-interior pairs

Corresponding angles and alternate angles are equal. Co-interior angles, on the same side between the lines, add to 180°.

Corresponding angles and alternate angles are equal. Co-interior angles, on the same side between the lines, add to 180°. Corresponding angles sit in matching positions at the two crossings. Alternate angles sit on opposite sides of the transversal, between the lines.

A step you were not asked for

A two-step problem first finds an angle that nobody asked for. That angle is the bridge from the given angle to the one wanted.

Every step carries its reason

Each step names its fact: a straight line, vertically opposite, corresponding, alternate or co-interior. The reasons are part of the answer, not an extra.
