Writing Numbers in Standard Form

The Sun is about 150,000,000 km away and a red blood cell is about 0.000008 m across. Standard form writes both in a few digits, so they can be read and compared at a glance.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A number is in standard form when it is written as a×10n, where a is at least 1 and less than 10, and n is a whole number.
  • A positive power of 10 makes the number larger and a negative power makes it smaller, so 1.5×108 =150,000,000 and 1.5×10−3=0.0015
  • The power counts the places the decimal point moves, not the number of zeros written.
  • A power can also be rewritten with a different base, because (am)n=a(m×n).

A power of a power

(23)4 is four lots of 23 multiplied together. So the indices multiply: (23)4=212
In general, (am)n=amn.

(23)4 means 23 used as a factor four times, so the indices add to 3×4=12
In general (am)n=amn.

Writing the base as a power

A base can be rewritten as a power of a smaller base. Since 8=23, 84 =(23)4=212

A power can take a new base when the old base is a power of it. Since 8=23, 84 =(23)4=212
