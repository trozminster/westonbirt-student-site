Order of Operations

Two people can look at the same string of numbers and operations and get completely different answers unless they agree on the order to work through it - BIDMAS is that agreement, and exam markers apply it strictly.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The order of operations convention is: brackets first, then indices, then multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right.
  • A negative sign directly in front of a power with no brackets applies after the power is evaluated, not to the base - so −22 means −(22)=−4, not (−2)2=4

Work in a fixed order: brackets, indices, then × and ÷, then + and -. Finish the bracket before applying a power.

An expression that mixes several operations has one fixed order for working through them: brackets first, then indices, then multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction left to right. Anything inside brackets must be fully resolved before applying a power or combining it with anything outside.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The same order applies with negative numbers. Without brackets, a minus in front of a power is applied after the power. So −22=−4, but (−2)2=4

The full order of operations - brackets, indices, multiplication and division left to right, addition and subtraction left to right - applies just the same when negative numbers are involved. A negative sign directly in front of a power, with no brackets around it, applies to the whole power afterwards, not to the base before it's raised.
