Topic 10.21 · Working with Proportion · Lesson 2 of 3

Best Buys and Currency

Bigger packs are not always better value, and prices abroad are in another currency. Today we compare fairly and convert between currencies.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two differently sized packs are compared by putting both on the same footing: either price per unit of content, or content per unit of price.
  • The two comparisons rank the packs in opposite directions, so the conclusion must say which measure was used.
  • A currency conversion is a direct proportion with the exchange rate as the multiplier, and the rate is inverted to convert back.
By the end you can

The same amount of each

Two packs are compared by finding the price of the same amount of each. The lower price per unit is the better value.

Say which measure

Price per gram and grams per pound rank the packs in opposite directions. So a conclusion says which measure was used.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Currency is direct proportion

Converting currency is direct proportion, with the exchange rate as the multiplier. If £1=€1.15, then £20=20×1.15=€23

Converting back divides

To convert back, divide by the rate instead of multiplying. So €46 is 46÷1.15=£40

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Convert £80 to dollars when £1=$1.25
Multiply by the rate80×1.25=100
