Topic 10.21 · Working with Proportion · Lesson 2 of 2

Best Buys and Currency

Bigger packs are not always better value, and prices abroad are in another currency. Today we compare fairly and convert between currencies.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two differently sized packs are compared by putting both on the same footing: either price per unit of content, or content per unit of price.
  • The two comparisons rank the packs in opposite directions, so the conclusion must say which measure was used.
  • A currency conversion is a direct proportion with the exchange rate as the multiplier, and the rate is inverted to convert back.
By the end you can
G3Decide which of two or more packs is the better value by calculating a price per unit.
G4Convert between two currencies using a given exchange rate, including converting back, and solve a problem set in two currencies.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The same amount of each

Two packs are compared by finding the price of the same amount of each. The lower price per unit is the better value.

Say which measure

Price per gram and grams per pound rank the packs in opposite directions. So a conclusion says which measure was used.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Which is better value: 6 eggs for £1.50 or 10 eggs for £2.30?
Divide the 6-pack£1.50 ÷ 6 = £0.25
Divide the 10-pack£2.30 ÷ 10 = £0.23
Better valuethe 10 eggs, at £0.23 an egg
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Which is better value: 400 g for £2 or 600 g for £3.30?
Find what £1 buys.
Divide the small pack400 ÷ 2 = 200 g
Divide the large pack600 ÷ 3.30 = 181.8... g
Better valuethe 400 g pack, as £1 buys more
YOUR TURN · a
Which is better value: 4 rolls for £1.20 or 6 rolls for £1.74?
30p against 29p a roll: the 6 rolls
YOUR TURN · b
Which is better value, a 2 litre bottle at £1.80 or a 3 litre bottle at £2.55, and by which measure?
Price per litre: 90p against 85p, so the 3 litre bottle

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Currency is direct proportion

Converting currency is direct proportion, with the exchange rate as the multiplier. If £1 = €1.15, then £20 = 20 × 1.15 = €23.

Converting back divides

To convert back, divide by the rate instead of multiplying. So €46 is 46 ÷ 1.15 = £40.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Convert £80 to dollars when £1 = $1.25.
Multiply by the rate80 × 1.25 = 100
In dollars$100
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Convert $150 to pounds when £1 = $1.25.
Divide by the rate150 ÷ 1.25 = 120
In pounds£120
YOUR TURN · c
Convert £60 to euros when £1 = €1.15.
60 × 1.15 = €69
YOUR TURN · d
When £1 = €1.15, is a jacket cheaper at €92 in Paris or at £75 in London?
€92 ÷ 1.15 = £80, so London
