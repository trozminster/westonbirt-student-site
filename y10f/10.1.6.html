Rounding and Estimation

A rounded answer with no estimate to back it up is a guess - today's about being able to sanity-check any calculator answer in seconds, which matters every time you hit a wrong button in an exam.

By the end you’ll know…
  • To round to a given decimal place or significant figure, look only at the single digit immediately after the place you're rounding to: 5 or more rounds the target digit up, less than 5 leaves it as it is; every digit after the rounding point is then removed, or replaced with zeros for significant figures before the decimal point.
  • To estimate a calculation, round every value involved to 1 significant figure first, then carry out the simplified calculation - a calculator answer wildly different from this estimate signals a mis-pressed button, not a wrong estimate.

Rounding depends only on the digit straight after the rounding place. If that digit is 5 or more, round up. So 5.472 rounds down to 5.47

Rounding to a given decimal place or significant figure depends on one digit only - the single digit immediately after the place being rounded to: if it's 5 or more, round the target digit up; if it's less than 5, leave it as it is. Every digit after the rounding point is then removed, or replaced with zeros for significant figures before the decimal point.

To estimate, round every value to 1 significant figure. Then work it out as normal. A calculator answer far from the estimate means a mis-press.

An estimate is a deliberately simplified version of the calculation: every value rounded to 1 significant figure, then worked out as normal, giving a quick check figure the true answer should be reasonably close to. If a calculator answer is wildly different from the estimate, that's a sign a button was mis-pressed, not that the estimate is wrong.
