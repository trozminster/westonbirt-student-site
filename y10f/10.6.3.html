Ratio Notation, Simplest Form and 1:n

A ratio is how a recipe, a scale drawing, a mixture of paint and a share of a bill are all described. It records a relationship rather than an amount, which is why the same ratio can describe a mug of squash and a swimming pool.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A ratio a:b records a multiplicative relationship between two quantities, so each part is a fraction of the other and of the whole. A ratio simplifies by dividing both parts by their highest common factor, with both parts in the same unit first; in the form 1:n, n comes from dividing both parts by the first part.

Divide to simplify, never subtract

A ratio compares parts by multiplication, so divide both parts to simplify. Dividing by the highest common factor gives the simplest form in one step.

A ratio records how many of one quantity go with how many of another, so what matters is the multiplicative relationship between the parts and not the size of the numbers used to write it. Dividing both parts by the same number leaves that relationship untouched, exactly as it does in an equivalent fraction, which is why dividing by the highest common factor gives the simplest form in one step. Subtracting the same amount from both parts does not leave the relationship untouched: it changes the ratio into a different one that happens to share a difference. The test of a correct simplification is that the two ratios describe the same mixture.

Units before anything else

Both parts must count the same unit before anything else is done. Once they share a unit, the unit disappears from the ratio.

Two parts of a ratio can only be compared when they count the same thing, so a ratio written with mixed units means nothing until both parts are in one unit. Until then the numbers are not comparable at all, and simplifying them produces a confident wrong answer rather than an obvious one. Converting is therefore the first step and not a tidying-up afterwards. Once both parts share a unit, the unit itself disappears from the ratio, because a ratio is a comparison and carries no units of its own.
SIMPLIFYING RATIOS
Simplify 18:24, then 45 min:2 h, then write 5:20 in the form 1:n.
Step 1: For 18:24, find the HCF of 18 and 24, which is 6. Step 2: Divide both parts by 6: 18:24 = 3:4
Step 3: For 45 min:2 h the units differ, so convert first: 2 h is 120 min. Convert to minutes: 45 min:2 h = 45:120
Step 4: The HCF of 45 and 120 is 15. Divide both parts by 15: = 3:8
Step 5: For 1:n, divide both parts by the first part. Divide both parts by 5: 5:20 = 1:4
NOT DIVIDING EXACTLY
Now one where the first part does not divide the second exactly. Write 4:10 in the form 1:n.
Step 1: Divide both parts by the first part, which is 4. Step 2: 4 ÷ 4 = 1 and 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5. Divide both parts by 4: 4:10 = 1:2.5
Step 3: The ratio is 1:2.5. A decimal is a correct answer here, because the form asks for a 1 on the left and says nothing about whole numbers.

the HCF of 12 and 18 is 6, so 12:18 = 2:3; 2 h is 120 min, so 30:120 = 1:4; and 4:12 = 1:3
YOUR TURN · b

the HCF of 30 and 42 is 6, so 30:42 = 5:7. Then 3 kg is 3000 g, so the ratio is 800:3000, and dividing both by 200 gives 4:15; the conversion comes first because two parts in different units cannot be compared. Then 6 ÷ 6 = 1 and 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5, so 6:15 = 1:2.5.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One part, two different fractions

In a:b the first part is ab of the other part. It is aa+b of the whole, and the wording decides which is wanted.

In the ratio a:b, one part can be compared with the other part or with the whole, and those are two different fractions. The first part is ab of the second, because the comparison is between the two parts only. It is aa+b of the whole, because the whole is both parts together. Choosing the wrong denominator is the commonest error here, and the wording of the question is what decides it: 'of the other' takes the other part, 'of the total' takes the sum. Both fractions are true of the same ratio at the same time.

One relationship, four notations

A ratio, a fraction, a decimal and a percentage record one relationship. Moving between them means moving from the parts to the total.

A ratio, a fraction of the whole, a decimal and a percentage are four ways of recording the same multiplicative relationship, so any one of them converts into any other. A ratio of 3:5 means 3 parts out of 8, which is the fraction 38, the decimal 0.375 and 37.5%. Being able to move between them is what lets a ratio question be answered with fraction or percentage work already secure. The move that has to be made deliberately is from the parts to the total, since a ratio names the parts and a percentage names a share of the whole.
RATIO TO FRACTIONS
In the ratio 3:5, express the first part as a fraction of the second and as a fraction of the whole, then as a percentage of the whole.
Step 1: As a fraction of the second part, the comparison is between the two parts alone. Compare with the second: 35
For the whole, add the parts first. Add the parts3 + 5 = 8
Compare with the whole38
3 ÷ 8 = 0.375
Multiply by 1000.375 × 100 = 37.5% The two fractions 35 and 38 are both true of this ratio and answer different questions.
THE OTHER WAY
Now the other way round. A class is 25 girls. Write the ratio of girls to boys.
Step 1: If 2 of every 5 are girls, then 3 of every 5 are boys. Find the boys: 5 - 2 = 3
Step 2: The ratio of girls to boys compares the parts, not a part with the whole. Step 3: Compare the parts: 2:3
