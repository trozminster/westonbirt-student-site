Parallel Lines and Their Gradients

Railway tracks, the lines on a page and the edges of a ruler never meet. In coordinates, one number decides whether two lines are parallel.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two straight lines are parallel exactly when they have the same gradient.
  • Parallel lines with different y-intercepts never meet, so the pair of equations has no common solution.
  • y=3x+1 and y=3x−4 are therefore parallel, while y=3x+1 and y=4x+1 are not, even though they cross the y-axis at the same point.

Same gradient, never meet

Parallel lines have the same gradient. With different y-intercepts, they never meet.

Parallel lines climb by the same amount for every step across, so they have the same gradient. With different y-intercepts they never meet.

A shared intercept is not enough

A shared y-intercept does not make lines parallel. y=3x+1 and y=4x+1 meet at (0,1).

The lines y=3x+1 and y=4x+1 cross the y-axis at the same point but climb at different rates. They meet there, so they are not parallel.
