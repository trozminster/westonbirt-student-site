Topic 10.16 · Constructions and Circle Properties · Lesson 7 of 7

Reasoning with Circle Properties

A detective states every clue and what it proves, one step at a time. Circle problems are solved the same way: each angle is found with a named reason, in order.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A geometrical reason names the property being used, not the arithmetic: 'the tangent is perpendicular to the radius' rather than '90 take away 40'.
  • Circle work also uses the angle facts already established: angles in a triangle sum to 180°, base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal, and any two radii of the same circle are equal, which makes many triangles in a circle isosceles.
  • A chain of reasoning states each step and the reason for it, in order.

Two radii make it isosceles

Any two radii of one circle are equal. So a triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles. Its base angles are equal.

Any two radii of one circle are equal. So a triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles, and its base angles are equal.

A circle problem is solved as a chain: each step finds one angle and names its reason. Each angle found is then used in the next step.
