Adding and Subtracting Fractions

Fractions only combine once their parts are the same size, so adding or subtracting always starts by finding a common denominator.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Fractions can only be added or subtracted once they share a denominator, since only equal-sized parts combine.
  • A common denominator is a common multiple of the denominators, and the LCM gives the smallest.
  • Each numerator is multiplied by whatever its own denominator was multiplied by.
  • Only the numerators are then added; the denominator names the part size and stays as it is.
  • With an algebraic numerator the whole numerator is bracketed before subtracting, so that every term changes sign.

Same rule, with letters on top

Algebraic numerators follow the same common-denominator rule. Only the numerators carry the letters.

An algebraic numerator follows exactly the same common-denominator rule; only the numerators, not the denominators, carry the letters.

Bracket a subtracted numerator

When subtracting, bracket the whole second numerator first. Then every one of its terms changes sign.

When subtracting, bracket the whole second numerator first, so every one of its terms changes sign.
