Topic 9.17 · Circle Theorems · Lesson 2 of 6

Centre and Same Segment

Two more theorems today, both about comparing an angle at the centre of a circle to angles at its edge – and both follow from the same isosceles-radii idea as last lesson, just used twice.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference when both stand on the same arc.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal: two angles at the circumference standing on the same arc are equal to each other.
  • That follows from the first theorem, since each is half of the same angle at the centre.
  • The arc both angles stand on is what has to be identified first, not the shape they appear to make.

Angle at the centre

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. Both must stand on the same arc.

The angle at the centre of a circle is twice the angle at the circumference, when both angles stand on the same arc. "Stand on the same arc" means both angles are drawn from the same two points on the circle out to a third point (or the centre).

Why it works

A line through the centre makes two isosceles triangles. Adding their angle facts proves the theorem.

Splitting the angle at the centre with a line through the centre and the circumference point creates two isosceles triangles (radii again). Each gives an equation linking its centre angle to its circumference angle, and adding them proves the theorem.

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference: 100=2 × angle ACBBoth angles stand on the same arc AB.

Learn

Angles in the same segment

Angles at the circumference on the same arc are equal. Each is half the same angle at the centre.

Two angles at the circumference standing on the same arc are equal to each other, since each one is half of the same angle at the centre. This follows directly from the first theorem, used twice on the same arc.

Spotting the same arc

Find the arc from the two points each angle is drawn from. Ignore the triangle's other sides.

The key is identifying which arc both angles stand on – look at which two points on the circle each angle is drawn FROM, not which points make up the triangle's other sides.
TWO ANGLES, ONE ARC
