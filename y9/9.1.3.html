Topic 9.1 · Prime Factors, HCF and LCM · Lesson 3 of 4

HCF and LCM

Sharing sweets into the biggest possible equal groups uses one idea; two flashing lighthouses lining up again uses a different one. Both come down to the same prime factorisations you've just learned to find - but only if you can tell which one, HCF or LCM, the problem actually needs.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The HCF is the product of the primes the two numbers share, each taken to the lower of its two powers.
  • The LCM is the product of every prime appearing in either number, each taken to the higher power.
  • In a Venn of the two factorisations, the HCF is the product of the intersection and the LCM the product of the whole diagram.
  • Listing gives the same answers, but becomes impractical as the numbers grow.
  • Splitting or grouping into equal parts needs the HCF; two repeating events coinciding needs the LCM.

HCF and LCM defined

The HCF is the largest number dividing into both exactly. The LCM is the smallest number both divide into exactly.

The Highest Common Factor (HCF) of two numbers is the largest number that divides into both exactly. The Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) is the smallest number that both divide into exactly. Listing the factors (for HCF) or multiples (for LCM) of each number and comparing works for small numbers, but becomes impractical as the numbers grow - there could be a long way to list before a common multiple appears, or many factors to check.

Finding HCF and LCM from prime factorisations

Put shared primes in the Venn overlap. HCF = product of the overlap, at the lower powers. LCM = product of the whole diagram, at the higher powers.

Write each number's prime factorisation, then draw a Venn diagram with one circle per number, placing shared prime factors in the overlap. The HCF is the product of everything in the overlap (each shared prime at the LOWER of its two powers). The LCM is the product of everything in the whole diagram (every prime that appears, each at the HIGHER of its two powers).

Deciding which one a problem needs

Splitting into equal groups with none left over needs the HCF. Repeating events coinciding again needs the LCM. Read the structure, not single words like biggest.

Some worded problems ask you to split or share things into equal groups or equal-sized pieces, with nothing left over - these need the HCF. Other problems describe two (or more) repeating, separate events and ask when they next happen at the same time - these need the LCM. Don't just hunt for words like 'biggest' or 'smallest' in the question - a HCF problem can ask for the 'greatest number of groups', and an LCM problem can ask for the 'first time' something coincides; the giveaway is the STRUCTURE (splitting into equal parts vs events coinciding), not a single word.

Solving once you've decided

Find the HCF or LCM as before. Then answer the question actually asked.

Once you know which one the problem needs, find it exactly as before - by listing, or by prime factorisation and a Venn diagram - then answer the question actually asked, which might need a further step, e.g. dividing by it, or adding it on to a start time.

A gardener has 42 tulip bulbs and 56 daffodil bulbs. She wants to plant them in identical rows, using the largest possible number of bulbs per row, with no bulbs left over and each row containing only one type. How many bulbs go in each row?
