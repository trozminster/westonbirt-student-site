Topic 9.1 · Prime Factors, HCF and LCM · Lesson 1 of 4

Prime Factor Decomposition

Every whole number bigger than 1 breaks down into primes in exactly one way - a numerical fingerprint. You'll use this decomposition constantly over the next few lessons to find HCF and LCM efficiently, so getting it fast and reliable now, by hand and by calculator, pays off immediately.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Every whole number above 1 is prime, or a product of primes in exactly one way.
  • A factor tree splits a number until every branch ends in a prime, and the primes are the same whichever pair is split first.
  • Index form collects the repeats: 360=23×32×5
  • A calculator's FACT function returns the prime factorisation directly, which matters for numbers too large to tree by hand.

Prime factor decomposition

Every number above 1 is prime or a unique product of primes. A factor tree splits until every branch is prime. Keep splitting a 4: 4=2×2

Every whole number greater than 1 is either prime, or can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way (the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic). A factor tree finds this decomposition by splitting the number into a factor pair, then splitting each factor again, until every branch ends in a prime - a number whose only factors are 1 and itself. Check each end branch is actually prime; don't stop at a number just because it looks small, e.g. 4=2×2, not prime, so a branch of 4 must keep splitting.

Index form and uniqueness

Index form collects repeated primes: 23×32×5
The index counts how often each prime appears. Any first split ends on the same primes.

Index form collects repeated primes: instead of writing 2×2×2×3×3×5, write 23×32×5 - the index counts how many times that prime appeared as a leaf. Whichever factor pair you split first, the tree always ends on the same set of primes, because the decomposition is unique - two different starting splits of 360 both end in 23×32×5

Why use a calculator for large numbers

Factor trees get slow for large numbers. The calculator's FACT function gives the full factorisation at once.

A factor tree works well for smaller numbers, but becomes slow and error-prone for large numbers with big prime factors - you'd need to test many possible splits by hand. A calculator's FACT function (found in the function or OPTN menu) returns the complete prime factorisation of any number you enter, instantly, in index form.

Reading the output correctly

The FACT output is the full decomposition, not a menu. Multiply it back to check it equals your number.

The FACT output is not a menu to choose factors from - it IS the full decomposition, already collected into index form. Always check it by multiplying the primes (with their powers) back together to confirm you've read the display correctly and it equals your original number.
