Changing and Combining Ratios

A ratio between two things can change when one of them changes, and two separate ratios can sometimes be joined into one. Today is about handling both, using the part that stays fixed as the way in.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When a quantity is added to or taken from one share the ratio changes, but the other share often stays fixed.
  • That fixed share is the way in, since it must represent the same amount in both ratios.
  • Scaling both ratios so the fixed share carries the same number of parts makes them comparable.
  • Two ratios sharing a common element combine into one three-part ratio by scaling both until the shared element matches.

What stays fixed

Adding to one share changes the ratio. The other share often stays fixed. That fixed share is the way in.

When a quantity is added to or taken from one share the ratio changes, but the other share often stays fixed. That fixed share is the way in, since it must represent the same amount in both ratios.

Scaling to match

Scale both ratios so the fixed share has the same number of parts. Then read the new ratio off directly.

Scaling both ratios so the fixed share carries the same number of parts makes them comparable. Once matched, the new ratio can be read off directly.
A RATIO THAT CHANGES
The ratio of adults to children on a coach is 2:5
Three more adults board, and the ratio becomes 1:2
Find the original number of children.
Scale so the children match2 : 5=4 : 10The LCM of 5 and 2 is 10
Scale the new ratio1 : 2=5 : 10
Compare the adults5−4=1 partThe adults grew by 1 part, and that part is the 3 people who boarded.
Find one part1 part=3 people
Original children10×3=30
YOUR TURN · a
Cats to dogs at a shelter is 3:4; two more dogs arrive and it becomes 3:5
Find the number of cats.
cats are fixed at 3 parts in both; dogs grew from 4 parts to 5, so 1 part =2 dogs. Cats =3×2=6 (dogs 8, then 10).
YOUR TURN · b
Red to blue marbles is 5:2; three more blue marbles make it 5:3
Find the number of red marbles.
red is fixed at 5 parts in both; blue grew by 1 part =3 marbles. Red =5×3=15 (blue 6, then 9).
YOUR TURN · c
Men to women in a room is 3:2; four more women enter and it becomes 3:4
By how many parts did the women's share grow?
men are fixed at 3 parts in both; women grew from 2 parts to 4, an increase of 2 parts (= 4 women, so 1 part =2).
YOUR TURN · d
Pens to pencils is 4:3; six more pencils make it 2:3
Scale the new ratio so pens match the 4, then find the number of pens.
2:3 scaled by 2 is 4:6; pencils grew from 3 parts to 6, so 3 parts =6 pencils and 1 part =2
Pens =4×2=8 (pencils 6, then 12).

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A shared element

Two ratios sharing an element combine into one three-part ratio. Scale both until the shared element matches.

Two ratios sharing a common element combine into one three-part ratio by scaling both until the shared element matches. This works because the shared element then represents the same amount in both ratios at once.

Building the combined ratio

With the common element matched, write the three parts together. Write the common element only once.

Once both ratios are scaled to share the same value for the common element, the three parts can be written together as one ratio. The common element itself is only written once.
COMBINING TWO RATIOS
A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5
Find A:B:C.
Scale the first by 42 : 3=8 : 12
Scale the second by 34 : 5=12 : 15Now B is 12 in both.
Combine the ratiosA:B:C=8 : 12 : 15

In a changing-ratio problem, how do you identify which quantity is fixed?
It is the quantity that is not changed by the event described in the problem, so it must represent the same amount in the ratio before and after.
