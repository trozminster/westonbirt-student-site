Topic 9.4 · Congruence and Parallel Lines · Lesson 3 of 4

Bearings with Parallel North Lines

A bearing is how ships, aircraft and hikers describe direction precisely enough to navigate by - a single angle, always measured the same way, from the same reference line. Get the convention wrong and the direction is wrong even if your protractor reading was perfect.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from North, always written with three figures.
  • North lines are parallel wherever they are drawn on a diagram.
  • The bearing from B to A, the back bearing, differs from the bearing from A to B by 180°.
  • Add 180 when the forward bearing is under 180, and subtract 180 when it is over.
  • Because North lines are parallel, the alternate and co-interior rules apply to bearing diagrams.

Definition

A bearing is an angle measured CLOCKWISE from North. It is always written with three figures: 045, not 45

A bearing is an angle measured CLOCKWISE from North, at the point you are measuring from. It is always written with three figures, so 8° is written 008, and 45° is written 045

Drawing North

Draw a North line at every point you measure from. Measure from that North line, never from the line joining the points.

Every point a bearing is measured from needs its own North line drawn (usually a vertical arrow pointing up the page) - the bearing is measured from that line, not from any other line in the diagram, such as the line joining the two points.

Measuring

Put the protractor's centre on the point, with 0° on the North line. Read clockwise, on the scale that starts at 0 on the North line.

Place the protractor's centre on the point you are measuring from, 0° on the North line, and read clockwise around to the line you want the bearing of. A protractor has two scales - always check which one starts at 0 on the North line, or the reading will come out as the supplement of the true bearing.

Plotting

Draw the North line at the starting point. Measure the given angle clockwise from North. Then draw the direction line.

To plot a bearing, draw the North line at the starting point, then use a protractor to measure the given angle clockwise from North and draw the direction line.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Back bearings

The back bearing, from B to A, differs from A to B by 180°. Turning to face back the way you came is a half turn.

The bearing from B to A - the back bearing - differs from the bearing from A to B by 180°, because turning to face back the way you came is a half turn.

Add or subtract

Forward bearing under 180: add 180 for the back bearing. Over 180: subtract 180 instead. A result outside 000 to 360 means the wrong operation.

If the forward bearing (A to B) is under 180, add 180 to find the back bearing. If it is over 180, subtract 180 instead. Either way the result must still fall between 0 and 360 (and be written as three figures) - a value outside that range signals the wrong operation was used.

Parallel North lines

Every North line on a diagram points the same way. So all North lines on one diagram are parallel.

Every North line drawn on a bearings diagram, wherever it is drawn, points in the same direction - so all North lines on one diagram are parallel to each other.

Applying angle facts

AB is a transversal across the parallel North lines at A and B. Alternate and co-interior angle facts then give a bearing without a protractor.

Because North lines are parallel, the alternate and co-interior angle facts from parallel lines apply directly between the North line at A and the North line at B, using line AB as the transversal - this is how a back bearing, or any unmeasured bearing in the diagram, can be worked out without a protractor.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
The bearing of B from A is 072°. Find the bearing of A from B.
072 is under 180, so the back bearing is 180 more.
Back bearings differ by 180°bearing=072+180
Bearing of A from B=252°
Check with alternate angles180+072=252°The North lines at A and B are parallel and AB is the transversal, so the angle at B between BA and North(B) extended backward is also 072°. Measured clockwise from North(B) all the way round, that is 180+072=252° - the two methods agree.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
The bearing of P from Q is 205°. Find the bearing of Q from P.
205 is over 180, so the back bearing is 180 less.
Back bearings differ by 180°bearing=205−180
Bearing of Q from P=025°
Check025+180=205The two bearings are exactly 180° apart, and 025° is a valid three-figure bearing between 000° and 360° - checking the range is part of confirming the answer.
