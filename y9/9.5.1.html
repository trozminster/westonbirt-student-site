Percentage Multipliers

Percentages appear everywhere - discounts, exam marks, interest, tax - and every one of them is really just a multiplication in disguise. Once you can turn any percentage into a multiplier, finding a percentage of an amount, and increasing or decreasing an amount by a percentage, all become the same one-line calculation. This is the tool the rest of the unit builds on.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Per cent means per hundred, so p% of an amount is a multiplication by p100
  • 35% of an amount is therefore a multiplication by 0.35
  • An increase of p% is a multiplication by (1+p100), so a 12% rise is x1.12
  • A decrease of p% is a multiplication by (1−p100), so a 12% fall is x0.88
  • A multiplier above 1 always increases an amount and one below 1 always decreases it.

Percentage means per hundred

Per cent means per hundred: 35%=35÷100=0.35
To find 35% of an amount, multiply by 0.35, the multiplier.

"Per cent" literally means "per hundred", so p% is the fraction p100, which is also the decimal p100
To find p% of an amount, multiply the amount by that decimal - called the multiplier. For example 35%=35÷100=0.35, so 35% of an amount is that amount × 0.35

Converting any percentage to a multiplier

To turn a percentage into a multiplier, divide by 100
8%=0.08, not 0.8
Over 100% gives a multiplier above 1: 125%=1.25

To convert a percentage to a decimal multiplier, divide by 100, which moves the digits two place values (equivalent to moving the decimal point two places left). 8%=0.08, not 0.8, because the digit 8 sits in the units place of the percentage but must land in the hundredths place of the multiplier. 60%=0.6, and 125%=1.25 - percentages over 100% give multipliers above 1, since "125% of" an amount gives more than the whole amount.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Increasing with a multiplier

To increase by p%, keep the 100% and add the extra p%. So a 12% rise is × 1.12, in one step.

To increase an amount by p%, you need the original 100% plus the extra p%, so the combined multiplier is (1+p100). A 12% rise is therefore a multiplication by 1.12, since 100%+12% =112% =1.12
Applying this single multiplier in one step finds the increased amount directly, without calculating the increase separately and adding it on.

Decreasing with a multiplier, and reading which is which

To decrease by 12%, keep 88%: the multiplier is 0.88
A multiplier above 1 increases an amount. A multiplier below 1 decreases it.

To decrease an amount by p%, you keep only (100−p)% of it, so the multiplier is (1−p100). A 12% fall is a multiplication by 0.88, since 100%−12% =88% =0.88
A multiplier above 1 always increases an amount, and a multiplier below 1 always decreases it - checking whether your multiplier is above or below 1 is a fast way to catch an increase/decrease mix-up before you calculate.
