Topic 9.14 · Simultaneous Equations and Graphs · Lesson 5 of 9

Elimination Problem Solving

A worded problem hides two equations inside its sentences. Today is about digging them out, solving them, and not being thrown when the answer is negative or a fraction.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Either variable may be eliminated, and choosing the one with the easier coefficients reduces the arithmetic.
  • A solution can be negative or fractional, and is checked in exactly the same way.
  • A worded problem gives two conditions, and each condition becomes one equation.
  • The two unknowns are named explicitly before either equation is written.

Two conditions, two equations

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Naming the unknowns

Name the two unknowns before writing either equation. Use letters that say what they stand for.

Before either equation is written, the two unknowns are named explicitly with letters that say what they stand for, not just x and y in the abstract.

Checking a context answer

Check any solution by substituting it into both original equations. e.g. a=7 and c=5 check in both.

A solution is checked in exactly the same way whether it is negative, fractional, or a whole number: substitute it back into both original equations.
RECTANGLE PERIMETER
A rectangle's length is 3 cm more than its width. Its perimeter is 26 cm. Find its length and width.
Write the equationsl=w+3(1)
2l+2w=26(2)
Substitute (1) into (2)2(w+3)+2w=26
Expand the bracket2w+6+2w=26
Collect like terms4w+6=26
Subtract 6 from both sides4w=20
Divide by 4w=5
Substitute into (1)l=5+3
=8
Width and length5 cm and 8 cm
