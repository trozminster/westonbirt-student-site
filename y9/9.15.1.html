Topic 9.15 · Ratio, Scales and Proportion · Lesson 1 of 6

Ratio and FDP Conversions

Ratio, fractions, decimals and percentages all describe the same kind of relationship between quantities. Today is about moving freely between the four, and simplifying a ratio to its clearest form.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A ratio records a multiplicative relationship between quantities.
  • In the ratio a:b the first part is ab of the second and aa+b of the whole.
  • A ratio simplifies by dividing both parts by their HCF, with both parts in the same unit first.
  • In the form 1:n, n comes from dividing both parts by the first part.
  • Fractions, decimals, percentages and ratios all express the same relationships, so any one converts into any other.
By the end you can

What a ratio records

A ratio a:b records a multiplicative relationship between two quantities. The first part is ab of the second part, and aa+b of the whole.

Simplifying

Both parts must share a unit first. Then divide both by their HCF. For 1:n, divide both by the first part.

A ratio simplifies by dividing both parts by their HCF, once both parts are in the same unit. Written in the form 1:n, n comes from dividing both parts by the first part.

One family, four faces

Fractions, decimals, percentages and ratios express the same relationship. A ratio a:b gives the fraction aa+b of the whole.

Fractions, decimals, percentages and ratios all express the same relationship between quantities, so any one converts into any other. A ratio a:b converts to the fraction aa+b, which then converts to a decimal or a percentage as usual.

Ratios in the form 1:n

The form 1:n makes ratios easy to compare. Divide both parts by the first part. n need not be a whole number.

Writing a ratio as 1:n makes two ratios easy to compare, since only n needs reading. Both parts are divided by the first part to reach that form, and n need not be a whole number.
