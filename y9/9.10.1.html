Speed and Density

A speed of 60 km/h is really a division: 60 kilometres for every hour. Once the units are read as a division, every speed and density problem is the same problem.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A compound measure combines two other measures into a single one.
  • Its units state the relationship: km/h is kilometres divided by hours, so speed = distance / time.
  • Rearranging gives distance = speed × time and time = distance / speed.
  • Density is mass divided by volume, in g/cm³ or kg/m³, and rearranges the same way.
  • The units are the check: if they do not come out right, the operation was the wrong one.

A compound measure divides

A compound measure is one quantity divided by another. Its units show which: km/h means speed = distance ÷ time.

A compound measure is one quantity divided by another. Its units say which: km/h is kilometres divided by hours, so speed = distance ÷ time.

The same relationship, rearranged

Rearranging gives distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed. The units check the choice: km ÷ km/h leaves hours.

Density is mass per volume

Density is mass divided by volume, in g/cm³ or kg/m³. It rearranges exactly as speed does.

Units must match

The mass and the volume must be in the units the density uses. Convert first, then divide.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A block has mass 540 g and volume 200 cm³. Find its density.
Divide mass by volumedensity=540÷200
Density=2.7 g/cm³
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Oak has density 0.75 g/cm³. Find the mass of 1200 cm³ of oak.
Rearrangemass = density × volume
Substitute=0.75×1200
Mass=900 g
YOUR TURN · e
A stone has mass 390 g and volume 150 cm³. Find its density.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Divide mass by volume → Density
390÷150=2.6 g/cm³
YOUR TURN · f
Gold has density 19.3 g/cm³. Find the volume of 386 g of gold.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Rearrange → Substitute → Mass
volume = mass ÷ density =386÷19.3=20 cm³
YOUR TURN · g
A metal bar has mass 2.4 kg and volume 300 cm³. Find its density in g/cm³.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Divide mass by volume → Density
2.4 kg =2400 g, so 2400÷300=8 g/cm³.
YOUR TURN · h
Block A has density 3 g/cm³ and volume 50 cm³. Block B has mass 160 g and density 4 g/cm³. Which is heavier?
Hint. Follow the worked example: Rearrange → Substitute → Mass
A is 3×50=150 g, so B, at 160 g, is heavier.
