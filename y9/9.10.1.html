Speed and Density

A speed of 60 km/h is really a division: 60 kilometres for every hour. Once the units are read as a division, every speed and density problem is the same problem.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A compound measure combines two other measures into a single one.
  • Its units state the relationship: kmh is kilometres divided by hours, so speed = distance / time.
  • Rearranging gives distance = speed x time and time = distance / speed.
  • Density is mass divided by volume, in gcm3 or kgm3, and rearranges the same way.
  • The units are the check: if they do not come out right, the operation was the wrong one.

A compound measure divides

A compound measure is one quantity divided by another. Its units show which: kmh means speed = distance ÷ time.

A compound measure is one quantity divided by another. Its units say which: kmh is kilometres divided by hours, so speed = distance ÷ time.

The same relationship, rearranged

Rearranging gives distance = speed × time and time = distance ÷ speed. The units check the choice: km ÷ kmh leaves hours.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
A car travels 90 km in 1 hour 30 minutes. Find its average speed.
Convert the time1 hour 30 minutes = 1.5 hours
Divide distance by timespeed = 90 ÷ 1.5
Average speed= 60 km/h
WORKED EXAMPLE B

Rearrangetime = distance ÷ speed

= 3.5 hours

Density is mass per volume

Density is mass divided by volume, in gcm³ or kgm³. It rearranges exactly as speed does.

Units must match

The mass and the volume must be in the units the density uses. Convert first, then divide.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A block has mass 540 g and volume 200 cm³. Find its density.
Divide mass by volumedensity = 540 ÷ 200
Density= 2.7 g/cm³
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Oak has density 0.75 g/cm³. Find the mass of 1200 cm³ of oak.
Rearrangemass = density × volume
Substitute= 0.75 × 1200
Mass= 900 g
YOUR TURN · e
A stone has mass 390 g and volume 150 cm³. Find its density.
390 ÷ 150 = 2.6 g/cm³
YOUR TURN · f
Gold has density 19.3 g/cm³. Find the volume of 386 g of gold.
volume = mass ÷ density = 386 ÷ 19.3 = 20 cm³
YOUR TURN · g
A metal bar has mass 2.4 kg and volume 300 cm³. Find its density in gcm³.
2.4 kg = 2400 g, so 2400 ÷ 300 = 8 g/cm³.
YOUR TURN · h
Block A has density 3 g/cm³ and volume 50 cm³. Block B has mass 160 g and density 4 g/cm³. Which is heavier?
A is 3 × 50 = 150 g, so B, at 160 g, is heavier.
