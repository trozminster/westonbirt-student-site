Mixed Probability Problems

Every probability tool from this topic – the andor rules, trees, complements – solves a different kind of structure. Today is about recognising which structure a problem has, and choosing the right tool for it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The structure of a problem decides the tool: a two-way table, a tree diagram, a Venn diagram, or direct calculation.
  • A tree suits a sequence of events, a Venn suits overlapping categories, and a table suits two characteristics at once.
  • The andor rules apply whichever representation is used, since the representation only organises the outcomes.
  • Every representation must account for the whole sample space, so its probabilities sum to 1.

Matching tool to structure

The problem's structure decides the tool. Tree for a sequence, Venn for overlaps, table for two characteristics.

The structure of a problem decides the tool: a two-way table, a tree diagram, a Venn diagram, or direct calculation. A tree suits a sequence of events, a Venn suits overlapping categories, and a table suits two characteristics at once.

The rules still apply

The andor rules apply whichever representation is used, since the representation only organises the outcomes. Every representation must account for the whole sample space, so its probabilities sum to 1.

Checking your choice

Before calculating, check the whole sample space is accounted for. Anything left out stops the probabilities summing to 1.

Before calculating, check the sample space is accounted for – every category, every branch, every outcome. A representation that leaves something out gives probabilities that do not sum to 1.

Combining tools in one problem

A mixed problem may need tools in sequence. Sort with a Venn, then combine with the andor rules.

A genuinely mixed problem may need more than one tool in sequence – a Venn diagram to sort categories, THEN the andor rules to combine a probability drawn from it with another event.
