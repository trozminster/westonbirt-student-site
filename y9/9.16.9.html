Mixed Probability Problems

Every probability tool from this topic – the and/or rules, trees, complements – solves a different kind of structure. Today is about recognising which structure a problem has, and choosing the right tool for it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The structure of a problem decides the tool: a two-way table, a tree diagram, a Venn diagram, or direct calculation.
  • A tree suits a sequence of events, a Venn suits overlapping categories, and a table suits two characteristics at once.
  • The and/or rules apply whichever representation is used, since the representation only organises the outcomes.
  • Every representation must account for the whole sample space, so its probabilities sum to 1

Checking your choice

Before calculating, check the whole sample space is accounted for. Anything left out stops the probabilities summing to 1

Before calculating, check the sample space is accounted for – every category, every branch, every outcome. A representation that leaves something out gives probabilities that do not sum to 1

Combining tools in one problem

A mixed problem may need tools in sequence. Sort with a Venn, then combine with the and/or rules.

A genuinely mixed problem may need more than one tool in sequence – a Venn diagram to sort categories, THEN the and/or rules to combine a probability drawn from it with another event.
