Topic 9.13 · Prisms and Cylinders · Lesson 5 of 5

Spheres, Pyramids, Surface Area

A cylinder's own net gives its surface area formula; spheres, hemispheres, cones and pyramids reuse formulae you are given, so the skill is choosing and substituting correctly.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A cylinder's net is two circles and a rectangle whose width is the circumference, 2 x pi x r.
  • So its total surface area is 2 x pi x r² + 2 x pi x r × h.
  • A prism's surface area is its two cross-sections plus the rectangle formed by the perimeter x length.
  • The sphere, hemisphere, cone and pyramid formulae are given in the exam, so what matters is choosing the right one and identifying the correct radius or height.
  • A hemisphere's surface area includes its flat circular face as well as the curved half.

A cylinder's net gives its formula

A cylinder's net is two circles and a rectangle. The rectangle's width is the circumference, 2πr. So surface area = 2πr² + 2πrh.

A cylinder's net is two circles and a rectangle whose width is the circumference, 2 × π × r, giving total surface area 2 × π × r² + 2 × π × r × h.

A prism's surface area sums its parts

A prism's surface area is its two ends plus one rectangle. The rectangle is the cross-section's perimeter × length. e.g. ends 6 cm², perimeter 12 cm, length 10 cm.

A prism's surface area is its two cross-sections plus the rectangle formed by the cross-section's perimeter × length.

The formulae are given

The sphere, hemisphere, cone and pyramid formulae are given in the exam. Choose the right one and the correct radius or height.

The sphere, hemisphere, cone and pyramid formulae are given in the exam, so what matters is choosing the right one and identifying the correct radius or height.

A hemisphere keeps its flat face

A hemisphere's surface area includes its flat circular face as well as its curved half.
