Topic 9.17 · Circle Theorems · Lesson 6 of 6

Circle Theorem Problem Solving

Everything from this topic comes together today: a multi-step problem where each circle theorem, once used, feeds a fact into the next step – and where naming every reason is what earns every mark.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A multi-step problem chains several theorems, each step feeding the next.
  • Every step needs its reason stated by name, since the reasoning is what carries the marks.
  • Circle theorems combine with the ordinary angle facts: triangles, straight lines, isosceles triangles and parallel lines.
  • On a goal-free diagram every angle that can be found is found, and the route to the target emerges from that.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Chaining theorems

Solve one small step at a time. Each new angle becomes a known fact for the next step.

A multi-step circle problem is solved one small step at a time – one theorem (or ordinary angle fact) finds ONE new angle, which then becomes a known fact the next theorem can use. No single theorem usually reaches the target angle alone.

Naming every reason

Every step states its reason by name. The reasoning is assessed, not just the final number.

Every single step states its reason by name – "isosceles triangle (radii)," "angle in a semicircle," "angles on a straight line," "cyclic quadrilateral" – since the reasoning, not just the final number, is what is being assessed at every stage.
TWO THEOREMS COMBINED
A circle has centre O. AB is a diameter. C is on the circumference, with angle BAC = 28°. D is also on the circumference, with angle ACD and angle ABD both standing on arc AD, and angle ABD = 34°. Find angle ACB and angle ACD.
The angle in a semicircle is 90°angle ACB = 90° AB is a diameter
(Then angle ABC = 180 - 90 - 28 = 62°.)
Angles in the same segment are equalangle ACD = angle ABD Both stand on arc AD.
Angle ACD= 34°

angle EGF = 90 (semicircle); angle EFG = 180-90-33 = 57°
YOUR TURN · b
O is the centre. K and L are on the major arc HJ, and angle HKJ = 38°. Find angle HOJ and angle HLJ.
angle HOJ = 2 × 38 = 76° (angle at the centre); angle HLJ = 38° (angles in the same segment as HKJ)
YOUR TURN · c
EF is a diameter and G is on the circumference. Angle FEG = x and angle EFG = 2x. Find x.
Angle EGF = 90° (angle in a semicircle); x + 2x = 90, x = 30°
YOUR TURN · d
O is the centre. H and J are on the circumference and K is on the major arc. Angle OHJ = 25°. Find angle HOJ, then angle HKJ.
OH = OJ (radii), so angle HOJ = 180 - 25 - 25 = 130°; angle HKJ = 65° (angle at the centre)

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Working goal-free

On a hard problem, find every angle you can. The route to the target emerges from what you find.

On a genuinely hard problem, do not search only for the target angle – find every angle that CAN be found, one theorem or angle fact at a time, and let the route to the target emerge from what accumulates.

Circle theorems plus ordinary facts

Most problems mix circle theorems with ordinary angle facts. The theorems are only part of the toolkit.

A full problem usually mixes circle theorems with triangle angle sum, straight line angle facts, isosceles triangles, and parallel line facts – the circle theorems are not the whole toolkit, only part of it.
TANGENT, CHORD, DIAMETER
A circle has centre O. AB is a diameter. C is on the circumference. A tangent touches the circle at C, and the angle between the tangent and chord CB is 25°. Find angle CAB and angle BCA.
Alternate segment theoremangle CAB = 25° Angle CAB is in the alternate segment to the 25° tangent-chord angle at C.
The angle in a semicircle is 90°angle BCA = 90°
YOUR TURN · e
DEFG is a cyclic quadrilateral with angle D = 96° and angle DGF = 40°. Find angle F and angle DEF.
angle F = 180-96 = 84 (cyclic quadrilateral); angle DEF = 40° (angles in the same segment as DGF)
YOUR TURN · f
HJ is a diameter and the tangent at K meets chord KJ at 30°. Find angle KHJ and angle HKJ.
angle KHJ = 30 (alternate segment theorem); angle HKJ = 90 (angle in a semicircle, since HJ is a diameter)
YOUR TURN · g
AB is a diameter and C is on the circumference. A tangent touches the circle at C, and the tangent-chord angle with chord CA is 64°. Find angle ABC and angle CAB.
Angle ABC = 64° (alternate segment); angle ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle); angle CAB = 180 - 90 - 64 = 26°
YOUR TURN · h
O is the centre. A, B, C and D lie on the circle, D on the major arc AC and B on the minor arc. Angle AOC = 140°. Find angle ADC, then angle ABC.
Angle ADC = 70° (angle at the centre); angle ABC = 180 - 70 = 110° (cyclic quadrilateral)
