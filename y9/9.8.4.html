Topic 9.8 · Factorising Quadratics · Lesson 4 of 4

Solving by Factorising

A quadratic equation cannot be solved by undoing one step at a time, because x appears twice. Factorising turns it into two brackets that multiply to zero, and that is the key.

By the end you’ll know…
  • If a product equals zero then at least one of its factors must be zero, which is the zero-product principle.
  • So (x+p)(x+q) =0 gives x=−p or x=−q.
  • A quadratic must have zero on one side before it is factorised, so every term is collected there first.
  • A quadratic equation usually has two solutions, and both are stated.

The zero-product principle

If two numbers multiply to zero, at least one is zero. So if (x+p)(x+q) =0, one bracket is zero.

If two numbers multiply to make zero, at least one of them is zero. So (x+p)(x+q) =0 means x+p=0 or x+q=0

Two solutions

Each bracket gives its own solution. A quadratic equation usually has two, and both are stated.
