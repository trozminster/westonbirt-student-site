Topic 9.11 · Speed-Time Graphs and Area · Lesson 3 of 5

Area Under a Graph

A velocity-time graph hides a distance inside it. The area under the line is how far the object went, and the shapes are the ones already known.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The area under a velocity-time graph is the distance travelled.
  • That area is found by splitting the shape into rectangles, triangles and trapeziums.
  • A rectangle is base × height, a triangle is half base x perpendicular height, and a trapezium is half the sum of the parallel sides times the height.
  • The area's units come from the two axes multiplied together, which is why they give a distance.
  • Under a curve the area can only be estimated, by splitting it into strips.

Three area formulae

A rectangle is base × height. A triangle is ½ × base × perpendicular height. A trapezium is 12(a+b)×h, with a and b parallel.

A rectangle is base × height, and a triangle is ½ × base × perpendicular height. A trapezium is half the sum of the parallel sides times the height between them.

Perpendicular, not slanted

The height is always measured at right angles to the base. A slanted side is never the height.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Area is distance

The area under a velocity-time graph is the distance travelled. Its units are the two axis units multiplied, m/s × s, which is metres.

Split it into shapes

Split the area into rectangles, triangles and trapeziums, then add. Under a curve the area can only be estimated, with strips.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the distance travelled.
A, a triangle: ½ × 4×20=40 m. B, a rectangle: 4×20=80 m. C, a triangle: ½ × 2×20=20 m. Distance =40+80+20=140 m.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the distance from 2 to 6 seconds.
A trapezium with parallel sides 10 and 20, width 6−2=4: 12(10+20)×4 =60 m.
