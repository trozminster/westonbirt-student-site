Circle Equations

A circle centred on the origin hides Pythagoras inside its equation. Today is about recognising that equation, sketching what it means, and extending elimination to three equations at once.

By the end you’ll know…
  • x2+y2=r2 is a circle centred on the origin with radius r.
  • It follows from Pythagoras, since every point on the circle is r away from the origin.
  • The radius is the square root of the number on the right, not that number itself.
  • Three unknowns need three independent equations, eliminated one variable at a time down to a pair.

A circle from Pythagoras

x2+y2=r2 is a circle centred on the origin. Every point on it is r from the origin, by Pythagoras.

x2+y2=r2 is a circle centred on the origin with radius r. It follows from Pythagoras, since every point on the circle is exactly r away from the origin.

Reading the radius

The radius is the square root of the number on the right. So x2+y2=25 has radius 5

The radius is the square root of the number on the right, not that number itself. A circle equation with a different number on the right is a different-sized circle, still centred on the origin.

Three unknowns, three equations

Three unknowns need three independent equations. Eliminate one variable at a time, down to a pair.

Three unknowns need three independent equations, eliminated one variable at a time down to a pair. The same elimination method from earlier lessons applies, just repeated.

Eliminating the SAME variable twice

Eliminate the same variable from two different pairs. That leaves a pair in the other two unknowns.

The same variable is eliminated from two different pairs of the three equations, which leaves two equations sharing the other two unknowns - a pair that is then solved as usual.
