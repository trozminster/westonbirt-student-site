Topic 9.8 · Factorising Quadratics · Lesson 2 of 4

Quadratics with Negatives

Last lesson every number in the quadratic was positive. Once b or c is negative, the signs in the brackets have to be decided as well as the numbers.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The signs of b and c together fix the signs of p and q.
  • A positive c means p and q share a sign, and that sign is b's.
  • A negative c means p and q have different signs, and the larger of the two takes b's sign.
  • Expanding the factorised form back out checks both the numbers and the signs.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The signs come from c, then b

A positive c means both numbers take the sign of b. A negative c means different signs. The larger number takes the sign of b.

A positive c means the two numbers share a sign, and it is the sign of b. A negative c means the signs differ, and the larger number takes the sign of b.

The pair still adds to b

With different signs, the pair differs by the size of b. e.g. for x2−2x−15, 3 and 5 differ by 2

With different signs, the two numbers add to b by their difference. So the pair is found among the factor pairs of c whose difference is the size of b.

Decide the signsc positive, b negative, so both negative
Find the pair(−3)+(−4)=−7
(−3)×(−4)=12
x2−7x+12=(x−3)(x−4)

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Expanding checks the signs

Expanding the brackets back out must give the original quadratic. A wrong sign shows up in the middle term or in the constant.

Two quick checks

The two numbers multiplied must give the constant, sign included. The two numbers added must give the middle coefficient, sign included.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Check that x2−x−20 =(x−5)(x+4).
Write the expression(x−5)(x+4)
Expand the brackets=x2 +4x −5x −20
Collect like terms=x2−x−20
Correct?yes - it matches x2−x−20
WORKED EXAMPLE D
