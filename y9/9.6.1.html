Topic 9.6 · Right-Angled Trigonometry · Lesson 1 of 5

Labelling Triangles and the Ratios

Every time a satnav calculates a route, a game engine renders a 3D scene, or a surveyor measures a hillside they can't climb, trigonometry is doing the work behind the scenes. Today you learn the three ratios that let you find any side or angle of a right-angled triangle - without ever having to measure it directly.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The hypotenuse is the longest side and always lies opposite the right angle.
  • The opposite and adjacent sides are named relative to the angle in use, so the same side changes label when the angle changes.
  • The three ratios are sin = opphyp, cos = adjhyp and tan = oppadj.
  • The ratio to use is decided by which two sides the question involves.
  • A ratio depends only on the angle and not on the size of the triangle, which is why a calculator can return it.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Naming the sides

The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. Opposite is across from the marked angle. Adjacent is the other side touching it.

In a right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is always the longest side, opposite the right angle. The other two sides are named relative to a chosen angle: "opposite" is directly across from it, "adjacent" is the other side touching it.

The three ratios

Each ratio compares two sides for a given angle. SOH CAH TOA says which sides go with sin, cos and tan.

Each pair of sides has a fixed ratio for a given angle: sine (sin) compares opposite and hypotenuse, cosine (cos) compares adjacent and hypotenuse, tangent (tan) compares opposite and adjacent. SOH CAH TOA is a common way to remember which sides go with which ratio.
