Topic 9.14 · Simultaneous Equations and Graphs · Lesson 1 of 9

Simultaneous Equations Graphically

Two equations can each be drawn as a line. Today is about the one point that belongs to both lines at once - the point where they cross - and what it means for a pair of equations.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Simultaneous equations are two equations in two unknowns that must both hold at once.
  • Every point on a line satisfies that line's own equation, so the point where two lines cross satisfies both.
  • That crossing point is therefore the solution, read off as a coordinate pair.
  • A line can only be drawn once its equation gives y from x, so an equation with x and y on the same side is rearranged first.
By the end you can
G5Draw two linear graphs and read off their point of intersection.
G6Rearrange an equation with x and y on the same side before drawing it.

One point on both lines

Simultaneous equations must both hold at once. Where two lines cross, the point lies on both.

Simultaneous equations are two equations in two unknowns that must both hold at once. Every point on a line satisfies that line's own equation, so the point where two lines cross satisfies both equations together.

Rearranging before drawing

An equation like 2y+4x=8 can't be plotted directly. Rearrange it into y = mx + c first.

An equation with x and y on the same side, such as 2y+4x=8, cannot be plotted directly. Rearranged into y = mx + c, its gradient and intercept are then read off in the usual way.

The pair, drawn together

Both equations may need rearranging before drawing. Then draw each line from a table of values.

Both equations of a pair might need rearranging before either is drawn. Once both are in y = mx + c form, the same table-of-values method draws each line.
