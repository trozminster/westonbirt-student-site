Volume of Cuboids

Volume fills three dimensions rather than two, so a cuboid's volume needs all three of its lengths, in matching units.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Volume is the space a solid occupies, measured in cubic units.
  • The volume of a cuboid is length × width × height.
  • All three dimensions must be in the same unit before multiplying.
  • A compound solid splits into cuboids whose volumes are added, or is treated as a larger cuboid with a piece removed.
  • Its surface area is not the sum of the parts' surface areas, because the joining faces are hidden inside.

Volume fills three dimensions

Volume is the space inside a solid. For a cuboid, multiply length × width × height.

Volume is the space inside a solid, found for a cuboid by multiplying length × width × height.

Split or complete the shape

Split a compound solid into cuboids and add their volumes. Or treat it as a larger cuboid with a piece removed.

A compound solid splits into simple cuboids whose volumes add, or is treated as a larger cuboid with a piece removed.

Hidden faces don't count twice

Joined faces are hidden inside the solid. Leave them out of the surface area.

A compound solid's surface area is not the sum of its parts' areas, because the faces where they join are hidden inside and must be left out.
