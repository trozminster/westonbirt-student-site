Best Buys and Inverse Proportion

Bigger packs are not always better value, and not every relationship between two quantities works the same way. Today is about finding a fair way to compare prices, and spotting when more of one thing means less of another.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A unit cost makes different pack sizes comparable, so the best buy is the lowest cost per unit.
  • An exchange rate is a direct proportion, so it works the same way in both directions.
  • In direct proportion, multiplying one quantity multiplies the other by the same factor, so their ratio stays constant.
  • In inverse proportion, multiplying one divides the other by the same factor, so their product stays constant.
  • More of one meaning less of the other signals inverse proportion; more meaning more signals direct.

Inverse proportion

In inverse proportion, multiplying one quantity divides the other. So their product stays constant.

In inverse proportion, multiplying one quantity divides the other by the same factor, so their product stays constant. More of one meaning less of the other signals inverse proportion.

Telling them apart

More meaning more signals direct proportion; more meaning less signals inverse. Checking which pattern a context follows, before choosing a method, avoids applying the wrong one.

WORKERS AND DAYS
8 workers finish a job in 15 days. How long would 12 workers take, at the same rate?
Find the total work in worker-days8×15=120The product of workers and days stays the same.
Days for 12 workers120÷12=10 days
