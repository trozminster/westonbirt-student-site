Nets and Surface Area

A cardboard box folds flat into a net before it folds up; knowing its faces lets you find how much material covers it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A net is the 2D shape that folds up to make a solid.
  • Surface area is the total area of all the faces, so it is measured in square units, not cubic ones.
  • A solid can have several valid nets, since its faces can be arranged in more than one way.
  • A cuboid has three pairs of identical faces, so its surface area is 2(lw + lh + wh).

A net folds up into the solid

A net is a 2D shape that folds into a 3D solid. Edges that meet must be the same length.

A net is a 2D shape that folds to make a 3D solid; matching edges must be the same length.

More than one net can work

A solid can have several different nets. Each arrangement of the faces still folds into the solid.

A cuboid can be drawn as several different valid nets, since its six faces can be arranged in more than one way that still folds correctly.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Add the three pairs of areas

A cuboid has three pairs of identical faces. Surface area = 2(lw + lh + wh). e.g. a 5×2×3 cm cuboid: 62 cm².

A cuboid's surface area is the sum of its three pairs of identical faces: 2(lw + lh + wh).

A cube uses one repeated face

A cube's six faces are identical squares. So its surface area is 6 × (edge length)²

Since all six of a cube's faces are identical squares, its surface area is simply 6 × (edge length)²

1
Explain why surface area is measured in square units, not cubic units.
Surface area is the total area of the solid's flat faces, and area (a 2D quantity) is measured in square units; cubic units measure volume, a 3D quantity.
