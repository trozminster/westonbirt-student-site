Topic 9.3 · Angles in Polygons · Lesson 3 of 4

Regular Polygons

Every traffic sign, snowflake pattern and football panel relies on a regular polygon's angles being predictable - today you'll find the quick shortcut for a regular polygon's interior angle, use it to work backwards from an angle to the number of sides, and solve trickier problems that combine everything you know about polygon angles so far.

By the end you’ll know…
  • In a regular polygon all interior angles are equal, so each one is (n - 2) × 180 ÷ n.
  • At every vertex the interior and exterior angles lie on a straight line, so they sum to 180 degrees.
  • Each interior angle can therefore be found more quickly as 180 - (360 ÷ n).
  • The number of sides follows from a given interior angle by finding the exterior angle first.

Interior angle of a regular polygon

In a regular polygon, every interior angle is equal. So each one is the total ÷ n: (n − 2) × 180 ÷ n.

In a regular polygon, all n interior angles are equal, and together they sum to (n - 2) × 180 (from yesterday). Since they are all equal, EACH interior angle is the total shared out n ways: (n - 2) × 180 ÷ n.

The quicker shortcut

Interior + exterior = 180°: they sit on a straight line, not round a point. So each interior angle is 180 − (360 ÷ n).

At every vertex of ANY polygon, the interior angle and the exterior angle sit on a straight line - so they always sum to 180° (NOT 360 - that is angles around a full point, not a straight line). Since a regular polygon's exterior angle is 360n, its interior angle can be found more quickly as 180 - (360n).

Working backwards from an interior angle

Given the interior angle, find the exterior first: 180 − interior. Then n = 360 ÷ exterior.

If you are given a regular polygon's INTERIOR angle and asked for the number of sides, you cannot divide 360 by the interior angle directly - the formula n = 360exterior angle only works with the EXTERIOR angle. So the first step is always to find the exterior angle: exterior = 180 - interior. THEN find n = 360 ÷ exterior.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the interior angle of a regular decagon (10 sides).
Method A (full formula)(10 - 2) × 180 = 1440
then 1440 ÷ 10 = 144°.
Method B (shortcut)exterior angle = 360 ÷ 10 = 36°
then interior = 180 - 36 = 144°.
Both methods agree144°.
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A regular polygon has an interior angle of 156°. Find the number of sides.
Find the exterior angle FIRST180 - 156 = 24° (NOT 360 - 156).
Then n = 360 ÷ 24 = 15 sides.

Combining angle facts

A multi-step problem may chain several angle facts. Plan which fact comes first before calculating.

A multi-step polygon problem might need you to use SEVERAL angle facts in sequence within the same question - for example, using the exterior angle to find n, then using n to find the interior angle sum, then using the sum to find a missing angle elsewhere in the diagram. Plan out which fact you need first before starting to calculate.

Keep exact values

When 360 ÷ n isn't whole, carry the exact value through. Round only the final answer.

When a division like 360n does not come out as a whole number, carry the EXACT value through the rest of the problem - rounding too early makes every later step in the chain drift further from the true answer. Only round the FINAL answer, and only if the question asks for it.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A regular polygon has an exterior angle of 15°. Find its interior angle sum.
First the number of sidesn = 360 ÷ 15
= 24.
Then the sum(24 - 2) × 180 = 22 × 180
= 3960°.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A regular polygon has 7 sides. Find its interior angle to 1 decimal place.
Exterior angles sum to 360°e = 3607
= 51.428571... Keep the full decimal - do not round yet.
Interior and exterior angles sum to 180°i = 180 - 51.428571...
= 128.571428...
Interior angle to 1 d.p.= 128.6° Round only at the very end.
