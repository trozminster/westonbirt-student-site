Topic 9.9 · Estimation and Bounds · Lesson 2 of 3

Estimating Complex Calculations

Some calculations are too awkward to round to 1 s.f. and stop. Today the rounding is chosen to make the arithmetic easy, and the direction of each rounding says whether the estimate is too big or too small.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The point of an estimate is a calculation that can be done mentally, so values are rounded to convenient figures rather than strictly the nearest ones.
  • A root is easiest to estimate by rounding to the nearest square number.
  • Rounding the numerator up, or the denominator down, makes the estimate too large.
  • Rounding the numerator down, or the denominator up, makes it too small.
  • Knowing which way each value was rounded is what makes an over- or under-estimate explainable.
By the end you can

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The direction of each rounding

Rounding up in the numerator makes the estimate bigger. Rounding up in the denominator makes it smaller.

When it cannot be decided

If two roundings push opposite ways, the estimate could be either. Say which way each value moved, and why.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Is 43÷7.8 ≈ 40÷8=5 an over- or under-estimate?
Round the numerator43 down to 40, so smaller
Round the denominator7.8 up to 8, so smallerA smaller top and a bigger bottom both make the answer smaller.
Over or underan underestimate
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Is 6.7×3.8 ≈ 7×4=28 an over- or under-estimate?
Round the first value6.7 up to 7, so bigger
Round the second value3.8 up to 4, so bigger
Over or underan overestimate
