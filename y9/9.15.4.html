Topic 9.15 · Ratio, Scales and Proportion · Lesson 4 of 6

One Share Given

Sometimes a ratio problem gives you one share instead of the total. Today is about working backwards from that one share to everything else.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Knowing the value of one share gives the value of one part, by dividing by that share's number of parts.
  • Every other share, and the total, then follow from that one part value.
  • This is the multiplicative reading of a ratio, and it is what solves problems where the total is unknown.
  • A given difference between two shares works the same way, over (a−b) parts instead of one share's parts.

Starting from one share

One known share gives the value of one part. Divide it by that share's own number of parts.

Knowing the value of one share gives the value of one part, by dividing by that share's own number of parts. Every other share, and the total, then follow from that one part value.

The multiplicative reading

This multiplicative reading solves problems where the total is unknown. A given difference works the same way, over (a−b) parts.

This is the multiplicative reading of a ratio, and it is what solves problems where the total is unknown. A given difference between two shares works the same way, over (a−b) parts instead of one share's parts.

The difference route

A given difference between two shares is the larger part minus the smaller. Dividing by that gives the value of one part.

This is the same multiplicative idea applied to a given difference rather than a given share. The difference between two shares in a:b is the larger part minus the smaller, so a given difference also gives the value of one part.

Choosing the right route

What is given decides what to divide by: total, one share or difference. Every route reaches the value of one part.

Whether the total, one share, or a difference is given decides which number of parts to divide by. All three routes reach the same value: the size of one part.
