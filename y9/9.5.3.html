Percentage Change and Reverse

So far every percentage question has given you the original amount and asked for the result. This lesson turns that around in two ways: first, working out what percentage change has happened when you know both amounts; and second, working backwards from a changed amount to recover the original it started from - the reverse percentage skill this whole unit is building towards.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Percentage change is the change divided by the original amount, then multiplied by 100
  • The original is always the denominator, which is why a rise and the matching fall are different percentages.
  • Profit and loss are percentage changes on the cost price, not on the selling price.
  • A multiplier turns the original into the new amount, so dividing the new amount by that multiplier recovers the original.

Percentage change as a formula

Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100
The change is the new amount minus the original. The original is always the denominator.

Percentage change measures how big a change is relative to where it started. It's calculated as (change / original) × 100, where the change is the new amount minus the original amount. The original amount is always the denominator - this is why a rise from 50 to 60 (a change of 10) is a 20% increase (1050×100), while the reverse fall from 60 to 50 (also a change of 10) is a smaller 16.7% decrease (1060×100), even though the change itself is the same size.

Profit and loss as percentage change on the cost price

Profit and loss are percentage changes on the cost price. Divide by what the seller paid, not the selling price.

Profit and loss are percentage changes on a specific original amount - the cost price (what the seller paid), not the selling price (what the item sold for). Profit is (selling price - cost price)/cost price × 100; loss is (cost price - selling price)/cost price × 100, and is reported as a positive percentage even though the value fell. Always identify the cost price first and use it as the denominator, whichever way the question is worded.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Reversing a multiplier

Original × multiplier = new amount. So new amount ÷ multiplier = original amount. Division undoes multiplication.

Every percentage change turns an original amount into a new amount using a multiplier: original × multiplier = new amount. Since multiplying by the multiplier moves forward from original to new, the reverse operation - dividing by the same multiplier - moves backward from new to original: new amount / multiplier = original amount. This works because division undoes multiplication, exactly as it does with any other multiplier.

Building the multiplier before dividing

Build the multiplier first. Divide the new amount by it, never by the raw percentage.

To find the original amount, first build the multiplier for the change described (exactly as in Lesson 9.5.1), then divide the new amount by it - never by the raw percentage. If a price rose by 20% to reach £96, the multiplier is 1.2, so the original is 96÷1.2, not 9620 or 96×0.2
Getting the multiplier right first is the single most important step, since dividing by the wrong number gives a wrong original however carefully the division itself is done.
FINDING THE ORIGINAL

Build the multiplier1+0.20=1.20
Call the original price pp×1.20=96
