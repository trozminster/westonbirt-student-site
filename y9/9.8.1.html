Factorising Quadratics

Expanding turned (x + 1)(x + 6) into x² + 7x + 6. Today runs that backwards: start from the quadratic and find the two brackets it came from.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quadratic has a squared term, and a monic quadratic is one where the coefficient of x² is 1.
  • x² + bx + c factorises into (x + p)(x + q), where p + q = b and pq = c.
  • This is the reverse of expanding: the middle term comes from p + q and the constant from pq.
  • The factor pairs of c are listed first, then the pair that adds to b is chosen.

Where the two numbers come from

(x + p)(x + q) expands to x² + (p + q)x + pq. So the two numbers add to b and multiply to c.

Expanding (x + p)(x + q) gives x² + (p + q)x + pq. So in x² + bx + c, the two numbers add to b and multiply to c.

Factor pairs first

A number has only a few factor pairs, so they are listed first. The pair that also adds to b is the one.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The pair becomes the brackets

The two numbers go straight into the brackets, as (x + p)(x + q). Expanding the brackets back out checks the answer.

Read b and c first

The terms of a quadratic can be written in any order. Rewrite it as x² + bx + c before choosing the pair.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Factorise x² + 7x + 10.
Find the pair2 + 5 = 7
2 × 5 = 10
x² + 7x + 10 = (x + 2)(x + 5)
Check by expandingx² + 5x + 2x + 10 = x² + 7x + 10
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Factorise 12 + x² + 8x.
Reorder the terms12 + x² + 8x = x² + 8x + 12
Find the pair2 + 6 = 8
2 × 6 = 12
x² + 8x + 12 = (x + 2)(x + 6)
