Factorising Quadratics

Expanding turned (x+1)(x+6) into x2+7x+6
Today runs that backwards: start from the quadratic and find the two brackets it came from.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quadratic has a squared term, and a monic quadratic is one where the coefficient of x2 is 1
  • x2+bx+c factorises into (x+p)(x+q), where p+q=b and pq=c.
  • This is the reverse of expanding: the middle term comes from p+q and the constant from pq.
  • The factor pairs of c are listed first, then the pair that adds to b is chosen.
By the end you can

Where the two numbers come from

(x+p)(x+q) expands to x2 +(p+q)x +pq. So the two numbers add to b and multiply to c.

Expanding (x+p)(x+q) gives x2 +(p+q)x +pq. So in x2 + bx + c, the two numbers add to b and multiply to c.

Factor pairs first

A number has only a few factor pairs, so they are listed first. The pair that also adds to b is the one.

The pair becomes the brackets

The two numbers go straight into the brackets, as (x+p)(x+q). Expanding the brackets back out checks the answer.

Read b and c first

The terms of a quadratic can be written in any order. Rewrite it as x2 + bx + c before choosing the pair.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Factorise x2+7x+10
Find the pair2+5=7
2×5=10
x2+7x+10=(x+2)(x+5)
Check by expandingx2 +5x +2x +10=x2+7x+10
