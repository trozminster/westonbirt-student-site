Topic 9.11 · Speed-Time Graphs and Area · Lesson 5 of 5

Mixed Graph Problems

Two graphs that look alike can mean different things. Today every problem starts by reading the axes, because they decide what a gradient and an area mean.

By the end you’ll know…
  • What a gradient represents depends entirely on what the two axes measure: it is always the y quantity per unit of the x quantity.
  • What an area represents depends the same way: it is the y quantity multiplied by the x quantity.
  • So on a distance-time graph the gradient is a speed, and the area means nothing useful.
  • On a velocity-time graph the gradient is an acceleration and the area is a distance.
  • Reading the axis labels and their units first is what decides which applies.

The axes decide

A gradient is always the y quantity per unit of the x quantity. An area is always the y quantity multiplied by the x quantity.

Read the labels first

So a gradient is a speed on one graph and an acceleration on another. Read the axis labels and their units before anything else.

Learn

Several steps

A multi-step problem may use both graphs. Find the quantity each graph gives, then combine them.

Keep the units matched

Time in minutes and distance in km do not give kmh. Convert before the last step.

Check your understanding

Pick an answer. If it's wrong, the feedback tells you which mistake it was.

A distance-time stage rises 7 km in 20 minutes. What is the speed?
Not quite. 7 ÷ 20 is km per minute. 20 minutes = ⅓ hour: 7 ÷ ⅓ = 21 km/h.
Yes. 20 minutes is ⅓ of an hour: 7 × 3 = 21 km/h.
Not quite. Speed = distance ÷ time: 7 ÷ ⅓, not the other way.
Not quite. Speed = distance ÷ time. Divide, don't multiply.
A ride is 12 km in 50 minutes, a 10-minute stop, then 6 km in 30 minutes. What is the average speed?
Not quite. Include the stop: 18 km in 90 minutes = 1.5 h. 18 ÷ 1.5 = 12 km/h.
Yes. 18 km in 1.5 hours, stop included: 12 km/h.
Not quite. 18 ÷ 90 is km per minute. Use hours: 18 ÷ 1.5 = 12.
Not quite. Don't average the speeds. Total distance ÷ total time: 18 ÷ 1.5.
What is the average speed over the 10 seconds?
Not quite. 8 m/s is the top speed. Average = distance ÷ time.
Not quite. 40 m is the distance (the area). Divide by 10 s: 4 m/s.
Not quite. That's a gradient. Average speed = area ÷ time.
Yes. Distance = ½ × 10 × 8 = 40 m. 40 ÷ 10 = 4 m/s.

= 3 + 30 + 4.5 = 37.5 m

EXT
A car moving at 20 m/s slows steadily to a stop, travelling 80 m as it brakes. How long does it take to stop? Find the deceleration.
Area: ½ × t × 20 = 80
10t = 80
t = 8 seconds
Deceleration: 20 ÷ 8 = 2.5 m/s²
