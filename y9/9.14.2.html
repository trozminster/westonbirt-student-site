Topic 9.14 · Simultaneous Equations and Graphs · Lesson 2 of 9

Graphical Solutions

Not every pair of lines behaves the same way - some cross once, some never meet, and some sit on top of each other. Today is about reading what a graph is telling you, in the maths and in the real world it might represent.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The solution is written as a coordinate pair, or as separate x = and y = values.
  • Parallel lines never meet, so a pair of equations with equal gradients has no solution.
  • Two equations describing the same line meet everywhere, so they have infinitely many solutions.
  • In a real context the axes carry meaning, so the intersection is where the two situations give the same value.
  • A graphical solution is only as accurate as the scale allows, so it may need reading to the nearest half square.

Three outcomes

Two straight lines cross once, never meet, or lie on top of each other. Parallel lines share a gradient and have no solution.

Two straight lines can cross once, never meet, or lie exactly on top of each other. Parallel lines share a gradient and never meet, so that pair has no solution.

How accurate is a reading

Write the solution as a coordinate pair, or as x = and y =. A reading is only as accurate as the scale.

The solution is written as a coordinate pair, or as separate x = and y = values. A graphical solution is only as accurate as the scale allows, so it may need reading to the nearest half square.

The axes carry meaning

In context, each axis is a quantity, like time or cost. The crossing point is where both situations give the same value.

In a real context, each axis represents a quantity - time, cost, distance - not just a number. The point of intersection is where the two situations described give the same value.

Interpreting, not just reading

Interpreting means saying what the solution means in context. Give units, not just a coordinate pair.

Interpreting a graphical solution means saying what it means in context, with units, not just stating a coordinate pair.

A distance-time graph for two runners, in seconds and metres, crosses at (4, 100). What does this mean?
Not quite. x is time and y is distance. (4, 100) means 4 s and 100 m.
Yes. At 4 seconds both runners are at 100 m.
Not quite. It's a moment when they meet, not the whole race.
Not quite. A crossing shows they're level at that moment, not that anyone stopped.
Two equations describe exactly the same line. How many solutions do the simultaneous equations have?
Not quite. Parallel lines give none. The same line meets itself everywhere.
Not quite. The lines lie on top of each other, so every point works.
Yes. Every point on the line fits both equations.
Not quite. The lines are the same, so every point on them is a solution.
