Topic 9.14 · Simultaneous Equations and Graphs · Lesson 2 of 9

Graphical Solutions

Not every pair of lines behaves the same way - some cross once, some never meet, and some sit on top of each other. Today is about reading what a graph is telling you, in the maths and in the real world it might represent.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The solution is written as a coordinate pair, or as separate x = and y = values.
  • Parallel lines never meet, so a pair of equations with equal gradients has no solution.
  • Two equations describing the same line meet everywhere, so they have infinitely many solutions.
  • In a real context the axes carry meaning, so the intersection is where the two situations give the same value.
  • A graphical solution is only as accurate as the scale allows, so it may need reading to the nearest half square.

Learn

The axes carry meaning

In context, each axis is a quantity, like time or cost. The crossing point is where both situations give the same value.

In a real context, each axis represents a quantity - time, cost, distance - not just a number. The point of intersection is where the two situations described give the same value.

Interpreting, not just reading

Interpreting means saying what the solution means in context. Give units, not just a coordinate pair.

Interpreting a graphical solution means saying what it means in context, with units, not just stating a coordinate pair.
