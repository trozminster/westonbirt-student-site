Tangent and Radius Theorems

A tangent brings a new right angle into circle problems – and, drawn twice from the same point, a symmetrical kite shape with more equal lengths and angles than it first appears to have.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point.
  • A tangent meets the radius at that point at a right angle.
  • Two tangents drawn from the same external point are equal in length.
  • Those two tangents and the two radii form a kite, symmetrical about the line to the centre.
  • The isosceles triangle made by the two equal tangents gives further equal angles.

The tangent-radius right angle

A tangent touches a circle at exactly one point. It meets the radius there at 90°.

A tangent touches a circle at exactly one point, and meets the radius drawn to that point at exactly 90°. This is a new right angle to add to a diagram, and often the start of a Pythagoras problem.

Two tangents from one point

Two tangents from one point are equal in length. With the two radii, they form a kite. The kite is symmetrical about the line to the centre.

Two tangents drawn from the same external point are equal in length. Together with the two radii (also equal), they form a kite, symmetrical about the line joining the external point to the centre – which bisects both the angle at the external point and the angle at the centre.

The kite from two tangents

The kite's line of symmetry joins the external point to the centre. It splits the kite into two congruent right-angled triangles.

Two tangents from the same external point, plus the two radii to their points of contact, form a kite. Its line of symmetry (external point to centre) splits it into two congruent right-angled triangles, each with equal tangent length as one side.

Using the kite's symmetry

The two halves of the kite are congruent. So an angle in one half gives its mirror angle free.

Since the two tangent lengths are equal and the two triangles are congruent, an angle found in one half of the kite gives its mirror-image angle in the other half for free.
TANGENTS FROM A POINT
O is the centre of a circle. TA and TB are tangents from external point T, touching at A and B. Angle ATO=35°. Find angle ATB and angle AOB.
Tangents from a pointTA=TBSo the kite TAOB is symmetrical about OT.
Kite symmetryangle ATB=2×35=70°
Tangent meets radius at 90°angle OAT=90°
Angles in a triangleangle AOT=180−90−35=55°
