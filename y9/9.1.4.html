Topic 9.1 · Prime Factors, HCF and LCM · Lesson 4 of 4

HCF and LCM in Context

Real problems don't stop at two numbers - a caterer might need to divide three different snack packs evenly, or three warning lights might all need to flash together. And once you're comfortable treating numbers as products of primes, you can do exactly the same HCFLCM thinking with algebraic terms like 12x³y.

By the end you’ll know…
  • With three numbers the HCF takes the lowest power of each prime shared by all three, and the LCM the highest power of every prime appearing in any of them.
  • Algebraic terms work the same way, treating each letter as a prime factor.
  • The HCF of algebraic terms takes the lowest power of each shared letter, so the HCF of 12x³y and 18x² is 6x².
  • The LCM takes the highest power of every letter appearing, so the LCM of those two terms is 36x³y.

HCF and LCM with three numbers

HCF: primes in all three numbers, at the lowest power. LCM: every prime in any of them, at the highest power.

The same rules extend directly to three (or more) numbers. The HCF is the product of each prime that appears in ALL THREE numbers, taken to the LOWEST power it has across all three. The LCM is the product of every prime that appears in ANY of the three numbers, taken to the HIGHEST power it has across all three.

Checking every number, not just two

With three numbers, compare all three for every prime. A prime missing from one number can't be in the HCF.

With two numbers it's easy to compare them directly. With three, compare all three for EVERY prime before deciding the power to use - a prime missing from even one of the three numbers can't be part of the HCF at all, however high its power in the other two.

Letters as prime factors

Treat an algebraic term like a prime factorisation. x³ means x three times, and y means y once. Different letters behave like different primes.

An algebraic term like x³y can be treated exactly like a prime factorisation: x³ means x is present three times, y means y is present once (y = y¹). Different letters behave like different primes - they don't combine with each other, and a letter's power is a separate count for that letter alone, just like a prime's power in a number.

HCF and LCM of algebraic terms

The HCF takes the lower power of each letter that appears in both terms. The LCM takes the higher power of every letter in either term.

The HCF takes the LOWER power of each letter that appears in BOTH terms, and drops any letter that isn't in both entirely (a missing letter can't be part of what's shared). The LCM takes the HIGHER power of every letter that appears in EITHER term, including a letter that only appears in one. For 12x³y and 18x²: HCF = 6x² (numbers: HCF of 12 and 18 is 6; letters: lower power of x is x²; y is missing from 18, so it's dropped). LCM = 36x³y (numbers: LCM of 12 and 18 is 36; letters: higher power of x is x³; y only appears in one term, so it's included at its power, y¹).
