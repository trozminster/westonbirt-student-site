Congruent Triangles

Two triangles can be proved identical from just three facts, and once they are, every other side and angle comes free. Today is about choosing the right three facts and writing the proof that earns them.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Congruent shapes are identical in both size and shape, whereas similar shapes share angles but not size.
  • Two triangles are congruent if they satisfy SSS, SAS (the angle between the two sides), ASA or RHS.
  • AAA proves similarity only, because equal angles fix a shape's proportions but not its size.
  • SSA is not a condition, since two different triangles can share two sides and a non-included angle.
  • Corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal, which is what makes congruence a tool for proving lengths and angles equal.

Congruent or similar

Congruent triangles are identical in size and shape. One fits exactly on the other, even turned over. Similar triangles share their angles at different sizes.

Congruent triangles are identical in size and shape, so one fits exactly on the other, even turned over. Similar triangles share their angles, but one is an enlargement of the other.

Four conditions

SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS each fix exactly one triangle. In SAS, the angle must be the one between the two sides.

SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS each fix exactly one triangle, so two triangles sharing those facts are identical. In SAS the angle must be the one between the two sides. ASA is two angles and a corresponding side: with two angles known, the third is known too, so the side may sit anywhere as long as it matches.

Equal angles, different sizes

Three equal angles fix a triangle's shape but not its size. So AAA proves two triangles similar, never congruent.

Two sides and the wrong angle

Two sides and an angle that is NOT between them can make two different triangles. So SSA is not a condition.

Two sides and an angle that is NOT between them can make two different triangles. So SSA is not a condition. RHS looks like SSA but works: the right angle is the largest angle, so the equal side cannot swing to a second place.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A reason for every fact

A congruence proof states three equal facts, each with its reason, then names the condition. A side both triangles share is equal to itself, and its reason is common.

Where the reasons come from

A reason is a given fact, a shape property or an angle fact. How the diagram looks, or a measurement, is never a reason.

A reason is something given, a property of the shape, or an angle fact such as vertically opposite or alternate angles. How the diagram looks, or a measurement, is never a reason.

Congruent, so every part equal

Congruent triangles have every corresponding side and angle equal. So ABM and ACM prove the base angles at B and C equal.

Once two triangles are proved congruent, every pair of corresponding sides and angles is equal. So ABM and ACM give angle B = angle C, the isosceles base angles proved rather than assumed. The order of the letters matches the parts: ABM with ACM pairs B with C.
