Topic 9.4 · Congruence and Parallel Lines · Lesson 2 of 4

Angles in Parallel Lines

Parallel lines never meet - and that single fact about direction, not distance, is what fixes every angle a transversal creates wherever it crosses them. Today's skill is naming which pattern (F, Z or C) a pair of angles forms, then using it to find missing angles with full reasoning.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A transversal is a line crossing two or more other lines.
  • Where a transversal crosses parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal (the F shape).
  • Alternate angles are equal (the Z shape).
  • Co-interior angles sum to 180° (the C or U shape).
  • All three follow from the parallel lines never converging, so the pattern of angles repeats at each crossing.

Transversal

A transversal is a straight line crossing two or more lines. When those lines are marked parallel, its angles follow fixed patterns.

A transversal is a straight line that crosses two or more other lines. When the two lines it crosses are parallel (shown by matching arrow marks), the angles it creates follow fixed patterns wherever the crossing happens.

Corresponding and alternate

Corresponding angles sit in the same position at each crossing (the F shape). Alternate angles sit between the lines, on opposite sides (the Z shape). Both pairs are equal.

Corresponding angles occupy the same position at each crossing (the F shape) and are equal. Alternate angles sit on opposite sides of the transversal, between the two parallel lines (the Z shape), and are equal.

Co-interior

Co-interior angles sit between the lines, on the same side (the C or U shape). They sum to 180°. They are not equal.

Co-interior angles sit on the same side of the transversal, between the two parallel lines (the C or U shape), and sum to 180° - not equal, because a straight line's worth of turning is shared between the two of them.

Why it works

A transversal makes the same angles at every parallel line it crosses. This only holds when the lines are marked parallel.

All three patterns exist because parallel lines never converge, so the angles a transversal makes with one line are mirrored exactly at every other parallel line it crosses. This only holds when the lines are actually marked parallel.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. Find x, y and z.
The parallel lines are marked with matching arrows.
Corresponding angles are equalx=62°x is in the same lower-right position at the bottom crossing.
Alternate angles are equaly=62°y is between the lines, on the opposite side of the transversal to the 62°.
Co-interior angles sum to 180°z=180−62z is between the lines, on the same side of the transversal as the 62°.
=118°
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A different parallel pair. Find x, y and z.
x: corresponding, the lower-left position at the bottom crossing. y: alternate, between the lines on the opposite side of the transversal. z: co-interior, between the lines on the same side.
Corresponding angles are equalx=73°
Alternate angles are equaly=73°
Co-interior angles sum to 180°z=180−73
=107°

Combining with straight-line and point facts

A parallel-line fact often finds only the first angle. A straight-line, point or triangle fact may finish the problem.

Parallel-line problems often need a second fact to finish - once a corresponding, alternate or co-interior angle is found, it may sit on a straight line, around a point, or inside a triangle with other unknowns still to find.

Multiple transversals

With two transversals, check each pair along ONE transversal at a time. Angles from different transversals make no F, Z or C pair.

When a diagram has more than one transversal crossing the same parallel lines, each pair of angles must be checked against ONE transversal at a time - mixing angles created by different transversals does not produce a valid F, Z or C pair.

Full reasoning

As before, every step - parallel-line fact or otherwise - must be named. A correct final angle with an unnamed reasoning chain is not a complete answer.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A second line forms a triangle with the transversal and the lower parallel line. Find a, b and c.
Three facts chained: corresponding, straight line, triangle.
Corresponding angles are equala=112°a is in the same upper-left position at the lower crossing.
Angles on a straight line sum to 180°b=180−112a and the triangle's base angle b sit on the lower line together.
=68°
Angles in a triangle sum to 180°c=180−68−55
=57°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Two transversals cross a pair of parallel lines and meet at C. Find u and c.
The triangle's base is part of the lower parallel line.
Corresponding angles are equalu=48°Corresponding angles are equal: the angle at the upper line matches the 48° at the lower line.
Angles in a triangle sum to 180°c=180−48−61
=71°
