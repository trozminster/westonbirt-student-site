Topic 9.6 · Right-Angled Trigonometry · Lesson 2 of 5

Finding a Side: Unknown on Top

You already know how to label a right-angled triangle and choose sin, cos or tan using SOH CAH TOA. Now you use that ratio to actually find a missing side - starting with the easiest case, where the unknown is on top of the fraction.

By the end you’ll know…
  • With the unknown on the numerator, the ratio is an equation solved in one step: multiply both sides by the denominator.
  • sin(x) = opphyp with the hypotenuse known therefore gives opp = hyp x sin(x).
  • The working is set out as a labelled equation before any numbers are substituted.
  • The calculator must be in degrees mode or every ratio will be wrong.
  • Rounding is done once, at the end, to the accuracy the question states.
By the end you can

G5Set out the working as a labelled equation, substitute, and round the answer to a stated accuracy.

Why the unknown-on-top case is a one-step equation

With the unknown on the top, the ratio is already a one-step equation. Multiply both sides by the known denominator, giving opp = hyp × sin(x).

A trig ratio like sin(x) = opphyp is just a fraction equal to a number. If the unknown side is on the numerator (the top) and the other two quantities - the angle and the denominator - are known, then the ratio is already an equation with the unknown isolated except for one operation: multiplying both sides by the denominator clears the fraction. So opp = hyp × sin(x) when the hypotenuse is known and the opposite is unknown. The same logic gives adj = hyp × cos(x), and opp = adj × tan(x).

Setting out the working properly

Write the equation with the unknown labelled before any numbers go in. Keep the calculator in degrees, and round only the final answer.

Before any numbers go in, the equation is written in words or symbols with the unknown labelled: opp = hyp × sin(x). Only then are the known values substituted. The calculator must be in degrees mode - if it is in radians, sin, cos and tan all return completely different numbers and every answer will be wrong, even though the button presses look identical. The final answer is rounded once, to the accuracy the question asks for (commonly 3 significant figures), never rounded partway through the calculation.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The unknown-on-top case with tan

In tan(x) = oppadj, an unknown opp is on the top. So opp = adj × tan(x), with no hypotenuse involved.

tan(x) = oppadj. If the adjacent side is known and the opposite is unknown, the unknown is again on the numerator, so the same one-step rule applies: multiply both sides by the adjacent side. opp = adj × tan(x). Unlike sin and cos, tan never involves the hypotenuse at all - it only ever relates the two legs of the triangle.

The angle can be marked at either acute vertex

Start from the marked angle, wherever it is. The side across from it is opposite. The other side touching it, not the hypotenuse, is adjacent.

A triangle only has two acute angles, and either one might be the angle you are given. Whichever vertex the angle is marked at, the side touching that angle (other than the hypotenuse) is adjacent, and the side across from it is opposite - the labelling always starts from the marked angle, not from a fixed position in the drawing. Rotating or reflecting the diagram does not change which side is which; only the position of the marked angle does.
