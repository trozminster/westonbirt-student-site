Velocity-Time Graphs

A velocity-time graph looks like a distance-time graph, and reads completely differently. Today a flat line means moving, and the slope means speeding up.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A velocity-time graph shows velocity against time, not distance against time.
  • A horizontal line therefore means constant speed, not stationary.
  • The gradient is the acceleration, in velocity units per unit of time.
  • A negative gradient is deceleration, and a gradient of zero is constant speed.
  • The area beneath the graph is the distance travelled.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A different graph

A velocity-time graph shows speed against time, not distance. So a horizontal line means a constant speed, not stationary.

Slowing is not returning

A line sloping down means slowing down. The object is still moving forward until the line reaches zero.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Describe the motion in each stage.
0 to 4 s: speeds up steadily from rest to 20 m/s. 4 to 8 s: a constant 20 m/s. 8 to 10 s: slows down steadily to a stop.
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Describe the motion, and say when it is fastest.
0 to 2 s: a constant 10 m/s. 2 to 6 s: speeds up steadily to 20 m/s. 6 to 10 s: slows down steadily to 5 m/s. Fastest at 6 seconds, at 20 m/s.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The gradient is the acceleration

The gradient of a velocity-time graph is the acceleration. It is the change in velocity divided by the change in time, in m/s²

A negative gradient

A negative gradient is a deceleration. The object is slowing down.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the acceleration in the first stage.
From rest to 20 m/s in 4 seconds: acceleration =20÷4=5 m/s²
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the acceleration from 2 to 6 seconds.
The velocity changes by 20−10=10 m/s over 6−2=4 seconds: acceleration =10÷4=2.5 m/s²
YOUR TURN · e
A velocity goes from 0 to 24 m/s in 8 seconds. Find the acceleration.
24÷8=3 m/s²
YOUR TURN · f
A velocity goes from 6 m/s to 21 m/s in 5 seconds. Find the acceleration.
15÷5=3 m/s²
YOUR TURN · g
A velocity falls from 20 m/s to 5 m/s in 3 seconds. Find the acceleration.
−15÷3=−5 m/s²: a deceleration of 5 m/s²
YOUR TURN · h

Which has the greater acceleration, and by how much?
