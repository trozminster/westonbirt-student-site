Topic 9.3 · Angles in Polygons · Lesson 2 of 4

Interior Angle Sum

Building on yesterday's exterior angles, today we flip to the INSIDE of a polygon - architects use interior angle sums to design roof trusses and tiling patterns, and you cannot find a single missing angle in any polygon diagram without first knowing what all its interior angles should add up to.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Any polygon splits into (n−2) triangles by drawing diagonals from a single vertex.
  • Each of those triangles contributes 180°, so the interior angles sum to (n−2)×180°.
  • This holds for irregular polygons just as much as regular ones.
  • A missing interior angle is found by subtracting the known angles from that sum.

Splitting into triangles

Draw every diagonal from one vertex to split a polygon into triangles. An n-sided polygon gives (n−2) triangles.

Any polygon can be split into triangles by drawing every diagonal from ONE single vertex. A polygon with n sides splits into exactly (n−2) triangles this way - two fewer triangles than the number of sides, because the two sides meeting at the chosen vertex do not need a diagonal.

Interior angle sum formula

Each triangle's angles sum to 180°. So the interior angles sum to (n−2)×180°. This works for any polygon, regular or not.

Each triangle's angles sum to 180°. Since the polygon is made of (n−2) triangles, its interior angles sum to (n−2)×180° in total. This works for ANY polygon, regular or irregular - the triangulation method does not care whether the sides or angles are equal.
WORKED EXAMPLE A

Learn

Missing interior angle

Find the total first, using (n−2)×180
Then subtract the known angles to leave the missing one.

If you know all but one interior angle of a polygon, you can find the missing one. First calculate the polygon's TOTAL interior angle sum using (n−2)×180 for its actual number of sides. Then subtract all the KNOWN angles from that total - whatever is left is the missing angle.

Not always 360

The total depends on the number of sides. Only a quadrilateral's angles sum to 360

The total you subtract from depends on the number of sides - it is NOT always 360
Only a quadrilateral's interior angles sum to 360; a polygon with more or fewer sides has a different total, given by (n−2)×180
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A quadrilateral has angles 100°, 85°, 95° and a. Find a.
Interior angles sum to (n−2)×180°sum=(4−2)×180
=2×180
=360°This is the one shape where the total genuinely is 360°.
Subtract the anglesa=360−(100+85+95)
Add inside the bracket=360−280
Angle a=80°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find angle a in this pentagon.
A pentagon has angles 100°, 110°, 90°, 95° and a.
Interior angles sum to (n−2)×180°sum=(5−2)×180
=3×180
=540°NOT 360° - this is a pentagon, not a quadrilateral.
Subtract the anglesa=540 −(100+110+90+95)
Add inside the bracket=540−395
Angle a=145°
