Topic 9.13 · Prisms and Cylinders · Lesson 4 of 5

Volume of Prisms and Cylinders

A prism's cross-section is the same shape all the way along its length, so its volume is just that area multiplied by the length; a cylinder is the round case.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A prism has the same cross-section along its whole length.
  • The volume of any prism is cross-sectional area x length.
  • A cylinder is a prism with a circular cross-section, so its volume is pi x r² × h.
  • The cross-section is the face that repeats, which is not always the face the solid appears to stand on.
  • A contextual problem may need the cross-sectional area found first, from a compound 2D shape.
