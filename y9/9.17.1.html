Topic 9.17 · Circle Theorems · Lesson 1 of 6

Radii and Semicircles

Every circle theorem is really an old fact in disguise – angles in a triangle, isosceles triangles, angles on a straight line – applied to the special triangles a circle's radii create. Today is the first two theorems, and the habit of naming the reason every time.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Every radius of a circle is the same length.
  • So any triangle formed by two radii is isosceles, and its base angles are equal.
  • The angle in a semicircle is a right angle: an angle at the circumference standing on a diameter is 90°.
  • Each circle theorem is a named fact, and that name is the reason quoted in the working.

Two radii make an isosceles triangle

Every radius of a circle is the same length. So two radii and a chord make an isosceles triangle. Its base angles are equal.

Every radius of a circle is the same length. So any triangle formed by joining the centre to two points on the circumference has two equal sides (both radii), making it isosceles – its base angles, opposite the equal sides, are equal.

The angle in a semicircle

An angle in a semicircle is always 90°. Quote each theorem by name: the name is the reason.

When one side of a triangle inscribed in a circle is a diameter, the angle at the opposite vertex (on the circumference) is always 90°, whatever the triangle's shape. Each theorem is quoted BY NAME in working – "isosceles triangle (two radii)" or "angle in a semicircle" – since the name is the reason.
TWO RADII
O is the centre of a circle. A and B are on the circumference, with OA and OB both radii. Angle OAB=35°. Find angle AOB.
Radii are equalOA=OBSo triangle OAB is isosceles.
Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equalangle OBA=35°
Angles in a triangle sum to 180°angle AOB=180−35−35
Angle AOB=110°

Learn

Using the semicircle theorem to find more

Place the semicircle's right angle first. Then solve the triangle like any right-angled triangle.

Once the right angle from the semicircle theorem is placed, the rest of the triangle is solved exactly like any right-angled triangle – angles in a triangle sum to 180, and if two sides are known, Pythagoras or trigonometry can follow.

Building up a full working

Name the theorem and use it to find one new fact. Then continue with ordinary angle facts until the question is answered.

A circle theorem answer states the theorem by name, applies it to find one new fact, and then continues with ordinary angle facts (triangle, straight line) until the question is answered – never stopping at the first theorem if more is needed.
TRIANGLE ON A DIAMETER
AB is a diameter of a circle, and C is a point on the circumference. Angle BAC=25°. Find angle ACB and angle ABC.
The angle in a semicircle is 90°angle ACB=90°AB is a diameter and C is on the circumference.
Angles in a triangle sum to 180°angle ABC=180−90−25
Angle ABC=65°

angle ADB=90° (angle in a semicircle); angle DAB=180−90−32=58°
YOUR TURN · f

Isosceles: angle OEF=(180−80)÷2=50°
YOUR TURN · g

angle ACB=90° (angle in a semicircle) – it does not depend on the 40° at all
YOUR TURN · h
O is the centre. G and H on the circumference, radii OG and OH. Angle OGH=15°. Find angle GOH.
Isosceles: angle OHG=15° too; angle GOH=180−15−15=150°
