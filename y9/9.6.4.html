Topic 9.6 · Right-Angled Trigonometry · Lesson 4 of 5

Finding Angles

So far the angle has always been given and a side has been unknown. Today that flips: both sides are known and the angle itself is missing - which needs a new kind of button on the calculator altogether.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Given two sides, the ratio is known but the angle is not.
  • The inverse functions sin-1, cos-1 and tan-1 recover the angle from its ratio.
  • The ratio is formed first from the two known sides, and the inverse function applied to it.
  • Choosing the ratio and choosing the solving route are separate decisions: which two sides are known, then where the unknown sits.
  • Any angle other than the right angle is less than 90 degrees, which checks the answer.

Why an inverse function is needed

sin, cos and tan turn an angle into a ratio. The inverse functions turn a known ratio back into its angle.

sin, cos and tan turn an angle into a ratio. To go the other way - turn a known ratio back into the angle that produced it - a different operation is needed: the inverse function. sin⁻¹ undoes sin, cos⁻¹ undoes cos, and tan⁻¹ undoes tan. Pressing sin, cos or tan on a ratio does not recover an angle; only the inverse (usually SHIFT + the ratio button on a calculator) does.

Forming the ratio, then inverting it

Form the ratio of the two known sides first. Then apply the inverse to it: x = sin⁻¹(opphyp). The angle must be under 90°.

Given two known sides, the ratio is formed first, exactly as before: write the ratio of the two known sides as a fraction: opposite and hypotenuse give sin, adjacent and hypotenuse give cos, opposite and adjacent give tan. Only then is the inverse function applied to the whole fraction to recover the angle: x = sin⁻¹(opphyp). Since the unknown angle in a right-angled triangle (other than the right angle itself) is always less than 90°, this is a quick check on the final answer.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Two separate decisions, every time

First choose the ratio from the two sides known or wanted. Then check if the unknown is on the top or the bottom.

Finding any side of a right-angled triangle needs two decisions made independently. First, which ratio: look at which two sides are known or wanted (opposite + hypotenuse = sin, adjacent + hypotenuse = cos, opposite + adjacent = tan). Second, which route: is the unknown on the numerator (multiply) or the denominator (divide)? Neither decision can be skipped or guessed from the other - a problem might use sin with the unknown on top, sin with the unknown on the bottom, cos with either, or tan with either, and only checking the diagram tells you which.

No labels this time

From now on, no side or ratio is named for you. Diagnose the triangle from the diagram before any calculation.

Up to now, problems have often told you directly which ratio to use or whether the unknown was on top or bottom. From here on, that scaffolding is removed - a right-angled triangle with an angle and one side given, and a second side asked for, has to be fully diagnosed from the diagram alone before any calculation begins.

Choose the ratiosin θ = opphyp opp and hyp, so sin.
Substitutesin(29°) = opp20 Which route? sin θ = opphyp and opp is unknown - on the numerator - so multiply.
Rearrangeopp = 20 × sin(29°) opp is on the numerator, so multiply.

HYPOTENUSE UNKNOWN

Choose the ratiosin θ = opphyp opp and hyp, so sin.
