Combining Compound Measures

A lorry's load is known by its volume and its material, but a bridge cares about its weight. Two compound measures chain together, and on a curved graph a rate has to be read at a single moment.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two compound measures combine when the output of one is the input of the other, as volume gives mass through density.
  • The units show the chain: the unit that cancels between the two is the shared quantity.
  • On a curved graph the rate of change is not constant, so it can only be read at a single point.
  • The gradient of a tangent drawn at that point gives the rate of change at that instant.

The shared quantity

Two measures combine when the output of one is the input of the other. Volume gives mass through density, and mass then gives a force or a cost.

The units show the chain

Write the units at each step. The unit that cancels is the shared quantity.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Oak has density 0.7 g/cm³ and costs £4 per kg. Find the cost of 5000 cm³ of oak.
Multiply density by volumemass = 0.7 × 5000

Cost= £14

YOUR TURN · a
Steel has density 8 g/cm³. Find the mass, in kg, of a steel block of volume 750 cm³.
8 × 750 = 6000 g = 6 kg
YOUR TURN · b
Copper has density 9 g/cm³ and costs £7 per kg. Find the cost of 2000 cm³ of copper.
9 × 2000 = 18000 g = 18 kg, so 18 × 7 = £126.
YOUR TURN · c
A lorry travels at 80 km/h for 45 minutes and uses 30 litres per 100 km. Find the fuel used.
80 × 0.75 = 60 km, so 30 ÷ 100 × 60 = 18 litres.
YOUR TURN · d
A concrete slab has volume 0.5 m³ and density 2400 kg/m³. Its weight in newtons is 10 times its mass in kg. It rests on 2 m². Find the pressure.
mass = 1200 kg, weight = 12000 N, so 12000 ÷ 2 = 6000 N/m².

A rate on a curve

On a curved graph the rate of change is different at every point. It can only be read at one moment.

The tangent gives it

Draw a straight line that touches the curve at that point. Its gradient, read from the axis scales, is the rate at that moment.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
The curve shows a car's distance over time. Estimate its speed at 4 seconds.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Why is the line from (0, 0) to (4, 16) not the speed at 4 seconds?
YOUR TURN · e
A tangent to a distance-time curve passes through (1, 2) and (5, 22). Find the speed at that point.
20 ÷ 4 = 5 metres per second
YOUR TURN · f
A tangent to a temperature-time curve passes through (0, 80) and (10, 30), with time in minutes and temperature in °C. Find the rate of change.
-50 ÷ 10 = -5°C per minute: the temperature is falling at 5°C a minute.
YOUR TURN · g
On a curve, the tangent at t = 1 has gradient 3 and the tangent at t = 4 has gradient 9. What does that say about the speed?
The speed is increasing: 3 metres per second at 1 second, and 9 at 4 seconds.
YOUR TURN · h
A student draws a line through two points on the curve and calls its gradient the speed at t = 3. What have they found instead?
The average speed between those two points, not the speed at t = 3.

Check your understanding

Pick an answer. If it's wrong, the feedback tells you which mistake it was.

A tangent passes through (2, 5) and (6, 33) on a distance-time graph in metres and seconds. What is the speed?
Not quite. 28 is the rise. Divide by the run: 28 ÷ 4 = 7.
Not quite. Gradient = rise ÷ run: 28 ÷ 4, not 4 ÷ 28.
Yes. Rise 33 − 5 = 28, run 6 − 2 = 4: 28 ÷ 4 = 7 m/s.
Not quite. Use the two points on the tangent: (33 − 5) ÷ (6 − 2) = 7.
What is a tangent to a curve?
Not quite. That's a chord. A tangent touches the curve at just one point.
Yes. A tangent just touches the curve at one point and shows the gradient there.
Not quite. A curve's gradient changes. A tangent gives it at one point.
Not quite. A tangent is a line touching the curve at one point. Read gradients from the axis scales.
Each grid square is 2 seconds across and 10 metres up. A tangent rises 3 squares in 1 square across. What is the speed?
Not quite. Use the scales: 3 squares up is 30 m, 1 across is 2 s.
Not quite. Use both scales: 30 m in 2 s.
Not quite. Divide: 30 m ÷ 2 s = 15 m/s.
Yes. 3 × 10 = 30 m up, 1 × 2 = 2 s across: 30 ÷ 2 = 15 m/s.
