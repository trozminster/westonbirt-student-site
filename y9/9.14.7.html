Simultaneous Equations in Context

Tickets, drinks, and deliveries all hide the same pattern: two conditions about the same two unknowns. Today is about spotting the two conditions inside the words and turning them into equations.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two unknowns need two independent equations, which a worded problem supplies as two separate conditions.
  • Two equations are independent only if neither is a rearrangement or a multiple of the other.
  • Costs and totals give the commonest pair: one equation for the quantities, one for the money.
  • The answer is interpreted back in context, with units, and checked for sense.
By the end you can
G6Form simultaneous equations from contexts such as costs and totals.
G6Solve them and interpret the answer back in context.

Costs and totals

CINEMA TICKETS
A cinema sells adult tickets at 8 pounds and child tickets at 5 pounds. 30 tickets are sold for a total of 195 pounds. Find how many of each.
Write the equationsa + c = 30 (1)
8a + 5c = 195 (2) a adult tickets and c child tickets.
Rearrange (1)c = 30 - a
Substitute into (2)8a + 5(30 - a) = 195
Expand the bracket8a + 150 - 5a = 195
Collect like terms3a + 150 = 195
Subtract 150 from both sides3a = 45
Divide by 3a = 15
Tickets of each15 adult and 15 child
YOUR TURN · a
A shop sells pens at £2 and rulers at £1. 25 items are sold for £38. Find how many of each.
p+r=25, 2p+r=38; subtract: p=13, r=12
YOUR TURN · b
A car park charges £3 for cars and £5 for vans. 40 vehicles pay a total of £156. Find how many of each.
c+v=40, 3c+5v=156; c=40-v; 3(40-v)+5v=156, 2v=36, v=18, c=22
YOUR TURN · c
A bakery sells small loaves at £2 and large loaves at £3.50. 20 loaves are sold for £55. Find how many of each.
s+l=20, 2s+3.5l=55; s=20-l; 2(20-l)+3.5l=55, 1.5l=15, l=10, s=10
YOUR TURN · d
A stall sells single scoops at £1.50 and double scoops at £2.50. 50 scoops are sold in a day for £99. Find how many of each.
x+y=50, 1.5x+2.5y=99; x=50-y; 1.5(50-y)+2.5y=99, y=24, x=26

Beyond costs and totals

Not every context is about costs. Ages, distances and mixtures also give two conditions each.

Not every context is about costs. Ages, distances, and mixtures all supply their own two conditions in the same way, once the two unknowns are named.
AGES PROBLEM
A father is 4 times as old as his son. In 8 years he will be 3 times as old. Find their current ages.
Write the equationsf = 4s (1)
f + 8 = 3(s + 8) (2) f is the father's age now, s the son's.
Substitute (1) into (2)4s + 8 = 3(s + 8)
Expand the bracket4s + 8 = 3s + 24
Subtract 3s from both sidess + 8 = 24
Subtract 8s = 16
Substitute into (1)f = 4 × 16
= 64
Their agesson 16, father 64
YOUR TURN · e
A mother is 3 times as old as her daughter. In 10 years she will be twice as old. Find their current ages.
m=3d; m+10=2(d+10); 3d+10=2d+20, d=10, m=30
YOUR TURN · f
Two numbers, x and y, satisfy x = 2y and x + y = 45. Find x and y.
x=2y; 2y+y=45, y=15, x=30
YOUR TURN · g
The sum of two ages is 60, and one is 6 more than twice the other. Find the ages.
