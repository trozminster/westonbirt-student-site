Topic 9.12 · Algebraic Fractions · Lesson 4 of 4

Factorising to Cancel

A sum can only cancel once it has been rewritten as a product, so factorising always comes before cancelling.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A fraction whose numerator or denominator is a sum can only cancel once that sum has been factorised.
  • Factorising turns the sum into a product, and only then are the common factors visible.
  • A bracket appearing in both numerator and denominator cancels, leaving 1 in its place.
  • A quadratic factorises into two brackets, either of which may be the common factor.
  • A difference of two squares factorises as (x+a)(x−a), which is often what makes the cancelling possible.

Factorise before you cancel

A sum cannot cancel until it is written as a product. Factorise the numerator or denominator first, then look for a shared bracket.

A shared bracket cancels to 1

A bracket that appears top and bottom cancels completely. It leaves 1 in its place, not 0

When the same bracket appears top and bottom, it cancels completely, leaving 1 in its place, not 0

Factorise the quadratic first

A quadratic numerator or denominator factorises into two brackets before any cancelling is possible.

Only one bracket needs to match

Either bracket of the quadratic may be the one that cancels.

Either of the quadratic's two brackets may be the one that cancels with the other side.
