Distance-Time Graphs

A distance-time graph tells the whole story of a journey: when it moved, how fast, and when it stopped. Today is reading that story, and finding each speed from the graph.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A distance-time graph shows distance from a starting point against time.
  • Its gradient is the speed, in the units of the two axes.
  • A horizontal line means stationary, since time passes but distance does not change.
  • A steeper line means a faster speed, and a line returning to the axis means travelling back to the start.
  • Average speed over a whole journey is total distance divided by total time, including any time spent stopped.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Reading a journey

A distance-time graph shows distance from the start against time. A horizontal line means stationary. A line back down to zero means a return to the start.

A distance-time graph shows distance from the start against time. A horizontal line means stationary, and a line back down to zero means a return to the start.

Steeper is faster

A steeper line covers more distance in the same time. So the steepest stage is the fastest.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Describe each stage of the journey.
0 to 2 hours: travels 40 km away from the start, at 40÷2=20 km/h. 2 to 3 hours: stationary, 40 km from the start. 3 to 5 hours: travels a further 20 km away, at 20÷2=10 km/h, ending 60 km from the start.
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Describe each stage, and say when it is fastest.
0 to 1 hour: travels 30 km away, at 30 km/h. 1 to 2 hours: stationary, 30 km from the start. 2 to 4 hours: travels 30 km back to the start, at 30÷2=15 km/h. Fastest in the first hour: its line is the steepest.
YOUR TURN · a
On a distance-time graph, a line runs from (0,0) to (3,45), with time in hours and distance in km. Describe it.
Travelling away from the start, 45 km in 3 hours.
YOUR TURN · b
The next stage is a horizontal line from (3,45) to (4.5,45). What is happening?
Stationary, 45 km from the start, for 1.5 hours.
YOUR TURN · c
A stage runs from (3,40) to (5,0). Describe it.
Travelling back to the start, 40 km in 2 hours.
YOUR TURN · d
One stage rises 20 km in 1 hour and another 30 km in 2 hours. Which is faster?
The first: its line is steeper, 20 km/h against 15 km/h.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The gradient is the speed

The gradient of a distance-time graph is the speed. Its units are the distance unit per time unit.

Average speed for the whole journey

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. The time stopped counts.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the speed for the first stage, in km/h.
8 km in 20 minutes. 20 minutes =13 hour, so speed =8÷13=24 km/h.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the average speed for the whole journey, in km/h.
Total distance: 8 km out and 8 km back =16 km. Total time: 60 minutes =1 hour, the 10-minute stop included. Average speed =16÷1=16 km/h.
YOUR TURN · e
A stage rises 15 km in 30 minutes. Find the speed in km/h.
15 km in 0.5 hours is 30 km/h.
YOUR TURN · f
A stage falls 12 km in 40 minutes. Find the speed in km/h.
12÷4060=18 km/h.
YOUR TURN · g
A journey goes 10 km out in 1 hour, stops for 30 minutes, then 10 km back in 1.5 hours. Find the average speed.
20 km ÷ 3 hours ≈ 6.67 km/h to 2 decimal places.
YOUR TURN · h
Why is that average speed not the mean of 10, 0 and 6.67 km/h?
The stages take different times, so they cannot be averaged equally: it is total distance ÷ total time.

A stage rises 9 km in 15 minutes. What is the speed?
Not quite. Speed = distance ÷ time: 9÷0.25, not the other way.

Not quite. Distance goes on top: 16÷2
Yes. Total distance ÷ total time, stop included: 16÷2=8 km/h.
Not quite. The stop counts in the total time: 16÷2=8 km/h.
What does the gradient of a distance-time graph give?

Not quite. Time is along the bottom. The gradient is distance ÷ time: speed.
