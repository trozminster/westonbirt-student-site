Topic 9.14 · Simultaneous Equations and Graphs · Lesson 8 of 9

Plotting Non-Linear Graphs

Not every equation draws as a straight line. Today is about plotting graphs whose shape curves, and reading that shape from the powers in the equation.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A table of values generates points for any equation, not only for linear ones.
  • A quadratic, highest power x2, gives a parabola, symmetrical about a vertical line through its turning point.
  • A cubic, highest power x3, gives an S-shape.
  • A reciprocal, with x in the denominator, gives a hyperbola in two separate pieces that never touch the axes.
  • An exponential, with x in the index, rises ever more steeply and never reaches the x-axis.
  • Non-linear points are joined with a smooth curve, never with straight segments.
By the end you can

Reading the shape from the equation

The highest power of x decides the shape. x2 gives a parabola. x3 gives an S-shape.

The highest power of x decides the shape - x2 a parabola, x3 an S-shape - so the shape can be predicted from the equation before any points are plotted.

Reciprocal and exponential

A reciprocal gives two pieces that never touch the axes. An exponential rises ever more steeply above the x-axis.

A reciprocal, with x in the denominator, gives a hyperbola in two separate pieces that never touch the axes. An exponential, with x in the index, rises ever more steeply and never reaches the x-axis.
