Rearranging and Reading Straight-Line Equations

Equations of lines turn up in many shapes, such as 3x+2y=12 from a budget or a mixture. Rearranged into y = mx + c, any of them shows its gradient and intercept at a glance.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The gradient and intercept can only be read off directly when the equation is in the form y = mx + c.
  • Any linear equation can be rearranged into that form, using the same inverse operations as solving.
  • Dividing through by the coefficient of y has to be applied to every term.
  • A negative coefficient of x makes m negative, which is easily lost during the rearrangement.
By the end you can

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Only y = mx + c shows m and c

The gradient and intercept show only when y is the subject. In 2x+y=5, the 2 is not the gradient.

Balance, then divide every term

Rearranging uses the same balancing moves as solving. Dividing by the coefficient of y divides every term.

Moving the x term flips its sign

Moving the x term across changes its sign. So 3x+y=4 has gradient −3 and slopes down.

A POSITIVE Y TERM
Write 6x+3y=12 in the form y = mx + c.
Subtract 6x from both sides3y=−6x+12
Divide every term by 3y=−2x+4
Gradient and interceptm=−2
c=4
A NEGATIVE Y TERM
Now write 2x−5y=10 in the form y = mx + c.
Subtract 2x from both sides−5y=−2x+10
Divide every term by −5y=25x−2
Gradient and interceptm=25

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Read c where the line crosses

The y-intercept is where the line crosses the y-axis. There x=0, so y=c.

Gradient from two grid corners

Two points on grid corners can be read exactly. The gradient is the change up divided by the change across.

A falling line has a negative gradient

A line falling from left to right has a negative gradient. Read each scale before counting squares.
