Bounds in Calculations

Every measurement in a design is rounded. Bounds give the worst case either way, so a lift or a dose is safe for every possible true value.

By the end you’ll know…
  • For a sum or a product, the upper bound uses both upper bounds and the lower bound uses both lower bounds.
  • For a difference a - b, the upper bound is the upper bound of a minus the lower bound of b, and the lower bound is the lower bound of a minus the upper bound of b.
  • For a quotient a÷b, the upper bound is the upper bound of a divided by the lower bound of b, and the lower bound is the lower bound of a divided by the upper bound of b.
  • A result is given to a suitable degree of accuracy by rounding both of its bounds: the answer is the most accurate rounding on which the two bounds agree.

Biggest with biggest

A sum or product is largest from both upper bounds. It is smallest from both lower bounds.

A sum or a product is largest when both values are at their upper bounds. It is smallest when both values are at their lower bounds.

The rounded answer is not a bound

The answer from rounded values lies between the bounds. It is not a bound itself.

The answer from the rounded values lies somewhere between the bounds. It is not a bound itself.

Opposite bounds for a difference or a quotient

A difference is largest from big minus small. A quotient is largest from upper ÷ lower.

A difference is largest when the first value is large and the second is small. A quotient is largest from the upper bound divided by the lower bound.

Round to where the bounds agree

A result is given where both bounds round alike. Bounds 8.04 and 8.12 agree only as 8

A result is given to the accuracy at which both of its bounds round to the same value. Bounds of 8.04 and 8.12 agree only when rounded to 8
THE UPPER BOUND OF A SPEED
A runner covers 100 m, to the nearest metre, in 12.5 seconds, to 1 decimal place: find the upper bound of her speed.
Divide upper by lower100.5÷12.45≈8.072...
Upper bound8.07 m/sTo 3 significant figures.
A SUITABLE ACCURACY
Now give her speed to a suitable degree of accuracy.
Divide lower by upper99.5÷12.55≈7.928...
Round both bounds7.928... and 8.072... both give 8
Speed8 m/sTo 2 significant figures the bounds give 7.9 and 8.1, which disagree.
