Venn Diagrams and Simple Shading

A Venn diagram turns a list of sets into a picture, so overlaps and gaps can be seen at a glance. It is the tool for every set and probability question that follows.

By the end you’ll know…
  • In a Venn diagram the rectangle is the universal set and each set is a closed region inside it; every element appears exactly once, in the region of all the sets it belongs to.
  • An element in both sets goes in the overlap, and an element in neither goes inside the rectangle but outside both circles.
  • A Venn diagram can show the elements themselves or the number of elements in each region.
  • An Euler diagram draws only regions that hold elements: a subset is drawn inside its set, and sets with nothing in common do not overlap.
  • A ∪ B is shaded as everything inside either circle, overlap included; A ∩ B is the overlap alone; A' is everything outside A.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Union

A ∪ B is everything inside either circle, overlap included. The word 'or' includes both.

Intersection

A ∩ B is the overlap alone. Its elements are in both sets at once.

Complement

A' is everything inside ξ but outside A. It includes the part of B outside A.

A UNION
Shade A ∪ B on a Venn diagram of A and B.
Union means in A or in B or in both, so shade the whole of both circles, overlap included.
A COMPLEMENT
Now shade B'.
B' is everything not in B: A only and the region outside both circles.
