Pythagoras in Three Dimensions

A box, a room or a pyramid hides right-angled triangles in three dimensions. Finding the right one turns a length inside a solid into Pythagoras, used twice.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A length inside a solid is a side of a right-angled triangle inside it, redrawn flat on its own.
  • In a cuboid, the space diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle whose other sides are a face diagonal and an edge.
  • So for a cuboid a by b by c, the space diagonal d satisfies d2 =a2+b2+c2
  • A pyramid's apex is above the centre of its base, so its height, half a base diagonal and a sloping edge make a right-angled triangle.
By the end you can
G7Find the face diagonal and the space diagonal of a cuboid, or a missing edge from its space diagonal, using Pythagoras' theorem.
G7Use Pythagoras' theorem in three dimensions to find lengths in pyramids and prisms, including a pyramid's vertical height and its sloping edges.

A length inside a solid

A length inside a solid is a side of a right-angled triangle. Find that triangle, then redraw it flat.

Two steps of Pythagoras

The face diagonal comes first, across the base. It then becomes a shorter side for the space diagonal.

One formula for a cuboid

The two steps combine into d2 =a2+b2+c2
Each edge of the cuboid is squared once.

Learn

The centre of the base

A pyramid's apex sits above the centre of its base. The centre is half a base diagonal from each corner.

Height, half-diagonal and edge

The height, half a base diagonal and a sloping edge make a right-angled triangle. The sloping edge is its hypotenuse.

Subtract for the height

The height is a shorter side, so its square is a subtraction. With base 8 cm and edges 9 cm, the height is 7 cm.
