Completing the Square for ax² + bx + c

Real curves often start with 2x2 or −x2, not x2
Taking that number out first lets the same square-completing step work on any quadratic.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When the coefficient of x2 is a, a is taken out as a factor of the x2 and x terms only: ax2+bx+c =a(x2+bax) +c.
  • The square is completed inside the bracket, and the square subtracted inside is multiplied by a when the bracket is expanded.
  • The result has the form a(x+p)2 +q, with the a outside the squared bracket.
  • When the x2 coefficient is negative the negative is taken out too, so 5+4x−x2 =9−(x−2)2
By the end you can
G8Write ax2 + bx + c, where a is a positive whole number, in the form a(x+p)2 +q.
G8Complete the square for a quadratic whose x2 coefficient is negative, writing it in the form q−a(x+p)2

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Take a out of the x terms

Factor a out of the x2 and x terms only. The constant waits outside the bracket.

Complete the square inside

The bracket is completed just as before. Then the subtracted square is multiplied by a.

The a stays outside the square

The result has the form a(x+p)2 +q. Here a is 2, p is 3 and q is −13

THREE TIMES A SQUARE
Write 3x2+12x+7 in the form a(x+p)2 +q.
Take 3 out of the x terms3x2+12x+7=3(x2+4x) +7
Complete the square inside=3((x+2)2−4) +7
Multiply the −4 by 3=3(x+2)2−5
A FRACTION INSIDE
Now write 2x2−6x+1 in the form a(x+p)2 +q.
Take 2 out of the x terms2x2−6x+1=2(x2−3x) +1
Complete the square inside=2((x−32)2−94) +1
Multiply the −94 by 2=2(x−32)2 −72

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Take out the negative too

A negative x2 term means taking out −1
Every sign inside the bracket then changes.

The minus flips the subtracted square

Inside, x2−4x is (x−2)2−4
The minus in front turns that −4 into +4
