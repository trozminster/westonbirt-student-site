Arithmetic Series Problems

Savings that grow by the same amount each week, seats that increase row by row, a stack of logs: each is an arithmetic series. The skill is deciding whether the question wants one term or the total.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quantity in context that rises or falls by the same amount each time forms an arithmetic sequence: the amount at step n is a+(n - 1)d, and the running total of the first n amounts is the sum of the series.
  • A question that asks for one step's amount needs the nth term; a question that asks for a total needs the sum.
  • Where the sum is given and a, d or n is unknown, substitute everything known into n2[2a+(n - 1)d] or n2(a+l), set it equal to the sum, and solve the equation for the unknown.

One term or the total

One week's amount is a term, from a+(n - 1)d. The total so far is a sum.

Name a, d and n first

Week 1 is the first term, so week n is n−1 steps on. Naming a, d and n first avoids miscounting.

A TOTAL
A runner runs 20 km in week 1 and 2 km more in each week after. How far does she run in total over 10 weeks?
Name a, d and na=20
d=2
n=10
A total, so the sum102×(2×20+9×2)
Total distance5×58=290 km
ONE WEEK
Now in which week does she first run 50 km?
One week, so the nth term20+(n−1)×2=50
n−1=15
Week number16

The sum as an equation

When the sum is given, the formula becomes an equation. Solve it for the one unknown.

Keep the n2

The n2 stays in the equation. Dropping it gives a wrong value.

FIND D
Find d for an arithmetic series with first term 3 whose first 12 terms add to 234
Put them in the formula122×(6+11d)=234
Divide both sides by 66+11d=39
Common differenced=3
FIND N
Now find how many terms an arithmetic series has if its first term is 4, its last term 61 and its sum 650
Use the a+l formn2×(4+61)=650
32.5n=650
Number of termsn=20
