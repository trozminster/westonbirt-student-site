Parallel Lines

Railway tracks, the lanes of a running track and the shelves of a bookcase never meet. On a graph, lines like these share one number: their gradient.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two straight lines are parallel exactly when they have the same gradient.
  • Parallel lines with different y-intercepts never meet, so the pair of equations has no common solution.
  • y=3x+1 and y=3x−4 are therefore parallel, while y=3x+1 and y=4x+1 are not, even though they cross the y-axis at the same point.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Parallel means the same gradient

Parallel lines rise by the same amount for every step across. So their gradients are equal.

A shared intercept is not enough

Lines with the same y-intercept but different gradients cross. They meet on the y-axis.

Rearrange before comparing

Only y = mx + c shows the gradient. So a line given as ax + by =c is rearranged first.

ONE LINE TO REARRANGE
Show that y=3x−2 and 6x−2y=5 are parallel.
Subtract 6x from both sides−2y=−6x+5
Divide every term by −2y=3x−2.5
Compare the gradients3 and 3, so parallel

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Keep m, find a new c

A parallel line keeps the same gradient. Only its intercept changes, fixed by the point it passes through.

The point gives c

The point's coordinates satisfy y = mx + c. So substituting them leaves an equation for c.

GIVEN AS Y = MX + C
Find the line parallel to y=3x−5 through (2,4).
Keep the gradientm=3
Substitute (2,4)4=3×2+c
c=−2
Equationy=3x−2
