Solving Equations with Pre-Drawn Graphs

Some equations, such as x2−2x−1=0 or 4x=5−x, are slow to solve by algebra. A drawn graph answers them by reading where two graphs meet.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The solutions of f(x)=k are the x-coordinates of the points where the curve y=f(x) meets the horizontal line y=k.
  • The solutions of f(x)=g(x) are the x-coordinates of the points where y=f(x) and y=g(x) cross, because there both give the same y.
  • A solution read from a graph is an estimate, and it is quoted to the accuracy the grid supports.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A horizontal line meets the curve

The solutions of f(x)=k are where y=f(x) meets y=k. Only the x-coordinates are needed.

Read down to the x-axis

Each crossing gives one solution, read on the x-axis. Between grid lines the answer is an estimate.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
The graph of y=x2−2x−1 is drawn. Solve x2−2x−1=2
Draw the line y=2
It crosses the curve at x=−1 and x=3
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Use the same graph to solve x2−2x−1=0
Use the x-axis, y=0
The curve crosses it between grid lines, at about −0.4 and 2.4

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Where two graphs cross

Where y=f(x) and y=g(x) cross, both give the same y. So those x values solve f(x) =g(x).

The other side is a straight line

A linear right-hand side is drawn as a straight line. Its crossings with the curve give the solutions.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
The graph of y=x2−3x is drawn. Solve x2−3x=x−3
Draw y=x−3, through (0,−3) and (4,1). It crosses the curve at x=1 and x=3
WORKED EXAMPLE D
The graph of y=4x is drawn. Solve 4x=5−x.
Draw y=5−x. It crosses the curve at x=1 and x=4
YOUR TURN · c
The graph of y=x2 is drawn. Solve x2=2x+3
Draw y=2x+3: x=−1 and x=3
YOUR TURN · d
The graph of y=x2−4 is drawn. Estimate the solutions of x2−4=x, to 1 decimal place.
Draw y=x: x≈−1.6 and x≈2.6
