Describing Shaded Regions

Exam questions often turn the last lesson round: here is the shaded region, now write the inequalities. Each boundary line gives one inequality, and one test point inside settles every sign.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A region bounded by straight lines is described by one inequality for each boundary line.
  • For each line, find its equation, then fix the sign by testing a point inside the region.
  • A solid boundary gives ≤ or ≥; a dashed boundary gives < or >.
  • A vertical boundary x = a gives an inequality in x alone, and a horizontal boundary y=b an inequality in y alone.
By the end you can
G6Write the inequality for a region bounded by one straight line, including a horizontal or a vertical line.
G6Write the set of inequalities that defines a region bounded by two or three straight lines.

One inequality per edge

Each boundary line of R gives one inequality. A triangle needs three.

One test point for all

One point inside R tests every sign at once. It must satisfy them all.
