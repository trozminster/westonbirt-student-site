Topic 10H.3 · Sets and Venn Diagrams · Lesson 3 of 4

Compound Shading and Venn Probability

Surveys sort people into overlapping groups: who owns a cat, who owns a dog, who owns both. A Venn diagram of the counts answers probability questions about any combination at a glance.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A compound set expression is read inside out: brackets first, and a complement applies only to what it is attached to.
  • (A ∪ B)' is everything outside both circles; A' ∩ B is the part of B outside A, in B but not in A.
  • The probability of an event shown on a Venn diagram is the number of elements in its regions divided by n(ξ); with counts, add the counts of every region in the event first.
  • The counts in all the regions add to n(ξ), so a missing count is found from the total or from a given probability.
By the end you can

Inside out

A compound expression is read inside out, brackets first. A complement applies only to what it is attached to.

In B but not in A

A' ∩ B is the part of B outside A. It is 'in B but not in A', not 'in neither'.

COMPLEMENT OF A BRACKET
Shade (A ∩ B)' on a Venn diagram of A and B.
Find the bracket first: A ∩ B is the overlap. Its complement is everything else: A only, B only and the outside region.
COMPLEMENT INSIDE
Now shade A ∪ B'.
B' is everything outside B: A only and the outside region. A ∪ B' adds the rest of A, the overlap, so only B only stays unshaded.
YOUR TURN · a
On a Venn diagram of A and B, shade A ∩ B'.
The part of A outside B
A only
YOUR TURN · b
On a Venn diagram of A and B, shade (A ∪ B)' ∪ (A ∩ B).
The overlap and the region outside both circles

Count over total

A probability from a Venn diagram is a count over n(ξ). Add every region the event covers first.

The missing count

The counts in every region add to n(ξ). A given probability can also fix a missing count.
