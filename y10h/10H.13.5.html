Perpendicular Lines

A wall and its floor, a ramp and its support: builders need right angles, and on a plan a right angle is a pair of gradients. Their product is always −1

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two lines are perpendicular when they meet at right angles.
  • The product of their gradients is then −1, so a line perpendicular to a line of gradient m has gradient −1m, the negative reciprocal.
  • Turning a slope triangle through 90° swaps its run and rise and changes the sign of one, which is why the reciprocal is negative.
  • A horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular too, but the product rule does not apply, because a vertical line has no gradient.
By the end you can

A quarter turn swaps run and rise

Turning a slope triangle through 90° swaps its run and rise. One of them changes sign.

The gradients multiply to −1

Perpendicular gradients multiply to −1
So each is the negative reciprocal of the other.

Level and upright lines

A horizontal line is perpendicular to a vertical one. The product rule fails here, as vertical lines have no gradient.
