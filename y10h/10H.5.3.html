Unit Fractional Indices

If 2 cubed is 8, what power of 8 gives back 2? The index laws answer that question, and the answer turns every root into a power.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A power of 1n means the nth root: a⁽¹²⁾ is the square root of a and a⁽¹³⁾ is the cube root, because (a⁽¹ⁿ⁾)ⁿ = a⁽ⁿⁿ⁾ = a¹ = a.
  • So 25⁽¹²⁾ = 5, 8⁽¹³⁾ = 2 and 81⁽¹⁴⁾ = 3: the denominator of the index names the root.
  • The root of a fraction is the root of its numerator over the root of its denominator: (49)⁽¹/²⁾ = 23.
  • A root can be written as a power: the cube root of x is x⁽¹³⁾.
By the end you can

A half power undoes squaring

Squaring 9⁽¹²⁾ gives 9 by the power law. So 9⁽¹²⁾ is the square root of 9, which is 3.

By the power law, (9⁽¹²⁾)² = 9¹ = 9. So 9⁽¹²⁾ is the number whose square is 9, which is the square root, 3.

The denominator names the root

In a power of 1n, the n says which root to take. So 64⁽¹³⁾ is the cube root of 64, not its square root.

In a power of 1n the n says which root to take: 2 for the square root, 3 for the cube root, 4 for the fourth. So 64⁽¹³⁾ is the cube root of 64, which is 4, not its square root, 8.

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Root the top and the bottom

The root of a fraction is the root of each part. So (49)⁽¹/²⁾ is 23.

Rooting a fraction roots its numerator and its denominator separately, because the root of a product is the product of the roots. So (49)⁽¹/²⁾ is √4 over √9, which is 23.

A decimal becomes a fraction first

A decimal is easier to root as a fraction. So 0.09⁽¹²⁾ is (9100)⁽¹/²⁾, which is 310.

A decimal is easier to root once it is written as a fraction over a power of 10. So 0.09⁽¹²⁾ is (9100)⁽¹/²⁾, which is 310, or 0.3.
