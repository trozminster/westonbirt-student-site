Mid-points and Lengths of Line Segments

A sat-nav works out how far apart two places are from their coordinates alone. The halfway point and the straight-line distance both come from the change across and the change up.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The midpoint of a line segment is the point halfway along it.
  • Its coordinates are the means of the end points' coordinates: the midpoint of (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is ((x1 + x2) ÷ 2, (y1 + y2) ÷ 2).
  • A line segment drawn on a coordinate grid is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle whose shorter sides are the horizontal and vertical differences between the end points.
  • Those differences are x2 - x1 and y2 - y1, and a negative difference is used as its positive size, because a length cannot be negative.
  • Pythagoras' theorem then gives length² = (x2 - x1)² + (y2 - y1)².

Halfway in each direction

The midpoint is halfway across and halfway up. Its coordinates are the means of the end points' coordinates.

The same step from each end

The midpoint is the same step from each end. So a missing end is that step again, past the midpoint.

THE MIDPOINT

Add and halve the x's(-2 + 6) ÷ 2 = 2
Add and halve the y's(5 + (-1)) ÷ 2 = 2
Midpoint(2, 2)
A MISSING END

1 - 3 = -2

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The segment is a hypotenuse

A segment on a grid is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle. Its shorter sides are the change across and the change up.

A negative change still counts

A change can be negative, but its square is positive. So a length is never negative.

A WHOLE-NUMBER LENGTH
Find the length of AB, where A is (-1, 2) and B is (5, 10).
Find the changes6 across and 8 up
Square and addAB² = 6² + 8²
= 100
Square rootAB = 10
A LENGTH TO 3 S.F.
Now find the length of PQ, where P is (3, -2) and Q is (-1, 5).
Find the changes-4 across and 7 up
Square and addPQ² = (-4)² + 7²
= 65
Square rootPQ = √65
= 8.06 to 3 s.f.
