Length Scale Factors

Maps, models and enlarged photos are all similar shapes. One number, the scale factor, links every length on one to its partner on the other.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Similar shapes have equal corresponding angles and corresponding sides in the same ratio.
  • The length scale factor k is a length on one shape divided by the matching length on the other, and every length on the second shape, its perimeter included, is k times its partner.
  • Corresponding sides are matched by the equal angles they sit opposite, not by where they sit on the page.
  • Going from the larger shape back to the smaller, each length is divided by k.
  • When one triangle sits inside another, sharing an angle, with the third sides parallel, the two triangles are similar, and the scale factor uses the whole sides, never only the parts.

One factor for every length

Similar shapes multiply every length by the same scale factor. It is a length divided by its matching length.

Similar shapes have every length multiplied by the same scale factor. The factor is one length divided by its matching length.

Match sides by their angles

Corresponding sides sit opposite equal angles. A turned or flipped shape still matches angle for angle.

Corresponding sides sit opposite equal angles, wherever the shapes are drawn. A turned or flipped shape still matches angle for angle.

The factor works both ways

The scale factor works both ways. From larger to smaller, each length is divided by it.

The scale factor works in both directions. From the larger shape back to the smaller, each length is divided by it.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Pull the triangles apart

A triangle inside another, with a parallel side, is similar to it. Drawing the two apart shows which sides match.

When DE is parallel to BC, triangle ADE sits inside triangle ABC and shares angle A. The two triangles are similar, and drawing them apart shows which sides match.

Whole sides, never parts

The scale factor compares whole sides, never parts. A part of a side is found by subtraction.

The scale factor compares whole sides, such as AB with AD, never the parts AD and DB. A part of a side is found afterwards by subtraction.
