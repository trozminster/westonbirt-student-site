Topic 10H.7 · Changing the Subject and Complex Equations · Lesson 1 of 4

Rearranging Formulae with Powers and Roots

A formula is built for one job, but the letter you know is not always the one it gives. Rearranging it once answers the question for every value at the same time.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Changing the subject undoes the operations on the wanted letter in reverse order, doing the same to both sides each time.
  • Squaring and taking a square root are inverses, as are cubing and taking a cube root, so from A = pi x r², r = the square root of (A ÷ pi).
  • A power or a root applies to the whole of the other side, which is treated as one bracket: from v² = u² + 2as, v = the square root of (u² + 2as), not u + the square root of 2as.
  • A term added to the subject's term is removed before any root is taken.

Squares and roots undo each other

Squaring and square-rooting undo each other. So a squared subject is freed by rooting both sides.

Squaring and square-rooting are inverse operations, as are cubing and cube-rooting. So a squared subject is freed by taking the square root of both sides, once everything else has been undone.

A root covers the whole side

A root acts on the whole of the other side. It never acts on each term separately.

A root acts on everything on the other side, not on each term. So that side is treated as one bracket before the root is taken.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Undo in reverse order

Rearranging undoes the operations in reverse order. The last operation applied is undone first.

A formula builds its subject up one operation at a time. Rearranging undoes those operations in the reverse order, so the last one applied is the first one removed.

Free the root, then square

A root is cleared by squaring both sides. First, the root must stand alone.

A subject under a root is freed by squaring both sides. Whatever multiplies the root is removed first, so the root stands alone before it is squared.
