Arc Length and Sector Perimeter

A running track's bend, a bicycle chain round a gear and the edge of a fan are each a curved length cut from a circle. The fraction that gave a sector's area last lesson gives its curved edge today.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An arc is part of a circle's circumference; an arc whose angle at the centre is theta is theta/360 of the circumference, so its length is theta/360 × π×d, the same as theta/360 × 2×π×r.
  • A sector's perimeter is its arc length plus the two radii, since the sector is closed by two straight edges.
  • Arc length is a length, measured in units such as cm, while a sector's area is in square units.
By the end you can
G6Calculate the length of an arc given the radius and the angle at the centre, exactly in terms of π or to a stated accuracy.
G6Calculate the perimeter of a sector, adding both radii to the arc length, including for a major sector.

An arc is a fraction of the circumference

An arc is part of the circle's edge. Its length is the same fraction of the circumference as its angle is of 360°.

An arc is the part of the circle's edge between two radii. It takes the same fraction of the circumference as its angle takes of 360°, so its length is θ/360 × π×d.

A length, not an area

Arc length is built on π×d, not π×r2
It is a length, so its units are cm, not cm²

The arc is part of the circumference, so its formula is built on π×d, not on π×r2
Being a length, it is measured in cm, never in cm²

A sector is closed by two radii

A sector's edge is its arc and two radii. So its perimeter is the arc length plus 2×r.

A sector's boundary is its curved arc and the two straight radii that close it. So its perimeter is the arc length plus two radii, never the arc alone.

The major sector has the long arc

The major sector's arc uses 360° minus the minor angle. Its two radii are the same as the minor sector's.

The major sector's angle is 360° minus the minor angle, so its arc is the long way round. Its two straight edges are the same two radii as the minor sector's.
