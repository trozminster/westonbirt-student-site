Calculating with Decimals

A calculator gives the digits, but number sense says where the point goes. Knowing roughly how big an answer is catches a wrong one.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A decimal multiplication has as many decimal places in the answer as the two numbers have between them.
  • So the calculation can be done with whole numbers first, and the point placed at the end.
  • Dividing by a decimal is made easier by scaling both numbers by the same power of ten until the divisor is whole.
  • Scaling both numbers equally leaves the answer unchanged, because the ratio between them is unchanged.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Whole numbers first, the point last

A decimal product is a whole-number product, rescaled. Its decimal places are the two numbers' places together.

A decimal product is a whole-number product with the point put back. The answer has as many decimal places as the two numbers together.

Both numbers' places count

Both numbers' places count. So 0.3×0.2 has two places: the answer is 0.06

The places in both numbers count, so 0.3×0.2 has two places in its answer. Lining up the points, as for adding, gives 0.6 and is wrong.

Less than 1 makes it smaller

Multiplying by a number below 1 makes an amount smaller. So 6×0.4 is 2.4, less than 6

Multiplying by a number between 0 and 1 gives an answer smaller than the number you started with. So 6×0.4=2.4, which is less than 6

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Scale both numbers together

Scaling both numbers by one power of ten keeps the answer. So 4.8÷0.6 is the same as 48÷6

Multiplying both numbers in a division by the same power of ten leaves the answer unchanged. So 4.8÷0.6 is the same as 48÷6, which is 8

Less than 1 gives more

Dividing by a number below 1 gives a larger answer. There are six halves in 3

Dividing by a number between 0 and 1 gives an answer larger than the number you started with. There are six halves in 3, so 3÷0.5=6
