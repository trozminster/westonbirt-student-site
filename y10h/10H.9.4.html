Topic 10H.9 · Solving Quadratic Equations · Lesson 4 of 6

Special Quadratics and Harder Rearranging

Some quadratics are missing a term, and some arrive tangled in brackets or fractions. Each one is a single step away from a form already solved.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quadratic with no constant term, ax2 + bx =0, factorises by taking out x and its common number: 3x2−12x=0 gives 3x(x−4) =0, so x=0 or x=4
  • A quadratic with no x term, ax2−c=0, is solved by making x2 stand alone and taking both square roots: 2x2=50 gives x2=25, so x=5 or x=−5
  • Both sides are never divided by x, because that loses the solution x=0
  • An equation with brackets is expanded, and one with x in a denominator is multiplied through by x, before every term is collected on one side.

No constant term means a factor of x

With no constant term, x is a common factor. So x=0 is always one solution.

When a quadratic has no constant term, x is a common factor of every term. Taking it out means x=0 is always one of the solutions.

No x term means two square roots

With no x term, x2 is made to stand alone. Both its square roots are solutions.

When a quadratic has no x term, x2 is made to stand alone first. Then both the positive and the negative square root are solutions.

Expand and collect first

Brackets are expanded and every term collected on one side. Only then is it factorised.

Brackets are expanded and every term is collected on one side. Only then is the quadratic in a form that factorises.

Clear x from a denominator

Multiplying every term by x clears it from a denominator. What results is a quadratic.

An x in a denominator is cleared by multiplying every term, on both sides, by x. The equation that results is a quadratic.
