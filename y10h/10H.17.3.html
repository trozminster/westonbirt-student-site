Topic 10H.17 · Pythagoras and Trigonometry in Three Dimensions · Lesson 3 of 3

Lengths and Angles in Three Dimensions

Surveyors find heights and angles they cannot reach by chaining right-angled triangles. The answer from one triangle becomes a side of the next.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When the angle between a line and a plane is given, the same right-angled triangle gives a missing length.
  • A length found in one triangle, often in the base, becomes a side of the next triangle.
  • An angle of elevation is measured up from horizontal ground, so a vertical mast, its foot and the observer make a right-angled triangle.
  • Lengths found on the way are kept unrounded on the calculator, and only the final answer is rounded.

The angle gives a length

A given angle with the plane sits in the same right-angled triangle. One known side then gives the others.

Multiply or divide

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Elevation from the ground

An angle of elevation is measured up from horizontal ground. A vertical mast stands at right angles to the ground.

Two triangles in a row

Many 3D problems are two right-angled triangles in a row. A side found in the first is a side of the second.

Keep it unrounded

Carry the unrounded value into the next step. Round only the final answer.

A MAST ON A FIELD
