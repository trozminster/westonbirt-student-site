Turning Points by Completing the Square

A thrown ball, or a business's profit, often follows a quadratic. Completing the square finds its highest or lowest point exactly, without plotting a single value.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A square is never negative, so (x+p)2+q is smallest when (x+p)2=0, that is when x=−p.
  • So the graph of y=(x+p)2+q has a minimum turning point at (−p,q) and the line of symmetry x=−p.
  • For y=q−(x+p)2 the turning point (−p,q) is a maximum, because the square is subtracted.
  • The y-intercept is the value of y when x=0, which is the constant term of the original expression.
By the end you can
G8Find the coordinates of the turning point of a quadratic graph by completing the square, and state whether it is a minimum or a maximum.
G8Draw a quadratic graph from its completed square form, marking its turning point, its line of symmetry and its y-intercept.

A square is never negative

The square (x−3)2 can never be negative. It is smallest, 0, when x=3

Adding q lifts the lowest point

So y=(x−3)2+2 is smallest when x=3
Its minimum point is (3,2).

A subtracted square gives a maximum

In y=5−(x−1)2 the square is taken away. So (1,5) is the highest point.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Three facts fix the drawing

The completed square gives the turning point and the line of symmetry. Putting x=0 gives the y-intercept.

Mirror the y-intercept

The y-intercept has a partner across the line of symmetry. It sits at the same height on the other side.
