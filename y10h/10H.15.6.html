Solving Quadratic Inequalities by Rearranging

Real conditions rarely arrive tidy, such as x2+2x<8 or x(x−3)>10
Gathering everything on one side turns each into an inequality we already know how to solve.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quadratic inequality is first rearranged so that one side is 0, using the same balancing steps as an equation.
  • The quadratic side is then factorised, or its roots found by the formula, to give the critical values.
  • Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign, so keeping the x2 term positive avoids that step.
  • A solution is checked by substituting one value from inside the solution set into the original inequality.
By the end you can

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One side must be zero

A quadratic inequality is solved with one side equal to 0
The critical values then come from the factors.

Dividing by x loses solutions

Dividing both sides by x throws away answers. Gathering terms and factorising keeps them all.

TERMS ON BOTH SIDES
Solve x2+2x<8
Subtract 8 from both sidesx2+2x−8<0
Factorise the left side(x+4)(x−2)<0
Solution−4<x<2
THE X TERM MOVED OVER
Now solve x2>5x.
Subtract 5x from both sidesx2−5x>0
Factorise the left sidex(x−5)>0
Solutionx<0 or x>5

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Expand the brackets first

Brackets are expanded before the terms are gathered. So x(x−3)>10 becomes x2−3x−10>0

Keep the x squared term positive

A negative x2 term makes the sketch a hill. Moving that term across keeps x2 positive instead.

Strict means no end points

For < or >, the critical values are not solutions. So 1<x<4 holds only the integers 2 and 3

BRACKETS FIRST
Solve x(x+2)≤15, and list the integers that satisfy it.
Expand and subtract 15x2+2x−15≤0
Factorise the left side(x+5)(x−3)≤0
Solution−5≤x≤3, so −5,−4,−3,−2,−1, 0, 1, 2, 3
A NEGATIVE SQUARE TERM
Now solve 12−x2>x.
Gather terms on the right0>x2+x−12
Factorise, then read right to left(x+4)(x−3)<0
Solution−4<x<3

x2−x−6>0, so (x−3)(x+2)>0: x<−2 or x>3
