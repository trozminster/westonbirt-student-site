Topic 10H.7 · Changing the Subject and Complex Equations · Lesson 4 of 4

Unknowns on Both Sides in Context

Two phone plans, two ages, two shapes with the same perimeter: each problem is two expressions for one quantity. Set them equal and the problem becomes an equation you already know how to solve.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation with the unknown on both sides is solved by expanding any brackets, collecting the unknown terms on one side by doing the same to both sides, then using inverse operations.
  • Collecting on the side with the larger coefficient keeps the coefficient positive.
  • A worded or geometric problem becomes an equation when a letter names one unknown quantity and two expressions for the same quantity are set equal, such as two equal perimeters or two ages after the same number of years.
  • The value of the letter is not always the answer, so the question is read again once the equation is solved.
By the end you can

G6Form an equation from a worded or geometric context with the unknown on both sides, solve it, and use the solution to answer the question asked.

Same move on both sides

A term is removed by doing its inverse to both sides. Taking the smaller x term keeps the coefficient positive.

A term on the other side is removed by doing its inverse to both sides, never by moving it unchanged. Taking away the smaller x term keeps the coefficient positive.

Brackets first

Brackets are expanded before any term is collected. The number in front multiplies every term inside.

Brackets are expanded before any term is collected. A number in front of a bracket multiplies every term inside, sign included.

Learn

Two expressions, one quantity

An equation says two expressions name the same quantity. A context gives two ways to write one thing.

An equation says that two expressions name the same quantity. So a context becomes an equation when it gives two ways of writing one thing, such as two equal perimeters.

Answer the question asked

The letter is often a step, not the answer. So the question is read again once x is found.

The letter is often a step on the way, not the answer. So once x is found, the question is read again and the quantity it asks for is worked out.
