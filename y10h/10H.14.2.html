Topic 10H.14 · Constructions and Circle Theorems · Lesson 2 of 6

Radii, Semicircles and the Angle at the Centre

Wheels, arches and stadium roofs are circles, and their angles obey fixed rules. Two radii and a diameter unlock most of them.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Every radius of a circle is the same length.
  • So any triangle formed by two radii is isosceles, and its base angles are equal.
  • The angle in a semicircle is a right angle: an angle at the circumference standing on a diameter is 90°.
  • Each circle theorem is a named fact, and that name is the reason quoted in the working.
  • The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference when both stand on the same arc.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal: two angles at the circumference standing on the same arc are equal to each other.

Learn

Two radii make an isosceles triangle

Every radius of a circle is the same length. So a triangle with two radii as sides is isosceles.

The angle in a semicircle

An angle at the circumference standing on a diameter is a right angle. It does not matter where on the circle the point is.

Why it is a right angle

Joining O to C makes two isosceles triangles. Their base angles fill 180°, so the angle at C is half of it.

ON A DIAMETER
AB is a diameter. Find angle ABC.
Use the semicircleangle ACB=90°
Use the triangle's sumangle ABC=180−90−35
=55°
TWO RADII AS WELL
Now find angle OCB.
Use the semicircleangle ACB=90°
Use the triangle's sumangle ABC=180−90−25=65°
Use radii OB and OCangle OCB=65°
YOUR TURN · a

Angle ACB=90°: the angle in a semicircle. 180−90−42=48°

Angle ACB=90°, so angle CAB=32°.OA=OC, so angle OCA=32°

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The angle at the centre is double

An angle at the centre is twice an angle at the circumference. Both must stand on the same arc.

Why it doubles

The line from C through O makes two isosceles triangles. Each one's angle at O is double its angle at C.

Angles on the same arc are equal

Two angles at the circumference on the same arc are equal. Each is half of the same angle at the centre.

CENTRE TO CIRCUMFERENCE
Find angle ACB.
Halve the centre angleangle ACB=128÷2
=64°
THEN THE RADII
Now find angle OAB.
Double the circumference angleangle AOB=104°
Use radii OA and OBangle OAB=(180−104)÷2
=38°
YOUR TURN · c
Angle AOB at the centre is 150°, and C is on the major arc. Find angle ACB.
150÷2=75°: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference
YOUR TURN · d
C is on the major arc AB, and angle ACB=35°.O is the centre. Find angle OAB.
Angle AOB=70°: twice the angle at the circumference. OA=OB, so angle OAB=(180−70)÷2=55°
