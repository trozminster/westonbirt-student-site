Arithmetic Series

Adding 1+2+3 + ... + 100 term by term takes minutes. Pairing the terms from each end takes seconds, because every pair adds to the same total.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An arithmetic series is the sum of the terms of an arithmetic sequence.
  • Pairing the first term with the last, the second with the second-last, and so on, gives n2 pairs that each add to a+l, so the sum of the first n terms is n2(a+l), where l is the last term.
  • Replacing l with a+(n - 1)d gives the sum as n2[2a+(n - 1)d], which needs only a, d and n.
  • Where only the last term is known, its position n comes from solving a+(n−1)d =l.

Pair from both ends

Writing a series forwards and backwards pairs terms with equal sums. So twice the sum is n×(a+l).

Two forms of the sum

Halving gives the sum: n2(a+l), with l the last term. Replacing l with a+(n−1)d gives n2[2a+(n−1)d].

First and last terms

When the last term is known, n2(a+l) is quicker. Only the number of terms is still needed.

Count the terms first

The last term's position comes from the nth term. Solving a+(n−1)d =l finds n.

TERMS GIVEN
An arithmetic series has 30 terms. Its first term is 7 and its last term is 94
Find its sum.
Pair the first and last7+94=101
Sum of 30 terms302×101=1515
