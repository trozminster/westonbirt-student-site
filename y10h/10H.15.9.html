Rearranging Equations to Solve Graphically

A curve is drawn once, but many equations can be solved from it. Rearranging each equation shows which straight line to add.

By the end you’ll know…
  • To solve an equation with a pre-drawn curve y=f(x), the equation is rearranged so that f(x) is one side; the other side is the line to draw.
  • The line is found by adding or subtracting terms until f(x) stands alone: with y=x2−2x drawn, x2−5x+1=0 becomes x2−2x =3x−1, so y=3x−1 is drawn.
  • The x-coordinates where the curve meets the line are the solutions, and the number of crossings is the number of real solutions.
By the end you can

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The curve's expression stands alone

The drawn curve's expression must be one whole side. What is left on the other side is the line to draw.

The same terms go on both sides

Balancing keeps the equation true. Here both sides of x2−5x+1=0 gain 3x−1

The crossings are the solutions

The x-coordinates where the line meets the curve solve the equation. The number of crossings is the number of solutions.

The same move works for any curve

Count the crossings

Each crossing is one solution. A line that misses the curve means no real solutions.
