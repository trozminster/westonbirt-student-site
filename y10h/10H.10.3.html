Volume Scale Factors

A model ship half as long as another needs only an eighth of the material. Volumes grow faster still, and the scale factor says exactly how much.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When every length of a solid is multiplied by the scale factor k, its volume is multiplied by k3, because volume has three length dimensions, while its surface area is multiplied by k2
  • So a length scale factor of 2 gives an area scale factor of 4 and a volume scale factor of 8
  • In reverse, the length scale factor is the cube root of the volume scale factor and the square root of the area scale factor.
  • To go between areas and volumes, the length scale factor is found first: surface areas in the ratio 4 : 9 come from lengths in the ratio 2 : 3, so the volumes are in the ratio 8 : 27
  • The masses and capacities of similar solids made of the same material scale like their volumes.

Areas and volumes meet at k

Areas and volumes link only through the length scale factor. So k is found first, then squared or cubed.

Areas and volumes are linked only through the length scale factor. So k is found first, then squared or cubed.

A cube root undoes cubing

From volumes back to lengths, the cubing is undone. So k is the cube root of the volume factor.

From volumes back to lengths, the cubing is undone. So the length scale factor is the cube root of the volume scale factor.
