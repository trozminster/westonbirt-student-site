Solving Non-Monic Quadratics

With a number in front of the x2, the bracket numbers no longer simply add to b. One extra step, splitting the middle term, makes the method work again.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A non-monic quadratic ax2 + bx + c, with a not 1, factorises as (px + q)(rx + s), so numbers that simply add to b and multiply to c no longer work.
  • Splitting the middle term finds two numbers that multiply to a×c and add to b, rewrites bx as their sum, then factorises in pairs: 2x2+7x+3 =2x2 +6x +x +3 =2x(x+3) +1(x+3) =(2x+1)(x+3).
  • Any common factor of all three terms is taken out first.
  • Expanding the brackets checks the factorisation.
  • Each bracket is then set to zero, and a bracket such as 2x+1 gives a fractional solution, x=−12

Split the middle term

The middle term is split into two terms. Their coefficients multiply to a×c and add to b.

In ax2 + bx + c, two numbers that multiply to a×c and add to b split the middle term into two. The four terms then factorise in pairs.

Each pair shares a bracket

After the split, each pair has a common factor. The same bracket is left in both, so it is a factor.

After the split, each pair of terms has a common factor. The same bracket is left in both pairs, so it is a factor of the whole.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Fractional solutions are normal

Each bracket is set to zero as before. A bracket like 2x+1 gives a fraction: x=−12

Once ax2 + bx + c=0 is in brackets, each bracket is set to zero. A bracket like 2x+1 gives a fraction, x=−12

Expanding checks the brackets

Expanding the brackets checks the factorisation. A wrong factorisation gives wrong solutions.

A wrong factorisation gives wrong solutions, however neatly they are found. Expanding the brackets back out shows whether they make the quadratic.
