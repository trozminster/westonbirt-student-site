Topic 10H.2 · Straight Lines and Graphical Inequalities · Lesson 1 of 5

Tables of Values and y = mx + c

Every straight line has an equation, and the equation already holds the picture: where the line crosses the y-axis and how steeply it climbs. The next four lessons shade regions bounded by these lines.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A table of values gives points on a line: each x is substituted into the equation to find its y, and each column is one point.
  • Where x and y are on the same side, as in 2x+y=6, y is made the subject first: y=6−2x, dividing every term by the coefficient of y where it is not 1
  • In y=mx+c, m is the gradient and c is the y-intercept, where the line crosses the y-axis.
  • A line can be sketched from its equation alone: mark (0, c), then step 1 across and m up, or down when m is negative.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

m and c

In y=mx+c, the number multiplying x is the gradient. The constant c is where the line crosses the y-axis.

A negative gradient

A negative gradient makes the line fall from left to right. Each step of 1 across goes down.

A RISING LINE
Sketch the line y=2x−1, marking where it crosses the y-axis.
Read the y-intercept: c=−1, so mark (0,−1). Read the gradient: m=2, so step 1 across, then 2 up, to (1,1). Join the points with a ruler and extend the line.
