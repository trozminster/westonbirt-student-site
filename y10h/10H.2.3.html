Shading Inequality Regions

Planning problems come with limits: at most so many hours, at least so many items. Each limit is an inequality, and on a graph each one cuts away part of the plane, leaving the region that meets them all.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A linear inequality in x and y describes a region: every point on one side of its boundary line satisfies it.
  • The boundary is the line with the inequality sign replaced by =; it is solid for ≤ or ≥, where points on the line are included, and dashed for < or >.
  • The side is found by testing a point not on the line, such as (0,0): if it satisfies the inequality, its side is the region.
  • x > a is the region to the right of the vertical line x = a, and y<b is the region below the horizontal line y=b.
  • Where several inequalities hold together, the region R is where all their regions overlap.
By the end you can
G5Draw the boundary line of a linear inequality in x and y, solid or dashed as the inequality requires, and shade the region it describes.
G6Shade and label the region R that satisfies two or three linear inequalities together.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One side of a line

A line divides the graph into two sides. An inequality in x and y describes one whole side.

Solid or dashed

A solid boundary is included, for ≤ and ≥. A dashed boundary is not included, for < and >.

Test a point

A point off the line decides the side. If it fits the inequality, its side is the region.

A SOLID BOUNDARY
Shade the region y≥2x+1
The boundary is y=2x+1, drawn solid because ≥ includes the line. Test (0,0): 0≥1 is false, so the region is the other side, above the line.
A DASHED BOUNDARY
Now shade the region x+y<5
The boundary is x+y=5, through (0,5) and (5,0), drawn dashed because < is strict. Test (0,0): 0<5 is true, so shade the side containing the origin.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Where all regions overlap

Each inequality gives its own region. R is where every one of them holds at once.

Vertical and horizontal lines

The region x > a lies right of the vertical line x = a. The region y≤b lies on or below y=b.

THREE SOLID LINES
Shade and label the region R satisfying x≥0, y≥2 and x+y≤6
x≥0 is the y-axis and everything right of it. Draw y=2 solid; R is on or above it. Draw x+y=6 solid through (0,6) and (6,0); test (0,0): 0≤6 is true, so R is below it. R is the triangle where all three hold.
MIXED BOUNDARIES
Now shade and label the region R satisfying y>x, x<4 and y≤5
Draw y=x dashed; test (0,1): 1>0, so R is above it. Draw x=4 dashed; R is left of it. Draw y=5 solid; R is on or below it.
