Topic 10H.5 · Negative and Fractional Indices · Lesson 1 of 4

Index Laws and Powers of Products

This week's negative and fractional powers all grow out of three laws met in Year 7 and Year 8
Today makes those laws fluent with letters, coefficients and brackets, so the new indices have something solid to stand on.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An index says how many times the base is used as a factor: 25 =2×2×2×2×2
  • Multiplying powers of the same base adds the indices, am×an =am+n; dividing subtracts them, am÷an =am−n; a power of a power multiplies them, (am)n=amn.
  • Any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1, since an÷an is both 1 and a0
  • A power outside a bracket applies to every factor inside, coefficients included: (ab)n =an×bn, so (3x2)3=27x6
  • The laws need the same base, so 23×32 cannot be combined into one power.

An index counts factors

An index counts how many times the base is a factor. Multiplying powers of one base adds those counts.

An index counts how many times the base appears as a factor. Multiplying two powers of the same base simply joins the two lists of factors, so the counts add; dividing cancels factors, so the counts subtract.

A power of a power multiplies

A power of a power, like (x2)3, is x2 used three times. So its indices multiply to give x6

A power of a power, such as (x2)3, is x2 used three times over. Its index is two added three times, so the indices multiply: (x2)3=x6

The power reaches every factor

A power outside a bracket applies to every factor inside. So (3x)2 is 3x×3x =9x2, not 3x2

A power outside a bracket applies to everything multiplied together inside it. So (3x)2 is 3x×3x, which is 9x2, and the 3 is squared along with the x.

Numbers are powered, indices multiply

Inside the bracket, each number is raised to the power. Each letter's index is multiplied by the power.

Inside the bracket, each number is raised to the power and each letter's index is multiplied by it. So (2x3)4 is 24×x12, which is 16x12
