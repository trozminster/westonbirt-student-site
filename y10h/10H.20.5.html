Histograms with Missing Data

Exam histograms often arrive half-finished: a bar missing, a frequency missing, or no scale at all. One known fact about one bar is enough to rebuild the rest.

By the end you’ll know…
  • One class whose frequency is known fixes the frequency density scale: its height is frequency / class width, which labels the whole vertical axis.
  • A missing frequency is frequency density x class width; a missing bar is drawn to the height frequency / class width.
  • A known total frequency also fixes the scale, since the total area of the bars is the total frequency.

One known bar sets the scale

One bar with a known frequency fixes the density scale. Its density tells you what its height is worth.

The total area works too

The total area of the bars is the total frequency. So a known total also fixes the scale.

ONE KNOWN BAR
The bar for 10<t≤15 holds 20 values. Find the frequency of 15<t≤30
Density of 10 to 1520÷5=4
Each grid line4÷8=0.5
Height of 15 to 302×0.5=1
Frequency of 15 to 301×15=15
THE TOTAL
Now a histogram of 60 values covers 30 squares in all. How many values does a bar of 7 squares hold?
