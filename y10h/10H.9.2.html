Solving Quadratics by Factorising

Most quadratic equations do not arrive in brackets. Factorising puts them there, and the zero-product principle finishes the job.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The zero-product principle needs zero on one side, so every term of a quadratic equation is collected on one side first, keeping the x2 term positive.
  • x2+bx+c=0 is factorised as (x+p)(x+q) =0, where p+q=b and pq=c, and each bracket is then set to zero.
  • The solutions are the numbers in the brackets with their signs changed: (x+3)(x−5) =0 gives x=−3 or x=5
  • Both sides are never divided by x, because that loses the solution x=0
  • Each solution can be checked by substituting it into the original equation.
