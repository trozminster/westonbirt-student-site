Finding the nth Term

Given a few terms, the nth term is the shortcut to every other: the 100th term, or whether 500 appears at all. Two numbers, d and c, are all it takes.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The nth term of an arithmetic sequence has the form dn + c, where d is the common difference.
  • c is the zero term, found by working back one step from the first term: c = first term - d.
  • For 5, 8, 11, 14, d=3 and c=5−3=2, so the nth term is 3n+2; for 20, 17, 14, d=−3 and c=23, so the nth term is 23−3n.
  • The next terms continue the common difference; a later term comes from substituting its position into the nth term.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The difference goes in front of n

The common difference is the number in front of n. Terms rising by 3 sit a fixed amount above 3n.

The zero term

Working back one step from the first term gives c. So c is the first term minus d.

Falling sequences

A decreasing sequence has a negative d. Its nth term is usually written constant first.
