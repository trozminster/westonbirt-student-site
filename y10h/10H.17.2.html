The Angle Between a Line and a Plane

A ramp, a roof or a pyramid meets the ground at an angle nobody can measure directly. The right-angled triangle inside the solid gives that angle.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its projection on the plane.
  • The projection runs from where the line meets the plane to the foot of the perpendicular from the line's other end.
  • The line, its projection and that perpendicular make a right-angled triangle, with the right angle at the foot of the perpendicular.
  • Two sides of the triangle, found by Pythagoras where needed, give the angle by sin, cos or tan.

The angle to the projection

The angle between a line and a plane is measured to the line's projection. The projection lies straight below the line, on the plane.

A right-angled triangle

The line, its projection and the perpendicular make a right-angled triangle. Redrawn flat, two of its sides give the angle.

Not along an edge

The projection runs across the base, not along an edge. So angle GAB is the wrong angle.

A sloping edge and the base

A pyramid's apex is above the centre of its base. So a sloping edge projects onto half a base diagonal.

The height is opposite

In triangle AME the height is opposite the angle. The sloping edge is the hypotenuse.

A wedge works the same way

A wedge's sloping diagonal projects onto the base diagonal. The vertical end closes the triangle.
