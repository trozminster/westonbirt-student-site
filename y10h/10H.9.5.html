The Quadratic Formula

Many quadratics never factorise neatly, yet every one can be solved. The formula, given on the exam sheet, works every time.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Any quadratic equation ax2+bx+c =0 is solved by the formula x=(−b plus or minus the square root of (b2−4ac))÷2a, which is given in the exam.
  • The equation is rearranged so that one side is zero first, and a, b and c are read with their signs: for 2x2−3x−4 =0, a=2, b=−3 and c=−4
  • The plus and the minus give the two solutions, and the whole numerator is divided by 2a.
  • A negative b makes −b positive, and b2 is never negative; the calculator needs brackets round a negative b.
  • The formula is used when a quadratic does not factorise, and the solutions are rounded only at the end.
  • If b2−4ac is negative the equation has no real solutions.
