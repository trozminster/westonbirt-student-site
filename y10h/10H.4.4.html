Topic 10H.4 · Arcs and Sectors · Lesson 4 of 4

Finding Sector Angles and Radii

Exam questions rarely hand over the radius and the angle and ask for the area. More often the area or the arc is given and the angle or the radius is missing: the same formula, solved as an equation.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The sector formulas can be used backwards: given an arc length or an area together with the radius or the angle, the missing quantity is found by forming an equation and solving it.
  • From theta/360 × 2 × π × r = arc length, the angle is theta = 360 x arc length ÷ (2 × π × r).
  • From theta/360 × π × r2 = area, r2 is found first and the radius is its positive square root.
  • A region between two sectors with the same centre has an area of the larger sector minus the smaller, and a perimeter of both arcs plus two straight edges, each the difference of the radii.
By the end you can
G7Find the angle or the radius of a sector given its arc length or its area.
G7Solve multi-step sector problems, such as finding a perimeter from a given area, or the area and perimeter of a region between two sectors.

The formula is an equation

The sector formulas link the angle, the radius and the length or area. Knowing two fixes the third.

The arc-length formula links three quantities: the angle, the radius and the arc. Knowing any two fixes the third, so the formula is set equal to what is given and solved like any equation.

An area gives r2, not r

Solving the area formula leaves r2 first. The radius is the positive square root of that.

Solving the area formula for the radius leaves r2 on its own first. A length cannot be negative, so the radius is the positive square root of that.

Take away areas, never radii

A piece between two sectors is the large sector minus the small one. Subtract the areas, never the radii.

A ring-shaped piece between two sectors with the same centre is the larger sector with the smaller one removed. The two areas are found separately and subtracted; subtracting the radii first gives a far smaller, wrong answer.

Every edge on the boundary counts

The strip's perimeter is two arcs and two straight edges. Each straight edge is the difference of the radii.

The strip's boundary is made of two arcs, the outer and the inner, and two straight edges. Each straight edge runs between the two arcs, so its length is the difference of the radii.
