Harder Regions and Integer Points

Real limits rarely arrive as y<2x+1: they look like 5x+2y≤20
And when the unknowns count whole things, such as people or boxes, only the whole-number points in the region are possible answers.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A boundary such as 5x+2y=20 is drawn from its intercepts: x=0 gives y=10, and y=0 gives x=4
  • The sign alone does not show the side: test a point not on the line, or rearrange to make y the subject.
  • Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses its sign: −2y≤6−4x becomes y≥2x−3
  • The integer points in a region are the points with whole-number coordinates that satisfy every inequality: points on a solid boundary count, and points on a dashed boundary do not.
By the end you can
G7Draw and shade the region defined by harder linear inequalities, such as y≥2x+1 and 5x+2y≤20, finding each boundary from its intercepts or by rearranging.
G7List the points with integer coordinates that lie in a region defined by linear inequalities.

Intercepts draw the line

A boundary like 5x+2y=20 is quickest from its intercepts. Put x=0, then put y=0

Test, don't guess

The sign alone does not say above or below. A test point decides the side.

FROM INTERCEPTS
Shade the region 3x+4y≤24
Find the intercepts: x=0 gives y=6, and y=0 gives x=8
Draw the line through (0,6) and (8,0), solid for ≤. Test (0,0): 0≤24 is true, so shade the side containing the origin.
TWO HARDER LINES
Now shade the region R satisfying y≥2x+1, 5x+2y≤20 and x≥0
Draw y=2x+1 solid; R is above it, as (0,2) gives 2≥1
Draw 5x+2y=20 through (0,10) and (4,0); (0,0) gives 0≤20, so R is below it. The lines meet at (2,5).

Whole-number points

Integer points have whole-number coordinates. Only those satisfying every inequality count.

Dashed edges exclude

A point on a dashed boundary is not in the region. A point on a solid one is.
