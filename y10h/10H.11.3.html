Percentage Problems in Context

Sales, pay rises, interest and shares are the same few calculations in different clothes. The skill is spotting which quantity is unknown.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Percentage problems differ by what is unknown: the change, the original amount, or the final amount.
  • An unknown final amount multiplies by the multiplier; an unknown original divides by it.
  • An unknown percentage comes from comparing the change with the original.
  • Fractions, decimals, percentages and ratios all express the same multiplicative relationships, so a problem can be converted into whichever form is easiest.

The unknown decides the method

Every percentage problem links an original, a multiplier and a final amount. The unknown one decides the method.

Every percentage problem links an original amount, a multiplier and a final amount. Which one is unknown decides whether to multiply or divide.

Repeated changes multiply

Successive changes multiply their multipliers. So 5% a year for two years is × 1.052

Successive percentage changes multiply their multipliers together, rather than adding. So 5% interest a year for two years is × 1.052

One share, four ways

One share can be a ratio, a fraction or a percentage. So 3 : 5 makes red 38, or 37.5%.

A ratio, a fraction, a decimal and a percentage can all describe the same share. Red and blue in the ratio 3 : 5 makes red 38, or 37.5%, of the total.

Name the whole each part is of

Each percentage is of a stated whole. 20% of the girls is of the girls only.

Each fraction or percentage in a problem is of a stated whole. 20% of the girls is a share of the girls, not of the class.
