Rearranging and Reading Straight Lines

Equations of lines arrive in many forms, and graphs arrive with no equation at all. Both are turned into y=mx+c, the form that shows the gradient and the intercept at a glance.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The gradient and intercept can be read off only when the equation is in the form y=mx+c.
  • Any linear equation is rearranged into that form with inverse operations, dividing every term by the coefficient of y.
  • From a drawn line, c is where it crosses the y-axis and m is the rise divided by the run between two points on it; a falling line has a negative gradient.
  • Parallel lines have the same gradient.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Only y=mx+c shows m and c

The gradient and intercept can be read only when y stands alone. Any other form is rearranged first.

Divide every term

Dividing by the coefficient of y applies to every term. One term left undivided gives a different line.

ADD, THEN DIVIDE
Rearrange 3y−6x=12 into the form y=mx+c.
Add 6x to both sides3y=6x+12
Divide every term by 3y=2x+4
SUBTRACT, THEN DIVIDE
Now rearrange 5x+2y=10 into the form y=mx+c.
Subtract 5x from both sides2y=−5x+10
Divide every term by 2y=−2.5x+5
YOUR TURN · a
Rearrange 4y−8x=20 into the form y=mx+c.
4y=8x+20y=2x+5
YOUR TURN · b
Rearrange 6x+3y=9 into the form y=mx+c, and state its gradient.
3y=−6x+9
y=−2x+3, gradient −2
