Topic 10H.15 · Quadratic Graphs and Inequalities · Lesson 4 of 10

Proving Positivity and Sketching Quadratics

Some quadratics can never be zero or negative, whatever x is. Completing the square proves it for every x at once, and the same key points sketch any quadratic quickly.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Because (x+p)2≥0 for every x, (x+p)2+q with q>0 is always positive, and its least value is q.
  • In the same way, q−(x+p)2 with q<0 is always negative, and its greatest value is q.
  • The critical values of a quadratic are the roots of y=0, where its graph crosses the x-axis.
  • A sketch shows the shape and labels the roots, the y-intercept and the turning point, but is not plotted to scale.
By the end you can
G8Prove that a quadratic expression is always positive, or always negative, by completing the square.
G7Sketch a quadratic graph from its factorised form, labelling the roots, the y-intercept and the turning point.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A square is at least zero

Any number squared is zero or positive. So (x−2)2≥0 for every x.

Add a positive number

Adding 1 makes (x−3)2+1 at least 1
So the expression is always positive.

A proof ends in words

The proof says why the square cannot be negative. Then it states what has been proved.

ALWAYS POSITIVE
Prove that x2+4x+7 is positive for every x.
Complete the squarex2+4x+7 =(x+2)2+3
Use the square(x+2)2≥0, so (x+2)2+3≥3
Conclusionx2+4x+7>0 for every x
ALWAYS NEGATIVE
Now prove that 2x−x2−5 is negative for every x.
Complete the square2x−x2−5 =−4−(x−1)2
Use the square(x−1)2≥0, so −4−(x−1)2≤−4
Conclusion2x−x2−5<0 for every x
YOUR TURN · a
Prove that x2−10x+26>0 for every x.
x2−10x+26 =(x−5)2+1, and (x−5)2≥0, so it is at least 1
YOUR TURN · b
Prove that 6x−x2−10<0 for every x.
6x−x2−10 =−1−(x−3)2, which is at most −1

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The factors give the roots

Each factor is zero at one critical value. So y =(x−1)(x+3) crosses at 1 and −3

The turning point is halfway

The line of symmetry is halfway between the roots. So the minimum is at x=−1, where y=−4

Putting x=0 gives the y-intercept

Here y =(0−1)(0+3) =−3
A sketch labels these points and needs no exact scale.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Sketch y =(x−2)(x+4).
The roots are x=2 and x=−4
Halfway between them, x=−1 and y =(−3)(3) =−9
The y-intercept is (0,−8).
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Sketch y =(3−x)(x+1).
The roots are x=3 and x=−1
Halfway between them, x=1 and y =(2)(2) =4
The y-intercept is (0,3).
