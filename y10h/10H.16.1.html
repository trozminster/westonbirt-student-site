Averages from Grouped Data

A table of grouped times hides every exact value. The class midpoints still give a good estimate of the mean, and counting through the frequencies still finds the middle class.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Grouping data loses the individual values, so only the class each value fell into is known.
  • The mean can therefore only be estimated, using each class midpoint to stand in for its values.
  • The estimated mean is the total of midpoint × frequency, divided by the total frequency.
  • The modal class is the class with the highest frequency, and the median is the class containing the middle value.
  • Both are classes rather than single values, which is why they are named rather than calculated.

Midpoints stand in for the values

The exact values in a class are lost. The class midpoint stands in for every value in it.

Divide by the total frequency

The estimated mean is the total of midpoint × frequency over the total frequency. It is an estimate, because the true values are unknown.

Count through to the middle value

The median class holds the n+12th value. The modal class has the highest frequency, and both are named as classes.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

An unknown frequency still has a midpoint

A missing frequency x still multiplies its midpoint. So that class adds 15x to the total.

The unknown is in both totals

The unknown frequency joins the total frequency too. So the top and the bottom of the mean both contain x.

Clear the fraction, then solve

Multiplying by the total frequency removes the fraction. The equation is then linear in x.

THE UNKNOWN ABOVE THE MEAN
Find x, given that the estimated mean is 24
Set the totals equal60+25x+70=24(6+x)
Simplify both sides130+25x=144+24x
x=14
THE UNKNOWN BELOW THE MEAN
Now find x, given that the estimated mean is 6
Set the totals equal2x+36+40=6(x+10)
Simplify both sides2x+76=6x+60
x=4
