Topic 10H.3 · Sets and Venn Diagrams · Lesson 4 of 4

Three-Set Venn Diagrams

With three groups the Venn diagram has eight regions, and survey totals overlap in a tangle. Filling the diagram from the centre outwards untangles every count, and every probability follows from it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A three-set Venn diagram has eight regions: one in all three sets, three in exactly two, three in exactly one, and one outside all three.
  • It is filled from the centre outwards: the count in all three first, then each 'exactly two' region by subtracting the centre from that pair's total, then each 'only' region by subtracting everything else already in that circle.
  • The counts in all eight regions add to n(ξ).
  • 'Exactly two' is the three pair regions without the centre; 'at least two' adds the centre.
  • A probability is the total count of the event's regions divided by n(ξ).

Eight regions

Three circles make eight regions, including the one outside them all. Each element sits in exactly one.

From the centre out

Fill the centre first, then each pair, then each single region. Subtract what is already placed each time.

FROM THE TOTALS
Of 60 students, 20 take art (A), 25 biology (B) and 22 chemistry (C)
5 take all three, 8 take A and B, 7 take A and C and 10 take B and C. Complete the Venn diagram.Centre: 5
Pairs: A and B only 8−5=3; A and C only 7−5=2; B and C only 10−5=5
Singles: A only 20−3−2−5=10; B only 25−3−5−5=12; C only 22−2−5−5=10
Inside the circles: 47, so outside 60−47=13

Exactly two or at least two

'Exactly two' is the three pair regions, without the centre. 'At least two' adds the centre.

Add the regions, divide by the total

A probability adds the counts of the event's regions. The sum is divided by n(ξ).
