Filling in Cumulative Frequency Tables

A cumulative frequency table says at once how many people took 30 minutes or less. It is the step before the graph that estimates the median and the quartiles.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Cumulative frequency is a running total: each class's cumulative frequency is its own frequency added to all the frequencies before it.
  • Each cumulative frequency counts the values up to the upper boundary of its class, so it says how many are less than or equal to that boundary.
  • The cumulative frequencies never decrease, and the last one equals the total frequency.
  • The number of values between two boundaries is the difference of their cumulative frequencies.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A running total

Cumulative frequency adds each frequency to all the ones before it. So the column never goes down.

Each total ends at a boundary

Each cumulative frequency counts the values up to its class's upper boundary. So 28 people took 30 minutes or less.

The last total is everyone

The last cumulative frequency equals the total frequency. It checks the running total.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Read 'at most' straight off

The number of values up to a boundary is read straight from the table. So 56 people took 50 minutes or less.

Subtract from the total for 'more than'

The number more than a boundary is the total minus its cumulative frequency. So 60−28=32 took over 30 minutes.

Subtract two totals for 'between'

The number between two boundaries is the difference of their cumulative frequencies. Between 20 and 40 minutes, 44−14=30
