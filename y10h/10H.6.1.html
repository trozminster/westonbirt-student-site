Topic 10H.6 · Rewriting Bases and Standard Form · Lesson 1 of 3

Rewriting Powers with a Common Base

Is 45 bigger than 83? As they stand it is hard to say, but written as powers of 2 the indices answer it at once.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A base that is itself a power can be rewritten: 8=23, so 84=(23)4=212 by the power law.
  • A reciprocal or a root of the base is a power of it too: 18=2−3 and the square root of 2=21/2.
  • Once every part is written over one base, a product or a quotient becomes a single power: 43×8=26×23=29
  • Two powers of the same base are equal only when their indices are equal, so 2ˣ=29 means x=9
By the end you can
G4Rewrite a power with a different base using the power law, for example writing 84 as 212
G7Write a product or quotient of powers as a single power of one base, and solve an equation with the unknown in the index by writing both sides as powers of the same base, such as 4ˣ=83 or 3ˣ+1=127

Learn

A base can be a power

A base that is a power can be written as one. The power law then multiplies the indices.

When the base is itself a power of a smaller number, it can be replaced by that power. The power law then multiplies the indices, so 84=(23)4=212

A reciprocal or a root

A reciprocal of the base takes a negative index. A root of the base takes a fractional index.

The negative and fractional indices of the last topic let reciprocals and roots join in. A reciprocal of the base takes a negative index and a root takes a fractional one, so 14=2−2 and 2=2 to the 12

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One base for every part

Powers combine only when they share a base. So every part is written over one base first.

Powers combine into one only when they share a base. So every part of a product or a quotient is first rewritten over one common base, and then the index laws apply.

Equal powers, equal indices

Equal powers of one base have equal indices. So both sides go over one base before the indices are matched.

Two powers of the same base are equal only when their indices are equal. So an unknown index is found by writing both sides over one base and then matching the indices, never before.
