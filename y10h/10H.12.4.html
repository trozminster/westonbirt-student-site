Fractional and Negative Scale Factors

A photo shrunk to a thumbnail is still an enlargement, with a scale factor below 1. A lens that flips its picture upside down works like a negative scale factor.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A scale factor between 0 and 1 makes the image smaller, though it is still called an enlargement.
  • The rules are unchanged: every length, and every distance from the centre, is multiplied by the scale factor.
  • A negative scale factor places the image on the opposite side of the centre.
  • It also turns the image upside down, so it is equivalent to a positive enlargement followed by a 180 degree rotation about the centre.
  • An invariant point does not move under a transformation, and the centre of enlargement is always invariant.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A fraction makes it smaller

A scale factor between 0 and 1 gives a smaller image. It is still called an enlargement.

Count from the centre, then scale

Count each step from the centre and multiply it by the fraction. With k = 13, 6 right and 3 up becomes 2 right and 1 up.

The reciprocal undoes it

Scale factor 2 about the same centre undoes scale factor 12. The object and the image are similar either way.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Through and beyond the centre

A negative scale factor puts the image on the other side of the centre. Each point goes through the centre and beyond.

Upside down, and the centre stays

The image is also upside down. Scale factor -2 is scale factor 2, then a half turn. The centre never moves.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Enlarge the flag by scale factor -2, centre (1, 1).
Distancesthe foot is 1 right and 1 up, so its image is 2 left and 2 down
Points(2, 2) to (-1, -1), (2, 3) to (-1, -3), (3, 3) to (-3, -3)
Imagetwice the size and upside down
