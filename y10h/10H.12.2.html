Reflections and Rotations

A kaleidoscope is built from reflections, and a wheel or a clock hand moves by rotation. A mirror line fixes one; a centre, an angle and a direction fix the other.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A reflection is specified by a mirror line, and every point of the image is the same perpendicular distance from that line as its object point, on the opposite side.
  • Mirror lines are given as equations such as x=1, y=2, y=x and y=−x.
  • A rotation is specified by a centre, an angle and a direction.
  • An anticlockwise rotation is a positive angle and a clockwise rotation is a negative angle.
  • Every point of the image is the same distance from the centre as its object point, and the centre itself does not move.
  • Reflections and rotations preserve length and angle, so the image is congruent to the object; a reflection reverses the sense and a rotation does not.

Learn

The same distance on the other side

Each point lands as far from the mirror, on the other side. The image faces the other way.

A mirror line is an equation

A vertical mirror line is x = a number. A horizontal one is y = a number. Every point on it shares that coordinate.

The lines y=x and y=−x

Reflecting in y=x swaps a point's coordinates. Reflecting in y=−x swaps them and changes both signs.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Centre, angle and direction

A rotation turns every point about one fixed centre. Each point keeps its distance from the centre.

Clockwise is negative

Anticlockwise is a positive angle and clockwise a negative one. So −90° is a quarter turn clockwise.

A half turn goes through the centre

In a half turn, each point goes straight through the centre. Its image is the same distance beyond.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
