Cyclic Quadrilaterals and the Alternate Segment

A machinist can check a circular part from its angles alone. Four corners on a circle, or a tangent touching it, fix angles that can be calculated.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A cyclic quadrilateral has all four of its vertices on the circumference.
  • Its opposite angles sum to 180 degrees.
  • The perpendicular bisector of any chord passes through the centre, so a radius drawn perpendicular to a chord bisects it.
  • A tangent meets the radius at that point at a right angle.
  • A chord drawn from the point where a tangent touches divides the circle into two segments.
  • The angle between the tangent and that chord equals the angle in the alternate segment, the one on the other side of the chord.

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral

When all four corners lie on a circle, opposite angles add to 180°. Neighbouring angles follow no such rule.

The perpendicular to a chord

The perpendicular from the centre to a chord cuts it in half. That makes a right-angled triangle with a radius.

OPPOSITE ANGLES
ABCD is cyclic. Find angle BCD.
Pair it with angle DABangle BCD = 180 - 78
= 102°
WITH A STRAIGHT LINE
Now find angle DCE.
Use opposite anglesangle BCD = 180 - 84 = 96°
Use the straight lineangle DCE = 180 - 96
= 84°
YOUR TURN · a
ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral, and angle ABC = 112°. Find angle ADC.
180 - 112 = 68°: opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°
YOUR TURN · b
PQRS is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle P = 3x and angle R = 2x + 30. Find x, then angle P.
3x + 2x + 30 = 180, so x = 30. Angle P = 90°

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A tangent meets the radius at 90°

A tangent touches the circle at one point only. There it is at right angles to the radius.

The alternate segment

The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the other segment. Look across the chord, not beside it.

TANGENT AND CHORD
Find angle TAB.
Use the alternate segmentangle TBA = 64°
Use the triangle's sumangle TAB = 180 - 64 - 48
= 68°
WITH THE RADIUS TOO
Now find angle TBA.
Use the radius at 90°angle between tangent and TA = 90 - 25 = 65°
Use the alternate segmentangle TBA = 65°
YOUR TURN · c
The tangent at T makes 52° with chord TA. B is on the circle, across TA. Find angle TBA.
52°: the alternate segment theorem
YOUR TURN · d
The tangent at T makes 70° with chord TA, and angle TAB = 55°. B is on the circle. Find angle ATB.
Angle TBA = 70°: the alternate segment theorem. Angle ATB = 180 - 70 - 55 = 55°
