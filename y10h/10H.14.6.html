Tangents from a Point Outside a Circle

From a ship's mast, the line of sight to the horizon is a tangent to the Earth. A tangent and a line through the circle give one more equal product.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When a tangent from a point X touches the circle at T, and a line from X cuts the circle at A and B, XT² = XA x XB.
  • This is the outside case of intersecting chords, with the two ends of one chord meeting at T.
  • Two tangents drawn from the same external point are equal in length.
By the end you can
G8Use the property XT² = XA x XB for a tangent and a line from the same point to find a missing length.
G6Use equal tangents to find missing lengths and angles.

The tangent squared

A tangent from X is a line whose two crossing points meet at T. So XT × XT equals XA × XB.

Every length starts at X

The lengths XT, XA and XB all start at X. The far length XB includes the chord AB.

FINDING THE TANGENT

Two tangents from one point

The two tangents from a point outside a circle are equal in length. The line to the centre is a line of symmetry.

A kite of radii and tangents

The radii meet the tangents at right angles. So the angles at O and X add to 180°.

EQUAL TANGENTS, A LENGTH
Find the perimeter of OTXS.
Use equal tangentsXS=12
Use equal radiiOS=5
