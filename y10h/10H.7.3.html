Topic 10H.7 · Changing the Subject and Complex Equations · Lesson 3 of 4

Equations with Algebraic Fractions

Exam questions often hide a simple linear equation inside fractions, sometimes with x in the denominator. Clear the fractions correctly and an ordinary equation is waiting underneath.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation with fractions is cleared by multiplying every term, on both sides, by a common multiple of the denominators, usually their LCM.
  • A numerator with more than one term is bracketed before multiplying, so 6×x+13 =2(x+1), and a minus sign in front of a fraction then multiplies the whole bracket.
  • An unknown in a denominator is cleared the same way, by multiplying both sides by the whole denominator: 8÷(x+3)=2 gives 8=2(x+3).
  • With a single fraction on each side, both denominators multiply through: 3÷(x+1) =2÷(x−2) gives 3(x−2) =2(x+1).

Clear every term

Multiplying by the denominator clears a fraction. Every term on both sides is multiplied.

Multiplying both sides by the denominator clears a fraction. Every term on both sides is multiplied, not just the fraction, or the equation stops balancing.

An unknown below the line

An unknown in a denominator clears the same way. Both sides are multiplied by the whole denominator.

A denominator holding the unknown clears the same way. Both sides are multiplied by the whole denominator, written as a bracket.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One multiplier for every fraction

Multiplying by the LCM of the denominators clears every fraction at once. Each numerator becomes a bracket.

With two denominators, multiplying through by their lowest common multiple clears both at once. Each fraction becomes a whole bracket, and a minus in front then multiplies all of it.

A fraction on each side

With one fraction on each side, both denominators multiply through. Each numerator meets the other denominator.

When each side is a single fraction, both denominators multiply through. Each numerator then meets the other side's denominator.
