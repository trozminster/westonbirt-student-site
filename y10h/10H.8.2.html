Pyramids, Cones and Compound Solids

Cones, pyramids and rockets are made by joining simple solids. Their volume and surface area come from the parts, once the hidden joins are left out.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A pyramid's volume is (13) x base area x perpendicular height: one third of the prism on the same base with the same height.
  • A cone is a pyramid on a circular base, so its volume is (13) x pi x r² × h, and its curved surface area is pi x r × l, where l is the slant height; both cone formulae are given in the exam.
  • The perpendicular height h, the radius r and the slant height l form a right-angled triangle, so l² = r² + h².
  • A compound solid's volume is the sum of its parts' volumes, minus any part cut away; a frustum is a large cone minus the small cone cut from its top.
  • A compound solid's surface area counts only the faces on the outside, leaving out any face where two parts join.

A pyramid is a third of its prism

A pyramid fills one third of the prism on the same base. So V = (13) × base area × height.

A pyramid fills exactly one third of the prism with the same base and the same perpendicular height. So its volume is (13) × base area × height.

A cone is a pyramid on a circle

A cone is a pyramid on a circle: V = (13)πr²h. Its height h is perpendicular, not the slant l.

A cone is a pyramid with a circular base, so its volume is (13)πr²h. The h is the perpendicular height, not the slant height l that runs down the side.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Volumes of parts add

A compound solid's volume is the sum of its parts. A part cut away is subtracted.

A compound solid's volume is the sum of the volumes of its parts, each found with its own formula. A part that is cut away or drilled out is subtracted instead.

Surface area counts the outside only

The face where two parts join is hidden inside the solid. It is left out of the surface area.

The face where two parts join is inside the solid, so nobody could paint it. It is left out of the total surface area.

A frustum is a cone minus a cone

A frustum is a cone with its top cut off. Its volume is the large cone minus the small cone.

A frustum is what is left when the top of a cone is cut off parallel to its base. Its volume is the large cone's volume minus the small cone's.
A CONE ON A HEMISPHERE
A solid is a cone of radius 3 cm and height 8 cm on a hemisphere of radius 3 cm. Find its volume, in terms of π.
Find the cone(13) × π × 9 × 8 = 24π
Find the hemisphere(23) × π × 27 = 18π
Volume= 24π + 18π
= 42π cm³
A FRUSTUM
Now a cone of radius 6 cm and height 9 cm has a cone of radius 2 cm and height 3 cm cut from its top. Find the volume of the frustum, in terms of π.
Find the large cone(13) × π × 36 × 9 = 108π
Find the small cone(13) × π × 4 × 3 = 4π
Volume= 108π - 4π
= 104π cm³
