Drawing a Cumulative Frequency Graph

A cumulative frequency graph turns the table into a curve. From the curve, 'how many were below 25 minutes?' can be answered for any value, not just at the class boundaries.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Each cumulative frequency is plotted at the upper boundary of its class, never at the midpoint.
  • The graph starts at the lower boundary of the first class with a cumulative frequency of 0, because no values lie below it.
  • The points are joined with a smooth curve, which rises from left to right and levels off at the total frequency.
  • To estimate how many values are less than a value, read up from it to the curve, then across to the cumulative frequency axis.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Read up, then across

To count the values below a value, read up to the curve, then across. Up from 25 minutes, the curve gives about 21

Subtract from the total for 'more than'

The number more than a value is the total minus the reading. So about 60−21=39 took over 25 minutes.

A reading is an estimate

The curve guesses how the values spread inside each class. So a reading is an estimate, not an exact count.
