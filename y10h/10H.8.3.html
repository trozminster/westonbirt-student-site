Topic 10H.8 · Using 3D Formulae · Lesson 3 of 3

Missing Lengths and Equal Volumes

A factory melting metal or filling a mould needs a length, not a volume. Every formula from this week also runs backwards.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A formula with every value but one known becomes an equation: the values are substituted and the equation is solved, so 43×π×r3 = 36pi gives r3=27 and r=3
  • Everything multiplying the unknown power is undone first; a cubed length is then found with a cube root and a squared length with a square root.
  • When a solid is melted and recast, or a liquid poured from one container into another, its volume stays the same, so the two volume expressions are set equal.
  • When π appears in every term on both sides it cancels, so the answer is exact with no π in it.
  • With letters for the lengths, the same equation gives one length in terms of another.
By the end you can
G6Find a missing radius, height or base length of a sphere, cone, cylinder or pyramid from its volume or surface area.
G7Solve problems where two solids have equal volume, including melting and recasting, and find one length in terms of another.

A formula becomes an equation

With one value unknown, a formula becomes an equation. Solving it gives the missing length.

Once every value in a formula is known except one, the formula is an equation in that unknown. Substituting the values and solving gives the missing length.

The power decides the root

A cubed length is freed by a cube root. The root comes last, once the power stands alone.

Everything multiplying the unknown power is undone first. Then a cubed length is freed by a cube root, and a squared one by a square root.
RADIUS FROM A VOLUME

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Melting keeps the volume

Melting and recasting changes the shape, not the volume. So the two volumes are set equal.

Melting and recasting changes a solid's shape but not the amount of material in it. So the volume before is set equal to the volume after.

π cancels

When π is on both sides, it cancels. The length that comes out has no π in it.

When π is a factor of every term on both sides, dividing both sides by π removes it. The length that comes out is exact, with no π in it.

Letters give one length in terms of another

With letters for lengths, the same equation is solved. It gives one length in terms of another.

With letters for the lengths, the same equation can still be solved. The answer is one length written in terms of the other.
MELTED AND RECAST
A metal sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast as a cylinder of radius 4 cm. Find the cylinder's height.
Set the volumes equalπ×16×h=43×π×216
Cancel π both sides16h=288
Heighth=18 cm
ONE LENGTH IN TERMS OF ANOTHER
Now a cone of radius r and height h has the same volume as a sphere of radius r. Find h in terms of r.
Set the volumes equal13×π×r2×h=43×π×r3
Divide by 13πr2h=4r
