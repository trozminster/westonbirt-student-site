Area Scale Factors

Double the size of a poster and it needs four times the paper. Areas grow faster than lengths, and the scale factor says exactly how much faster.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When every length of a shape is multiplied by the scale factor k, every area is multiplied by k2, because area has two length dimensions.
  • So a length scale factor of 3 gives an area scale factor of 9, and this holds for any area: a face, a cross-section or a whole surface.
  • In reverse, the length scale factor is the square root of the area scale factor, so areas in the ratio 25 : 4 come from lengths in the ratio 5 : 2
  • The area scale factor is the ratio of two areas, so an area is never divided by a length to find it.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Square-rooting undoes squaring

From areas back to lengths, the squaring is undone. So the length factor is the square root of the area factor.

Going from areas back to lengths reverses the squaring. So the length scale factor is the square root of the area scale factor.

The area factor comes first

Two areas divide to give the area scale factor. Its square root then scales the lengths.

Dividing one area by the other gives the area scale factor. Its square root then multiplies the lengths.
