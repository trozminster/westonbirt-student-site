Direct and Inverse Proportion in Context

Shops, banks and builders all rely on proportion: what one costs, what a pound buys, how long a job takes. Telling direct from inverse decides whether to multiply or divide.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A unit cost makes different pack sizes comparable, so the best buy is the lowest cost per unit.
  • An exchange rate is a direct proportion, so it works the same way in both directions.
  • In direct proportion, multiplying one quantity multiplies the other by the same factor, so their ratio stays constant.
  • In inverse proportion, multiplying one divides the other by the same factor, so their product stays constant.
  • More of one meaning less of the other signals inverse proportion; more meaning more signals direct.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The product stays the same

In inverse proportion, doubling one quantity halves the other. So their product stays the same.

Direct or inverse?

More of one with more of the other is direct. More of one with less of the other is inverse.

INVERSE
6 pumps empty a tank in 10 hours. How long would 4 pumps take?
Fewer pumps means more time, so this is inverse proportion.
Find the constant product6×10=60
Hours for 4 pumps60÷4=15
DIRECT
Now 5 kg of rice feeds 20 people. How many people does 8 kg feed?
More rice feeds more people, so this is direct proportion.
Find people per kg20÷5=4
People for 8 kg8×4=32
YOUR TURN · c
A journey takes 3 hours at 60 km/h. How long does it take at 45 km/h?
Inverse: 3×60=180 km
180÷45=4 hours
YOUR TURN · d
8 taps fill a pool in 9 hours. How many taps fill it in 6 hours?
Inverse: 8×9=72 tap-hours
72÷6=12 taps
