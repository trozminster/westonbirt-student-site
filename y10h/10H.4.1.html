Circumference and Area of Circles

Every arc and sector in this topic is a fraction of a whole circle, so the whole circle has to be secure first. Today revisits the two circle formulas, and the semicircle, where they most often go wrong.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The circumference of a circle is C = pi x d (or C = 2 x pi x r) and its area is A = pi x r squared; the radius is squared before multiplying by pi, since the index applies only to r. A semicircle's area is half the full circle's area, but its perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter - the straight edge must be added separately.

One ratio, two formulas

Every circle's circumference ÷ diameter is the same number, π. So the circumference is π × d and the area is π × r².

Every circle, whatever its size, has a circumference a little over three times its diameter, and that fixed ratio is the number π. So one measurement describes the whole circle: the circumference is π × d, a length round the edge, and the area is π × r², a covering of the inside in square units.

The index belongs to the radius alone

In π × r², only the radius is squared. Squaring the diameter gives four times the area.

In π × r² the index sits on the r and nowhere else, so the radius is squared first and then multiplied by π. Squaring the diameter instead gives an answer four times too large, because doubling a length quadruples its square.

Half a circle is not half of everything

A semicircle's area is half the circle's area. Its perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter.

Cutting a circle in half halves the space it covers, so a semicircle's area is half the circle's area. But the cut makes a new straight edge, the diameter, that the circle never had, so the perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter.

Only outside edges count

Each part of a compound shape takes its own formula. An edge where two parts join is not on the perimeter.

In a compound shape each part is found by its own formula and the parts are added. An edge where two parts join is inside the finished shape, so it is not part of the perimeter.
A SEMICIRCLE
A semicircle has diameter 16 cm. Find its perimeter to 1 d.p.
Halve the circumferenceπ × 16 ÷ 2
= 25.132...
Add the diameter25.132... + 16
Perimeter41.1 cm Leaving out the 16 would describe an arc, not a closed shape.
A COMPOUND SHAPE
Now a rectangle 12 cm by 10 cm has a semicircle on one 10 cm side. Find its area to 1 d.p.
Find the rectangle12 × 10 = 120
Find the semicircleπ × 5² ÷ 2
= 39.269...
Add the parts120 + 39.269...
Area159.3 cm²
YOUR TURN · c
A semicircle has radius 9 cm. Find its area and its perimeter, each to 1 d.p.
Area = π × 9² ÷ 2 = 127.2 cm²; perimeter = π × 18 ÷ 2 + 18 = 46.3 cm
YOUR TURN · d
A rectangle 15 cm by 6 cm has a semicircle on each 6 cm end. Find the perimeter of the shape to 1 d.p.
The two semicircles make one circle: π × 6 = 18.849...; perimeter = 15 + 15 + 18.849... = 48.8 cm
