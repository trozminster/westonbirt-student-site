Perpendicular and Angle Bisectors

A phone mast placed fairly between two towns, or a path equally far from two walls, can be found exactly. Compasses and a straight edge do it with no measuring.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The perpendicular bisector of a line segment is the line that cuts it in half at right angles.
  • Every point on it is the same distance from both end points, which is why it is drawn with two arcs of equal radius centred on those end points.
  • The radius must be more than half the length of the segment or the arcs do not cross.
  • The bisector of an angle is the line that divides it into two equal angles.
  • Every point on it is the same distance from both arms of the angle, which is why it is drawn from an arc centred on the vertex and then two equal arcs from where that arc meets the arms.
By the end you can
G4Use a perpendicular bisector to find the set of points equidistant from two given points.
G4Use an angle bisector to find the set of points equidistant from two given lines.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Equal arcs from both ends

Equal arcs from A and B cross at points equally far from both. The line through those crossings is the perpendicular bisector.

Every point on it is equidistant

Every point on the perpendicular bisector is as far from A as from B. So it is the locus of points equidistant from A and B.

Nearer to one point

Points on A's side of the bisector are nearer to A than to B. The bisector is the boundary between the two regions.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

An arc, then equal arcs

An arc from the vertex marks two points equally far from it. Equal arcs from those points meet on the line that halves the angle.

Equidistant from two lines

Every point on the angle bisector is as far from one arm as the other. Distance to a line is measured at right angles.

Two points or two lines

Equally far from two points needs a perpendicular bisector. Equally far from two lines needs an angle bisector.

TWO FENCES

Draw an arc from Ocrossing both fences
Draw equal arcsfrom those two crossings
Join O to their meetingequally far from both
NEARER TO ONE WALL

Bisect angle BACan arc, then equal arcs
Draw the bisectorfrom A through their meeting
Shade AB's sidenearer to AB than to AC
YOUR TURN · c
Two straight roads meet at 70°. A lamp must be equally far from both roads. Where can it go?
On the bisector of the 70° angle, 35° from each road
YOUR TURN · d
P is inside angle AOB, 3 cm from OA and 5 cm from OB. Is P on the bisector? Which arm is P nearer?
No: its distances are different. P is nearer to OA, so it is between OA and the bisector
