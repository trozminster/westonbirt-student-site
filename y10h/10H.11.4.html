Recurring Decimals to Fractions

A calculator shows 0.272727 and stops, but the digits never do. Every recurring decimal is an exact fraction, and algebra finds it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A recurring decimal repeats one block of digits for ever, and a dot over the first and the last digit of the block marks it, so 0.272727... is written 0.27 with dots over the 2 and the 7.
  • Every recurring decimal is a fraction.
  • To find it, call the decimal x and multiply by 10 for a one-digit block, 100 for a two-digit block and 1000 for a three-digit block, so that the digits after the point line up.
  • Subtracting x then removes the part that recurs, leaving a whole number: 9x, 99x or 999x equals it.
  • So a block of n digits gives a denominator of n nines before simplifying: 0.272727... = 2799 = 311.
By the end you can
G6Convert a recurring decimal whose repeating block of one or two digits starts straight after the point, such as 0.777... or 0.363636..., into a fraction in its simplest form, using the algebraic method.
G7Convert a recurring decimal with a repeating block of three digits, or with a whole-number part, such as 0.216216... or 2.454545..., into a fraction or a mixed number in its simplest form.

Dots mark the repeating block

A recurring decimal repeats a block for ever. Dots mark the block's first and last digits.

A recurring decimal repeats one block of digits for ever. A dot goes over the first digit and the last digit of the block.

Shift by one block, then subtract

Multiplying by 100 lines up a two-digit block. Subtracting cancels the recurring part.

Multiplying by 100 moves a two-digit block one whole block left, so the digits after the point line up. Subtracting then cancels the recurring part exactly.

The nines give the denominator

One recurring digit gives ninths, and two give ninety-ninths. So 0.272727... is 2799, or 311.

A one-digit block gives a denominator of 9, and a two-digit block gives 99. So 0.272727... is 2799, which simplifies to 311.

Three digits, three places

A three-digit block needs x multiplied by 1000. The subtraction leaves 999x.

A three-digit block needs the point moved three places, so x is multiplied by 1000. The subtraction then leaves 999x.

A whole part stays whole

A whole-number part leaves the method unchanged. So 2.454545... is 2 + 4599.

A whole-number part changes neither the method nor the denominator. So 2.454545... is 2 + 4599, which is 2 511.
