Recurring Decimals to Fractions

A calculator shows 0.272727 and stops, but the digits never do. Every recurring decimal is an exact fraction, and algebra finds it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A recurring decimal repeats one block of digits for ever, and a dot over the first and the last digit of the block marks it, so 0.272727... is written 0.27 with dots over the 2 and the 7
  • Every recurring decimal is a fraction.
  • To find it, call the decimal x and multiply by 10 for a one-digit block, 100 for a two-digit block and 1000 for a three-digit block, so that the digits after the point line up.
  • Subtracting x then removes the part that recurs, leaving a whole number: 9x, 99x or 999x equals it.
  • So a block of n digits gives a denominator of n nines before simplifying: 0.272727... =2799 =311
By the end you can
