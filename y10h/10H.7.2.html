Topic 10H.7 · Changing the Subject and Complex Equations · Lesson 2 of 4

Rearranging When the Subject Appears Twice

Some formulae hide the letter you want in two places at once. No inverse operation can reach both, so today's method gathers them into one and factorises.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When the wanted letter appears in more than one term, inverse operations alone cannot isolate it.
  • Every term containing the subject is collected on one side and every other term on the other, then the subject is factorised out: ax+2=bx+5 gives ax−bx=3, so x(a−b)=3
  • Dividing both sides by the whole bracket makes it the subject: x=3a−b.
  • A fraction holding the subject is cleared first, by multiplying both sides by its whole denominator: y=x+2x−3 gives y(x−3)=x+2

When the subject appears twice

Inverse operations cannot free a letter that appears twice. So its terms are gathered on one side first.

Inverse operations cannot free a letter that appears in two terms, because undoing one term leaves the other. So every term containing the subject is gathered on one side first.

Divide by the whole bracket

Factorising leaves the subject times one bracket. Dividing by the whole bracket frees it.

Factorising leaves the subject multiplied by a single bracket. Dividing both sides by the whole bracket then frees the subject.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Clear the fraction first

A subject inside a fraction cannot be gathered yet. Multiplying by the whole denominator clears the fraction.

A subject inside a fraction cannot be gathered while the fraction is there. Multiplying both sides by the whole denominator clears it, and expanding shows the subject twice.

Then gather and factorise

With the fraction cleared, the subject appears twice. So the gather-and-factorise method finishes it.

Once the fraction is cleared, the subject appears in two terms. The first cycle's method finishes the job: gather, factorise, divide by the bracket.
SUBJECT ON TOP AND BELOW
