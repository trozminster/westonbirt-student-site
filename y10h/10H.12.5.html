Describing Transformations

An exam question shows two shapes and says 'describe fully': each mark sits in one fact. Naming the move is not enough; the vector, mirror, centre, angle or scale factor fixes it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A complete description names the transformation and gives everything that specifies it: a translation needs its column vector, a reflection its mirror line, a rotation its centre, angle and direction, and an enlargement its centre and scale factor.
  • Translations, reflections and rotations preserve length and angle, so they produce a congruent image; an enlargement with k not equal to 1 does not.
  • A mirror line is halfway between each point and its image, a centre of rotation is as far from each point as from its image, and a centre of enlargement is where lines through matching points meet.
By the end you can
G4Give a complete description of a single transformation shown on a grid.
G6Give a complete description of a rotation about a centre other than the origin, or of an enlargement with a fractional or negative scale factor, finding the centre first.

Learn

What each description needs

A full description names the transformation and every fact that fixes it. Leave one out, and the image could land elsewhere.

Which way round it faces

A reflected image faces the other way round. A rotated or translated image faces the same way.

Congruent or only similar

Translations, reflections and rotations keep every length: the image is congruent. An enlargement changes the lengths: the image is only similar.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Finding the centre of a rotation

The centre is as far from each point as from its image. Tracing paper pinned there turns the shape onto its image.

The sign of the scale factor

The scale factor is image length over object length. It is negative when the image is across the centre.
