Topic 10H.9 · Solving Quadratic Equations · Lesson 6 of 6

Quadratics in Context

Areas, paths and number puzzles often lead to an x2 term. Solving gives two answers, and the context decides which one is real.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A worded or geometric problem becomes a quadratic equation when a letter names the unknown length or number and two expressions for one quantity, such as an area, are set equal.
  • The equation is rearranged so that one side is zero and solved by factorising or by the formula.
  • A solution that is impossible in the context, such as a negative length, is rejected with a reason; a negative number the context allows is kept.
  • The value of the letter is not always the answer, so it is substituted back to find the quantity the question asks for.
By the end you can

Reject the impossible

A length or a time cannot be negative. So an impossible solution is rejected, with a reason.

A quadratic gives two solutions, but a length or a time cannot be negative. An impossible solution is rejected, with a reason given.

Once x is known, it is substituted into whatever the question asks for. A length of x+3, or a perimeter, still has to be worked out.

Find the perimeter of a rectangle (x+2) cm long and x cm wide with an area of 63 cm².
Make one side zerox2+2x−63=0x(x+2)=63, expanded.
Keep the positive rootx=7(x+9)(x−7) =0 gives x=7 or x=−9; −9 is rejected, as a width cannot be negative.
Perimeter2×(7+9)=32 cm
A FORMULA ANSWER
Now find the width x of a path round a 10 m by 6 m garden if the total area is 120 m², to 2 decimal places.
Make one side zerox2+8x−15=0(10+2x)(6+2x) =120 expands to 4x2+32x+60 =120; subtract 120, then divide by 4
Substitute into the formulax=−8±1242
Path widthx=1.57 mThe other solution, −9.57, is rejected: a width cannot be negative.
