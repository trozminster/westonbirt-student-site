Negative Indices

Last lesson's division law gives answers like x² ÷ x⁵, where the index comes out negative. A negative index is not a mistake: it has a meaning, and that meaning is a reciprocal.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A negative index means a reciprocal: a⁻ⁿ = 1aⁿ for any non-zero base, which follows from the division law, since a² ÷ a⁵ = a²⁻⁵ = a⁻³ and also 1a³.
  • A negative index makes a value smaller, never negative: 2⁻³ = 18, not -8.
  • A fraction raised to a negative power is turned over and the power made positive: (ab)⁻ⁿ = (ba)ⁿ, so (23)⁻² = 94.
  • The index laws hold unchanged for negative indices.
By the end you can
G4Evaluate and simplify expressions with a negative index for any base (not only base 10), including combining with the multiplication and division laws.
G6Evaluate a negative power of a fraction or a decimal by turning it over, and find an unknown index such as x in 2ˣ = 18.

Where a negative index comes from

The division law turns 2³ ÷ 2⁵ into 2⁻². Cancelling gives 12², so 2⁻² means 12².

Working out 2³ ÷ 2⁵ by the division law gives 2⁻², but cancelling the factors gives 1 over 2². Both are right, so 2⁻² has to mean 12², the reciprocal of 2².

Smaller, never negative

Each step down in the index divides by the base again. The values get smaller but stay positive.

Each step down in the index divides by the base again, so the powers keep halving past 2⁰ = 1 without ever reaching zero or turning negative.

A fraction turns over

A negative power of a fraction is the reciprocal of its positive power. So the fraction turns over and the index becomes positive.

The reciprocal of a fraction is the fraction turned over. So a negative power of a fraction turns it over and makes the index positive: (23)⁻² = (32)² = 94.

An unknown index

Write both sides as powers of one base and the indices must match. Since 18 = 2⁻³, the equation 2ˣ = 18 gives x = −3.

When both sides are written as powers of the same base, the indices must be equal. Since 18 = 12³ = 2⁻³, the equation 2ˣ = 18 has x = −3.
