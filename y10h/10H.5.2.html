Negative Indices

Last lesson's division law gives answers like x2÷x5, where the index comes out negative. A negative index is not a mistake: it has a meaning, and that meaning is a reciprocal.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A negative index means a reciprocal: a−n=1an for any non-zero base, which follows from the division law, since a2÷a5 =a2−5 =a−3 and also 1a3
  • A negative index makes a value smaller, never negative: 2−3=18, not −8
  • A fraction raised to a negative power is turned over and the power made positive: (ab)−n =(ba)n, so (23)−2=94
  • The index laws hold unchanged for negative indices.

Where a negative index comes from

The division law turns 23÷25 into 2−2
Cancelling gives 122, so 2−2 means 122

Working out 23÷25 by the division law gives 2−2, but cancelling the factors gives 1 over 22
Both are right, so 2−2 has to mean 122, the reciprocal of 22

Smaller, never negative

Each step down in the index divides by the base again. The values get smaller but stay positive.

Each step down in the index divides by the base again, so the powers keep halving past 20=1 without ever reaching zero or turning negative.

A fraction turns over

A negative power of a fraction is the reciprocal of its positive power. So the fraction turns over and the index becomes positive.

The reciprocal of a fraction is the fraction turned over. So a negative power of a fraction turns it over and makes the index positive: (23)−2 =(32)2 =94

An unknown index

Write both sides as powers of one base and the indices must match. Since 18=2−3, the equation 2ˣ=18 gives x=−3

When both sides are written as powers of the same base, the indices must be equal. Since 18 =123 =2−3, the equation 2ˣ=18 has x=−3
