Combining Transformations

A dance routine, a robot's path and a tiling pattern are each one move after another. Often the two moves can be replaced by one single move.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Transformations can be applied one after another, each acting on the image the previous one produced.
  • The result of two transformations can sometimes be described as a single transformation.
  • Two reflections in parallel mirror lines give a translation; two reflections in perpendicular mirror lines give a rotation of 180°.
  • The order in which two transformations are applied can change the result.
By the end you can
G4Carry out a sequence of two transformations on a shape.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Two moves, one description

Reflecting in both axes gives a half turn about (0,0). So the pair is one rotation.

Parallel or perpendicular mirrors

Two reflections in parallel mirrors make a translation of twice the gap. Two in perpendicular mirrors make a half turn.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A shape is reflected in y=1, then in y=4
Describe the single transformation.
The mirrors are parallel, so the pair is a translation. The gap is 3, so every point moves twice as far, 6, upwards.
