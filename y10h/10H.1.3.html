The Formula for the nth Term

Exam questions name a sequence by its first term a and its common difference d. One formula turns those two numbers, or any two terms, into every term of the sequence.

By the end you’ll know…
  • In an arithmetic sequence with first term a and common difference d, the nth term is a+(n - 1)d, because the nth term is n−1 steps of d after the first.
  • a+(n−1)d expands to dn+(a−d), the same rule as dn+c.
  • Two terms fix the sequence: from the second term to the fifth term is 3 steps, so d = (fifth term - second term)/3, and a is found by working back from either term.
By the end you can
G5Use the formula a+(n - 1)d to find a given term, or the nth term, of an arithmetic sequence from its first term and common difference.
G6Find the first term and the common difference of an arithmetic sequence from two of its terms, and use them to find another term.

Count the steps

The 4th term is 3 steps of d after the first. The nth term is n - 1 steps after it.

The formula

So the nth term is a+(n - 1)d. The bracket counts the steps.

The same rule as dn + c

Expanding a+(n−1)d gives dn+(a−d). Both forms give the same terms.

Steps between two terms

From the second term to the 5th term is 3 steps of d. So 3d is their difference.

Then back to a

With d known, step back from either term to the first. The second term is one step after a.
