Finding y = mx + c from Two Points

Two readings are often all you have, such as a phone bill in two different months. Two points fix a straight line, so they fix its equation too.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The gradient of the line through (x1,y1) and (x2,y2) is (y2−y1)÷(x2−x1): the change in y divided by the change in x, both taken from the same point to the other.
  • When m is known but the line's crossing point is not on the grid, c is found by substituting the coordinates of any point on the line into y = mx + c and solving for c.
  • A point lies on a line exactly when its coordinates satisfy the equation, and that is the check on every answer here.
By the end you can
G5Calculate the gradient of a straight line from the coordinates of two points on it, including negative and fractional gradients.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Change in y over change in x

The gradient is the change in y divided by the change in x. Any two points on the line give the same value.

Subtract in the same order

Both changes are taken from the same point to the other. Then the sign of the gradient comes out right.

POSITIVE COORDINATES
Find the gradient of the line through (2,3) and (6,11).
Find the change in y11−3=8
Find the change in x6−2=4
Gradientm=8÷4
=2
NEGATIVES IN THE POINTS
Now find the gradient of the line through (−3,4) and (5,−2).
Find the change in y−2−4=−6
Find the change in x5−(−3)=8
Gradientm=−6÷8
=−34
YOUR TURN · a
Find the gradient of the line through (1,−2) and (4,10).
(10−(−2))÷(4−1)=12÷3=4
YOUR TURN · b
Find the gradient of the line through (−6,1) and (−2,−5).
(−5−1)÷(−2−(−6))=−6÷4=−32

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The gradient first, then c

Two points give the gradient first. Either point then gives c.

The other point is the check

The equation must fit both points. Substituting the second point checks the answer.

A WHOLE-NUMBER GRADIENT
Find the equation of the line through (1,4) and (3,10).
Find the gradientm=6÷2
=3
Substitute (1,4)4=3×1+c
c=1
Equationy=3x+1
A FRACTIONAL GRADIENT
Now find the equation of the line through (−2,5) and (4,2).
Find the gradientm=−3÷6
=−12
Substitute (4,2)2=−12×4+c
c=4
Equationy=−12x+4
YOUR TURN · c
Find the equation of the line through (2,1) and (5,13).
m=12÷3=4; 1=4×2+c, so c=−7: y=4x−7
YOUR TURN · d

m=−4÷6=−23; 2=−23×(−3)+c, so c=0: y=−23 x
