The Zero-Product Principle

A quadratic equation usually has two answers, not one. Written as brackets multiplied together, both answers can be read straight off.

By the end you’ll know…
  • If two numbers multiply to make zero, at least one of them is zero: if ab=0 then a=0 or b=0
  • So a product of brackets equal to zero is solved by setting each bracket equal to zero in turn: (x−3)(x+5) =0 gives x=3 or x=−5
  • A factor of x on its own gives the solution x=0, so x(x−4)=0 gives x=0 or x=4
  • A bracket with a coefficient of x gives a fraction: 2x−3=0, so x=32
  • The principle works only for zero: (x−1)(x−2) =6 does not give x−1=6 or x−2=6

A lone x is a factor too

A lone x is a factor too. It gives the solution x=0

A factor of x on its own counts as a bracket. Setting it to zero gives the solution x=0, which is easy to lose.

A coefficient makes a fraction

A bracket like 2x−3 is solved as a linear equation. Its solution can be a fraction.

A bracket with a coefficient of x is solved like any linear equation. Its solution is often a fraction, not a whole number.
