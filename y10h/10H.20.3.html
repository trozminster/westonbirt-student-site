Interpreting Histograms

A histogram hides its frequencies in areas. Reading one means multiplying back, density times width, and never trusting the tallest bar at a glance.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Reading a histogram reverses drawing it: the frequency of a class is frequency density x class width, the area of its bar.
  • So a tall, narrow bar can hold fewer values than a short, wide one, and the bars are compared by area, never by height.
  • The number of values in part of a class is estimated by assuming they are spread evenly, so it is the density x the width of that part.

Frequency is the area

Reading a histogram reverses drawing it. Each class's frequency is its density times its width.

Compare areas, not heights

The tallest bar need not hold the most values. Compare the areas instead.

ONE BAR
The histogram shows 80 journey times. Find the frequency of 5<t≤15
Read the height3.5
Find the width15−5=10
Frequency3.5×10=35
EVERY BAR

Each area10, 35, 25 and 10

Part of a bar

Values in a class are taken as spread evenly. So part of a bar holds the matching part of its area.

The part's width, not a bound

Multiply the density by the width of the part. A bound on its own is not a width.

ACROSS THREE BARS
Use the histogram of 80 journey times. Estimate how many took between 10 and 30 minutes.
Part of 5 to 153.5×5=17.5
Part of 20 to 400.5×10=5
Estimate17.5+25+5=47.5
