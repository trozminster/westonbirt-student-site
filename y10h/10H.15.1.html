Completing the Square for x² + bx + c

A quadratic such as x2+6x+2 hides its smallest value. Written as (x+3)2−7, it shows that value at a glance.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Completing the square writes x2+bx+c in the form (x+p)2+q, where p is half of b.
  • Expanding (x+p)2 gives x2 +2px +p2, so the bracket makes the x2 and x terms but adds an extra p2
  • That extra p2 is subtracted to keep the expression equal: x2+bx+c =(x+b2)2 −(b2)2 +c.
  • When b is odd, p is a fraction: x2+5x+1 =(x+52)2 −254 +1 =(x+52)2 −214
By the end you can
G7Write a quadratic expression x2+bx+c, where b is even, in the form (x+p)2+q.
G7Complete the square for x2+bx+c where b is odd, giving p and q as fractions.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A squared bracket makes the x term

Expanding (x+3)2 gives x2+6x+9
The 6x is double the 3 in the bracket.

Halve b for the bracket

The bracket's number is half the coefficient of x. So x2+6x comes from (x+3)2

Take away the extra square

The bracket brings an extra 9 with it. Subtracting the 9 keeps the expression equal.

A POSITIVE COEFFICIENT

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

An odd b gives a fraction

Half of an odd number is a fraction. So x2+5x comes from (x+52)2

Square the top and the bottom

The square of 52 is 254, not 252
Both the top and the bottom are squared.

Write q over one denominator

The constant joins the fraction over a common denominator. So −254+1 is −214
