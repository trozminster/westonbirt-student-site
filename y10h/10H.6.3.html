Multiplying and Dividing in Standard Form

Light travels 3 × 10⁵ km every second, and a year is about 3.2 × 10⁷ seconds. Standard form turns the distance light travels in a year into one short multiplication.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Multiplying in standard form multiplies the A values and adds the powers of 10, because the factors can be taken in any order: (3 × 10⁴) × (5 × 10⁶) = 15 × 10¹⁰ = 1.5 × 10¹¹.
  • Dividing divides the A values and subtracts the powers: (2 × 10³) ÷ (8 × 10⁻²) = 0.25 × 10⁵ = 2.5 × 10⁴.
  • A power of a number in standard form raises both parts: (3 × 10⁴)² = 9 × 10⁸; to take a square root, first make the power of 10 even: 1.6 × 10⁹ = 16 × 10⁸, whose square root is 4 × 10⁴.
  • The answer is rewritten at the end if A has left 1 <= A < 10.

Multiply the parts separately

A product can be taken in any order. So the A values multiply and the powers of 10 add.

The factors of a product can be taken in any order. So the A values are multiplied together, and the powers of 10 are multiplied by adding their indices.

Divide the parts separately

Division works part by part too. The A values divide and the indices subtract, and a negative index needs care with its sign.

Learn

A power acts on both parts

A power or a root acts on both parts. A square root needs an even power of 10 first.

A power or a root of a product acts on every factor, as the index laws showed. So squaring 3 × 10⁴ squares the 3 and the 10⁴; a square root needs an even power of 10 first.

How many times larger

Comparing by how many times larger is a division. The larger quantity goes on top.

Comparing two quantities by how many times larger one is means dividing them. The larger goes on top, and the answer is a plain number with no unit.
