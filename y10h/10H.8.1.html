Spheres and Hemispheres

Planets, ball bearings and domes are spheres or halves of one. Two short formulae, given on the exam sheet, say how much they hold and how much covers them.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A sphere of radius r has volume (43) x pi x r³ and surface area 4 x pi x r²; both formulae are given in the exam, and the radius is half the diameter.
  • The radius is cubed or squared first, before it is multiplied by 43 or 4 and by pi.
  • A hemisphere is half a sphere, so its volume is (23) x pi x r³ and its curved surface area is 2 x pi x r².
  • A solid hemisphere also has a flat circular face of area pi x r², so its total surface area is 3 x pi x r².
  • An answer in terms of pi keeps pi as a symbol, such as 36pi cm3; a rounded answer evaluates pi at the end.
By the end you can
G6Calculate the volume and surface area of a sphere, giving answers in terms of pi or to a stated accuracy.
G6Calculate the volume and total surface area of a hemisphere, including its flat circular face.

Volume cubes the radius

A sphere's volume cubes its radius. The radius is cubed before the 43 and π multiply it.

A sphere's volume depends on the cube of its radius, because volume is three-dimensional. The radius is cubed first, and only then multiplied by 43 and by π.

Surface area squares the radius

A sphere's surface area squares its radius. It equals four circles of the same radius.

A sphere's surface area depends on the square of its radius, because area is two-dimensional. It is exactly four times the area of a circle with the same radius.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A hemisphere is half a sphere

A hemisphere's volume is half a sphere's: (23)πr³. Its curved surface is half as well: 2πr².

A hemisphere is exactly half a sphere, so its volume is half of (43)πr³, which is (23)πr³. Its curved surface is half of the sphere's surface, 2πr².

A solid hemisphere has a flat face

Cutting a sphere in half makes a new flat circle. So the total surface area is 2πr² + πr² = 3πr².

Cutting a sphere in half makes a new flat face, a circle of area πr². So a solid hemisphere's total surface area is the curved 2πr² plus the flat πr², which is 3πr².
