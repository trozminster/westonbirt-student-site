Quadratic Inequalities from a Sketch

A designer may need every width for which an area stays under a limit. That is where a quadratic is negative, and a sketch shows it at once.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quadratic inequality such as x2−x−6<0 asks for the x values where the graph of y=x2−x−6 lies below the x-axis.
  • Its critical values are the roots of x2−x−6=0, and a sketch shows which side of them is which.
  • For a valley-shaped graph, < 0 is the one interval between the roots, a<x<b, and > 0 is the two outer parts, x<a or x>b.
  • The solution set is shown on a number line, with a closed circle for ≤ or ≥ and an open circle for < or >.
By the end you can

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Below the axis means negative

A quadratic is negative wherever its graph lies below the x-axis. The critical values mark where it crosses.

Less than zero is the middle

For a valley, < 0 is the part between the roots. So x2−x−6<0 gives −2<x<3

A square below a number has two ends

Solving x2<25 needs both square roots. The answer is −5<x<5, not just x<5

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Solve (x−1)(x+4)<0
The critical values are x=1 and x=−4
The valley is below the axis between them, so −4<x<1
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Solve x2≤16
The critical values are x=4 and x=−4
The curve y=x2 is at or below 16 between them, so −4≤x≤4

Greater than zero is the outside

For a valley, > 0 is the two parts outside the roots. The solution comes in two separate pieces.

Two pieces need the word or

So the answer is x<−2 or x>3
