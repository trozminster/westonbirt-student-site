Frequency Density and Drawing Histograms

Data grouped into unequal classes need a fair picture. Frequency density makes every bar's area match its frequency, so a wide class cannot look bigger than it is.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Frequency density = frequency / class width, so frequency = frequency density x class width, which is the area of the bar.
  • The class width is the upper bound minus the lower bound, read from the inequality: 10<t≤25 has width 25−10=15
  • Each bar runs from its class's lower bound to its upper bound on a continuous horizontal scale, with no gaps, and its height is the frequency density.

Density is frequency per unit

Frequency density is the frequency for each unit of the class. Divide the frequency by the class width.

Width from the bounds

The class width is the upper bound minus the lower bound. Read both from the inequality.

ONE CLASS

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Bars span their classes

Each bar runs from its class's lower bound to its upper bound. The bars touch, with no gaps.

Height is the density

Each bar's height is its frequency density. Its area is then its frequency.

DRAW A HISTOGRAM
Draw a histogram of 49 masses, with 10 in 0<m≤20, 18 in 20<m≤30, 12 in 30<m≤35 and 9 in 35<m≤50 grams.
Find each class width: 20, 10, 5 and 15
Divide each frequency by its width to get the frequency densities 0.5, 1.8, 2.4 and 0.6
Draw a mass scale from 0 to 50 and a frequency density scale from 0 to 2.5, labelled. Draw each bar from its lower bound to its upper bound at its density, touching its neighbours.
