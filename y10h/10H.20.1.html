Bar Charts and Histograms

A bar chart and a histogram look alike but say different things. Knowing which one you are reading stops a tall, thin bar being mistaken for a large group.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A bar chart shows frequency by the HEIGHT of each bar; for categories the bars have equal widths and equal gaps, and for continuous data in equal classes the bars stand on a number scale and touch.
  • A histogram shows continuous grouped data, with no gaps, and the AREA of each bar is its frequency, so its vertical axis is frequency density = frequency / class width.
  • When every class has the same width, heights are in proportion to frequencies, so a histogram looks like a bar chart with touching bars; when the widths differ, a bar's height alone misleads.

Continuous bars touch

Continuous data sits on a number scale. So the bars touch, with no gaps between the classes.

Equal widths

When every class has the same width, height can show frequency. Read across from the top of a bar to the scale.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A histogram measures area

In a histogram, the area of each bar shows its frequency. Its height is the frequency density.

Two charts that look alike

A bar chart shows frequency by height. A histogram shows it by area, whatever the widths.

WHICH AXIS
Plant heights are grouped 0 to 10 cm, 10 to 30 cm and 30 to 35 cm. Should the vertical axis show frequency or frequency density?
