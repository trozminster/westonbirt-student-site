Working with Vectors

Velocity, displacement and force all have a direction as well as a size, so mechanics works with them as vectors. Finding a speed, a distance or a bearing from i, j form is a step in every later problem about motion and forces.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Displacement, velocity, acceleration and force are vectors; distance, speed, time and mass are scalars.
  • Speed is the magnitude of velocity, and distance is the magnitude of displacement.
  • In mechanics, vectors are written in i, j form, and their magnitudes and directions are found as in Pure chapter 11

Learn

Vectors have a direction, scalars do not

A vector quantity has a size and a direction; a scalar has a size only. Each vector in mechanics has a scalar partner that is its size.

Speed is the size of velocity

The speed is the magnitude of the velocity vector. Distance is the magnitude of the displacement in the same way.

WORKED EXAMPLE A

Speed13ms−1

The same calculation, now for a displacement.

Distance6.32 km to 3 s.f.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Direction from the components

The angle a vector makes with i comes from the tangent of its two components. Sketch the vector first, so you know which angle you have found.

A bearing is measured from north

A bearing is measured clockwise from north, and north is the j direction. Find the angle with j first, then turn it into the bearing.

Position after a time

At constant velocity, the displacement after t seconds is the velocity × t. Add it to the starting position to find where the object is.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the angle between the velocity (5𝐢+2𝐣)ms−1 and the vector i.
Sketch5 across and 2 up, so the angle at the origin has opposite 2 and adjacent 5
