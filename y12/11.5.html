Chapter 11 · Vectors · Lesson 4 of 5

Solving Geometric Problems

With vectors a geometric fact becomes algebra: parallel is a multiple, collinear is a multiple through a shared point, and a ratio is a fraction of a route. These are the exam's proof questions, and comparing coefficients is the tool for the hardest of them.

By the end you’ll know…
  • In a geometric problem, write each vector as a route through the shape in terms of the given vectors, such as a and b.
  • If a and b are not parallel and pa + qb = ra + sb, then p=r and q=s: compare the coefficients.
  • A point that divides a line in a given ratio, or a midpoint, can be written with vectors.
By the end you can
Write vectors in a geometric figure in terms of given vectors, including to a point that divides a line in a given ratio.
Prove a geometric result with vectors, such as that lines are parallel or points are collinear, or find a ratio by comparing coefficients.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Parallel and a shared point

If AB→ = k BC→, the two lines are parallel. They also share B, so A, B and C lie on one straight line: they are collinear.

Compare coefficients

When a and b are not parallel, there is only one way to make a vector from them. So two expressions for the same vector have equal coefficients of a and of b.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
OA→ = a, OB→ = b and OC→ = 3b−2a. Show that A, B and C are collinear.
Find AB→ and BC→.
AB→ = b−a
BC→ = 3b−2a−b =2(b−a)
BC→ = 2AB→ and B is shared: collinear
WORKED EXAMPLE D
OABC is a parallelogram with OA→ = a and OC→ = c. The diagonals OB and AC meet at X. Show that X is the midpoint of OB.
Two routes to X.
Route 1OX→=λ(a+c)
Route 2OX→=a+μ(c−a)
=(1 − μ)a + μc
λ =1 − μ and λ = μ, so λ =12
YOUR TURN · c
OA→ = a, OB→ = b and OC→ = 4b−3a. Show that A, B and C are collinear, and find AB : BC.
AB→ = b−a and BC→ = 3b−3a = 3AB→
B is shared, so collinear, and AB : BC=1 : 3
YOUR TURN · d
OA→ = a and OB→ = b.X is on AB with OX→ = λ(2a + b). Find λ.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Route 1 → Route 2
X on AB: OX→ = a + μ(b − a)=(1 − μ)a + μb
2λ =1 − μ and λ = μ, so λ =13
