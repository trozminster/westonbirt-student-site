Magnitude and Direction

A vector's two parts are the two short sides of a right-angled triangle, so its length and its angle come from Pythagoras and trigonometry. The unit vector and the magnitude-and-direction form set up speed, resultant forces and bearings in mechanics.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The magnitude of a vector is found by Pythagoras: the magnitude of 3𝐢+4𝐣 is 32+42=5
  • A unit vector in the direction of a is a/|a|.
  • The direction of a vector is the angle it makes with a given direction, such as i or north, found with trigonometry from a sketch.

The angle from the triangle

The angle with i comes from tanθ=j part ÷ i part, using the sizes of the parts. A sketch shows which angle the question wants.

From magnitude and angle to i and j

A vector of magnitude r at an angle θ above i has parts r cosθ and r sinθ. So magnitude 10 at 30° above i is 53𝐢+5𝐣.
