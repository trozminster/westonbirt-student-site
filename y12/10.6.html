Chapter 10 · Trigonometric Identities and Equations · Lesson 6 of 6

Equations and Identities

Exam trigonometric equations are often quadratics in disguise, or need an identity first so that only one function is left. This lesson joins factorising, the identities and the interval methods of the chapter into the exam's 'show that ..., hence solve' question.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quadratic in a trigonometric function, such as 2sin2θ−sinθ−1 =0, is solved by factorising with sinθ as the variable, then solving each factor.
  • A value outside −1≤sinθ≤1 or −1≤cosθ≤1 gives no solutions and is rejected.
  • Use sin2θ+cos2θ≡1 to write the equation in one function, and never divide by a function that could be zero: factorise it out.

Learn

A quadratic in disguise

An equation such as 2cos2θ+cosθ−1 =0 is a quadratic in which cosθ plays the part of x. It factorises as a quadratic, and each bracket gives a simple equation.

Reject the impossible

The values of sinθ and cosθ always lie between −1 and 1, so a value outside that range gives no solutions. Reject that bracket and solve the others.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Solve 2cos2θ−cosθ−1 =0 for 0°≤θ≤360°.
Treat cosθ as x2x2−x−1=(2x+1)(x−1).
Factorise(2cosθ+1)(cosθ−1) =0
Each bracketcosθ=−12
or cosθ=1
Solutionsθ=0°, 120°, 240°, 360°
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Solve 2sin2θ +3sinθ −2 =0 for 0°≤θ≤360°.
sinθ=−2 is below −1, so that bracket gives no solutions.
Factorise(2sinθ−1)(sinθ+2) =0
Each bracketsinθ=12
sinθ=−2 is impossible
Solutionsθ=30°, 150°

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One function only

An equation in both sinθ and cosθ will not factorise until one of them is replaced. Replace cos2θ with 1−sin2θ, or sin2θ with 1−cos2θ, and keep the bracket.

Never divide by a function that can be zero

Dividing by sinθ or cosθ loses every solution where it is zero. Factorise it out instead.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Solve 2cos2θ+5sinθ =4 for 0°≤θ≤360°.
Replace cos2θ with 1−sin2θ2 −2sin2θ +5sinθ =4 rearranges to the quadratic
sinθ=2 is impossible.
Replace cos2θ2sin2θ −5sinθ +2=0
Factorise(2sinθ−1)(sinθ−2) =0
Solutionsθ=30°, 150°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Solve 3sinθtanθ=8 for 0°≤θ≤360°.
Write tanθ as sinθcosθ and multiply by cosθ: 3sin2θ=8cosθ. Then replace sin2θ with 1−cos2θ.
Use the identities3cos2θ +8cosθ −3=0
Factorise(3cosθ−1)(cosθ+3) =0
Solutionsθ=70.5°, 289.5°
