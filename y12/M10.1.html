Force Diagrams

Every problem in mechanics starts with a force diagram: if a force is missing from the picture, every equation after it is wrong. Resolving the forces in each direction then tells us whether a body accelerates, which leads to F=ma next lesson.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A force diagram shows every force on a body: its weight W=mg, the normal reaction R, and any tension, thrust, friction or resistance.
  • Newton's first law: a body stays at rest, or moves with constant velocity, unless a resultant force acts on it.
  • The resultant force is found by adding the forces in each direction; a body in equilibrium has zero resultant force.
By the end you can
Draw a force diagram for a body, labelling its weight, the normal reaction and any tension, friction or resistance.
Find the resultant force on a body, or an unknown force when it is in equilibrium, by resolving horizontally and vertically.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Every force on the body is drawn

A force diagram shows each force acting on the body as an arrow, labelled with its size or a letter. The weight W=mg acts downwards, and the normal reaction R acts upwards from the surface.

Strings pull, friction opposes

Tension in a string pulls away from the body, along the string. Friction and resistance act against the direction of motion.

Balanced forces mean constant velocity

Newton's first law says a body stays at rest, or moves with constant velocity, unless a resultant force acts on it. A car at a steady speed has its driving force exactly balanced by the resistance.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
A book of mass 2 kg rests on a horizontal table: draw a force diagram for the book.
Weight first2×9.8=19.6 N, down from the book. Then the normal reaction, up from the table.
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A 5 kg sledge is pulled over rough ground by a horizontal rope: draw its force diagram.
Weight 5×9.8=49 N down
reaction R up
tension T forwards along the rope
friction F backwards, against the motion.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Resolve in each direction

The resultant force in one direction is the sum of the forces that way, minus the forces the opposite way. Horizontal and vertical forces are resolved separately.

Equilibrium means zero resultant

A body at rest, or at constant velocity, is in equilibrium. The forces in each direction balance, so an unknown force can be found.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A box on smooth horizontal ground is pushed by 30 N to the right and pulled by 12 N to the left: find the resultant force.
Take right as positive.
Resolve horizontally30−12=18
Resultant18 N to the right
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A lamp of mass 2 kg hangs at rest from two vertical cables, each with tension T: find T.
At rest, so the upward forces balance the weight.
Resolve vertically2T=2×9.8
2T=19.6
TensionT=9.8 N
