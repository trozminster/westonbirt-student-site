Proof by Deduction

A proof shows that a statement is true in every case, not just the cases checked. Writing even, odd and consecutive numbers in algebra, and completing the square, turn claims about all numbers into a few lines of working; next lesson proves identities and geometric results the same way.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A proof is a logical argument that shows a statement is true in every case, working step by step from known facts to the conclusion.
  • An even number can be written 2n and an odd number 2n+1, where n is an integer, and consecutive integers can be written n, n+1, n+2
  • A proof ends with a sentence that states what has been shown.
By the end you can
Prove a statement about integers by deduction, writing even, odd and consecutive numbers algebraically.
Prove that an expression is always positive, or can never take a certain value, by completing the square.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Examples are not a proof

Checking examples shows that a statement is true for those examples only. A proof shows that it is true for every case at once.

Write the numbers in algebra

With n an integer, 2n is even, 2n+1 is odd, and n, n+1, n+2 are consecutive. Two different numbers need two different letters.

End with what is shown

The algebra must reach a form that shows the result, such as twice an integer. Then a sentence states what has been proved.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Prove that the square of any odd number is odd.
One number, so one letterwrite the odd number as 2n+1, where n is any integer.
Square 2n+1(2n+1)2=4n2+4n+1
=2(2n2+2n) +1
Concludeone more than a multiple of 2, so odd
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Prove that the sum of any two odd numbers is even.
Two different numbers need two different letterswrite them as 2n+1 and 2m+1
Add the odd numbers(2n+1) +(2m+1)=2n+2m+2
=2(n+m+1)
Concludea multiple of 2, so even

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A square is never negative

Any real number squared is 0 or positive. So (x+p)2≥0, and it equals 0 only when x=−p.

Complete the square to prove

Writing a quadratic as (x+p)2+q shows that its least value is q. When q>0, the quadratic is positive for every x.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Prove that x2+4x+9>0 for all real x.
A square is never negative, so the expression is at least the number left over.
Complete the squarex2+4x+9 =(x+2)2+5
(x+2)2≥0 for every x
Conclude(x+2)2 +5≥5>0
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Prove that x2−2x+4 can never equal 2
The least value is 3, which is more than 2
Complete the squarex2−2x+4 =(x−1)2+3
(x−1)2≥0, so x2−2x+4≥3
Conclude3>2, so it never equals 2

Hint. Follow the worked example: Complete the square → Conclude
