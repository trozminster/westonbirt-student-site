Modelling with Vectors

Velocity, displacement and force are all vectors, so everything in this chapter now models motion: speed is a magnitude, a position is a start plus a velocity times time, and forces add as vectors. It leads straight into Mechanics chapter 8 and the forces of chapter 10

By the end you’ll know…
  • The magnitude of a velocity vector is the speed, and its direction is the direction of motion.
  • For a constant velocity, position = starting position + velocity × time.
  • The resultant of several forces is their vector sum, and a body is in equilibrium when the resultant force is zero.

Speed is a magnitude

The speed is the magnitude of the velocity, and the direction of the velocity is the direction of motion. So a velocity of (3𝐢+4𝐣)ms−1 is a speed of 5ms−1.

Start, then velocity times time

With a constant velocity v from a starting position a, the position after t seconds is r=a+tv. The start must be added.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
A particle moves with velocity (6𝐢−8𝐣)ms−1. Find its speed and how far it travels in 5 seconds.
Speed is the magnitude.
Speed62+82=10ms−1
Distance10×5=50 m
WORKED EXAMPLE B

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Forces add as vectors

The resultant of several forces is their vector sum: add the i parts, then the j parts. Its magnitude and direction come from Pythagoras and tan.

Equilibrium means no resultant

A particle is in equilibrium when the resultant force is zero. So an unknown force is minus the sum of the others.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Forces (3𝐢+2𝐣) N and (5𝐢−8𝐣) N act on a particle. Find the resultant, its magnitude and its angle with i.
Add the parts.
R=(8𝐢−6𝐣) N
|R|=64+36=10 N
Angletan−1(6÷8) =36.9° below i
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Forces (3𝐢+2𝐣) N, (5𝐢−8𝐣) N and F act on a particle in equilibrium. Find F.
The three forces add to zero.
Sum of the known forces(8𝐢−6𝐣) N
F =−(8𝐢−6𝐣)
F =(−8𝐢+6𝐣) N
