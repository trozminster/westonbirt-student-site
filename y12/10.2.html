Exact Values of Trigonometric Ratios

The ratios of 30°, 45° and 60° have exact values, read from two triangles, and A-level questions ask for them exactly, not as a calculator's decimals. With last lesson's CAST they give exact values for angles such as 150° and 300°, which the equations of this chapter need.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The exact values for 30°, 45° and 60° come from half an equilateral triangle with sides 1, 3 and 2, and an isosceles right-angled triangle with sides 1, 1 and 2
  • sin30°=12, sin45°=22 and sin60°=32; cos30°=32, cos45°=22 and cos60°=12
  • tan30°=33, tan45°=1 and tan60°=3
  • With CAST, these give the exact values for related angles such as 150°, 225° and −60°.

Two triangles hold the values

Half an equilateral triangle of side 2 has sides 1, 3 and 2, with angles 30° and 60°. Half a square of side 1 has sides 1, 1 and 2, with two angles of 45°.

Each ratio is a quotient of sides

Every ratio of 30°, 45° or 60° divides one side of a triangle by another, so its value is exact. A surd in a denominator is rationalised.

Exact means keep the surd

An exact value keeps 2 or 3 and never becomes a decimal. A question that says exact wants the surd form.

Exact values for related angles

An angle whose related acute angle is 30°, 45° or 60° has an exact value too. Its size comes from the acute angle and its sign from CAST.

The minus comes from the quadrant

The exact value of the acute angle is always positive. A minus sign appears only when CAST says the ratio is negative in that quadrant.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the exact value of cos210°.
210° is in the third quadrant, where only tan is positive.
Related acute angle210°−180°=30°
Sign from CASTcos210°=−cos30°
=−32
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the exact value of tan(−120°).−120° turns clockwise past −90°, so it ends in the third quadrant.
