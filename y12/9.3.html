Areas of Triangles

The formula 12absinC finds the area of any triangle from two sides and the angle between them, and it works backwards to an unknown side or angle. Next lesson combines it with the sine and cosine rules to solve problems.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The area of any triangle is 12absinC, where C is the angle between sides a and b.
  • Because sinC =sin(180°−C), an area and two sides give two possible values of the angle between them.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Two sides and the angle between

The area of any triangle is 12absinC, where C is the angle between sides a and b. The height is bsinC, so this is ½ × base × height.

The angle must be between the sides

The angle in 12absinC is the one where sides a and b meet. An angle opposite one of the sides gives the wrong area.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the area of triangle ABC with a=8 cm, b=5 cm and C=30°.
C is between a and b, so it is the included angle.
SubstituteArea=½ × 8×5×sin(30°)
=10 cm²
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Triangle ABC has AB=AC=8 cm and angle ABC=70°. Find its area.
The triangle is isosceles, so angle ACB=70° too. The angle between AB and AC is at A.
Included angleA=180°−140°
=40°
SubstituteArea=½ × 8×8×sin(40°)
=20.6 cm²

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Work backwards from the area

Substitute the area and the known sides, then solve for what is unknown. To find the angle, rearrange to sinC=2×Areaab.

Two angles give the same area

Because sinC =sin(180°−C), an acute angle and an obtuse angle give the same area. Both are answers unless the question rules one out.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Triangle ABC has area 18 cm², a=6 cm and b=9 cm. Find the possible values of C.
½ × 6×9=27
The second angle is 180°−41.8°.
Substitute27sin(C)=18
Divide by 27sin(C)=0.667
C=41.8° or 138.2°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Triangle ABC has area 30 cm², b=8 cm and C=50°. Find a.
½ × a×8=4a.
Substitute4asin(50°)=30
Rearrangea=30÷(4sin(50°))
a=9.79 cm
YOUR TURN · c
A triangle has area 12 cm² and sides 5 cm and 7 cm. Find the possible angles between those sides.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Substitute → Divide by 27
17.5sin(C)=12, so sin(C)=0.686
C=43.3° or 136.7°
YOUR TURN · d
A triangle has sides x cm and (x+2) cm with an angle of 30° between them. Its area is 6 cm². Find x.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Substitute → Rearrange
12x(x+2)sin(30°) =6, so x(x+2)=24
x2+2x−24 =0, and x>0, so x=4
