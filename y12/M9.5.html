Vertical Motion Under Gravity

A body thrown straight up is the first real use of the formulae with a known acceleration: g=9.8ms−2, with one direction chosen as positive. Chapter 10 then asks what force causes that acceleration.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A body moving freely under gravity has a constant acceleration of g=9.8ms−2 downwards, unless a question gives another value.
  • Take one direction as positive, usually upwards, so a=−9.8ms−2 for a body projected upwards.
  • At its highest point the body's velocity is zero.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Gravity gives a constant acceleration

A body moving freely under gravity accelerates downwards at g=9.8ms−2. With upwards as positive, a=−9.8ms−2 on the way up and on the way down.

At the top, v=0

At its highest point the body stops for an instant, so its velocity there is zero. Its acceleration is still −9.8ms−2.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
A ball is projected vertically upwards at 14ms−1. Find its greatest height.
At the top v=0, and t is not wanted, so use v2=u2+2as with a=−9.8
Substitute0=142−2×9.8×s
Greatest heights=196÷19.6
=10 m
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Find the time until a ball projected vertically upwards at 14ms−1 returns to its starting point.
Back at the start, s=0: use s =ut+12at2
Substitute0=14t−4.9t2
Factoriset(14−4.9t)=0
Time of flightt=14÷4.9
=2.86 s

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Below the start is negative

Measure every displacement from the point of projection, with upwards positive. The sea 20 m below a cliff top is at s=−20 m.

Two roots, two moments

Solving for a given height can give two positive times, one on the way up and one on the way down. A negative root is a time before the throw, so it is rejected.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the times when a ball projected vertically upwards at 14.7ms−1 is 9.8 m above its point of projection.
Two positive rootsonce on the way up, once on the way down.
Substitute9.8=14.7t−4.9t2
Divide by 4.9t2−3t+2=0
Timest=1 s
t=2 s
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A stone is projected vertically upwards at 14.7ms−1 from a cliff top 19.6 m above the sea. Find its speed when it hits the sea.
The sea is below the start, so s=−19.6, and a=−9.8
Substitutev2=14.72 +2×(−9.8)×(−19.6)
v2=216.09+384.16
=600.25
Speed24.5ms−1
