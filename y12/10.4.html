Simple Trigonometric Equations

Unlike a linear equation, sinθ=k has infinitely many solutions, so an exam always gives an interval and wants every solution in it. The calculator gives one, and the graph or CAST gives the rest. It is the method every harder equation in this chapter comes back to.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation such as sinθ=k has infinitely many solutions; the calculator gives the principal value, and the graph or CAST gives the rest.
  • In an interval, the second solution of sinx=k is 180°−x, of cosx=k is 360°−x, and of tanx=k is x+180°.
  • An equation such as 3sinθ=2cosθ becomes tanθ=23 when both sides are divided by cosθ.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One value, many angles

The line y=k meets the sine curve again and again, so sinθ=k has infinitely many solutions. The interval in the question says which of them to give.

The second solution comes from symmetry

The calculator gives one solution, the principal value x. The symmetry of each graph gives the second.

Every solution in the interval

A solution outside the interval is moved in by adding or subtracting 360°. A negative principal value usually needs this first.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Solve sinθ=0.4 for 0°≤θ≤360°.
Keep the full calculator value until the end.
Principal valueθ=sin−1(0.4)=23.6°
Second solutionθ=180°−23.6°
=156.4°
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Solve cosθ=0.3 for 0°≤θ≤360°.
The cosine graph is symmetric about 180°, so the second solution is 360° minus the first.
Principal valueθ=cos−1(0.3)=72.5°
Second solutionθ=360°−72.5°
=287.5°

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Sin over cos is tan

Since tanθ=sinθcosθ, an equation with one sinθ term and one cosθ term becomes an equation in tanθ alone. Dividing both sides by cosθ does it.

Then solve for tanθ

The equation is now tanθ=k, solved as before. Its second solution is 180° after the first.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Solve 4sinθ=3cosθ for 0°≤θ≤360°.
Divide both sides by 4cosθ.
Divide by 4cosθtanθ=0.75
Principal valueθ=36.9°
Solutionsθ=36.9°, 216.9°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Solve sinθ+2cosθ=0 for 0°≤θ≤360°.
Subtract 2cosθ first, then divide by cosθ. The principal value is negative, so add 180° and 360°.
Divide by cosθtanθ=−2
Principal valueθ=−63.4°, outside
Solutionsθ=116.6°, 296.6°
