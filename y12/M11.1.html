Chapter M11 · Mechanics Chapter 11: Variable Acceleration · Lesson 1 of 5

Functions of Time

Real motion rarely has a constant acceleration, so from now on displacement and velocity can be any function of time. Reading a body's position, its moments at rest and the times its model holds from that function sets up differentiating and integrating it in the next lessons.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When the acceleration is not constant, displacement and velocity are functions of time, such as s=2t3−5t.
  • The constant acceleration formulae cannot be used when the acceleration varies.
  • A body is at rest when v=0, and back at its starting point when s=0

Position as a function of time

When the acceleration varies, s and v are given as functions of t. Substituting a time tells you where the body is then.

At rest or back at the start

A body is at rest at the moments its velocity is zero. It is back at its starting point when its displacement is zero.

The formulae need a constant acceleration

The constant acceleration formulae hold only when the velocity changes by the same amount each second. When v=3t2, it does not.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The graph from the function

Factorising s shows the times the body is at O, where the graph meets the t-axis. Between those times, the sign of s tells you which side of O the body is.

Each model has its valid times

A model holds only for a stated interval of time, such as 0≤t≤4
Outside it, the function can give values that make no sense, such as a negative height.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find where the graph of s=4t2−t3 meets the t-axis, and say what each point means.

t=4
Meaningit starts at O and is back at O when t=4 s
