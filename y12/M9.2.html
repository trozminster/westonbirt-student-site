Velocity-Time Graphs

A velocity-time graph shows how a body speeds up and slows down. Its gradient is the acceleration and the area under it is the displacement. The constant acceleration formulae of the next two lessons are read straight from this graph.

By the end you’ll know…
  • On a velocity-time graph the gradient is the acceleration, and the area between the graph and the time axis is the displacement.
  • A negative gradient is a deceleration, and an area below the time axis is a negative displacement.
  • The area of a trapezium-shaped graph is found in one step, as half the sum of the parallel sides times the distance between them.
By the end you can
Sketch a velocity-time graph for a journey in stages, and find the acceleration of each stage from its gradient.
Use the area under a velocity-time graph to find a distance, or an unknown time or speed.

The gradient is the acceleration

On a velocity-time graph, the gradient of each straight stage is the acceleration on that stage. A horizontal stage means a constant velocity, not that the body is at rest.

A negative gradient is a deceleration

A stage sloping down means the body is slowing down, so its acceleration is negative. A deceleration of 4ms−2 is an acceleration of −4ms−2.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
A car's velocity rises steadily from 4ms−1 to 22ms−1 in 6 s. Find its acceleration.

Find the gradient(22−4)÷6=3
Acceleration3ms−2

Find the gradient(6−30)÷8 =−3

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The area is the displacement

The area between a velocity-time graph and the time axis is the displacement. Split it into triangles and rectangles, or treat the whole shape as a trapezium.

A trapezium in one step

The area of a trapezium is half the sum of the parallel sides times the distance between them. Here the parallel sides are times and the distance between them is the velocity.

Area below the axis is negative

Where the velocity is negative the body moves backwards, so an area below the time axis is a negative displacement. For the total distance, add the sizes of the areas.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A car goes from rest to 12ms−1 in 4 s, holds that speed for 10 s, then stops in 2 s. Find the distance it travels.
Sketcha trapezium, top 10 s, base 16 s, height 12
Parallel sides10 s and 16 s
Distance12(10+16)×12 =156 m
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A car goes from rest to 12ms−1 in 4 s, holds it for T s, then stops in 2 s, covering 204 m. Find T.
The same trapezium, now with the area giventop T, base T+6
Form the equation12(T+T+6)×12=204
Divide by 62T+6=34
T=14
