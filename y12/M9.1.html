Displacement-Time Graphs

A displacement-time graph shows where a body is at every moment, and its gradient shows how fast it moves and in which direction. The next lesson's velocity-time graphs build on this one, and together they lead to the constant acceleration formulae.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Displacement is the distance from a fixed point in a given direction, so it can be negative; distance is its size only.
  • On a displacement-time graph the gradient is the velocity, and a horizontal line means the body is at rest.
  • Average velocity is the total displacement divided by the total time; average speed is the total distance divided by the total time.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Displacement has a direction

Displacement is measured from a fixed point in a chosen positive direction, so it can be negative. Distance is its size only, and is never negative.

The gradient is the velocity

On a displacement-time graph, the gradient of each straight stage is the velocity on that stage. A horizontal stage has gradient zero, so the body is at rest.

A negative gradient means coming back

A stage sloping down means the displacement is falling, so the body moves back towards O. Its velocity is negative, and its speed is the size of the velocity.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Average velocity uses displacement

Average velocity is the total displacement divided by the total time. A journey that ends where it began has an average velocity of zero.

Average speed uses distance

Average speed is the total distance divided by the total time. When a body turns back, the distance is more than the displacement, so the two averages differ.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A car drives 600 m east in 40 s, then 300 m further east in 20 s. Find its average speed and average velocity.
All one way, so the distance and the size of the displacement are equal.
Average speed900÷60=15ms−1
Average velocity900÷60=15ms−1 east
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A runner runs 600 m east in 100 s, then 200 m west in 60 s. Find her average speed and average velocity.
She turns backdistance 800 m, displacement 400 m east.
Average speed800÷160=5ms−1
Average velocity400÷160=2.5ms−1 east
