Solving Triangle Problems

Problems from surveying and navigation do not say which rule to use. Choosing the rule from what is known, and keeping full accuracy through several steps, is what exam questions on triangles test.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Choose the rule from what you know: the sine rule needs a side and the angle opposite it; the cosine rule needs three sides, or two sides and the angle between them.
  • A bearing is measured clockwise from north, and a problem may need two or more triangles in turn.
  • Draw and label a diagram first, and keep unrounded values until the final answer.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Look for a complete pair

A side with its opposite angle calls for the sine rule. With no complete pair, use the cosine rule. It needs three sides, or two sides and the angle between them.

Keep the unrounded value

Store each intermediate answer in the calculator and use it in the next step. Round only the final answer.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
In triangle PQR, PQ=9 cm, QR=12 cm and angle PQR=58°. Find PR.
No side is known with its opposite angle. Q is between PQ and QR, so use the cosine rule.
Find a pairnone, so the cosine rule

WORKED EXAMPLE B

PQ is opposite R, so PQ and R are a complete pair. QR is opposite P.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

North lines are parallel

The north lines at two points are parallel, so co-interior angles between them add up to 180°. Work out each angle inside the triangle from the bearings before using a rule.

Measure from the right point

The bearing of B from A is measured clockwise from north at A. The bearing of A from B differs by 180°.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A ship sails 12 km from A on a bearing of 060° to B, then 8 km on a bearing of 170° to C. Find the distance AC.
The bearing of A from B is 240°, and BC is on 170°, so the angle between them is 70°.
Use the north linesangle ABC=240°−170°=70°
Cosine ruleAC²=144+64−192cos70°
AC=11.9 km
WORKED EXAMPLE D
For the same journey, find the bearing of C from A.
Keep AC=11.930... stored. C is clockwise from B as seen from A.
Sine rulesin(BAC)=8sin(70°)11.930
angle BAC=39.1°
Add to 060°bearing 099.1°
