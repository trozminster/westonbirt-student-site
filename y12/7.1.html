Algebraic Fractions

Algebraic fractions simplify exactly as number fractions do: only a factor of the whole top and the whole bottom cancels. Factorising to cancel, and clearing a fraction inside a fraction, are the moves that polynomial division and the factor theorem build on next.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An algebraic fraction is simplified by factorising the numerator and the denominator fully, then cancelling any common factor.
  • Only a factor of the whole numerator and the whole denominator can be cancelled, never a single term.
  • A fraction with fractions inside it is simplified by multiplying its numerator and denominator by the same number or expression.
By the end you can
Simplify an algebraic fraction by factorising its numerator and denominator, including quadratics with a≠1
Simplify a fraction whose numerator or denominator contains fractions, by multiplying the top and bottom by the same expression.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Only factors cancel

A number or bracket cancels only when it is a factor of the whole top and the whole bottom. A term of a sum is not a factor, so it never cancels.

Factorise fully, then cancel

Factorise the numerator and the denominator fully. Then cancel every bracket or factor they share.

A cancelled factor leaves 1

A factor divided by itself is 1, not 0
When every factor of the top cancels, the numerator is 1

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Clear the inner fractions

A fraction inside a fraction is cleared by multiplying its top and its bottom by the same thing. Choose the lowest common multiple of the inner denominators.

The same multiplier top and bottom

Multiplying the top and the bottom by the same expression leaves the value unchanged. Multiplying only one of them changes it.
