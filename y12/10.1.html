Angles in All Four Quadrants

Sine, cosine and tangent are defined for every angle, not just those in a right-angled triangle, by a point turning round the unit circle. Every angle is tied to an acute angle with a sign from CAST, which the exact values and the equations of this chapter use next.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An angle is measured anticlockwise from the positive x-axis, and a negative angle clockwise.
  • For the point P(x,y) on the unit circle at angle θ, cosθ=x, sinθ=y and tanθ=yx.
  • The signs follow CAST: all three ratios are positive in the first quadrant, only sin in the second, only tan in the third and only cos in the fourth.
  • Each angle has a related acute angle, the angle it makes with the x-axis.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Angles turn from the positive x-axis

An angle is measured from the positive x-axis, anticlockwise when it is positive and clockwise when it is negative. The axes split one turn into four quadrants at 90°, 180° and 270°.

The related acute angle

Every angle makes an acute angle with the x-axis, called its related acute angle. It is measured to the nearer half of the x-axis, never to the y-axis.

One rule per quadrant

The related acute angle is the distance from the angle to 0°, 180° or 360°, whichever is nearest. So each quadrant has its own subtraction.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the quadrant and the related acute angle of 240°.
Compare 240° with 180° and 270°. It is past 180°, so measure back to the negative x-axis.
Compare180°<240°<270°, the third quadrant
Related acute angle240°−180°=60°

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Ratios from the unit circle

The point at angle θ on a circle of radius 1 is (cosθ,sinθ), and tanθ=yx. So each ratio takes its sign from the coordinates of that point.

CAST

All three ratios are positive in the first quadrant, then only sin, only tan and only cos as θ turns. Reading anticlockwise from the fourth quadrant spells CAST.

Size from the acute angle, sign from CAST

sin, cos or tan of any angle equals the same ratio of its related acute angle. The quadrant decides whether a minus sign goes in front.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Write sin200° in terms of the sine of an acute angle.
Find the related acute angle first, then take the sign from CAST.
Related acute angle200°−180°=20°
Sign from CASTthird quadrant, sin is negative
sin200°=−sin20°
