Proving Identities and Geometric Results

An identity is true for every value of x, so it is proved by turning one side into the other, never by checking values. Coordinates let the same method prove geometric facts, such as a right angle or a parallelogram; next lesson adds proof by exhaustion and disproof by counter-example.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An identity is true for every value of the variable, and is written with the symbol ≡.
  • To prove an identity, start from one side and manipulate it until it equals the other side; never work on both sides at once.
  • Geometric results can be proved with coordinates: gradients show that lines are parallel or perpendicular, and the distance formula shows that lengths are equal.
By the end you can
Prove an algebraic identity by manipulating one side until it equals the other, showing every step.
Prove a geometric result with coordinates, such as that a triangle is right-angled or a quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

An identity holds for every x

An equation is true for some values of x, but an identity is true for every value. The symbol ≡ marks an identity.

Work from one side

Start with one side and change it, step by step, until it is the other side. Never change both sides at once.

WORKED EXAMPLE A

Factorise=x(x+8), the right side

Simplify=12n, the right side

Gradients test the angle

Parallel lines have equal gradients. The gradients of perpendicular lines multiply to −1

Distances test the lengths

The distance between two points is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle, found by Pythagoras. Two equal distances prove that two sides are equal.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A(0,1), B(2,5) and C(6,3). Prove that triangle ABC is right-angled.
Find the gradients of the two sides that meet at B. Their product is −1, so AB is perpendicular to BC.
Find gradient AB5−12−0=2
Find gradient BC3−56−2=−12
Conclude2×(−12)=−1
angle ABC=90°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
P(1,1), Q(4,2), R(6,5) and S(3,4). Prove that PQRS is a parallelogram.
A parallelogram needs both pairs of opposite sides parallel.
Find gradients PQ and SR13 and 13
Find gradients QR and PS32 and 32
Concludeboth pairs of opposite sides are parallel, so PQRS is a parallelogram
