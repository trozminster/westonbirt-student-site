Dividing Polynomials

Dividing a polynomial by (x+p) or (x−p) works just like long division of numbers, and writes it as (x−p)q(x) +r. A remainder of 0 means (x−p) is a factor, which the factor theorem uses next lesson to factorise and solve cubics.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A polynomial is divided by (x+p) or (x−p) by long division: divide the leading terms, multiply back, subtract, then bring down the next term.
  • A missing power of x is written in with a zero coefficient, such as 0x2, before dividing.
  • The result can be written f(x) =(x−p)q(x) +r, where q(x) is the quotient and r is the remainder.
By the end you can
Divide a cubic or quartic polynomial by (x+p) or (x−p), writing in any missing powers of x.
Write a cubic in the form (x+p)(ax2 + bx + c)+d, and use the quotient to factorise it fully.

Divide, multiply back, subtract

Polynomial long division repeats the cycle of number long division. Divide the leading terms, multiply back, subtract, then bring down the next term.

A missing power keeps its place

Each power of x below the leading one needs its own column. A missing power is written in with a zero coefficient before dividing.

Learn

Quotient and remainder

Dividing f(x) by (x−p) gives a quotient q(x) and a remainder r. So f(x) =(x−p)q(x) +r, where r is a number.

A remainder of 0 gives a factor

When the remainder is 0, (x−p) is a factor and f(x) =(x−p)q(x). Factorise the quadratic quotient to finish.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Write x3 +2x2 −5x +4 in the form (x+3)(ax2 + bx + c)+d.
Divide by (x+3), as in cycle 1the remainder is d.
Divide by (x+3)x2−x−2, remainder 10
Write the form(x+3)(x2−x−2) +10
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Divide x3 +2x2 −5x −6 by (x+3), then factorise it fully.
The remainder is 0, so (x+3) is a factor: factorise the quotient.
Divide by (x+3)x2−x−2, remainder 0
Factorise the quotient(x+3)(x−2)(x+1)
