The Cosine Rule

The cosine rule extends Pythagoras to every triangle: it finds a side from two sides and the angle between them, or an angle from three sides. The sine rule, next lesson, covers the cases it cannot.

By the end you’ll know…
  • In any triangle ABC, side a is opposite angle A, and a2 =b2 +c2 −2bccosA.
  • Use it to find a side from two sides and the angle between them, or an angle from three sides, using cosA =b2+c2−a22bc.
  • An obtuse angle has a negative cosine.
By the end you can
Use the cosine rule to find an unknown side, given two sides and the angle between them.
Use the cosine rule to find an unknown angle, given three sides, including an obtuse angle.
Use the cosine rule to form and solve an equation for an unknown length, such as a quadratic in x.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The angle between two sides

The cosine rule links the three sides of any triangle to one angle. It finds the side opposite the angle that lies between two known sides.

The last term is one product

The term 2bccosA is worked out first and then subtracted. When A is obtuse, cosA is negative, so subtracting it adds.

Pythagoras is the special case

When A=90°, cosA=0 and the last term vanishes. The cosine rule then reads a2=b2+c2

WORKED EXAMPLE A
In triangle ABC, b=8 cm, c=5 cm and A=60°. Find a.
Write the rule firsta2=b2 +c2 −2bccosA.
Substitutea2=64+25−80cos60°

=49
Square roota=7 cm

Substitutea2=36+100−120cos120°

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Rearrange the rule for the angle

To find an angle, use cosA =b2+c2−a22bc. The side opposite the angle comes last, after the minus sign.

A negative cosine means obtuse

When b2+c2 is less than a2, cosA is negative and A lies between 90° and 180°. The inverse cosine on a calculator gives this obtuse angle directly.

A side in x gives an equation

When a side is written in terms of x, the cosine rule becomes an equation in x. It is often a quadratic, and a length must be positive.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A triangle has sides 12 cm, 5 cm and 9 cm. Find its largest angle.
The largest angle is opposite the longest side, 12 cm.
Rearranged rulecosA=25+81−14490
=−0.422
Inverse cosineA=115.0°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
In triangle ABC, AB=x cm, AC=(x+3) cm, BC=7 cm and angle BAC=60°. Find x.
2×x(x+3)×cos60° =x(x+3). A length is positive, so reject x=−8
Substitute49=x2 +(x+3)2 −x(x+3)
Collectx2+3x−40=0
Factorise(x+8)(x−5)=0
x=5
