Stacked Particles and Lifts

You feel heavier as a lift sets off upwards and lighter as it slows: the floor's push on you changes, and F=ma measures exactly how much. The same method of one diagram per body, then one for the whole system, solves boxes stacked on each other and leads to pulleys next.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When one body rests on another, or a person stands in a lift, each exerts an equal and opposite normal reaction on the other (Newton's third law).
  • Draw one force diagram for each body, and one for the whole system.
  • In a lift accelerating upwards, the reaction from the floor is greater than the person's weight; accelerating downwards, it is less.
By the end you can
Find the reaction between a person and the floor of a lift, or between two stacked boxes, when the lift moves with a given acceleration.
Find the tension in a lift cable, or the acceleration of the lift, from force diagrams for the whole system and for each body.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Bodies in contact push on each other equally

Newton's third law says that when one body pushes on another, the other pushes back with an equal and opposite force. A box resting on a second box pushes down on it with the same force R that pushes up on the top box.

In a lift the reaction changes

Accelerating upwards, the floor pushes up with more than the person's weight, and accelerating downwards, with less. F=ma upwards on the person gives R - mg =ma.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
A woman of mass 60 kg stands in a lift accelerating upwards at 1.2ms−2: find the reaction from the floor.
Upwards positiveR helps, the weight opposes.
Resolve upwardsR−60×9.8=60×1.2
R−588=72
ReactionR=660 N
WORKED EXAMPLE B
The same woman stands in the lift as it accelerates downwards at 1.2ms−2: find the reaction from the floor.
Downwards positive nowthe weight helps, R opposes.
Resolve downwards60×9.8−R=60×1.2
588−R=72
ReactionR=516 N

Hint. Follow the worked example: Upwards positive → Resolve upwards → Reaction

R=49+10=59 N
YOUR TURN · b

Hint. Follow the worked example: Downwards positive now → Resolve downwards → Reaction
Top box alone: R−2×9.8=2×0.2
R=19.6+0.4=20 N

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The cable holds up everything

The cable supports the lift and everything in it, so the whole system's mass goes into F=ma. The tension is the only upward force on the whole system.

One body for the reaction

The reaction between the person and the floor is internal to the whole system, so it cancels there. Drawing the person alone brings it into the equation.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A lift of mass 400 kg carries a man of mass 80 kg and accelerates upwards at 0.5ms−2: find the tension in the cable.
Whole system480 kg. The tension is the only upward force.
Whole systemT−480×9.8=480×0.5
T−4704=240
TensionT=4944 N
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A lift of mass 500 kg carries a woman of mass 60 kg, and the tension in the cable is 5040 N: find the acceleration of the lift.
Whole system560 kg. A negative answer means downwards.
Whole system5040−560×9.8=560a
−448=560a
a=−0.8
Acceleration0.8ms−2 downwards
YOUR TURN · c
A lift of mass 600 kg carries a load of 150 kg and accelerates downwards at 1.4ms−2. Find the tension in the cable.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Whole system → Whole system → Tension
750×9.8−T=750×1.4
T=7350−1050=6300 N
YOUR TURN · d
A lift of mass 300 kg carries a man of mass 70 kg, and the tension in the cable is 3996 N. Find the acceleration of the lift and the reaction on the man.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Whole system → Whole system → Acceleration
3996−370×9.8=370a, so a=1ms−2 upwards
R−70×9.8 =70×1, so R=756 N
