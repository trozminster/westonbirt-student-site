Transforming Trigonometric Graphs

The transformations of chapter 4 work on trigonometric graphs unchanged, so a period, a height or a shift can be read straight from an equation. Reading the constants back from a sketch is a standard exam step, and these graphs return when chapter 10 solves equations such as sin(2x)=k.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The transformations of chapter 4 apply to trigonometric graphs.
  • y=3sinθ is a vertical stretch of y=sinθ with scale factor 3, so its maximum value is 3
  • y=sin(bθ) is a horizontal stretch with scale factor 1b, so the period becomes 360°/b.
  • y=sinθ+c is a translation up by c, and y=sin(θ+c) is a translation left by c.

02

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A number outside acts vertically

The graph of y=3sinθ is y=sinθ stretched vertically by scale factor 3, so it runs from −3 to 3
Adding a number outside, as in y=sinθ+1, moves the graph up.

A number inside acts horizontally

The graph of y=sin(2θ) is y=sinθ stretched horizontally by scale factor ½, so the period is 360°÷2=180°. Adding a number inside, as in y =sin(θ+90°), moves the graph left.

Asymptotes move with the graph

The graph of y=tan(θ+60°) is y=tanθ moved 60° to the left. Its asymptotes move too, from 90° to 30° and from 270° to 210°.

A HORIZONTAL STRETCH
Sketch y=cos(2θ) for 0°≤θ≤360°, giving its period.
The 2 is inside the function, so the stretch is horizontal with scale factor ½. The period is 360°÷2=180°. The maxima are at 0°, 180° and 360°, and the minima at 90° and 270°.
A TRANSLATION LEFT
Now sketch y=tan(θ+45°) for 0°≤θ≤360°, giving its asymptotes.
Adding 45° inside moves the graph 45° to the left. The asymptotes move from 90° and 270° to 45° and 225°. It crosses the θ-axis at 135° and 315°, and meets the vertical axis at (0°,1).

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The height gives a

The graph of y=asin(bθ) or y=acos(bθ) runs from −a to a. So a is the maximum value on the sketch.

The period gives b

One repeat of y=acos(bθ) takes 360°÷b. So b is 360° divided by the period read from the sketch.

A shift moves a known point

The zeros and turning points of y=sinθ are known. Match one of them to the sketch to find the shift c in y=sin(θ+c).

WORKED EXAMPLE A
The graph of y=acos(bθ) has a maximum at (0°,5) and period 120°. Find a and b.
The maximum value is a. The period of y=cos(bθ) is 360°÷b.
Read the maximuma=5
Divide 360° by the periodb=360°÷120°

y=5cos(3θ)
