Pulleys: When the String Goes Slack

A pulley problem does not end when one particle hits the ground: the string goes slack and the other particle moves on by itself. Splitting the motion into stages, each starting from the last one's speed, is how the exam's longest mechanics questions are solved.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When one particle hits the ground, the string goes slack and the tension becomes zero: the other particle moves on under gravity alone, or at constant speed on a smooth table.
  • The speed at the moment of impact is the initial speed for the next stage.
  • Use the constant acceleration formulae for each stage in turn.
By the end you can
Find how much further a particle travels, or the greatest height it reaches, after the string goes slack.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A slack string has no tension

When the falling particle hits the ground, the string goes slack and its tension becomes zero. The other particle then moves on under gravity alone.

The speed at impact starts the next stage

The speed when the string goes slack is the initial speed for the next stage. The rising particle keeps rising, slowing at g, until it stops.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Particles of 3 kg and 2 kg hang over a smooth pulley and accelerate from rest at 1.96ms−2 until the 3 kg particle hits the ground 2 m below: find how much further the 2 kg particle rises.
Stage 1 gives the speed at impact. After that the tension is 0, so the 2 kg particle slows at 9.8

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Add the stages for the greatest height

The greatest height above the start is the distance risen while the string is taut plus the distance risen after it goes slack. Each stage has its own acceleration.

On a smooth table the block moves on steadily

When the hanging particle lands, a block on a smooth table has no horizontal force on it. It moves on at its speed at that moment until it reaches the pulley.

Units and sensible accuracy

Every answer carries its unit. An answer found with g=9.8 is given to 2 or 3 significant figures.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Particles of 5 kg and 3 kg hang over a smooth pulley and accelerate from rest at 2.45ms−2 until the 5 kg particle hits the ground 2 m below: find the greatest height the 3 kg particle reaches above its start.
It rises 2 m while the string is taut, then more under gravity.
Speed at impactv2=2×2.45×2
=9.8
Rise after slacks=9.8÷19.6
=0.5
Greatest height2+0.5=2.5 m
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A block of mass 2 kg on a smooth table is joined over a pulley at the edge to a particle of mass 0.5 kg, released from rest 0.6 m above the floor: find the block's speed when the particle lands.
