Position Vectors

A position vector pins a vector to the origin, so points become vectors and the vector between two points is a subtraction. This lets every coordinate problem, from distances to parallelograms, be done with vectors, and it is how mechanics gives a particle's position.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The position vector of a point A is the vector from the origin O to A, written a.
  • The vector from A to B is b−a.
  • The distance between A and B is |b−a|.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

From the origin

The position vector of a point A is OA→, written a. The vector from A to B is end minus start: AB→ = b−a.

Distance is a magnitude

The distance AB is the magnitude of AB→. So AB=|b−a|, by Pythagoras on its parts.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
A and B have position vectors 2𝐢+5𝐣 and 7𝐢−𝐣. Find AB→.
End minus start.
AB→ = b−a
=(7−2)𝐢 +(−1−5)𝐣
=5𝐢−6𝐣
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A and B have position vectors 2𝐢+5𝐣 and 7𝐢−𝐣. Find the distance AB exactly.
The distance is a magnitude.
AB→ = 5𝐢−6𝐣
AB =52+(−6)2
=61

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Opposite sides are equal vectors

In a parallelogram ABCD, AB→ = DC→ and AD→ = BC→. So D is found from C by going back along AB→: d =c−(b−a).

A distance gives an equation

If AB is known and one part of AB→ is unknown, Pythagoras gives an equation. Squaring means there are often two answers.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
ABCD is a parallelogram with a = 𝐢+2𝐣, b=5𝐢+3𝐣 and c=6𝐢+7𝐣. Find d.
Opposite sides are equal vectors.
DC→ = AB→ = 4𝐢+𝐣
d=c − DC→
d=2𝐢+6𝐣
WORKED EXAMPLE D
a=2𝐢+𝐣 and b=5𝐢+k𝐣, and AB=5
Find k.
Pythagoras gives two values.
AB→ = 3𝐢+(k−1)𝐣
32+(k−1)2 =25
k−1=±4, so k=5 or k=−3
