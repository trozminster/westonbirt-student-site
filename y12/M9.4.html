Constant Acceleration Formulae 2

Three more formulae complete the set, so that any constant acceleration problem needs only the right choice of formula. They also handle questions with two answers, and two bodies moving at once, which the next lesson uses for a body thrown upwards.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Three more formulae: v2=u2+2as, s =ut+12at2 and s =vt−12at2
  • Each formula leaves out one of s, u, v, a and t: choose the one without the quantity you neither know nor need.
  • A formula can give two values of t, such as the two times a body passes the same point; check each against the context.

Each formula leaves one out

Each of the five formulae leaves out one of s, u, v, a and t. Choose the one without the quantity you neither know nor need.

New formulae from the first two

Substituting v=u+at into s =12(u+v)t gives s =ut+12at2
Eliminating t instead gives v2 =u2+2as.

A quadratic in t can give two times

The formula s =ut+12at2 is a quadratic in t, so it can give two values of t. Both can be valid, such as the two times a body passes the same point.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One clock, one origin

Measure both bodies' times from the same moment and their displacements from the same point. One catches the other when their displacements are equal.

A journey in stages

The final velocity of one stage is the initial velocity of the next. Work through the journey one stage at a time, carrying v across.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find when car B, starting from rest at O with acceleration 2.5ms−2, catches car A, which passes O at a constant 10ms−1 as B starts.
Both are timed from the same moment and measured from O
A has s=10t, B has s=1.25t2
Set the displacements equal1.25t2=10t
Factorise1.25t(t−8)=0
Timet=8 s
