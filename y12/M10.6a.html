Particles Connected Over a Pulley

A pulley turns a falling weight into a pull in another direction: lifts, cranes and many exam questions run on it. Writing F=ma for each particle in its own direction of motion finds the acceleration and the tension, and next lesson follows the system on after the string goes slack.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A light inextensible string over a smooth pulley has the same tension on both sides, and both particles have the same magnitude of acceleration.
  • Write F=ma for each particle separately in its own direction of motion, then solve the equations simultaneously.
  • The force the string exerts on the pulley is the vector sum of the two tensions.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Same tension, same acceleration

Over a smooth pulley, a light inextensible string has the same tension on both sides, and both particles have the same size of acceleration. The heavier particle moves down and the lighter one moves up.

One equation for each particle

F=ma is written for each particle in its own direction of motion. Adding the two equations removes T and gives the acceleration.

One particle on a table

When one particle is on a smooth horizontal table, only the tension pulls it along. The hanging particle's weight drives the whole system.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The string pulls on the pulley

The string pulls the pulley with both of its tensions, so the force on the pulley is their vector sum. Two vertical strings give a force of 2T downwards.

At a table's edge the tensions are at right angles

When one string is horizontal and the other vertical, the force on the pulley comes from Pythagoras, T√2
It acts at 45° below the horizontal, towards the table.

Each assumption does one job

Every modelling assumption makes one step of the method work. State the assumption and the effect it has.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
