Maxima and Minima Problems

The greatest height of a ball, the top speed of a car and the furthest a particle gets from O are all stationary values, found by setting a derivative to zero. Justifying that a value is the greatest, and checking the ends of the interval, is what turns a stationary point into a full exam answer.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The greatest or least displacement occurs where dsdt=0, and the greatest or least velocity where dvdt=0
  • Use the second derivative, or the context, to decide whether a value is a maximum or a minimum.
  • Check the end points of the interval for which the model is valid.
By the end you can
Find the maximum displacement or the maximum velocity of a body, and the time at which it occurs.
Find the greatest speed of a body in a given interval, checking the end points and justifying that it is a maximum.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The top of the displacement graph

The displacement is greatest where the s−t graph turns, so where dsdt=0
That is the moment the body is at rest.

The top of the velocity graph

The velocity is greatest where the v−t graph turns, so where dvdt=0
That is the moment the acceleration is zero.

The second derivative decides

At a stationary value, a negative second derivative means a maximum and a positive one means a minimum. The context can confirm it too.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Speed is the size of the velocity

The greatest speed can come from a negative velocity. Compare the sizes of the greatest and least velocities, not their signs.

Check the end points

On an interval, the greatest value can be at an end, not at a stationary point. Work out the velocity at each end as well.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the greatest speed of a particle with v=t2−8t+7 for 0≤t≤5
Find the stationary value, then compare its size with the values at both ends: v=7,−9 and −8 at t=0, 4 and 5
Set a to zero2t−8=0
t=4
Compare sizes9 at t=4, against 7 and 8 at the ends
Greatest speed9ms−1 at t=4
