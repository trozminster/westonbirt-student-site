The Sine Rule

The sine rule solves a triangle whenever a side and the angle opposite it are known, the cases the cosine rule cannot reach. Its ambiguous case, where one sine gives two angles, returns in the area formula next lesson.

By the end you’ll know…
  • In any triangle ABC, asin(A) =bsin(B) =csin(C), and sin(A)a =sin(B)b is the form for finding an angle.
  • Use it when you know a side and the angle opposite it.
  • Because sinθ =sin(180°−θ), an angle found from its sine may have a second value: the ambiguous case.
  • The second value gives a second triangle when it and the known angle add up to less than 180°.

Use the sine rule to find an unknown side or an unknown angle.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Each side with its opposite angle

In any triangle, each side divided by the sine of the angle opposite it gives the same value. Use the sine rule when a side and its opposite angle are both known.

The unknown goes on top

To find a side, put the sides on top: asin(A) =bsin(B). To find an angle, put the sines on top: sin(A)a =sin(B)b.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
In triangle ABC, A=40°, B=75° and a=8 cm. Find b.
a and A are a complete pair, and b is opposite B.
Pair the sidesbsin(75°)=8sin(40°)
Rearrangeb=8sin(75°)÷sin(40°)
b=12.0 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE B
In triangle ABC, A=50°, a=9 cm and b=7 cm. Find B.
b < a, so B<AB is acute and there is one answer.
Pair the sinessin(B)7=sin(50°)9
Multiply by 7sin(B)=7sin(50°)9=0.596
B=36.6°

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Two angles share a sine

Because sinθ =sin(180°−θ), an angle found from its sine may have a second value. The calculator gives only the acute one.

Check that the second angle fits

The second angle gives a triangle only if it and the known angle add up to less than 180°. Then two different triangles fit the same facts.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
In triangle ABC, A=30°, c=10 cm and a=6 cm. Find the possible values of C.
The second value is 180°−56.4°, and 123.6°+30°<180°, so both fit.
Pair the sinessin(C)=10sin(30°)6=0.833
Inverse sineC=56.4°
Use 180°−CC=56.4° or 123.6°
WORKED EXAMPLE D
In triangle ABC, A=50°, c=7 cm and a=9 cm. Find the possible values of C.
The second value would be 143.4°, but 143.4°+50°>180°.
Pair the sinessin(C)=7sin(50°)9=0.596
Inverse sineC=36.6°
Test 180°−CC=36.6° only
