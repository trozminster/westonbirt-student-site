Trigonometric Identities

sin2θ+cos2θ≡1 is Pythagoras on the unit circle, and with tanθ≡sinθcosθ it links all three ratios. Today it gives exact values from one known ratio and proves identities; the equations of the next three lessons use it to leave one function.

By the end you’ll know…
  • For every angle θ, sin2θ +cos2θ≡1, and tanθ≡sinθcosθ where cosθ≠0
  • The first rearranges to sin2θ≡1−cos2θ and cos2θ≡1 −sin2θ.
  • Given one ratio exactly, find another with sin2θ+cos2θ≡1 or a right-angled triangle, then use the quadrant of θ to choose its sign.
  • To prove an identity, start from the more complicated side and work until it equals the other side; common denominators, factorising and the difference of two squares help.
By the end you can
Given the exact value of one trigonometric ratio and the quadrant of θ, find the exact values of the other two.
Prove a trigonometric identity, such as (sinθ+cosθ)2≡1 +2sinθcosθ, working from one side to the other.
Show that an equation can be written in terms of one trigonometric function, such as a quadratic in cosθ.

Pythagoras on the unit circle

The point (cosθ,sinθ) lies on a circle of radius 1, so sin2θ+cos2θ≡1 for every θ. Dividing its y-coordinate by its x-coordinate gives tanθ≡sinθcosθ.

One ratio fixes the size of the others

Knowing one ratio exactly, the identity gives the size of another. The square root leaves a ± until the quadrant decides.

The quadrant fixes the sign

The same size of ratio appears in two quadrants. CAST says which sign belongs to θ.

Learn

Prove from one side

An identity is true for every θ, so it is proved, not solved. Start from the more complicated side and change it, one step at a time, until it is the other side.

Spot the identity in disguise

The identity sin2θ+cos2θ≡1 also reads 1−sin2θ≡cos2θ and 1 −cos2θ≡sin2θ. Factorising and the difference of two squares often reveal one of them.

One function only

Replacing sin2θ or cos2θ leaves an equation with only one trigonometric function in it. It is then a quadratic in that function, ready to solve.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Prove that (1−sin2θ)tanθ≡sinθcosθ.

Replace tanθ=cos2θ×sinθcosθ
=sinθcosθ = RHS
