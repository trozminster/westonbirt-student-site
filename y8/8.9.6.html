Calculating in Standard Form

Calculating directly in standard form is what makes very large or very small quantities usable in a real calculation, from astronomical distances to the mass of a single atom, without ever writing out all the zeros.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Multiplying in standard form multiplies the A values and adds the indices.
  • Dividing divides the A values and subtracts the indices.
  • The result is then adjusted if its A falls outside 1≤A<10
  • Adding and subtracting need a common index first, because only matching powers of ten combine.
  • Alternatively both numbers can be written in ordinary form, added or subtracted, and converted back.

Multiplying and dividing

To multiply, multiply the A values and add the indices. To divide, divide the A values and subtract the indices. Then bring A back between 1 and 10

Multiplying in standard form multiplies the A values and adds the indices; dividing divides the A values and subtracts them. The result may then need adjusting back into standard form if A no longer sits between 1 and 10

Adding and subtracting

To add or subtract, the indices must match first. Otherwise, go through ordinary form and convert back.

Adding and subtracting in standard form need a common index first, because only matching powers of ten combine directly - otherwise, both numbers can be written in ordinary form, added or subtracted, and converted back.
