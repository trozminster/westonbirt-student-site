Surface Area of Cylinders

The label on a tin is a rectangle that wraps exactly once around it. Today we unroll cylinders to find their surface area, and decide when a real problem needs area and when it needs volume.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A cylinder's net is two circles and one rectangle.
  • The curved surface unrolls into a rectangle whose width is the circumference, 2×π×r, and whose height is h.
  • So the curved surface area is 2×π×r×h, and the total surface area is 2×π×r2 +2×π×r×h.
  • Surface area is in square units and volume in cubic units, which is what decides which a context needs.
  • An irregular solid can be approximated by breaking it into prisms and cylinders.

The net of a cylinder

A cylinder's net is two circles and one rectangle. The rectangle wraps round the circles. So its width is the circumference, 2×π×r.

The total surface area

The curved surface is 2×π×r×h. Each circle is π×r2
So a closed cylinder's total surface area is 2πr2 +2πrh.

The curved surface is 2×π×r×h, and the two circles are π×r2 each. So the total surface area of a closed cylinder is 2πr2 +2πrh.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Area or volume

Painting, wrapping and labelling cover a surface, so they need surface area in square units. Filling, holding and pouring need volume in cubic units.

Estimating an awkward shape

An irregular solid can be approximated by prisms and cylinders. A human arm, for example, is roughly a cylinder, so its volume can be estimated.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A closed cylindrical tank has radius 1 m and height 2 m. How many litres of paint, at 5 m² a litre?

Find the curved surface2×π×1×2=4π
2π+4π=6π
