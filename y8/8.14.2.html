Sequences and Rules

Number patterns turn up everywhere, from floor tiles to rabbits breeding. A good rule finds any term without writing out every one before it.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A term-to-term rule gives the next term from the previous one, so earlier terms must be found first.
  • A position-to-term rule, the nth term, gives any term directly from its position.
  • Substituting a position number into the nth term rule generates that term.
  • The square numbers are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36; the cube numbers are 1, 8, 27, 64, 125.
  • The triangular numbers 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 are the running totals of the counting numbers, and the Fibonacci sequence adds the two previous terms.

Two kinds of rule

A term-to-term rule gets each term from the one before. The nth term gives any term straight from its position.

A term-to-term rule gets each term from the one before, so the terms come in order. A position-to-term rule, the nth term, gives any term straight from its position.

Substituting a position

To use the nth term, replace n with the position. For 3n + 2, the 10th term is 3 × 10 + 2 = 32.

The nth term gives a term by replacing n with its position. So for 3n + 2 the 10th term is 3 × 10 + 2 = 32, with no need for the nine before it.

Three special sequences

Square, cube and triangular numbers are three special sequences. Triangular numbers are the running totals of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

The square numbers are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25 and the cube numbers 1, 8, 27, 64, 125. The triangular numbers 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 are the running totals of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.

Fibonacci adds the two before

In the Fibonacci sequence each term is the sum of the two terms before it. So 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 continues with 13, then 21.
