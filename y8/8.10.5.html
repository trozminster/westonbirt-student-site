Topic 8.10 · Changing the Subject · Lesson 5 of 5

Rearranging Shape Formulae

An architect often knows the area or the volume a design needs and must work out a length. Rearranging a shape formula turns it into a formula for that missing length.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A geometric formula can be rearranged to make any of its variables the subject.
  • A = pi x r² rearranges to r = the square root of (A ÷ pi), dividing before rooting.
  • Only the positive root is taken, since a length cannot be negative.
  • The units follow the formula through the rearrangement, so an area formula still returns a length.
By the end you can

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Any variable can be the subject

A shape formula can be rearranged to make any of its variables the subject. A = πr² becomes r = √(Aπ), dividing by π before taking the root.

A length is positive

A length takes only the positive root. A length found from an area is in cm, not cm².

Only the positive root is taken, because a length cannot be negative. The units follow the formula, so a length found from an area is in cm, not cm².
WORKED EXAMPLE A
A triangle has area 42 cm² and base 12 cm. Find its height.
Rearrangeh = 2Ab
Substituteh = 2 × 42 ÷ 12
h = 7 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A circle has area 150 cm². Find its radius, to 1 d.p.
Rearranger = √(Aπ)
Substituter = √(150 ÷ π)
= 6.909...
r = 6.9 cm
YOUR TURN · a
A rectangle has area 54 cm² and length 7.5 cm. Find its width.
w = Al = 54 ÷ 7.5 = 7.2 cm
YOUR TURN · b
A cylinder of volume V = πr²h has volume 500 cm³ and height 8 cm. Find its radius, to 1 d.p.
r = √(Vπh) = √(500 ÷ 8π) = 4.46..., so r = 4.5 cm

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One formula feeds the next

One formula can feed a value into a second one. Rearrange before substituting to keep values exact.

A contextual problem often needs one formula to find a value that a second, rearranged formula then uses. Rearranging before substituting keeps every value exact until the final step.

Check the answer fits

The answer is checked against the context. It should be a positive length, in the right units and of a sensible size.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A circle has the same area as a square of side 8 cm. Find its radius, to 1 d.p.
Square the sideA = 8²
= 64 cm²
Rearranger = √(Aπ)
Substituter = √(64 ÷ π)
r = 4.5 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE D

Rearrangeh = Vlw

Multiply the base= 9000 ÷ 300
h = 30 cm
