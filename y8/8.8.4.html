Topic 8.8 · Area and Circumference of Circles · Lesson 4 of 4

Semicircles and Compound Shapes

A running track's inside lane is a rectangle with a semicircle bolted onto each end - and using a full circle's circumference for that curved section is a classic mistake worth several marks. Today you extend circles to their fractions - semicircles and quarter circles - then combine circles with rectangles and triangles into full compound shapes.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A semicircle is half a circle and a quarter circle a quarter, so their areas are the whole circle's area halved or quartered.
  • Their perimeters are not a simple fraction of the circumference, because the straight edges must be added.
  • A semicircle's perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter; a quarter circle's is a quarter of the circumference plus two radii.
  • A compound shape is split into circles, rectangles and triangles whose areas are added, or subtracted where a piece has been removed.
  • Only the outside edges count towards a compound shape's perimeter.

Areas halve, perimeters do not

A semicircle's area is half the circle's area. Its perimeter is not half the circumference. Its straight edge must be added on.

A semicircle is exactly half a circle, and a quarter circle exactly a quarter, so their areas are simply the whole circle's area halved or quartered. Their perimeters are NOT a simple fraction of the circumference, though, because a semicircle or quarter circle also has straight edges that a full circle doesn't have - these must be added on.

The straight edges, added on

Semicircle perimeter: half the circumference + the diameter. Quarter circle: a quarter of the circumference + two radii.

A semicircle's perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter (the straight edge across it). A quarter circle's perimeter is a quarter of the circumference plus two radii (the two straight edges bounding it).

Splitting a compound shape

Split a compound shape into pieces, each with its own area formula. Add the areas, or subtract a piece that was cut away.

A compound shape combining circles (or circle fractions) with rectangles and triangles is split into its separate pieces, each area found using its own formula, then the pieces added together - or subtracted, where one shape has been cut or removed from another.

Only the outside edges count

A perimeter counts only the edges on the outside boundary. The joining edge between two pieces is not part of it.

For a compound shape's perimeter, only the edges actually on the outside boundary count; where two shapes are joined, the edge between them sits inside the shape and is not part of the perimeter at all.
