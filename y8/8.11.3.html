Topic 8.11 · Pythagoras' Theorem · Lesson 3 of 4

Multi-Step Pythagoras

A shortcut across a field, a diagonal through a frame or the distance between two points on a map each hides a right-angled triangle, and sometimes two. Chaining Pythagoras finds them all.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A multi-step problem chains two applications of the theorem, the first result becoming an input to the second.
  • Rounding an intermediate value carries that error into the final answer, so exact values are kept until the end.
  • The calculator's store function holds an exact intermediate value for reuse.
  • On a coordinate grid the horizontal and vertical differences between two points are the two shorter sides, so their distance apart follows from the theorem.
  • Inside a 2D shape a right-angled triangle must first be found or constructed before the theorem applies.

Distance on a grid

The horizontal and vertical differences are the shorter sides. The distance between the points is the hypotenuse.

The horizontal and vertical differences between two points are the shorter sides of a right-angled triangle. The distance between the points is its hypotenuse.

A right angle inside a shape

Inside a shape, find or draw a right-angled triangle first. A rectangle's diagonal makes one.

Inside a 2D shape, a right-angled triangle must be found or drawn before the theorem applies. A rectangle's diagonal is one way to make it.
