Laws of Indices

Index laws turn a page of repeated multiplying or dividing into a couple of keystrokes - and knowing them cold is what makes every later topic that uses powers, from standard form to Pythagoras, go faster rather than slower.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An index says how many times the base is used as a factor: 25=2×2×2×2×2
  • Multiplying powers of the same base adds the indices: am×an=am+n.
  • Dividing subtracts them: am÷an=am−n.
  • A power of a power multiplies them: (am)n=amn.
  • Any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1, since an÷an is both 1 and a0
  • 10n is a place-value shift, so multiplying by 103 moves every digit three places to the left.

The index laws

Same base multiplied: add the indices. Divided: subtract them. A power of a power: multiply them.

When the same base is multiplied, the indices add; when it is divided, they subtract. Raising a power to a further power multiplies the two indices, since the outer power repeats the inner one that many times.

Powers of ten shift the digits

Multiplying by 10n shifts every digit n places left. Dividing shifts them right. No written calculation is needed.

10n is a place-value shift: a positive n moves every digit n places to the left, and the same rule works in reverse. This is why multiplying or dividing by a power of 10 never needs a written calculation, only a shift of the digits.
