Constructing Parallel Lines

Railway tracks, the lines on a page and the edges of a road all stay the same distance apart forever. Today we construct lines like that exactly, and build one construction on top of another.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Two lines are parallel when every point on one is the same perpendicular distance from the other.
  • So a parallel line at a fixed distance is constructed by erecting perpendiculars and marking that distance along them.
  • The perpendicular distance is measured at right angles to the line, not in any other direction.
  • Constructions combine within one diagram because each only needs the circles and rhombuses it creates, which stay visible.
By the end you can
G5Construct a line parallel to a given line at a fixed distance.
G5Combine two or more constructions accurately within a single diagram, identifying the circles and rhombuses used.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The same distance everywhere

Parallel lines stay the same perpendicular distance apart everywhere. Measure that distance at right angles, never along a slant.

Two lines are parallel when every point on one is the same perpendicular distance from the other. That distance is measured at right angles, never along a slant.

Perpendiculars, then the distance

Draw a perpendicular at two points of the line. Set compasses to the distance and mark U and W. UW is parallel: it is that distance away all along.

A perpendicular at two points of the line gives two paths at right angles to it, and compasses set to the distance mark U and W on them. The line UW is then that distance from the line all along its length, so it is parallel. U and W are marked on the same side of the line.

Compasses at 3 cmmark each perpendicular above AB

Compasses at 2 cmmark both on the side below CD
Parallel linethrough the two marks, sloping like CD

Learn: Constructions build on each other

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Constructions build on each other

The perpendicular from P meets AB at M. A second perpendicular to PM is built at P. AB and the new line are both at right angles to PM: parallel.

The perpendicular from P to AB meets it at M, and a second perpendicular, to PM at P, is built on it. A line at right angles to PM, which is itself at right angles to AB, is parallel to AB. Each construction needs only the arcs and rhombuses it creates, and they stay on the page, so each step can be checked.
