Equations with Fractions

A recipe scales by a fraction of the original quantity, or a triangle's area is given as an expression divided by 2 - clearing the fraction is the one new move you need before every equation-solving skill you already have takes over. Today you learn to clear fractions from an equation, including when the unknown itself is on the bottom, then use that to form and solve equations from real contexts.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An equation containing a fraction is cleared by multiplying every term by the denominator.
  • Where there is more than one denominator, multiplying by their LCM clears them all at once.
  • Every term must be multiplied, including any term that is not itself a fraction.
  • An unknown in the denominator is cleared the same way, by multiplying both sides by that denominator.
  • A worded or geometric context becomes an equation once the unknown is named and the relationship written down.

Clearing a fraction

To clear a fraction, multiply EVERY term on both sides by the denominator. Not just the fraction: the equation must stay balanced.

an equation containing a fraction is cleared by multiplying every single term on both sides by the denominator - not just the fractional term, because the equation must stay balanced.

More than one denominator

With different denominators, multiply every term by their LCM. That clears all the fractions in one move.

where an equation has two or more different denominators, multiplying every term by their LCM clears all the fractions in one move, rather than clearing them one at a time.

Unknown in the denominator

An unknown in the denominator clears the same way: multiply both sides by it. Then you multiply by the unknown instead of dividing by it.

an unknown that sits in the denominator is cleared the same way as any other denominator - multiply both sides of the equation by it - which turns the unknown from something you're dividing by into something you're multiplying by.

Learn

Multiple denominators

Two different denominators clear in one step with their LCM. Multiply every term by it. The LCM is not always their product.

When an equation has two different denominators, one multiplier - their LCM - clears both fractions in a single step, rather than clearing them one at a time. Multiplying every term by that LCM removes both denominators at once.

Every term, still

The LCM multiplies every term on both sides. That includes terms that are not fractions.

the LCM must multiply every term on both sides, including any term that isn't itself written as a fraction - the same rule as before, just with a bigger multiplier.

Forming an equation from a context

Name the unknown with a letter. Write the words as an equation. Only then solve it, by the same clearing method.

a worded or geometric problem becomes an equation once the unknown is named with a letter and the relationship described in the words (or shown in the diagram) is written down algebraically - only then is it solved using the same clearing method.

1
Explain why every term in an equation must be multiplied by the denominator (or LCM) when clearing a fraction, not just the fractional term.
Multiplying only the fractional term would unbalance the equation - both sides must be multiplied by the same amount to keep it true, so every term on both sides needs multiplying, not just the one with the fraction.
