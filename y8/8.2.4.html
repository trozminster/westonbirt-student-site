Topic 8.2 · Solving Inequalities · Lesson 5 of 6

Unknowns on Both Sides

Two taxi firms charge different call-out fees and per-mile rates. Finding the mileage where they charge the same means solving an equation with the unknown on both sides; finding when one firm is cheaper means solving the identical problem as an inequality. Today you master both, plus the one rule that changes when a negative gets involved.

By the end you’ll know…
  • However complicated an equation looks, the method is unchanged: expand any brackets, collect the unknown onto one side, then apply inverse operations.
  • Collecting the smaller unknown term onto the other side avoids a negative coefficient.
  • An inequality is solved by the identical method, with one exception: multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the sign.
  • An equation gives a single value, whereas an inequality gives a range.

Learn

Method

The method never changes. Expand any brackets, collect the unknown onto one side, then apply inverse operations.

However complicated an equation looks, the method is unchanged: expand any brackets, collect the unknown onto one side, then apply inverse operations.

Choosing a side

Collect the SMALLER unknown term onto the side with the larger. Then the remaining coefficient stays positive.

When two unknown terms sit on opposite sides, collect the SMALLER one onto the side with the larger, so the remaining coefficient stays positive - e.g. with 5x and 3x, move the 3x, leaving 2x rather than −2x.

Order

Expand every bracket on BOTH sides first, like 7(x+2). Collect the unknown onto one side next. Only then apply inverse operations.

Expand every bracket on BOTH sides before you collect anything. Only once both sides are fully expanded and the unknown is on one side do you apply inverse operations to isolate it.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Same method

An inequality with unknowns on both sides is solved like an equation. Expand brackets, collect the unknown, apply inverse operations.

A multi-step inequality with unknowns on both sides is solved by the identical method as an equation: expand brackets, collect the unknown, apply inverse operations.

The one exception

Multiplying or dividing BOTH complete sides by a negative reverses the sign. A negative merely appearing in the working does NOT trigger it.

whenever you multiply or divide BOTH complete sides by a negative number, reverse the direction of the inequality sign. This is NOT triggered just because a negative number appears somewhere in the working - only by that specific step.

Different answers

an equation's solution is a single value; an inequality's solution is a range. Write your final answer with the inequality sign, never with "=".

WORKED EXAMPLE C
5x−3<2x+9
Subtract the smaller term, 2x, from both sides3x−3<9
Add 33x<12
Divide by 3 (positive)x<4
No sign flip - the coefficient stayed positive throughout.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
3(2−x)>x+6
Expand6−3x>x+6
Subtract 6−3x>x.
Subtract x from both sides−4x>0
Now divide by −4 - because this divides both COMPLETE sides by a negative, reverse the sign: x<0
YOUR TURN · c

(Hint: collect the smaller unknown term, 2x, first - does the coefficient of x stay positive?)
4x+1>13, so 4x>12, x>3 - no sign reversal, the coefficient stayed positive

Expand, collect the unknown, and watch for whether the final step needs the sign reversed.
8−2x>x+5, so −3x>−3, x<1 - the sign reverses because both sides are divided by −3
