Algebraic Fractions

Algebraic fractions follow exactly the same rules as number fractions. Cancelling and multiplying them now is the groundwork for the harder equations of GCSE.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Algebraic fractions cancel by common factors in exactly the same way as numerical ones.
  • Only factors cancel, never individual terms of a sum.
  • Powers of a letter cancel by subtracting indices, so x5÷x2=x3
  • Multiplying algebraic fractions multiplies numerators and denominators; dividing multiplies by the reciprocal.
  • Cancelling before multiplying keeps the expressions manageable.

The same rules as numbers

An algebraic fraction cancels by a common factor exactly as a number fraction does. Powers of a letter cancel by subtracting indices, so x5÷x2=x3

Only factors cancel

Only a factor of the whole top and bottom can cancel. One term of a sum never cancels. x+66 cannot become x+1

Only a factor of the whole numerator and the whole denominator can cancel, never one term of a sum. So x+66 cannot be simplified to x+1
When the numerator is a sum, factorise it first: 2x+42=2(x+2)2=x+2

Multiply straight across

Multiplying algebraic fractions multiplies the numerators together and the denominators together. Cancelling any numerator with any denominator first keeps the expressions small.

Divide by flipping the second

Dividing by an algebraic fraction multiplies by its reciprocal. So dividing by x5 is multiplying by 5x.
