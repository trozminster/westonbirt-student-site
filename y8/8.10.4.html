Topic 8.10 · Changing the Subject · Lesson 4 of 5

Rearranging by Factorising

Some formulae hide the wanted letter in two places at once. Factorising gathers it into one place, and then it can be freed.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Where the wanted subject appears more than once, no sequence of inverse operations alone will isolate it.
  • Every term containing the subject is collected onto one side and every other term onto the other.
  • The subject is then factorised out, leaving it multiplied by a bracket.
  • Dividing both sides by that bracket makes it the subject.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

One letter in two places

If the subject appears twice, inverse operations alone cannot isolate it. Collect every subject term on one side first.

Where the subject appears twice, inverse operations alone can never isolate it. Every term containing the subject is collected on one side, and every other term on the other.

Factorise, then divide

The subject is then factorised out, leaving it multiplied by a bracket. Dividing both sides by that bracket makes it the subject.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Make x the subject of ax + b = cx.
Subtract ax from both sidesb = cx - ax
Factoriseb = x(c - a)
Divide by the bracketx = bc - a
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Make x the subject of y = x + 3x.
Multiply both sides by xxy = x + 3
Subtract xxy - x = 3
Factorisex(y - 1) = 3
Divide by (y - 1)x = 3y - 1
YOUR TURN · a
Make x the subject of px = qx + r.
px - qx = r, so x(p - q) = r and x = rp - q
YOUR TURN · b
Make a the subject of 2a + 3b = ab.
3b = ab - 2a = a(b - 2), so a = 3bb - 2

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A common factor as a step

Factorising out a common factor can be the step that frees the subject. The terms sharing the subject become one product of the subject and a bracket.

Check by expanding

Expanding the bracket again gives back the terms that were collected. So a factorised step can always be checked.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
The perimeter of a semicircle is P = 2r + πr. Make r the subject.
FactoriseP = r(2 + π)
Divide by the bracketr = P2 + π
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Make x the subject of 4x + 6 = 2kx.
Subtract 4x from both sides6 = 2kx - 4x
Factorise fully6 = 2x(k - 2)
Divide by 2(k - 2)x = 3k - 2
YOUR TURN · c
Make r the subject of P = 4r + πr.
P = r(4 + π), so r = P4 + π
YOUR TURN · d
Make x the subject of 3x + 12 = 3ax.
12 = 3ax - 3x = 3x(a - 1), so x = 4a - 1
