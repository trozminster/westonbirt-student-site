Loci and Regions

A goat on a rope, a phone mast's range, a sprinkler in a garden: each marks out exactly the places that obey a rule. Today we draw those places and find where several rules hold at once.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The locus of points a fixed distance d from a point is a circle of radius d.
  • From a straight line it is a pair of parallel lines, one either side, each d away.
  • From a line segment it is a racetrack: two parallel segments joined by a semicircle of radius d at each end.
  • From a polygon it is the outline with its corners rounded into arcs of radius d.
  • An exact distance defines a locus, whereas an inequality ('at least', 'within', 'closer to') defines a region bounded by it.
  • Where several conditions hold at once, the answer is the region where they all overlap.

Learn

From a point and from a line

A fixed distance d from a point gives a circle of radius d. From a straight line it gives a parallel line d away on each side.

The locus of points a fixed distance d from a point is a circle of radius d. From a straight line it is a pair of parallel lines, one on each side, each d away.

Around a line segment

Beside the segment the locus is two parallel sides. Beyond each end the nearest point is the end itself. So each end adds a semicircle of radius d: a racetrack.

Beside the segment the locus is two parallel sides, but beyond each end the nearest point is the end itself. So each end adds a semicircle of radius d, making a racetrack.

Around a shape

Around a polygon the locus follows each side at distance d. At each corner the outline turns on an arc of radius d.

Around a polygon the locus follows each side at distance d. At each corner the nearest point is the corner, so the outline turns on an arc of radius d.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Inequalities make regions

An exact distance gives a locus. 'Within', 'at least' or 'closer to' gives a region bounded by it. Within 3 cm of A is the circle and its inside.

An exact distance gives a locus, but 'within', 'at least' or 'closer to' gives a region bounded by it. Within 3 cm of A is the circle and all of its inside.

Where every condition holds

Each condition gives its own region, and the answer is only their overlap. Closer to B than to A is B's side of the perpendicular bisector of AB.
