Topic 8.10 · Changing the Subject · Lesson 2 of 5

Rearranging with Powers and Roots

Area and volume formulae hide a length behind a square or a cube. Undoing powers and roots is how a length is found from an area or a volume.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Squaring and taking a square root are inverse operations, as are cubing and the cube root.
  • A squared subject is freed by rooting both sides; a subject under a root is freed by raising both sides to that power.
  • The root or power applies to the whole of the other side, so that side is bracketed first.
  • Rearranging before substituting keeps the working shorter and avoids rounding partway through.

Powers and roots undo each other

Squaring and square rooting undo each other. Root both sides to free a squared subject. Square both sides to free a rooted one.

Squaring and taking a square root are inverse operations, and so are cubing and the cube root. A squared subject is freed by rooting both sides, and a subject under a root by squaring both sides.

Root the whole side

A root applies to the whole of the other side. Rooting each term separately gives a wrong value.

A root applies to the whole of the other side, so that side is bracketed before rooting. Rooting each term separately gives a different, wrong value.

Rearrange before substituting

Rearranging a power or root formula before substituting keeps the working short. It also avoids rounding a value partway through the calculation.

Work out the whole side first

After substituting, work out the whole side under the root. Only then take the root.

Once the values are in, the whole of the side under the root is worked out before the root is taken.
