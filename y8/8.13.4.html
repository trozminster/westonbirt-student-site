Velocity-Time Graphs

Sprinters, lift engineers and speed cameras all care about how quickly a speed changes. A velocity-time graph shows it, and it reads differently from a distance-time graph.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A velocity-time graph shows velocity on the vertical axis against time on the horizontal.
  • Its gradient is the acceleration, in velocity units per unit of time.
  • A horizontal line means constant speed, not stationary, which is the key difference from a distance-time graph.
  • A negative gradient is deceleration.
  • The area beneath the graph is the distance travelled.

Velocity up the side

A velocity-time graph plots speed against time, not distance. So a horizontal line means moving at a constant speed, not standing still.

Sloping down is slowing down

A line sloping down means the object is slowing down while still moving forward. It stops only when the line reaches the time axis.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The gradient is the acceleration

The gradient of a velocity-time graph is the acceleration. Acceleration = change in velocity ÷ change in time, in ms².

The gradient of a velocity-time graph is the change in velocity divided by the change in time, which is the acceleration. Its units are metres per second per second, written ms².

Slowing, and the area

A negative gradient is a deceleration. The area under a velocity-time graph is the distance travelled.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A car speeds up from rest to 24 m/s in 8 seconds. Find its acceleration.
Subtract the velocities24 - 0 = 24 m/s
Divide by the time24 ÷ 8
Acceleration3 m/s²
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the acceleration from 6 to 10 seconds.
Change in velocity0 - 10 = -10 m/s, a change and not a reading
Change in time10 - 6 = 4 s
Acceleration-10 ÷ 4 = -2.5 m/s², a deceleration
YOUR TURN · c

12 ÷ 3 = 4 m/s²
YOUR TURN · d

(3 - 12) ÷ 6 = -1.5 m/s², a deceleration
