Angles in Quadrilaterals

Every quadrilateral's angles sum to 360° - and once you can prove that using triangles you already know, you can find missing angles in any quadrilateral, including ones where an angle is hidden behind a letter instead of a number.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Angles in a quadrilateral sum to 360 degrees.
  • This follows from splitting any quadrilateral into two triangles, each summing to 180 degrees.
  • A quadrilateral's own properties give further facts: opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal, and co-interior angles between its parallel sides sum to 180 degrees.
  • An angle written as an algebraic expression turns the angle sum into an equation to solve.

The 360 sum

The four angles inside any quadrilateral sum to 360°. One diagonal splits it into two triangles. Two triangles of 180° each make 360°.

The four angles inside any quadrilateral sum to 360°. This follows directly from splitting the quadrilateral into two triangles with a single diagonal: each triangle's angles sum to 180°, and together the two triangles' angles make up all four of the quadrilateral's angles, so 180+180=360.

Extra properties

A parallelogram has equal opposite angles. Its co-interior angles, between parallel sides, sum to 180°. These extra facts apply only to shapes with that property.

Some quadrilaterals give more than the 360 total. In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal, and angles between a pair of parallel sides on the same side (co-interior) sum to 180° - these extra facts only apply to shapes that actually have that property, not to every quadrilateral.

Algebra

Algebraic angles turn the angle sum into an equation. Add the four expressions and set the total equal to 360. Then solve for the letter.

When an angle is written as an algebraic expression rather than a number, the angle sum becomes an equation: add all four angle expressions, set the total equal to 360, and solve for the letter.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
A quadrilateral has angles 84, 97, 110 and x.
Modeldraw the diagonal to split the quadrilateral into two triangles - each triangle's angles sum to 180, so the whole quadrilateral's four angles sum to 180+180=360. 84+97+110=291
x = 360-291
= 69°.
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A parallelogram has one angle of 73°.
Modelopposite angles in a parallelogram are equal, so the angle opposite 73° is also 73°.
The two remaining angles are co-interior to 73° (between the parallel sides), so each is 180-73=107°.
Check73+107+73+107=360.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Three kinds of fact

Multi-step problems chain three kinds of fact. They are parallel-line facts, angle sums, and a quadrilateral's own properties.

Multi-step problems in this topic chain together three kinds of fact: parallel-line angle facts (corresponding, alternate, co-interior), triangle and quadrilateral angle sums, and a quadrilateral's own extra properties such as a parallelogram's equal opposite angles.

Choosing which fact applies

At each stage, decide which fact fits which pair. A parallel-line fact needs a transversal crossing parallel lines. Angles that just sit near each other do not count.

Solving one means working out, at each stage, exactly which fact applies to which pair of angles - a parallel-line fact only applies to angles actually formed by a transversal crossing genuinely parallel lines, not to any two angles that happen to sit near each other in the diagram.

Finishing with an equation

With an algebraic angle, the chain ends in an equation. Form it from whichever sum applies.

Where an angle is algebraic, the chain finishes by forming an equation from whichever sum applies at that final step.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A parallelogram ABCD has side CD extended beyond D. The angle outside the parallelogram at D, between the extension and DA, is 108°. Angle p is the parallelogram's angle at B.
Modelthe outside angle and angle ADC sit on the straight line CD extended, so angle ADC=180-108=72° (angles on a straight line sum to 180°). p is opposite angle ADC
p=72° (opposite angles in a parallelogram are equal).
No single fact joins 108° to pit takes both steps.
The other two angles are each 180-72=108° (co-interior angles sum to 180°).
Check72+108+72+108=360.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find each angle.
Modelthe four angles of a quadrilateral sum to 360° (split it into two triangles).
Add the four angles3x + (2x + 10) + (4x − 5) + (x + 15) = 360.
Collect like terms10x + 20 = 360.
Subtract 20 from both sides10x = 340.
Divide by 10x = 34.
Substitute 34 for xthe angles are 102°, 78°, 131° and 49°.
Check102 + 78 + 131 + 49 = 360.
YOUR TURN · c
A quadrilateral's angles are x, 2x, 3x and 4x. Find x.
the angles sum to 360°, so 10x = 360 and x = 36°.
YOUR TURN · d
A trapezium has one parallel side extended. Find q, naming the fact. Must the angle opposite q also be 65°?
q = 65° (alternate angles are equal across the marked parallel sides). You cannot assume the opposite angle equals q: only a parallelogram guarantees equal opposite angles, and a trapezium has just one pair of parallel sides.
