Finding Shorter Sides

A ladder, a ramp or a guy rope usually has a known length, and the question is how far it reaches. Pythagoras' theorem finds a shorter side as well as the hypotenuse.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The same theorem finds a shorter side, rearranged to a² = c² - b².
  • Finding a shorter side subtracts, whereas finding the hypotenuse adds.
  • Identifying the hypotenuse first is what decides which of the two applies.
  • The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and is always the largest of the three values.
  • A shorter side coming out larger than the hypotenuse signals the subtraction was done the wrong way round.

Rearranged for a shorter side

To find a shorter side, rearrange: a² = c² − b². Finding a shorter side subtracts the squares.

The same theorem finds a shorter side when it is rearranged to a² = c² - b². Finding a shorter side subtracts, while finding the hypotenuse adds.

Square, subtract, then root

The squares are subtracted before the root is taken. Subtracting the sides themselves, 13 - 5, gives a different and wrong length.

Find the hypotenuse first

Identifying the hypotenuse first decides whether to add or to subtract. It is opposite the right angle and is always the largest of the three lengths.

Check the size

A shorter side must be smaller than the hypotenuse. If it is larger, the squares were added by mistake.

A shorter side must come out smaller than the hypotenuse. A shorter side larger than the hypotenuse means the squares were added when they should have been subtracted.
