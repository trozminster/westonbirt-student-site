Topic 8.4 · Similarity and Congruence · Lesson 1 of 3

Similar Shapes and Scale Factors

Architects' models, maps and photo enlargements all rely on one idea: shapes that look the same but are different sizes. Today you'll learn to spot when two shapes are truly similar, and use a single number - the scale factor - to link every length between them.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Similar shapes have equal corresponding angles and corresponding sides in the same ratio.
  • The scale factor is the ratio of a length on one shape to the matching length on the other.
  • A scale factor above 1 enlarges and one below 1 reduces.
  • A missing length is found by multiplying the corresponding length by the scale factor.
  • Corresponding sides are matched by their position in the shape, not by how they look on the page.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

What makes two shapes similar

Similar shapes need both: equal corresponding angles AND sides in the same ratio. Corresponding sides sit opposite equal angles, not in the same place on the page.

Two shapes are similar if their corresponding angles are all equal AND their corresponding sides are all in the same ratio - both conditions are needed together, not just one on its own. Corresponding sides are identified by the equal angles they sit opposite (their position in the shape's structure), not by how they happen to be drawn on the page.

Finding the scale factor

Scale factor = one length ÷ its corresponding length. Above 1 is an enlargement. Below 1 is a reduction.

The scale factor is the ratio of a length on one shape to the matching length on the other, found by dividing one length by its corresponding length. A scale factor above 1 means an enlargement (the shape gets bigger); a scale factor below 1 means a reduction (the shape gets smaller).
FINDING THE SCALE FACTOR
Model: Triangle ABC and triangle PQR are similar. Angle A=50°, Angle B=70°, Angle C=60°. Angle P=50°, Angle Q=70°, Angle R=60°. Side AB=6 cm, side PQ=15 cm. Step 1: Both triangles have all three angles equal, so both conditions for similarity are met. Step 2: Match corresponding sides by the equal angles they sit opposite, not by their position on the page - side AB is opposite angle C(60°), and side PQ is opposite angle R(60°), so AB and PQ correspond. Step 3: Calculate the scale factor from triangle ABC to triangle PQR by dividing the length on PQR by the matching length on ABC: 15÷6=2.5
Step 4: Since 2.5 is above 1, PQR is an enlargement of ABC.
AB and PQ correspond, as both are opposite the 60° angle
Divide PQ by AB15÷6
Scale factor2.5

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Multiplying by the scale factor

Multiply a known length by the scale factor to find its corresponding length. This works in the direction you calculated it: FROM one shape TO the other.

Once you know the scale factor between two similar shapes, you can find any missing length. Multiply the known length by the scale factor to find its corresponding length on the other shape - as long as you're going in the same direction as the scale factor was calculated (from the shape you calculated it FROM to the shape you calculated it TO).

Going the other way

Going back the other way, divide by the scale factor instead. First check which shape you are finding a length ON.

If you need to go the other way - from that second shape back to the first - divide by the scale factor instead, since that is the same as multiplying by the reciprocal. Always check which shape you're finding a length ON before deciding whether to multiply or divide.
USING THE SCALE FACTOR
Model: Triangle KLM and triangle STU are similar, with scale factor from KLM to STU of 3 (already calculated). Side LM=6 cm (known, on the smaller triangle). Step 1: Side TU corresponds to LM (both opposite the matching angle at KS). Step 2: Going from KLM to STU is the same direction as the scale factor was found, so multiply: TU =LM×3 =6×3 =18 cm. Now suppose instead side ST=24 cm is known (on the larger triangle) and side KL (on the smaller triangle) is missing. Step 3: Going from STU back to KLM is the OPPOSITE direction to the scale factor, so divide instead of multiply: KL =ST÷3 =24÷3 =8 cm.
