Significant Figures and Estimating

Rounding to significant figures is what lets a huge or awkward calculation be estimated in your head before you trust a calculator's exact answer.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Significant figures are counted from the first non-zero digit.
  • Leading zeros are never significant; zeros sitting between other digits always are.
  • Rounding to a given number of significant figures is decided by the next digit, rounding up at 5 or above.
  • Estimating rounds every value in a calculation to 1 significant figure first.
  • An estimate checks whether an answer is plausible, so it is written with an 'approximately equals' sign rather than an equals sign.

Estimating to 1 s.f.

To estimate, round every value to 1 s.f. first. Then work it out in your head. Write ≈, not =.

Estimating rounds every value in a calculation to 1 significant figure first, so the whole thing can be done in your head; the result checks whether a calculator's exact answer is plausible, which is why it uses the 'approximately equals' sign rather than an equals sign.
