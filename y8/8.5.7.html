Changing Ratios

A youth club has boys and girls in the ratio 3:5
Four more girls join, and the ratio becomes 3:7 - but the number of BOYS hasn't changed at all. Today you learn to use that fixed share as the way into problems where a ratio itself changes.

By the end you’ll know…
  • When a quantity is added to or taken from one share, the ratio changes but the other share often stays fixed.
  • That fixed share is the way into the problem, since it must represent the same amount in both ratios.
  • Scaling both ratios so the fixed share carries the same number of parts makes them directly comparable.
  • Fractions, decimals, percentages and ratios all express the same multiplicative relationships, so a problem can be converted into whichever form is easiest.

The fixed share

When one share changes, the OTHER share often stays the same amount. That fixed share is the way into the problem.

When a quantity is added to or taken from ONE share, the ratio changes, but the OTHER share often stays exactly the same amount. That fixed share is the way into the problem - it must represent the same real amount in both the old ratio and the new ratio.

Scaling to match

Scale each ratio separately so the fixed share has the SAME number of parts. Then find the value of one part.

To use the fixed share, scale BOTH ratios (separately) so the fixed share carries the SAME NUMBER OF PARTS in each - once the parts line up, the value of one part in the new ratio can be found directly, since the fixed share's real amount is already known from the old ratio.
A CHANGING RATIO
A youth club has boys and girls in the ratio 3:5
Four more girls join, and the ratio becomes 3:7
Find the original number of boys and girls.
The boys' share is fixed (only girls joined) and is already written as 3 parts in both ratios. Boys stay at 3 parts, girls go from 5 to 7 parts, a difference of 2 parts, which equals the 4 girls who joined
Match the new girls2 parts=4 girls
Divide by 21 part=2 girls
Multiply by 55 parts=10 girls
Multiply by 33 parts=6 boys
Original numbers6 boys and 10 girls
Checkoriginally 6:10 (=3:5)
after 4 more girls, 6:14 (=3:7). Correct.
YOUR TURN · a
Flour and sugar are in the ratio 4:3, and adding 6 spoons of sugar makes it 4:5
Find the original amounts.
flour is fixed and is already 4 parts in both ratios; sugar goes from 3 to 5 parts, so 2 parts =6 spoons and 1 part =3 spoons. Flour =12 spoons, sugar =9 spoons.
YOUR TURN · b
Red and blue counters are in the ratio 2:3, and adding 10 blue counters makes it 4:7
How many red counters are there?
red is fixed, so first write 2:3 as 4:6 to match red's 4 parts; blue goes from 6 to 7 parts, so 1 part =10 counters. Red =4×10=40 counters (check: 40:60 = 2:3, 40:70 = 4:7).

One structure, many forms

Ratios, fractions, decimals and percentages express the same relationships. Convert to whichever form is easiest before combining anything.

Fractions, decimals, percentages and ratios all express the same underlying multiplicative relationships - a problem given in one form can always be converted into whichever of the others is easiest to work with, provided the conversion is done accurately before combining anything.

Combining forms in one problem

When a problem mixes forms, convert every piece into ONE form first. Decimals often combine most easily.

Where a problem mixes forms - for example a ratio and a percentage discount together - convert every piece into a SINGLE common form (often decimals, since they combine most easily by multiplying) before doing any arithmetic between them. Never combine a fraction and a percentage, or a ratio and a decimal, directly without converting first.
RATIO THEN PERCENTAGE
A sports drink is mixed with water and concentrate in the ratio 4:1
A 250 ml serving is mixed, then 20% of the drink is poured away. How much concentrate is left?
Concentrate is 1 part of 5
Take 15 of 250 ml250÷5=50 mlof concentrate
Subtract 20% from 100%100%−20%=80%(the fraction remaining)
Write 80% as a decimal80%=0.80
Pouring away 20% of the drink removes 20% of every ingredient
Multiply by 0.8050×0.80=40 mlof concentrate remaining
YOUR TURN · c
Blue and white paint are mixed 3:7 in a 400 ml tin, and 25% of the tin is used. How much blue paint is left?
blue is 3 parts of 10: 400÷10×3=120 ml. 75% is left: 120×0.75=90 ml of blue.
YOUR TURN · d
Orange and water are mixed 2:3, and after 40% of the jug is drunk, 120 ml of orange is left. How much drink was in the full jug?
60% of the orange is left, so the orange was 120÷0.6=200 ml. Orange is 2 parts, so 1 part =100 ml and the jug held 5×100=500 ml.
