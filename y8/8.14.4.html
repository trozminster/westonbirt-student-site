Factorising and Expanding

Expanding and factorising are the same move in opposite directions. Fluency both ways is what makes simplifying, solving and proving possible later on.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Factorising is the inverse of expanding: the expression is rewritten as a product with a bracket.
  • The factor taken out is the highest common factor of all the terms, letters as well as numbers.
  • Fully factorised means the terms left inside share no common factor, so the HCF of 6x2 and 9x is 3x.
  • Expanding multiplies every term inside the bracket by the term outside.
  • Multiplying the terms follows the index laws, so 3x(2x+5) =6x2+15x.

Factorising undoes expanding

Factorising rewrites an expression as a product, with a common factor outside a bracket. Expanding the answer always gives back the original expression.

The highest common factor, letters too

The factor taken out is the HCF of every term, letters as well as numbers. Fully factorised means the terms left inside share no common factor.

Every term inside

Expanding multiplies every term inside the bracket by the term outside. The area grid shows each product in its own cell.

The index laws inside

The coefficients multiply and the powers of a letter add. So x times x is x2, and 2x times 3x is 6x2, not 5x2

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Expand 4x(3x−2).
Write the expression4x(3x−2)
Multiply each term by 4x=4x×3x +4x×(−2)
From the grid=12x2−8x
