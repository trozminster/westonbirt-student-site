Topic 8.18 · Transformations · Lesson 3 of 5

Positive Integer Enlargements

A photo blown up to poster size keeps its shape exactly while every length grows. Today we enlarge shapes precisely, and work backwards to find how an enlargement was made.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An enlargement changes a shape's size but not its shape, so corresponding angles stay equal and the two shapes are similar.
  • The scale factor multiplies every length.
  • The centre of enlargement fixes where the image sits, and rays drawn from it through each vertex locate the image points.
  • The distance from the centre to an image point is the scale factor times the distance to the matching object point.
  • The scale factor is found by dividing an image length by the matching object length.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The scale factor from matching sides

The scale factor is an image length divided by the matching object length. Only a matching pair of sides gives it.

Lines through matching points

A line through each pair of matching points passes through the centre. Two lines fix the centre, and a third checks it.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
