Congruent Triangles

Two triangles cut from the same template are identical, but you do not need to measure every part to know it. Three well-chosen facts are enough, and today we learn which three.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Congruent shapes are identical in both size and shape, whereas similar shapes share angles but not size.
  • Two triangles are congruent if they satisfy SSS, SAS (the angle between the two sides), ASA or RHS.
  • AAA proves similarity only, because equal angles fix a shape's proportions but not its size.
  • SSA is not a condition, since two different triangles can share two sides and a non-included angle.
  • A congruence proof names the condition and gives a reason for each equal side or angle used.

Same shape and same size

Congruent shapes have the same size and shape. One fits exactly on the other, even turned over. Similar shapes share angles but not size.

Congruent shapes are identical in both size and shape, so one fits exactly on the other, even turned over. Similar shapes share their angles but not their size.

Four conditions

SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS each prove two triangles congruent. Each condition fixes one triangle. So a second triangle with the same facts is identical.

Three sides (SSS), two sides and the angle between them (SAS), two angles and a corresponding side (ASA), or a right angle, the hypotenuse and one side (RHS) each prove two triangles congruent. Each fixes one triangle, so a second triangle with the same facts must be identical.

Two that do not

AAA proves only similarity: equal angles fix shape, not size. SSA fails: two different triangles can share two sides and an angle not between them.

AAA proves only similarity, because equal angles fix a shape but not its size. SSA fails, since two different triangles can share two sides and an angle that is not between them.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A reason for every fact

A congruence proof gives each equal side or angle with its reason. Then it names the condition. A shared side is equal to itself: the reason is 'common'.

A congruence proof states each equal side or angle with its reason, then names the condition. A side shared by both triangles is equal to itself, and its reason is 'common'.

Where equal angles come from

Crossing lines give vertically opposite angles, which are equal. Parallel lines give equal alternate angles.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
ABCD is a kite with AB=AD and CB=CD. Prove that triangles ABC and ADC are congruent.
AB=AD: given
CB=CD: given
AC=AC: common
ConditionSSS, so ABC and ADC are congruent
WORKED EXAMPLE D
AB is parallel to DE and AC=CE. Prove that ABC and EDC are congruent.
AC=EC: C is the midpoint of AE
Angle ACB = angle ECD: vertically opposite
Angle BAC = angle DEC: alternate angles, AB parallel to DE
ConditionASA, so ABC and EDC are congruent
