Measuring and Plotting Bearings

A bearing is just an angle - but measured a very particular way, from North, clockwise, always as three figures - because ships, planes and hikers all need to describe direction without ambiguity. Because every North line on a map points the same way, everything you learned last lesson about parallel lines lets you find bearings you haven't even measured.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from North, always written with three figures.
  • North lines are parallel wherever they are drawn on a diagram.
  • The bearing from B to A, the back bearing, differs from the bearing from A to B by 180°.
  • Add 180 when the forward bearing is under 180, and subtract 180 when it is over.
  • Because North lines are parallel, the alternate and co-interior angle facts apply to bearing diagrams.

What a bearing is

A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from North, from 0° up to 360
It is always written with three figures. Write 45° as 045° and 8° as 008°.

A bearing describes a direction as an angle measured clockwise from North, starting at 0° and going up to 360
It is always written using three figures, so an angle of 45° is written as a bearing of 045°, and 8° is written as 008°.

Reading a bearing

Measure the angle at the point you measure FROM. Start at the North line there and turn clockwise to the line joining the points. All the North lines are parallel.

To state the bearing of one point from another, you measure the angle at the point you're measuring FROM, starting at the North line there and turning clockwise until you reach the line joining the two points. Every point in a bearings diagram has its own North line, and because all North lines point the same way, they are parallel to each other wherever they are drawn.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Point A has a North line drawn at it, and a line joins A to point B. The angle measured clockwise from North at A to the line AB is 62°.
Modelstate the bearing as three figures: the bearing of B from A is 062°.

The back bearing

The back bearing, from B to A, is always 180° different from A to B. Facing back the way you came is a half turn.

The bearing from B to A - the back bearing - is always 180° different from the bearing from A to B, because turning to face back the way you came is a half turn.

Add or subtract 180

Forward bearing under 180°: add 180 for the back bearing. Forward bearing over 180°: subtract 180
Either way the result stays between 0 and 360

If the forward bearing is under 180°, add 180 to find the back bearing; if it is over 180°, subtract 180 instead - either way the result must still fall between 0 and 360

Why the parallel North lines matter

The North lines at A and B are parallel. So alternate and co-interior angle facts apply in a bearings diagram. That finds any unmeasured bearing without a protractor.

Because the North lines at A and B are parallel, the alternate and co-interior angle facts from parallel lines apply directly to the angles in a bearings diagram, which is how a back bearing - or any unmeasured bearing in the diagram - can be worked out without a protractor.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
The bearing of A from B is not given directly, but the bearing of B from A is 072°. Find the bearing of A from B.
Model072 is under 180, so add 180: 072+180=252
The bearing of A from B is 252°.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
The bearing of C from D is 296°. Find the bearing of D from C.
Model296 is over 180, so subtract 180
Subtract 180296−180=116
The bearing of D from C is 116°.

YOUR TURN · d
The bearing of E from F is 138°. Use the parallel North lines to find the bearing of F from E.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Model → Subtract 180
138 is less than 180, so add: 138+180=318°. The North lines at E and F are parallel, so the two angles from those North lines to EF on the same side are co-interior and sum to 180: 180−138=42 at E, and measured clockwise from North that is 360−42=318 - the same answer.

Exit ticket

On paper, on your own, then check
1
Explain why a bearing is always written as three figures, and why the North lines at two different points on the same diagram must be parallel.
Three figures keep every bearing the same fixed length (e.g. 062, not 62), avoiding confusion with an ordinary angle. The North lines must be parallel because they all point to true North, a single fixed direction, the same everywhere on the diagram.
2
The angle measured clockwise from North to a given direction is 5°. Write this as a bearing. Then state the bearing shown by an angle of 260° measured clockwise from North.
005°; and 260° (already three figures).
3
The bearing of W from V is 214°. Find the bearing of V from W.
034°(214 is over 180, so subtract 180: 214−180=34).
