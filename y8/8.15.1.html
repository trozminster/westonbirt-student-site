Topic 8.15 · Simplifying Fractions · Lesson 1 of 3

Cancelling Fractions

Cancelling keeps the numbers small, and small numbers mean fewer slips. Today we cancel before multiplying, and use the reciprocal to turn every division into a multiplication.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A fraction is simplified by dividing numerator and denominator by a common factor.
  • Dividing both by their HCF simplifies it in one step.
  • Cancelling before multiplying gives the same answer with smaller numbers to handle.
  • Multiplying fractions multiplies the numerators and the denominators separately.
  • Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal.
By the end you can
G4Simplify fractions by cancelling, and multiply fractions cancelling common factors first.
G4Divide fractions by multiplying by the reciprocal, cancelling before multiplying.

The same fraction, smaller numbers

Dividing the numerator and the denominator by the same common factor gives an equivalent fraction. Dividing by their HCF reaches the lowest terms in one step.

Cancel before multiplying

Multiplying fractions multiplies the numerators together and the denominators together. Cancelling a numerator with any denominator first gives the same answer with smaller numbers.

Multiply by the reciprocal

Dividing by a fraction gives the same answer as multiplying by its reciprocal. The reciprocal of 25 is 52, so dividing by 25 is multiplying by 52.

Only the second fraction flips

Only the fraction you divide by is flipped. Cancel after the flip, never before it.

Only the fraction being divided by is turned upside down, and the first stays as it is. Any cancelling is done after the flip, never before it.
