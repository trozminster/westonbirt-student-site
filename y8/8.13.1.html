Distance-Time Graphs

A journey can be told as a graph: every stage, every stop and every return shows up as a straight section. Today we read those stories and draw our own.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A distance-time graph shows distance from a starting point on the vertical axis against time on the horizontal.
  • A horizontal line means stationary, since time passes but distance does not change.
  • A steeper line means a faster speed.
  • A line returning to the horizontal axis means travelling back to the start.
  • Each straight section is one stage of the journey, travelled at a constant speed.
By the end you can
G4Read and interpret distance-time graphs, describing each stage of a journey.
G4Draw a distance-time graph from a described journey.

What the axes say

A distance-time graph plots distance from the start against time. Each straight section is one stage of the journey, travelled at a constant speed.

Flat, steep and back

A horizontal section means the object is stationary. A steeper section means a faster speed. A section back down to zero is a return to the start.

A horizontal section means stationary, since time passes but distance does not change. A steeper section is a faster speed, and a section back down to zero is a return to the start.

On a distance-time graph, what does a horizontal section mean?
Not quite. A steady speed is a straight sloping line. Flat means the distance isn't changing.
Yes. Flat means the distance isn't changing, so the object has stopped.
Not quite. The graph isn't a picture of the route. Flat means not moving.
Not quite. Returning is a line sloping down. Flat means stopped.
A distance axis goes up 2 km per grid square. A stage rises 3 squares. How far is travelled?
Not quite. Each square is 2 km, so 3 squares is 3 × 2.
Not quite. 2 km is one square. Three squares is 3 × 2 = 6 km.
Yes. 3 squares × 2 km = 6 km.
Not quite. Multiply the squares by the scale: 3 × 2, not 3 ÷ 2.
Stage P covers 6 km in 30 minutes and stage Q covers 6 km in 10 minutes. Which section is steeper?
Not quite. A longer section took longer, so it's less steep. Q is faster.
Not quite. Same distance, but Q takes less time, so Q is steeper.
Not quite. The graph isn't a picture of hills. Steeper means faster: Q.
Yes. Same 6 km in less time, so Q rises faster: it's steeper.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Each stage starts where the last ended

Draw a journey one stage at a time. Each line starts where the last one ended. Distance is always measured from the start.

A journey is drawn one stage at a time, each line starting where the previous one ended. Distance is always measured from the start, not from the last stop.

Choose the scales first

Choose axes that reach the end time and the furthest distance. Then a stop is a horizontal line.

The time axis must reach the end of the journey and the distance axis its furthest point. A stop is then a horizontal line, and a return comes back down to zero.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Draw the graph: 3 km from home in 30 minutes, a 15-minute rest, then home in 45 minutes.
Stage 1(0, 0) to (30, 3)
Rest(30, 3) to (45, 3)
Home(45, 3) to (90, 0)
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Draw the graph: 20 km in 30 minutes, a 10-minute stop, then a further 15 km in 20 minutes.
Stage 1(0, 0) to (30, 20)
Stop(30, 20) to (40, 20)
Further(40, 20) to (60, 35), since 20 + 15 = 35 km from the start

Check your understanding

Pick an answer. If it's wrong, the feedback tells you which mistake it was.

A walker goes 4 km in 1 hour, then a further 2 km in 1 hour. Where does the second stage end, as (hours, km)?
Not quite. Distance keeps adding up: 4 + 2 = 6 km, not 2.
Not quite. Time keeps adding up: 1 + 1 = 2 hours.
Yes. Time 1 + 1 = 2 hours and distance 4 + 2 = 6 km: (2, 6).
Not quite. Time goes across first: (hours, km) = (2, 6).
A cyclist stops for 20 minutes, 8 km from home. How is this drawn?
Yes. Stopped means the distance stays at 8 km: a flat line, 20 minutes long.
Not quite. Sloping down means heading home. Stopping is flat.
Not quite. The cyclist is still 8 km away, so the line stays at 8 km.
Not quite. Time goes across, not up. Stopping is flat for 20 minutes.
On a distance-time graph, what does a steeper section show?
Not quite. Steeper isn't longer. It means more distance each minute: faster.
Yes. Steeper means more distance per unit of time: faster.
Not quite. The graph isn't a picture of hills. Steeper means faster.
Not quite. Steepness compares distance with time, so it shows speed.
