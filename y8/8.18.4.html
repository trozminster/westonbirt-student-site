Fractional and Negative Enlargements

An enlargement does not have to make a shape bigger, and it does not even have to keep it the right way up. Today the scale factor becomes a fraction, and then negative.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A scale factor between 0 and 1 makes the image smaller, though it is still called an enlargement.
  • The rules are unchanged: every length, and every distance from the centre, is multiplied by the scale factor.
  • A negative scale factor places the image on the opposite side of the centre.
  • It also turns the image upside down, so it is equivalent to a positive enlargement followed by a 180° rotation about the centre.
  • An invariant point does not move under a transformation, and the centre of enlargement is always invariant.

Smaller is still an enlargement

A scale factor between 0 and 1 makes the image smaller. It is still called an enlargement. Every length and every distance from the centre is multiplied by it.

A scale factor between 0 and 1 makes the image smaller, but it is still called an enlargement. Every length, and every distance from the centre, is multiplied by it.

The centre does not move

A point that does not move under a transformation is invariant. The centre of an enlargement is always invariant, whatever the scale factor.

Through and beyond the centre

A negative scale factor puts the image across the centre. Each image point lies on the line from its object through the centre. Continue it that multiple of the distance.

A negative scale factor puts the image on the opposite side of the centre. Each image point lies on the line from its object point through the centre, continued that multiple of the distance.

Upside down as well

The image is turned upside down as well as scaled. So scale factor −2 is scale factor 2 then a half turn about the centre.

The image is turned upside down as well as scaled. So scale factor −2 is scale factor 2 followed by a half turn about the centre.
