Bisecting Lines and Angles

Every construction today hides the same shape: a rhombus. Once you can see it, you know why each line comes out exactly at right angles or exactly in half.

By the end you’ll know…
  • To bisect is to cut exactly in half.
  • The perpendicular bisector of AB is the locus of points equidistant from A and B.
  • An angle bisector is the locus of points equidistant from the two lines forming the angle.
  • Both constructions build a rhombus, which is itself two congruent isosceles triangles.
  • A rhombus's diagonals bisect each other at right angles, and each diagonal bisects the angles it passes through.

Equidistant from two points

To bisect is to cut exactly in half. The perpendicular bisector of AB is every point equidistant from A and B. Not just the two crossings.

To bisect is to cut exactly in half. The perpendicular bisector of AB is the locus of every point equidistant from A and B, not just the two crossings.

A perpendicular through P

Equal arcs from P mark E and F. So P is the midpoint of EF. The perpendicular bisector of EF passes through P, as a diagonal of rhombus ERFS.

Equal arcs from P mark E and F on the line, so P is the midpoint of EF. The perpendicular bisector of EF then passes through P, as a diagonal of the rhombus ERFS.

From a point off a line

One arc from S cuts the line at E and F. Equal arcs from E and F cross at T. ESFT is a rhombus: ST is perpendicular to the line.

One arc from S cuts the line at E and F, and arcs from E and F at that same width cross at T on the far side. ESFT is a rhombus, so its diagonal ST meets the line at right angles. The arc from S must be wide enough to cut the line twice, and S is then equidistant from E and F.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Equidistant from two lines

The angle bisector is every point equidistant from the two arms of the angle. Distance from a line is measured at right angles to it.

The angle bisector is the locus of points equidistant from the two arms of the angle. Distance from a line is measured at right angles to it.

The angle bisector

One arc from V marks E and F. Arcs of that width from E and F cross at R. VERF is a rhombus: diagonal VR halves the angle at V.

One arc from the vertex V marks E and F, and arcs from E and F at that same width cross at R. VERF is a rhombus, so its diagonal VR halves the angle at V. VE and VF are equal, each one compass width, so VERF has four equal sides. A rhombus's diagonal bisects the angles it passes through.
