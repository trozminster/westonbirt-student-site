Parallelograms and Trapeziums

A field, a roof panel or the side of a ramp is often a parallelogram or a trapezium. Each can be rearranged into a shape whose area is already known.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The area of a parallelogram is base x perpendicular height.
  • This follows from cutting a triangle off one end and moving it to the other, which makes a rectangle.
  • The area of a trapezium is half the sum of the parallel sides times the perpendicular height: 12 × (a + b) × h.
  • The height is always perpendicular to the parallel sides, never a slant side.
  • A missing length is found by rearranging the formula and dividing.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A rectangle in disguise

Move a triangle from one end of a parallelogram to make a rectangle. So area = base × perpendicular height, never × the slant side.

Cutting a triangle off one end of a parallelogram and moving it to the other end makes a rectangle. So its area is base × perpendicular height, never base × slant side.

Half the sum of the parallel sides

Trapezium area: half the sum of the parallel sides × the height. That is ½ × (a + b) × h.

The area of a trapezium is half the sum of its parallel sides times the perpendicular height. That is ½ × (a + b) × h.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
A parallelogram has base 9 cm, a slant side of 6 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. Find its area.
Area = base × height = 9 × 5 = 45 cm²
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A trapezium has parallel sides 7 cm and 11 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm. Find its area.
Area = ½ × (7 + 11) × 4 = 36 cm²
YOUR TURN · a

12 × 7.5 = 90 cm²
YOUR TURN · b
A trapezium has parallel sides 5.5 cm and 8.5 cm and perpendicular height 6 cm. Find its area.
12 × (5.5 + 8.5) × 6 = 7 × 6 = 42 cm²

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Working backwards

A missing length is found by rearranging the area formula and dividing. For a parallelogram, the height is the area divided by the base.

A trapezium backwards

For a trapezium, double the area first. Then divide by the sum of the parallel sides.

For a trapezium the area is doubled before dividing by the sum of the parallel sides. Forgetting to double gives half the true height.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A parallelogram has area 72 cm² and base 9 cm. Find its perpendicular height.
h = A ÷ b = 72 ÷ 9 = 8 cm
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A trapezium has area 60 cm² and parallel sides 7 cm and 13 cm. Find its perpendicular height.
h = 2 × 60 ÷ (7 + 13) = 6 cm
YOUR TURN · c
A parallelogram has area 52 cm² and perpendicular height 6.5 cm. Find its base.
52 ÷ 6.5 = 8 cm
YOUR TURN · d
A trapezium has area 45 cm², height 5 cm and one parallel side 7 cm. Find the other parallel side.
a + b = 2 × 45 ÷ 5 = 18, so the other side is 11 cm
