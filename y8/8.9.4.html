Topic 8.9 · Indices and Standard Form · Lesson 4 of 6

Ordering Standard Form

Once several numbers are all in standard form, comparing them is a two-second check on the index alone - as long as every number is genuinely in standard form first.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Numbers in standard form are ordered first by index, and only by A when the indices are equal.
  • A larger index always means a larger number, whatever the A values are.
  • A negative index is always smaller than a positive one.
  • A number is not in standard form unless 1≤A<10
  • Adjusting A back into range changes the index to compensate: 34×105 =3.4×106, and 0.7×10−3 =7×10−4

Order by the index first

Order standard form numbers by index first. Compare A only when the indices are equal. A negative index is smaller than a positive one.

Standard form numbers are ordered first by index, and only by A when two indices are equal, because a bigger index always represents a bigger value regardless of A. A negative index is always smaller than a positive one, for the same reason.

Learn: Bringing A back into range

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Bringing A back into range

34×105 is not standard form: A must be between 1 and 10
Move the index the opposite way to A.

A number is not in standard form unless A sits between 1 and 10, so 34×105 has to be rewritten before it can be compared or calculated with. Adjusting A back into range always moves the index the opposite way to compensate, so the value itself does not change.
FIXING STANDARD FORM
Rewrite 46×103 in correct standard form.
Divide A by 1046×103=4.6×10×103(to bring A into range)
Add 1 to the index=4.6×104(to compensate: 3+1=4)
