Topic 8.3 · Parallel Lines and Quadrilaterals · Lesson 4 of 5

Properties of Quadrilaterals

Squares, rectangles, parallelograms, rhombuses, trapeziums and kites all look different, but each is defined by an exact combination of sides, angles, diagonals and symmetry - not by how it looks at a glance. Today you learn the properties themselves, and then how to reason from diagonal and symmetry evidence alone to prove which shape you're looking at.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A quadrilateral is classified by its sides, angles, diagonals and symmetry.
  • A square has four equal sides and four right angles; a rectangle has four right angles with opposite sides equal.
  • A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel sides and equal opposite angles; a rhombus is a parallelogram with four equal sides.
  • A trapezium has exactly one pair of parallel sides; a kite has two pairs of adjacent equal sides.
  • The diagonals separate them further: those of a square and rhombus cross at right angles, those of a square and rectangle are equal, and a kite's longer diagonal is a line of symmetry.

The families

Classify a quadrilateral by its sides, angles, diagonals and symmetry together. A square is a rectangle with four equal sides. A rhombus is a parallelogram with four equal sides.

A quadrilateral is classified by its sides, angles, diagonals and symmetry together, not any single feature alone. A square has four equal sides and four right angles. A rectangle has four right angles, with opposite sides equal (not necessarily all four). A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel sides and equal opposite angles. A rhombus is a parallelogram with the extra condition that all four sides are equal.

The rest

A trapezium has exactly one pair of parallel sides. A kite has two pairs of adjacent equal sides. Check a shape against every condition, not just the first that fits.

A trapezium has exactly one pair of parallel sides - no more. A kite has two pairs of adjacent equal sides, next to each other rather than opposite. Classifying a shape means checking it against every one of these conditions, not stopping at the first one that seems to fit.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
A quadrilateral has four right angles, and its opposite sides are equal but adjacent sides are different lengths.
Modelfour right angles alone matches square or rectangle - check the sides next.
Opposite sides equal but adjacent sides different rules out square (which needs ALL sides equal), so this is a rectangle.
WORKED EXAMPLE B
A quadrilateral has two pairs of parallel sides, and all four sides are the same length.
Modeltwo pairs of parallel sides matches parallelogram or rhombus - check the sides next.
All four sides equal is the extra condition that makes it a rhombus, not just a parallelogram.

Learn: Classifying from the evidence

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Classifying from the evidence

Check diagonal and symmetry evidence against every candidate shape. One property that does not match rules a shape out. A shape is confirmed only when it matches all the evidence.

A classification from diagonal or symmetry evidence is justified by checking each property against what is guaranteed for every candidate shape, and ruling out any shape that doesn't match every piece of evidence given. A single property that doesn't match is enough to rule a shape out, even if another property seemed to fit - a shape is only confirmed once it matches everything the evidence shows.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A quadrilateral's diagonals cross at right angles but are NOT equal.
Square, rhombus and kite all have perpendicular diagonals, but only the square also has equal diagonals. Which shapes are left, and why not a square? Model: perpendicular diagonals narrows it to square, rhombus or kite. Diagonals equal is a property of square (and rectangle), not rhombus or kite. Since the diagonals here are NOT equal, it cannot be a square - so it is a rhombus or a kite, and the diagonal evidence alone cannot separate those two. To settle it you need the symmetry evidence: a kite's longer diagonal is a line of symmetry.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
A quadrilateral has one line of symmetry, along its longer diagonal, and no parallel sides.
Its sides are not all equal. Which shape must it be, and why not a parallelogram? Model: the longer diagonal being a line of symmetry matches the kite property directly. No parallel sides rules out a trapezium, parallelogram or rhombus, all of which require at least one pair of parallel sides. So it must be a kite - and its equal sides are adjacent, not opposite.
YOUR TURN · c
A quadrilateral's diagonals are equal AND cross at right angles. Which shape is it?
a square - the only shape with both equal and perpendicular diagonals.
YOUR TURN · d
A quadrilateral's diagonals are equal but do NOT cross at right angles. Which shape must it be, and why not a square?
a rectangle. Equal diagonals fit the square and the rectangle; the square is ruled out because a square's diagonals ALSO cross at right angles, and these do not - one mismatched property is enough to rule a shape out.

Exit ticket

On paper, on your own, then check
1
State one property that a square and a rectangle share, and one property that tells them apart.
Shared: both have four right angles (and two pairs of parallel sides). Different: a square has all four sides equal, a rectangle only has opposite sides equal.
2
A quadrilateral has two pairs of parallel sides and all four angles equal to 90°, but its sides are not all equal. What is it?
A rectangle.
3
A quadrilateral has diagonals that cross at right angles, but the diagonals are NOT equal in length. Which shapes does this evidence leave open, and which shape does it specifically rule out? Justify your answer using the diagonal properties.
A rhombus or a kite: both have perpendicular diagonals. Not a square: its diagonals are also equal.
