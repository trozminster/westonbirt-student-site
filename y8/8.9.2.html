Standard Form: Large Numbers

Standard form is how scientists, and your calculator, write a number too large to read comfortably - so instead of counting zeros, you read off the power of 10 that says how large it is.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Standard form writes a number as A×10n, where 1≤A<10 and n is an integer.
  • A positive index means a large number, and n counts how many places the digits shift left.
  • 10n is 1 followed by n zeros, so 3.6×104=36000
  • Standard form exists so that very large numbers can be written and compared without counting zeros.
  • A calculator shows standard form on its display and takes it in through the x10ˣ key, not by typing the power out.

What standard form is

Standard form is A×10n, with 1≤A<10
n counts the places the digits shift. A larger n means a larger number.

Standard form writes a number as A×10n, where A is between 1 and 10 (but never reaching 10) and n counts how many places the digits have shifted left from A. A larger n therefore always means a larger number.

Standard form on a calculator

On a calculator, use the power-of-ten key. Typing × 10 and the power can change the calculation.

A calculator's own display shows standard form the same way it is written by hand, but it is entered through a dedicated power-of-ten key rather than typing the multiplication and the power separately - using the ordinary keys can silently change the calculation.
STANDARD FORM ON A CALCULATOR
