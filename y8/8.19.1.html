Nets and Surface Area

Every cereal box starts life as a flat sheet of card, cut and folded. Today we unfold solids into nets, and use them to find how much card a box really needs.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A net is the 2D shape that folds up to make a solid.
  • Surface area is the total area of all the faces, so it is measured in square units, not cubic ones.
  • A solid can have several different valid nets, since its faces can be arranged in more than one way.
  • A cuboid has three pairs of identical faces, so its surface area is 2(lw + lh + wh).
By the end you can
G3Draw and identify the nets of cubes, cuboids and other 3D shapes.

A net folds into a solid

A net is the flat shape that folds up to make a solid. A cube has several different nets, each of six squares joined edge to edge.

Not every six squares fold

Six squares make a cube only if no faces overlap when folded. Fold each face in your head to test it.

Six squares make a cube only if no two faces land in the same place when folded. Folding each face in your head tests it.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Three pairs of faces

A cuboid has three pairs of identical faces. So its surface area is 2(lw + lh + wh).

A cuboid has three pairs of identical faces: top and bottom, front and back, and the two ends. So its surface area is 2(lw + lh + wh).

Square units, not cubic

Surface area adds areas, so it is in square units such as cm². Volume multiplies three lengths, so it is in cubic units such as cm³.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
Find the surface area of this cuboid.
Faces6 × 3 = 18
6 × 4 = 24
3 × 4 = 12
Pairseach face has an identical partner
Total2 × (18 + 24 + 12) = 108 cm²
