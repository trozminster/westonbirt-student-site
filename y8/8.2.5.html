Formulae with Powers and Roots

Engineers use the formula v² = u² + 2as to link a vehicle's speed to its braking distance. Formulas like this hide unknowns inside squares and square roots - which is why solving them needs two new inverse moves: rooting to escape a power, and squaring to escape a root.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A formula is a rule relating two or more variables, with one of them as the subject.
  • Substituting known values into a formula leaves an equation in the remaining unknown.
  • Squaring and taking a square root are inverses, as are cubing and the cube root.
  • An unknown inside a power is freed by rooting both sides; an unknown under a root is freed by raising both sides to that power.
  • A square root has a positive and a negative value, so x² = 25 gives x = 5 or x = -5.

What a formula is

A formula is a rule relating variables, with one as the subject. Substitute known values for every other variable. That leaves an equation in the one unknown.

A formula is a rule relating two or more variables, with one written as the subject. Substituting known values into every other variable leaves an equation in the one remaining unknown.

Inverse pairs

Powers and roots are inverse operations. An unknown INSIDE a power needs the matching root on both sides. One UNDER a root needs the matching power.

Squaring and square-rooting are inverse operations, as are cubing and cube-rooting. An unknown trapped INSIDE a power is freed by applying the matching root to both sides; an unknown trapped UNDER a root is freed by raising both sides to the matching power.

Two answers

A square root has a positive and a negative value. So x² = 25 gives x = 5 or x = -5. Give both unless the context rules one out.

A square root has a positive and a negative value: x² = 25 gives x = 5 or x = -5. Always give both, unless the context rules one out (e.g. a length cannot be negative).
WORKED EXAMPLE A
The formula for a square's area is A = s².
Given A = 49, find s.
Substitute49 = s².
Free s by square-rooting both sidess = 7
or s = -7.
Since s is a length, reject the negatives = 7.
WORKED EXAMPLE B
The formula for a fall time is T = √(h4).
Given T = 3, find h.
Substitute3 = √(h4).
Free h by squaring both COMPLETE sides9 = h4.
Multiply by 4h = 36.

Learn

Reverse order

Undo a formula's operations in REVERSE order. Start with the outermost and work inward, powers and roots included.

A multi-step formula's inverse operations must be undone in the REVERSE of the order they were originally applied - outermost first, working inward, powers and roots included.

Which letter?

A formula's subject is just the letter already isolated. You can be asked to find ANY letter. Treat every other letter as a known number.

A formula's "subject" is just whichever letter is already isolated when it's written down - you can be asked to find ANY letter in it. Before choosing your steps, identify exactly which letter you are solving for and treat every other letter as a known number.

Putting both together

Choose the target letter first. Then apply reverse-order inverse operations to THAT letter. A letter not inside a power or under a root needs no rooting or squaring.

get the target letter right first, then apply reverse-order inverse operations to THAT letter. A letter that isn't trapped inside a power or under a root needs no rooting or squaring step at all, however complicated the rest of the formula looks.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
E = 3(v² - w).
Given E = 42, w = 2, find v.
Substitute42 = 3(v² - 2).
Undo in reverse order - divide by 314 = v² - 2.
Add 2v² = 16.
Square rootv = 4
or v = -4.
WORKED EXAMPLE D
E = 3(v² - w) again, now given E = 36 and v = 4, find w. v² = 16, so 36 = 3(16 - w).
Divide by 312 = 16 - w.
Rearrangew = 16 - 12
= 4.
Noticew is not trapped inside a power here, so no rooting step is needed at all - only linear inverse steps, because it's a different letter with a different job in the formula.
YOUR TURN · c
The formula P = 2(t² + 1) gives P = 20; find t.
divide by 2: t² + 1 = 10, so t² = 9 and t = 3 or t = -3
YOUR TURN · d
The formula M = 4(k² + 3n) relates two quantities. Given M = 48 and n = 1, find k. Decide the reverse order of steps before you start.
divide first: 48 ÷ 4 = 12, so k² + 3 = 12; then k² = 9 and k = 3.

Exit ticket

1
What two values of x satisfy x² = 49, and why are there two?
x = 7 or x = -7, because both 7² and (-7)² equal 49 - squaring a negative gives the same result as squaring its positive.
2
The formula for a square tile's area is A = s². Given A = 144 cm², find s.
s = 12 cm (the negative root, -12, is rejected since s is a length).
3
The formula E = 3(v² - w) is used. Given E = 30 and v = 4, find w.
30 = 3(16 - w)
10 = 16 - w
w + 10 = 16
