Topic 8.1 · Prime Factors and Calculation Skills · Lesson 1 of 5

Prime Factor Decomposition

Every whole number bigger than 1 breaks apart into prime factors in exactly one way - its own unique fingerprint. Today you'll learn to find that fingerprint with a factor tree, then use it to find the HCF and LCM of two numbers without listing every factor by hand.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Every whole number above 1 is prime, or a product of primes in exactly one way.
  • A factor tree splits a number until every branch ends in a prime, and the primes are the same whichever pair is split first.
  • Index form collects the repeats: 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5.
  • The HCF takes the lower power of each prime the two numbers share; the LCM takes the higher power of every prime appearing in either.
  • In a Venn of the two factorisations, the HCF is the product of the intersection and the LCM the product of the whole diagram.

Prime factor decomposition

Every number above 1 is prime or a product of primes. A factor tree splits it until every branch ends in a prime. Any starting pair gives the same primes.

Every whole number above 1 is either prime itself, or can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way (ignoring the order you write them in). A factor tree finds this by splitting a number into a pair of factors, then splitting each factor again, and again, until every branch ends in a prime - it doesn't matter which pair you split first, the same set of primes comes out at the end.

Index form

Index form collects repeated primes as powers. For example, 2×2×2×3×3 = 2³ × 3².

Once every branch ends in a prime, index form collects the repeated primes together as powers, e.g. 2×2×2×3×3 is written 2³ × 3². This is just a shorter way of writing the same product - nothing about the value changes.
PRIME FACTORS IN INDEX FORM
Decompose 72 into prime factors and write the answer in index form.
Split 72 = 8 × 9. Neither 8 nor 9 is prime, so keep splitting.
8 = 2 × 4, and 4 = 2 × 2, so 8 = 2 × 2 × 2.
9 = 3 × 3, which is already two primes, so stop there.
Read the branch ends72 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 (every branch now ends in a prime)
Write in index form= 2³ × 3² (three 2s and two 3s)
Check2³ × 3² = 8 × 9
= 72. Correct.

HCF from prime factors

The HCF takes the lower power of each shared prime. A prime in only one number is left out.

The HCF (highest common factor) of two numbers is the product of the LOWER power of each prime the two numbers share. A prime that appears in only one of the two numbers is not included at all.

LCM from prime factors

The LCM takes the higher power of every prime in either number. Primes in only one number still count.

The LCM (lowest common multiple) is the product of the HIGHER power of every prime appearing in either number - including primes that only appear in one of them.

Venn diagram

A Venn diagram puts shared primes in the overlap. The HCF is the product of the overlap. The LCM is the product of all, overlap counted once.

Placing each number's primes in a Venn diagram, with shared primes in the overlap, makes this visible: the HCF is the product of everything in the overlap; the LCM is the product of everything in the whole diagram, overlap included once.
PRIME FACTOR VENN
Find the HCF and LCM of 36 and 60 using prime factors.
Factorise 3636 = 2² × 3²
Factorise 6060 = 2² × 3 × 5
Draw a Venn diagramthe overlap holds the primes both numbers share, at the lower power each has it - 2² (both have at least 2²) and 3¹ (36 has 3² but 60 only has 3¹, so only one 3 goes in the overlap). The other 3 from 36 sits in the 36-only region. The 5 from 60 sits in the 60-only region, since 36 has no factor of 5 at all.
Multiply the overlapHCF = 2² × 3
= 12
Multiply the whole diagramLCM = 2² × 3² × 5
= 180
Check12 divides both 36 and 60 exactly, and 180 is a multiple of both.

12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3²; the overlap is 2 × 3, so HCF = 6, and the whole diagram is 2² × 3², so LCM = 36.
