Conversion and Time-Series Graphs

A conversion graph turns one unit into another at a glance, and a time-series graph shows how something changes over months or years. Both are read by going across to the line and then to the other axis.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A conversion graph is a straight line through the origin, because the two units are in direct proportion.
  • It converts in both directions: read across from one axis and down to the other.
  • Values beyond the plotted line can be found by scaling, since the relationship is proportional.
  • A time-series graph shows a quantity measured at intervals over time, with the points joined to show the trend.
  • Only the plotted points are measured values; the joining lines are an estimate of what happened in between.

A straight line through the origin

A conversion graph is a straight line through (0,0). The two units are in direct proportion. Read across to the line, then to the other axis.

A conversion graph is a straight line through (0,0), because the two units are in direct proportion. It converts both ways: across to the line, then to the other axis.

Beyond the line

Beyond the line, scale up a value you can read. If 10 miles is 16 km, 100 miles is 160 km.

A value beyond the end of the line is found by scaling a value that can be read. If 10 miles is 16 km, then 100 miles is ten times as far, 160 km.

Measured over time

A time-series graph plots one quantity measured at regular intervals, with time across. The points are joined so the trend can be seen.

Only the points are measured

The plotted points are the only real measurements. A value read from a joining line is an estimate of what happened in between.
