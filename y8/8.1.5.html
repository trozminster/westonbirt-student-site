Calculating with Time

Time doesn't work in tens like the rest of maths - it works in sixties - and a calculator's decimal answer for a time calculation can easily be misread if you forget that. Today you'll learn to convert confidently between decimal time and clock time, then use that to solve real duration, timetable and rate problems.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Time is measured in base 60: 60 seconds make a minute and 60 minutes an hour.
  • Decimal time and clock time are different, so 1.5 hours is 1 hour 30 minutes, not 1 hour 50 minutes.
  • A decimal fraction of an hour converts to minutes by multiplying by 60, and minutes convert back by dividing by 60.
  • The calculator's DMS key converts between the two forms directly.
  • A rate given per hour applies to decimal time, so a duration must be converted before multiplying.

Base 60

Time uses base 60, not base 10. The decimal part of an hour is a fraction of 60 minutes. So 3.25 hours is 3 hours 15 minutes.

Time is measured in base 60, not base 10: 60 seconds make a minute, and 60 minutes make an hour. This means a decimal time like 3.25 hours is NOT read digit-by-digit like money - the decimal part is a fraction of 60 minutes, not a number of minutes itself.

Converting between decimal time and clock time

Decimal hours × 60 gives minutes. Minutes ÷ 60 gives decimal hours. The DMS key converts either way.

A decimal fraction of an hour converts to minutes by multiplying by 60 (since there are 60 minutes in an hour). Minutes convert back to a decimal fraction of an hour by dividing by 60. The calculator's DMS key performs this conversion directly, in either direction.
DECIMAL HOURS
Convert 3.25 hours into hours and minutes, then convert 2 hours 45 minutes into decimal hours.
Split off the whole hours3.25 hours = 3 hours + 0.25 hours
Multiply 0.25 by 600.25 × 60 = 15 minutes (the decimal part is a fraction of an hour)
3.25 hours = 3 hours 15 minutes
THE REVERSE
Now the reverse: 2 hours 45 minutes.
Divide 45 by 6045 ÷ 60 = 0.75 (the minutes become a decimal fraction of an hour)
2 hours 45 minutes = 2.75 hours
On a calculator, the DMS key would do both conversions directly, without the manual multiplying or dividing by 60.

Adding and subtracting clock times

Clock times add in base 60. At 60 or more minutes, carry 1 hour and subtract 60.

Clock durations add and subtract in base 60, not base 10 - if the minutes total 60 or more, carry 1 into the hours and subtract 60 from the minutes, the same way you'd carry 1 into the tens column at 10 in ordinary arithmetic.

Rates given per hour

Rates per hour need decimal hours. Convert hours and minutes first, then multiply.

A rate given 'per hour' applies to DECIMAL hours, not to hours-and-minutes notation directly - a duration written as hours and minutes must be converted to decimal hours first, before multiplying by the rate.
ARRIVAL TIME AND FUEL
A train departs at 09:45 and the journey takes 1 hour 24 minutes. Find the arrival time, then find how much fuel is used if the train uses fuel at 10 litres per hour.
Add the minutes45 + 24 = 69
Carry 1 hour69 - 60 = 9 minutes (69 is 60 or more)
Add the hours9 + 1 + 1 = 11 (9, plus 1 for the journey, plus 1 carried)
Arrival time11:09
Convert to decimal hours24 ÷ 60 = 0.4 (the rate is per hour, so the duration must be in decimal hours first)
Add the whole hour1 + 0.4 = 1.4 hours
Multiply by the rate10 × 1.4 = 14 litres
YOUR TURN · c
A train departs at 14:20 and the journey takes 50 minutes; find the arrival time. (Hint: do the minutes carry into the hours?)
20 + 50 = 70 minutes, which carries an hour and leaves 10, so the arrival time is 15:10.
YOUR TURN · d
A coach journey of 2 hours 15 minutes uses fuel at 12 litres per hour; find the fuel used. (Hint: what is 15 minutes in decimal hours?)
15 ÷ 60 = 0.25, so the journey is 2.25 hours, and 12 × 2.25 = 27 litres.
