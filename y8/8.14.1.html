Verifying and Disproving

Mathematicians never trust a pattern just because it keeps working. Today is about checking a claim, and about how a single example can destroy one.

By the end you’ll know…
  • To verify a statement is to check that it holds for one particular case.
  • Verifying is not proving: a statement can hold for many cases and still be false.
  • A counter-example is a single case meeting the conditions but not the conclusion, and it disproves the statement.
  • Proving a statement always true needs an argument covering every case, usually an algebraic one.
  • Counter-examples usually sit at the edges: 0, 1, 2, negatives and fractions.

One counter-example is enough

A counter-example meets a statement's conditions but not its conclusion. One is enough to show the statement is false.

A counter-example is a single case that meets the conditions of a statement but not its conclusion. One is enough to show the statement is false.

Look at the edges

Counter-examples usually sit at the edges, such as 0, 1, 2, negatives and fractions. A case only counts if it meets the statement's conditions.
