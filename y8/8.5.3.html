Map Scales and Scale Drawings

A hiking map, an architect's floor plan and a model aeroplane all use the same idea - shrinking (or enlarging) real distances by a fixed ratio, the scale. Today you learn to convert confidently between map and real distances, and to interpret and produce scale drawings.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A map scale is a ratio of distance on the map to the matching real distance.
  • Both parts of the scale must be in the same unit before it is used.
  • A scale written 1:n means one unit on the map represents n of the same units in reality.
  • Map to real multiplies by the scale, and real to map divides by it.
  • A scale drawing preserves angles and shape, so only the lengths change.

What a map scale is

A map scale is map distance : real distance. Both parts of the scale use the SAME unit.

A map scale is a ratio of distance on the map to the matching real distance, e.g. 1:50000 means 1 unit on the map represents 50000 of the same unit in reality. Both parts of the scale must be in the SAME unit before it is used.

Converting both ways

Map to real: MULTIPLY by the scale. Real to map: DIVIDE by the scale. Give real distances in a sensible unit, like m or km.

Map distance to real distance MULTIPLIES by the scale (the real distance is many times bigger); real distance to map distance DIVIDES by the scale. Always check your final answer is in a sensible unit - a real distance in cm is usually better converted to m or km.
MAP TO REAL DISTANCE
A map has a scale of 1:50000. A road measures 3.4 cm on the map. Find the real distance, in km.
Multiply by 500003.4 × 50000 = 170000 cm
Divide by 100170000 cm = 1700 m (convert to a sensible unit)
Divide by 1000= 1.7 km

Producing a scale drawing

Convert each real length to the drawing's unit. Divide by the scale to get the length to draw. Every angle stays exactly the same.

A real object is drawn to scale by converting every real length to the same unit and then dividing by the scale to give the length to draw. The drawing changes size, but every angle stays exactly as it is in reality.

Interpreting a scale drawing

To find a real length, multiply the drawn length by the scale. Give the answer in a sensible unit.

To find a real length from a drawing, measure the length on the drawing and multiply by the scale - always check both the measured length and the final answer carry sensible units.
DRAWING A SCALE PLAN
A garden is 15 m long and 9 m wide. Draw a scale plan using a scale of 1:300, and state the drawn dimensions.
Convert the length to cm15 m = 1500 cm (cm is the scale's usual working unit)
Divide by 3001500 ÷ 300 = 5 cm
Convert the width to cm9 m = 900 cm
Divide by 300900 ÷ 300 = 3 cm
Drawn dimensions5 cm × 3 cm - the angles stay as right angles, exactly as in the real garden
YOUR TURN · c
Draw a plan of a shed 4 m by 2 m at a scale of 1:200, stating the drawn dimensions.
4 m = 400 cm and 2 m = 200 cm; dividing by 200 gives a plan 2 cm by 1 cm
YOUR TURN · d
A room is 6 m long and 4 m wide. Draw a scale plan using a scale of 1:100, and state the drawn dimensions. (Hint: convert to cm first, then divide by 100.)
