Proving with Congruence

Congruence is not just a label for two triangles: it is a way to prove that two lengths or two angles are equal without ever measuring them. Today we use it as a tool.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Proving two triangles congruent establishes that every pair of corresponding sides and angles is equal.
  • That is what makes congruence a tool for proving two lengths or angles equal, rather than an end in itself.
  • Corresponding parts are matched by the order the vertices are named, so ABC congruent to DEF means AB=DE.
  • A structured proof states each fact with its reason, then the condition satisfied, then the conclusion drawn.

Congruence gives every part

Once two triangles are proved congruent, every pair of corresponding sides and angles is equal. So congruence proves a length or angle that was never given.

The order of the letters

ABC congruent to DEF matches A with D, B with E, C with F. So AB=DE and angle B = angle E, wherever they sit.

Triangle ABC congruent to DEF matches A with D, B with E and C with F. So AB=DE and angle B = angle E, wherever the triangles sit on the page.

The shape of a proof

Each line states one fact with its reason in brackets. The condition comes next, then the conclusion the question asked for.

What counts as a reason

A reason is a geometric fact: given, common, vertically opposite, alternate angles. How it looks, a measurement, or the thing being proved is never a reason.

A reason is a geometric fact: given, common, vertically opposite, alternate angles or a property of the shape. What it looks like, a measurement, or the thing being proved are never reasons.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
ABCD is a parallelogram. Prove that triangles ABD and CDB are congruent.
AB=CD: opposite sides of a parallelogram
Angle ABD = angle CDB: alternate angles, AB parallel to DC
BD=DB: common
ConditionSAS, so ABD and CDB are congruent
WORKED EXAMPLE D
AB=DE and AB is parallel to DE. Prove that C is the midpoint of AE.
Angle BAC = angle DEC: alternate angles
Angle ABC = angle EDC: alternate angles
AB=ED: given
CongruentABC and EDC, by ASA
C is the midpointAC=EC

Hint. Follow the worked example: Condition
AM=BM (M is the midpoint); AD=BC (square); angle A = angle B=90°: SAS, so MD=MC.
YOUR TURN · d
O is the centre of a circle and M is the midpoint of a chord AB. Prove that OM is at right angles to AB.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Congruent → C is the midpoint
OA=OB (radii), AM=BM, OM common: SSS, so angles OMA and OMB are equal and add to 180°, so each is 90°.
