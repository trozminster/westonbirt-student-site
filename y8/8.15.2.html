Multiplying and Dividing Mixed Numbers

Recipes, fabric and timber are measured in mixed numbers, and scaling them means multiplying and dividing. Converting to improper fractions first makes every case the same method.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Mixed numbers are converted to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing.
  • Multiplying the whole parts and the fraction parts separately does not work, because it ignores the bracket the mixed number stands for.
  • Each whole is one denominator's worth of parts, which is what makes the conversion work.
  • The answer is converted back to a mixed number where the context calls for it.
  • 'of' means multiply, so a fraction of a mixed-number quantity is a multiplication.

Convert first

Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing. 2 34 = 84 + 34 = 114.

Mixed numbers are converted to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing. Each whole is one denominator's worth of parts, so 2 34 = 84 + 34 = 114.

Not the parts separately

Don't multiply the wholes and the fractions separately. 2 12 × 2 12 is 6 14, not 4 14. The cross products would be missed.

Multiplying the whole parts and the fraction parts separately gives the wrong answer. 2 12 × 2 12 is 6 14, not 4 14, because the two cross products are missed.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

'Of' means multiply

A fraction of a quantity means multiply, even for a mixed number. 23 of 4 12 kg = 23 × 92 = 3 kg.

A fraction of a quantity is a multiplication, even when the quantity is a mixed number. So 23 of 4 12 kg is 23 × 92 = 3 kg.

Dividing counts the pieces

Dividing by a fraction asks how many pieces of that size fit. So 6 ÷ 34 = 8, because eight pieces of 34 m make 6 m.
