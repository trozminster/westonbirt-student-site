Shading Compound Regions

A food app filter for “vegan AND not gluten-free” is a compound condition, not a simple one – exactly the kind of expression you'll shade today, combining union, intersection and complement in a single diagram.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A compound expression combines union, intersection and complement.
  • It is read inside out: brackets first, then union and intersect, with the complement dash applying to whatever it follows.
  • A' intersect B means in B but not in A; (A union B)' means in neither.
  • Different expressions can describe the same region, so (A union B)' and A' intersect B' are the same set.
  • With three sets the same reading applies, across eight regions rather than four.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Reading order

Read brackets first, then union and intersect. A dash applies only to what it immediately follows. That is one set, or a whole bracket.

a compound expression is read inside out – brackets first, then union and intersect, with the complement dash applying only to whatever it immediately follows (a single set, or a whole bracket), never to the rest of the expression, and never ignored.

Two named patterns

A' ∩ B means in B but not in A.(A ∪ B)' means in neither A nor B.

A' ∩ B means “in B but not in A” (start from B, remove anything also in A – the complement applies to A, not B).(A ∪ B)' means “in neither A nor B” (the dash applies to the whole bracket, so shade outside both circles).
SHADING A COMPOUND REGION
Shade A' ∩ B on a two-set Venn diagram.
Read inside outA' first – everything outside circle A. This covers B-only and “neither”.
Now intersect with B – restrict to only the part of A' that is also in B. That leaves just the B-only region.
Final shadingB-only, and nothing else. This is “in B but not in A”.
YOUR TURN · a
Shade B' on a two-set Venn diagram.
everything outside circle B is shaded - A-only and the "neither" region - and the whole of B, its overlap with A included, is left unshaded.
YOUR TURN · b
Shade (A ∪ B)' on a two-set Venn diagram. (Hint: the dash applies to the whole bracket – find A ∪ B first, then take everything outside it.)
A ∪ B is the whole of both circles; the complement of that is only the “neither” region, outside both circles – nothing inside either circle is shaded.

05

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

Reading a region backwards

Name a region by the sets it sits inside and outside. ∩ means in both, ∪ means in either, and a dash excludes.

a shaded region is named from which set(s) it sits inside and which it sits outside, joined by the connecting symbol – ∩ for “in both”, ∪ for “in either” – and a dash for anything excluded.

Equivalent expressions

Different expressions can describe the same region. (A ∪ B)' and A' ∩ B' both mean in neither. Check every equivalence against the actual region.

different expressions can describe the same region – (A ∪ B)' and A' ∩ B' both describe “in neither” – but check every equivalence against the actual region, since sharing a surface feature (like both having a dash) doesn't automatically mean two expressions match. With three sets the same reading applies, across eight regions instead of four.
NAMING A SHADED REGION
A diagram shows only the region outside both circles A and B shaded. Write two equivalent expressions for this.
The shaded region is “in neither A nor B”.
First expressioneverything NOT in the union of A and B – (A ∪ B)'.
Second expressioneverything not in A, AND not in B, at the same time – A' ∩ B'. Both describe the identical shaded region, checked by working out what each actually covers, not just by their surface similarity.
YOUR TURN · c
Write the set notation for the region inside both circles A and B.
A ∩ B - the intersection, the only region that is in A and in B at the same time.
YOUR TURN · d
One region of this Venn diagram is shaded. Write the set notation for this shaded region.
A ∩ B' (in A, not in B).

In the expression (A ∪ B)', what does the complement dash apply to – just B, or the whole bracket? Explain how you know.
The whole bracket - the dash sits outside the brackets, so (as with any bracketed expression) it applies to everything inside them, i.e. to the union A∪B as a single region, not just to B.
