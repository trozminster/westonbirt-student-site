Finding Gradients

Gradient turns "steep" into a number. It says how fast one quantity changes as another increases, on a hill sign or on a phone bill.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Gradient measures steepness: the vertical change divided by the horizontal change.
  • The gradient of a straight line is the same wherever along it it is measured.
  • A positive gradient rises from left to right and a negative gradient falls.
  • A larger size of gradient means a steeper line, whatever its sign.
  • A horizontal line has gradient 0

Steepness as a number

The gradient is the vertical change divided by the horizontal change. On a straight line it is the same wherever it is measured.

Up, down or level

A line rising left to right has a positive gradient. A falling line has a negative gradient. The steeper the line, the larger the gradient's size.

A line rising from left to right has a positive gradient, and a falling line a negative one. A horizontal line has gradient 0, and the larger the size of the gradient, the steeper the line.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

A gradient is a rate

In context, the gradient is the change up for each one unit across. Its units are vertical unit per horizontal unit.

In context, the gradient is how much the vertical quantity changes for each one unit across. Its units are the vertical unit per horizontal unit.

Sign and size

A positive gradient means the quantity is increasing. A negative one means it is decreasing. The larger the size, the faster the change.

A positive gradient means the quantity is increasing, and a negative one that it is decreasing. The larger the size, the faster the change, whatever the sign.
