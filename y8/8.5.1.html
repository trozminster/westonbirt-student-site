Ratio and Equivalence

A cordial bottle says "dilute 1 part squash to 4 parts water" - get that ratio wrong and the drink is undrinkable either way. Today you learn to simplify, compare and interpret ratios, the tool behind every recipe, mixing instruction and scale.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Any two quantities can be linked multiplicatively, not only obvious pairs such as 3 and 6 but any pair such as 3 and 5.
  • A ratio a:b records that relationship, and multiplying or dividing both parts by the same number gives an equivalent ratio.
  • A ratio is simplified by dividing both parts by their HCF, and both parts must be in the same unit before that is done.
  • In the form 1:n, n is found by dividing both parts by the first part.
  • In the ratio a:b, the first part is ab of the second and aa+b of the whole.
By the end you can
G3Simplify ratios, including those with mixed units and those written in the form 1:n.
G5Express each part of a ratio as a fraction of the other part and of the whole, using a ratio table to show equivalent ratios.

Ratio and equivalence

A ratio a:b records how two quantities are linked. Multiply or divide BOTH parts by the same number for an equivalent ratio.

Any two quantities can be linked multiplicatively - not just familiar pairs like 3 and 6, but any pair such as 3 and 5. A ratio a:b records this relationship. Multiplying or dividing BOTH parts by the same number gives an equivalent ratio - the relationship between the parts hasn't changed, only how it's written.

Simplifying

Simplify by dividing both parts by their HCF. Write both parts in the SAME UNIT first.

A ratio is simplified by dividing both parts by their HCF, leaving no common factor behind. Both parts must be written in the SAME UNIT first - a ratio can't be simplified while one part is in cm and the other in m.

The 1:n form

For the form 1:n, divide BOTH parts by the first part. Then n is whatever the second part becomes.

To write a:b in the form 1:n, divide BOTH parts by a (the first part) - this makes the first part 1, and n is whatever the second part becomes.

Fraction of the other part, fraction of the whole

In a:b, the first part is ab of the second part. It is aa+b of the whole. Check which one the question asks for.

In the ratio a:b, the first part is ab of the second part, and aa+b of the whole. These are two different fractions, from two different questions - always check which one is being asked for.

Ratio tables

A ratio table lists equivalent ratios, each row scaled by one multiplier. Here 3 : 4 is scaled by 2 and by 3.

A ratio table lists a sequence of equivalent ratios, each row scaled from the original ratio by the same multiplier - useful for reading off any equivalent pair without recalculating from scratch each time.
RATIO AS FRACTIONS

Explain what it means for a ratio to be "simplified", and explain why both parts must be in the same unit before you do it.
A simplified ratio has no common factor left between its parts (other than 1). Both parts must be in the same unit first, or the numbers being divided wouldn't represent the same kind of quantity, making the simplification meaningless.
