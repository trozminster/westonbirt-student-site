Expanding Multiple Brackets

Real expressions rarely have just one bracket. Expanding several at once, and handling a minus in front, is the skill behind every perimeter and area expression you will form.

By the end you’ll know…
  • An expression with several brackets is expanded one bracket at a time, then simplified by collecting like terms.
  • A negative outside a bracket changes the sign of every term inside.
  • Subtracting a whole bracket changes the sign of every term in it: 5−(x−2) =5−x+2
  • A perimeter adds all the side lengths and an area multiplies two of them, so both give expressions to expand and simplify.
By the end you can
G5Expand and simplify expressions with two or more single brackets, including with a negative outside.
G6Form and simplify expressions from perimeter and area contexts.

One bracket at a time

An expression with several brackets is expanded one bracket at a time. Only then are the like terms collected.

A negative outside

A negative outside a bracket changes the sign of every term inside. Subtracting a bracket does the same: 5−(x−2) =5−x+2

Perimeter adds, area multiplies

A perimeter adds every side length, so its expression collects like terms. An area multiplies two lengths, so its expression expands a bracket.

Split a compound shape

A compound shape is split into rectangles whose areas are added. Each area is an expression, expanded before the like terms are collected.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A rectangle is (3x+1) cm long and (x−2) cm wide. Find an expression for its perimeter.
Add all four sidesP=2(3x+1)+2(x−2)
Expand both brackets=6x+2+2x−4
Collect like terms=8x−2
