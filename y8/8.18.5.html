Topic 8.18 · Transformations · Lesson 5 of 5

Describing Transformations

Two shapes on a grid: which single move takes one onto the other, and have you said enough for someone else to repeat it exactly? Today we describe transformations completely, and combine them.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A transformation is fully described only when all its defining information is given.
  • A translation needs its column vector, and a reflection needs its mirror line.
  • A rotation needs a centre, an angle and a direction; an enlargement needs a centre and a scale factor.
  • One transformation is named, not several, unless a combination is what the question asks for.
  • Two transformations applied in turn can often be described as a single one, and the order they are applied in can change the result.

What each description needs

A translation needs a column vector, a reflection a mirror line. A rotation needs a centre, an angle and a direction. An enlargement needs a centre and a scale factor.

A translation needs a column vector, and a reflection needs its mirror line. A rotation needs a centre, an angle and a direction; an enlargement needs a centre and a scale factor.

Which way round it faces

A reflection flips a shape, so its image faces the other way round. A rotation or a translation keeps it facing the same way round.

Two moves, one description

Two transformations in turn can often be replaced by one. Reflect in x=0, then y=0: a half turn about (0,0).

Two transformations in turn can often be replaced by a single one. Reflecting in x=0 and then in y=0 is the same as a half turn about (0,0).

The order can matter

The same two transformations in the other order can give a different image. So a combination is carried out, and described, in the order given.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
