Percentage Multipliers

A shop advertises "20% off, then an extra 10% off at the till" - and that is NOT the same as 30% off, however tempting the shortcut looks. Today you learn to turn any percentage change into a single multiplier, and combine successive changes correctly.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A percentage multiplier turns a percentage change into a single multiplication.
  • p% of an amount is a multiplication by p100, so 35% of an amount is x0.35
  • An increase of p% is a multiplication by (1+p100), so a 12% rise is x1.12
  • A decrease of p% is a multiplication by (1−p100), so a 12% fall is x0.88
  • Successive changes multiply their multipliers together, and the result is not the same as adding the percentages.

The multiplier

A percentage multiplier makes a percentage one multiplication. For p% of an amount, multiply by p100

A percentage multiplier turns a percentage change into a single multiplication. To find p% of an amount, multiply by p100 - so 35% of an amount is the same as multiplying by 0.35

Building the multiplier

Divide the percentage by 100 to get the decimal multiplier. Then multiply the amount by it directly.

Convert the percentage to a decimal by dividing by 100 (moving the decimal point two places), then multiply the amount by that decimal directly - no need to find 10% or 1% blocks first, though they're a good check.

Increase and decrease multipliers

An increase of p% multiplies by (1+p100). A decrease of p% multiplies by (1−p100). The 1 keeps the original 100%.

An increase of p% is a multiplication by (1+p100) - so a 12% rise is × 1.12
A decrease of p% is a multiplication by (1−p100) - so a 12% fall is × 0.88
The "1" represents the original 100% being kept, before the change is added or removed.

Successive changes

For two changes in a row, multiply the TWO MULTIPLIERS. Never add the percentages. The second change acts on the new amount, not the original.

Applying two percentage changes one after another means multiplying the TWO MULTIPLIERS together, not adding the two percentages - the second change applies to the amount AFTER the first change, not to the original amount.
TWO DISCOUNTS IN A ROW
A £40 jacket is first reduced by 20% in a sale, then a further 10% is taken off at the till. Find the final price using multipliers.
Write the first multiplier1−0.20=0.80(a 20% decrease)
Multiply by 0.80£40×0.80=£32
Write the second multiplier1−0.10=0.90(a 10% decrease)
Multiply the NEW price£32×0.90=£28.80
Multiply the two multipliers0.80×0.90=0.72
Check£40×0.72=£28.80
A single 30% off would give £28, so it is not the same(£40×0.70=£28)
