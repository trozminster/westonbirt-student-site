Volume of Prisms and Cylinders

A tin of beans, a Toblerone box and a swimming pool are all prisms: one shape pushed along a length. Today we find how much any prism holds, and work backwards from what it holds.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A prism has the same cross-section along its whole length.
  • The volume of any prism is cross-sectional area x length.
  • A cylinder is a prism with a circular cross-section, so its volume is pi x r² × h.
  • All dimensions must be in the same unit before multiplying, and a volume unit converts by the cube of the length factor: 1 m³ = 1000000 cm³.
  • A missing dimension is found by dividing the volume by the rest of the formula.

Learn

Read each card, then follow the worked examples one step at a time.

The same cross-section all through

A prism has the same cross-section along its whole length. So its volume is the area of that cross-section × the length.

A cylinder is a prism

A cylinder's cross-section is a circle, so its volume is π × r² × h. It is the radius that is squared, never the diameter.

One unit first

Every length must be in the same unit before multiplying. A volume converts by the cube of the length factor. So 1 m³ is 1 000 000 cm³.

Every length must be in the same unit before multiplying. A volume converts by the cube of the length factor, so 1 m³ is 1 000 000 cm³.

Working backwards

A missing dimension is found by dividing the volume by the rest of the formula. For a cylinder, h = V ÷ (π × r²).

Isometric drawings

On isometric paper vertical edges stay vertical. The other edges follow the grid's slanted lines. Only the visible edges are drawn.

On isometric paper the vertical edges stay vertical and the other edges follow the grid's slanted lines. Only the edges that can be seen are drawn.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
A cylinder has volume 500 cm³ and radius 5 cm. Find its height.
