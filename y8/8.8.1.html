Topic 8.8 · Area and Circumference of Circles · Lesson 1 of 4

Parts of a Circle

Every wheel, plate and running track is built from the same handful of circle parts - and confusing a radius with a diameter, or a chord with a tangent, is the easiest way to get every circle calculation this year wrong from the very first step. Today you learn to name every part precisely, then use the simplest circle formula to find the distance all the way around.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The radius runs from the centre to the edge and the diameter right across through the centre, so the diameter is twice the radius.
  • The circumference is the distance all the way around the circle.
  • A chord joins two points on the circle, and a tangent touches it at exactly one point.
  • An arc is part of the circumference; a sector is bounded by two radii and an arc; a segment is bounded by a chord and an arc.
  • The circumference is C=π×d, so from a radius the diameter is found first.
By the end you can
G3Label the parts of a circle correctly and convert between radius and diameter.
G4Calculate the circumference of a circle using C=π×d, given the radius or the diameter.

The circle's own measurements

The radius runs from the centre to the edge. The diameter goes straight through the centre: twice the radius. The circumference is the distance all the way round.

A circle is defined by its centre and its radius - the fixed distance from the centre to every point on the edge. The diameter runs from one edge of the circle to the other, straight through the centre, so it is always exactly twice the radius. The circumference is the total distance around the outside of the circle - the circle's own perimeter.

Lines and regions

A chord joins two points on the circle. A tangent touches it at exactly one point. A sector is cut off by radii, a segment by a chord.

A chord joins two points on the circle; a diameter is the chord through the centre. A tangent touches the circle at exactly one point. A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc, and a segment by a chord and an arc. An arc is any part of the circumference. A sector is the pie slice; a segment is the piece a chord cuts off.
NAMING CIRCLE PARTS
A circle has a diameter of 14 cm. Convert this to the radius, then identify the name of a line drawn from the centre to the edge, and the name of a line touching the circle at exactly one point.
The diameter is 14 cm. The radius is half the diameter, since the diameter runs across through the centre and the radius only reaches the centre: r=14÷2=7 cm.

A line from the centre to the edge is a radius (this is what we just found).
A line touching the circle at exactly one point, without crossing it, is a tangent - it does not pass through the circle at all, unlike a chord.

The circumference formula

Circumference: C=π×d. That is π times twice the radius.

The circumference - the distance all the way around a circle - is C=π×d. Since the diameter is twice the radius, this is the same as saying the circumference is π times twice the radius.

When the radius is given

Given the radius, double it first to get the diameter. Then use the formula.

If a question gives the radius rather than the diameter, the diameter must be found first, by doubling, before the formula is used.
CIRCUMFERENCE FROM DIAMETER
