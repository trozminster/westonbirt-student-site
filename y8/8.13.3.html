Topic 8.13 · Distance-Time and Conversion Graphs · Lesson 3 of 5

Speed from Gradients

Speedometers, sat-navs and race splits all rest on one fact: the gradient of a distance-time graph is the speed. Today we read speeds off graphs and find the average speed of a whole journey.

By the end you’ll know…
  • The gradient of a distance-time graph is the speed.
  • Its units come from the axes: distance units divided by time units.
  • A steeper section is a faster speed, and a horizontal section a speed of zero.
  • Average speed over a whole journey is total distance divided by total time, including any time spent stopped.
  • Average speed is not the average of the separate speeds, unless each stage took the same time.

The gradient is the speed

The gradient of a distance-time graph is distance divided by time, which is the speed. Its units come from the axes, such as kilometres per hour.

Minutes into hours

Change minutes into hours before dividing, for a speed in km/h. 15 minutes is 0.25 hours, never 0.15

A time in minutes must become hours before dividing, if the speed is to be in km/h. So 15 minutes is 0.25 hours, never 0.15

  2. Find the speed of the last stage.

Last stage10÷1=10 km/h(60−50=10 km in 1 hour)

Learn

Average speed uses totals

Average speed is the total distance divided by the total time for the whole journey. Any time spent stopped still counts in the total time.

Not the mean of the speeds

Stages that take different times cannot simply be averaged. The longer stage has more effect on the average speed.

WORKED EXAMPLE C
A van drives 90 km in 1.5 hours, stops for 30 minutes, then drives 60 km in 1 hour. Find its average speed.
Add the distances90+60=150 km
Add the times1.5+0.5+1=3 hours
Divide distance by time150÷3
Average speed50 km/h
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Find the average speed
for the whole journey in km/h.
Total distance15+15=30 km
Total time120 minutes=2 hours
Average speed30÷2=15 km/h
YOUR TURN · c
A runner runs 6 km in 40 minutes, rests for 10 minutes, then runs 4 km in 30 minutes. Find the average speed in km/h.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Add the distances → Add the times → Divide distance by time → Average speed
10 km in 80 minutes; 10÷80×60=7.5 km/h
YOUR TURN · d
A car travels for 2 hours at 50 km/h, then for 1 hour at 80 km/h. Find its average speed.
Hint. Follow the worked example: Total distance → Total time → Average speed
100+80=180 km in 3 hours; 180÷3=60 km/h, not 65
