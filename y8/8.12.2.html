Compound Shapes

Floor plans, gardens and car parks are rarely one simple shape. Splitting them into shapes you know, with every length in the same unit, gives their area and perimeter.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A compound shape is split into rectangles, triangles, parallelograms and trapeziums whose areas are added.
  • It can instead be treated as a larger shape with a piece subtracted.
  • Only the outside edges count towards the perimeter, and missing lengths are deduced from the ones given.
  • All lengths must be in the same unit before any area is calculated.
  • Converting an area between units squares the conversion factor, so 1 m² =10000 cm².

Learn

Split or subtract

Split a compound shape into simple shapes and add their areas. Or take a piece away from a larger shape.

A compound shape can be split into rectangles, triangles, parallelograms and trapeziums whose areas are added. It can instead be treated as a larger shape with a piece taken away.

The perimeter is the outside

Only the outside edges count towards the perimeter. Any missing length is deduced from the lengths that are given.

WORKED EXAMPLE A
Find the area of the shape.
Split into two rectangles: 10×4+6×4 =40+24 =64 cm².
WORKED EXAMPLE B
Find the perimeter of the shape.
The missing sides are 12−7=5 cm and 9−5=4 cm, so the perimeter is 12 +5 +5 +4 +7 +9 =42 cm.

One unit throughout

All lengths must be in the same unit before any area is calculated. Mixing metres and centimetres gives an area that is wrong by a factor of 100

Converting an area squares the factor

A 1 m square is 100 cm by 100 cm. So 1 m² =10000 cm², not 100 cm².

Since 1m=100 cm, a square of side 1 m is 100 cm by 100 cm. So 1 m² =10000 cm², not 100 cm².
