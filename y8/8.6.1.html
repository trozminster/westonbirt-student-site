Topic 8.6 · Solving Harder Equations · Lesson 1 of 3

Multi-Step Equations

A phone contract charges a fixed monthly fee plus a rate per gigabyte, and a rival charges a different fee and rate. Finding where they cost the same needs an equation with brackets and the unknown on both sides - today you solve these, including answers that come out negative or fractional.

By the end you’ll know…
  • However complicated an equation looks, the method is unchanged: expand any brackets, collect the unknown onto one side, then apply inverse operations.
  • Brackets on both sides are expanded before anything is collected.
  • Collecting the smaller unknown term onto the other side avoids a negative coefficient.
  • A solution may be negative or fractional, and is checked the same way, by substituting it back.

Learn

The method never changes

Every equation uses the same three steps, in order. Expand every bracket, collect the unknown on one side, then isolate it.

however complicated an equation looks, the same three steps apply in order: expand every bracket, collect the unknown onto one side, then apply inverse operations to isolate it.

Expand everything first

every bracket on both sides must be expanded before any collecting begins. Collecting terms while a bracket is still unexpanded misses whatever is hidden inside it.

Choosing which side to collect onto

Collect the smaller unknown term onto the side with the larger one. That keeps the coefficient positive.

once both sides are expanded, collect the smaller unknown term across to the side with the larger one. This keeps the remaining coefficient positive, which is easier to work with than a negative one.

Learn

Same method, new outcomes

Solve with the same three steps: expand, collect, isolate. A negative or fractional answer is not a mistake.

solving for the unknown on both sides uses exactly the same three steps as before - expand, collect, isolate - but the answer is not guaranteed to be a positive whole number. A solution may come out negative or fractional, and that is not a sign of a mistake.

Checking any solution

Check any solution the same way. Substitute it into BOTH sides of the ORIGINAL equation. The two sides must come out equal.

whatever the solution looks like, it is checked the same way every time - substitute it back into BOTH sides of the ORIGINAL equation and confirm the two sides come out equal.

Negative and fractional answers are exact

A negative or fractional solution is still exact. Never round it or adjust it.

a negative solution is simply a value below zero, and a fractional solution is simply not a whole number - neither needs rounding or adjusting, because the equation has been solved exactly.
