Angles in Parallel Lines

Parallel lines turn up everywhere from road markings to the back bearings you'll calculate next lesson - and every angle they create follows just three shapes, once you can spot them. Today you learn to name and use corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles with confidence.

By the end you’ll know…
  • A transversal is a line crossing two or more other lines.
  • Where a transversal crosses parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal (the F shape).
  • Alternate angles are equal (the Z shape).
  • Co-interior angles sum to 180° (the C or U shape).
  • These relationships hold only when the lines are genuinely parallel, which is why diagrams mark them with arrows.

Transversal

A transversal is a line that crosses two or more other lines. Across parallel lines, marked by matching arrows, it creates three angle relationships.

A transversal is a line that crosses two or more other lines. When it crosses a pair of parallel lines (marked with matching arrows), it creates three angle relationships.

The three shapes

Corresponding angles form an F shape and are equal. Alternate angles form a Z shape and are equal. Co-interior angles form a C or U shape and sum to 180°.

Corresponding angles sit in the same position at each crossing point and form an F shape - they are equal. Alternate angles sit on opposite sides of the transversal, between the two parallel lines, and form a Z shape - they are equal. Co-interior angles sit on the same side of the transversal, between the two parallel lines, and form a C or U shape - they sum to 180°.

The condition

These facts hold only when the lines are parallel, shown by matching arrows. Without the arrows, no relationship is guaranteed.

These facts only hold when the two lines are genuinely parallel - shown by matching arrows on the diagram. Without that marking, no relationship is guaranteed.
WORKED EXAMPLE A
Two parallel lines, marked with arrows, are crossed by a transversal. Angle a sits above the top parallel line and to the right of the transversal. Angle b sits in the matching position at the bottom crossing.
Modela and b occupy the same position at each crossing point, forming an F shape, so they are corresponding angles: a=b.
Given a=65°, b=65°.
WORKED EXAMPLE B
On the same diagram, angle c sits below the top parallel line, to the left of the transversal, inside the space between the two parallel lines
angle d sits above the bottom parallel line, to the right of the transversal, also inside that space. Model: c and d sit on opposite sides of the transversal, both inside the space between the parallel lines, forming a Z shape, so they are alternate angles: c=d. Given c=108°, d=108°.

Naming the shape, then the rule

Identify the shape the angle pair forms: F, Z or C/U. Apply the rule: equal for F and Z, sum to 180 for C/U. State the reason with the answer.

Finding missing angles in parallel-line diagrams follows the same idea as any angle problem: identify which shape (F, Z, or C/U) the angle pair forms, apply the matching rule - equal for corresponding and alternate, sum to 180 for co-interior - and state that reason alongside the answer.

Two-step diagrams

Some diagrams need two steps. An angle found with one parallel-line fact often feeds the next.

Where a diagram needs two steps, an angle found using one parallel-line fact often becomes the information needed for the next.
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal
one angle is given as 63°. Find the angle that is co-interior to it. Model: co-interior angles sum to 180, so the missing angle=180-63=117°. Reason: co-interior angles sum to 180 (lines marked parallel).
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. The angle above the top line, right of the transversal, is 75°. Angle k sits above the bottom line, left of the transversal.
Modelthe angle above the bottom line, right of the transversal, sits in the same position as 75°, so it is 75° (corresponding angles are equal, lines marked parallel). k sits beside it on the bottom line, so k=180-75=105° (angles on a straight line sum to 180°).
No single fact joins 75° to kit takes both steps.

1
In your own words, what is a transversal, and why do the corresponding, alternate and co-interior facts only apply when the two lines it crosses are parallel?
A line that crosses two or more other lines. Only parallel lines meet it at the same angle, so only then are the pairs fixed.
2
In a diagram, angle j and angle k form a Z shape between two parallel lines. What type of angle pair is this, and what can you say about their sizes?
They are alternate angles, so j=k.
3
Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. One angle is 71°. Find the angle that is co-interior to it, naming the fact you use.
109° (co-interior angles sum to 180: 180−71=109).
