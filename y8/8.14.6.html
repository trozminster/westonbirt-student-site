Expanding Double Brackets

Two brackets multiplied together make the quadratic expressions at the heart of GCSE algebra. Today is every term times every term, and the patterns that make some expansions quick.

By the end you’ll know…
  • Expanding double brackets multiplies every term in the first bracket by every term in the second, giving four products before simplifying.
  • (x+a)2 means (x+a)(x+a), so it expands to x2 +2ax +a2, not x2+a2
  • (x+a)(x−a) gives x2−a2, the difference of two squares, because the two middle terms cancel.
  • Triple brackets are expanded two at a time, and that result is then multiplied by the third.
  • A bracket in a factorised expression is itself a factor of the whole expression.
By the end you can

Every term by every term

Multiply every term in one bracket by every term in the other. That gives four products before collecting like terms.

Expanding double brackets multiplies every term in the first bracket by every term in the second. That gives four products before any like terms are collected.

Squared brackets and two squares

(x+a)2 =x2 +2ax +a2, not x2+a2
(x+a)(x−a) =x2−a2: the middle terms cancel.

Learn

More terms, more products

Two terms times three terms gives six products. Expand triple brackets two at a time, then multiply by the third.

A two-term bracket times a three-term bracket gives six products. Triple brackets are expanded two at a time, and that result is then multiplied by the third.

A bracket is a factor

Each bracket in (x+2)(x+5) is a factor of the expression. So x+2 divides x2+7x+10 exactly.

In a factorised expression such as (x+2)(x+5), each bracket is a factor of the whole expression. So x+2 divides x2+7x+10 exactly. To show that an expansion is true, expand one side until it matches the other, and finish with "as required".
WORKED EXAMPLE C
Expand and simplify (x+2)(x2−3x+4).
Write the expression(x+2)(x2−3x+4)
From the grid=x3 −3x2 +4x +2x2 −6x +8(x times each term, then 2 times each term)
Collect like terms=x3 −x2 −2x +8
WORKED EXAMPLE D
Show that (x+1)(x+2)(x+3) =x3 +6x2 +11x +6
Write the left side(x+1)(x+2)(x+3)
Expand the first two=(x2+3x+2)(x+3)
Term × term=x3 +3x2 +3x2 +9x +2x +6
Collect like terms=x3 +6x2 +11x +6
This is the right side, as required
